RATES · CURVE · By G. Mehlman

The 10-year sits at 4.99% as the curve holds a steep 96bp over bills

Longer-dated US Treasury yields are holding near 5%, and the gap between short and long rates is wide. As of October 23, the 10-year yield stood at 4.99%, the 30-year at 5.32%, and the 3-month bill at 4.03%. The 3-month-to-10-year spread of about 96 basis points is the steepest part of the curve, while the 10-year-to-30-year gap is only about 33bp. Most of the steepening sits between the front end and the belly, not at the long end.

That shape is consistent with a rise in term premium, meaning investors are demanding more compensation for holding longer-dated debt. The data points that way, but it does not show why that compensation is rising. Whether the 10-year can close above 5% and stay there is the test. A sustained close above that level would put pressure on duration-sensitive sectors such as long-dated bonds, rate-sensitive equities and mortgage-linked assets, and would tighten financial conditions further.

Watch whether the 10-year holds the 5% line in the coming sessions, and whether the 5-year-to-30-year spread of about 51bp widens or narrows as the story develops. The full article sets out what it would take for the level to hold.
