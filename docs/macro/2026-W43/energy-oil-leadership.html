Energy's 12% month: how much of the oil move is priced?

In one sentence
XLE is up 11.75% over 20 days against 14.29% for USO, so energy shares have not outrun crude, while falling natural gas makes this an oil story.

Energy is the one corner of the US equity market that is winning on both short and longer horizons, and it is doing so on the back of crude oil. The Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) leads all sectors over five and 20 days. The United States Oil Fund (USO), a widely held proxy for crude futures, closed at 169.53. That is up 4.80% over five days and 14.29% over 20. The question for the sector is whether shares have already priced that move, or whether they are still catching up to it.

The raw numbers lean toward the second answer, with caveats.

The scoreboard

XLE has gained 1.60% over five days and 11.75% over 20. USO has gained 14.29% over the same 20 days. Equities captured roughly 82 cents of every dollar of percentage gain in the fund, which is a respectable pass-through for a basket of producers, refiners and services companies. It does not suggest shares have sprinted ahead of the commodity.

The most recent week points the same way, and more sharply. Over five days USO rose 4.80% against 1.60% for XLE, about a third of the move. Either energy equities are discounting part of the latest oil rally as temporary, or they are lagging a move that has yet to be absorbed. Both readings fit the data. The tape alone cannot separate them.

Two cautions apply. USO holds futures contracts and rolls them, so its return can differ from the spot price of crude. XLE also holds companies with their own balance sheets, hedges and mix of businesses. The comparison is a guide to direction and scale, not a precise measure of what is priced.

An oil story, not an energy story

The gas market points the other way. The United States Natural Gas Fund (UNG) fell 10.13% over five days and 13.93% over 20. Whatever is lifting oil is not lifting every fuel. Gas has its own drivers, including storage, weather and regional supply, and over this window those have pushed prices down.

That split limits how broadly the sector's strength can be read. XLE is dominated by oil-linked companies, so it can rise while gas falls. The 20-day gain is a bet on crude and on the companies that sell it. It is not evidence of a broad energy upswing. Readers following the commodity divide across regions can find the exporter-importer angle in [commodity-split-exporters-importers].

What a reversal would do

The asymmetry runs through the same pass-through ratio. If USO gave back its 20-day gain, a pass-through near 0.8 would suggest XLE surrenders a large share of its own. Because energy has been the sector with both short- and long-window leadership, a retreat would remove a leader rather than trim a laggard. The effect would extend beyond energy, because the sector's strength has been a defining feature of the market's recent tone, as discussed in [energy-led-defensive-tape].

There is a second channel. Higher oil feeds costs elsewhere in the economy and sits alongside a long end of the Treasury curve near 5%. The danger that desks flag is the two moving together, not either alone, a point taken up in [oil-and-long-yields-tail] and in [us-energy-cost-long-end]. A fall in oil would relieve one of those pressures even as it dented energy shares.

What to watch

The cleanest test is the gap itself. If USO holds near 169 and XLE closes some of the distance, the lag was real and the equity gain is not stretched. If USO slips while XLE holds, the shares will have run ahead. The next weekly fund and sector prints will show which, along with whether natural gas stops falling, which would widen the sector's base beyond crude.
