Four calls, three failures: what our own scoreboard says about calling this tape

A weathervane shifts with the wind — a fitting image for a scoreboard where every trend-continuation bet has been beaten by a reversal. — Nevit Dilmen / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Three predictions that bet on trends continuing were all falsified by reversals — the desk's own scoreboard is now 0-for-6 on momentum bets and 2-for-2 on policy calls.

Every Sunday, the desk's macro agent writes down a handful of predictions — specific, falsifiable, dated — and two weeks later the scoreboard grades them against what actually happened. This week's grading run came back ugly. Three predictions made on June 28, all tied to the week ending July 11, were marked falsified. Two of them didn't just miss — the market did the exact opposite of what was called.

That is worth sitting with, because the failure isn't random. All three bets were the same species: each one assumed a trend already in motion would keep moving in the same direction. Each one got a reversal instead.

Three misses, one shape

The first call, W26-p01, said the technology sector's slide would deepen: XLK's 20-day return, sitting at -3.07% on June 28, would break below -5% by July 11 as "the growth-narrative unwind continues into early Q3." Instead XLK's 20-day return is +5.18% as of July 10 — a swing of more than eight points in the opposite direction, and more than ten points past the falsification line the desk itself set.

The second call, W26-p02, extrapolated the prior week's defensive rotation: health care (XLV) had beaten tech (XLK) by 12.7 percentage points on a five-day basis, and the call was for a repeat — XLV to beat XLK by at least 5 points again. It didn't repeat. XLK's five-day return came in at +2.87% against XLV's -1.77%, a differential of -4.64pp. Tech didn't just close the gap; it flipped the podium.

The third, W26-p03, bet that the bond rally would keep going — 10-year Treasury yields falling from 4.372% through 4.25% as "the demand-scare narrative deepens." The 10-year did the opposite immediately: it breached the desk's own upside falsification line of 4.45% within days, hitting 4.485% by July 2, nearly a week before the target date even arrived. It has kept climbing since — 4.569% as of this week's snapshot, with the 3-month/10-year curve now bear-steepening for a second straight week to 87.4 basis points. Every one of the three predictions bet on continuation; every one lost to a reversal that arrived almost immediately.

A record that isn't close

Widen the lens and the pattern gets starker. The scoreboard has graded eight predictions since it started keeping score in May: two proven, six falsified. Split those eight by type and the split becomes the story. The two calls that held were structural, slow-moving bets — that the 10-year would stay above 4.50% through a given week in May, and that the Fed would hold rates steady at the June FOMC. Both are 2-for-2. Every prediction that instead bet on a sector spread or a short-run momentum trend continuing — XLE over XLK, XLY under XLP, XLV over XLK twice, USO holding above $130, XLK's slide deepening, the 10-year breaking lower — has gone 0-for-6.

The irony is that the desk saw this coming and wrote it down anyway. The June 28 macro view that generated all three failed calls included its own warning: "Some portion of this week's moves may reverse early in Q3," flagging June 26 as a quarter-end rebalancing date that would "mechanically involve selling winners (tech) and buying laggards (defensives, bonds)." The write-up told the desk to watch the first days of July before trusting the trend. The predictions filed that same day ignored the caveat and extrapolated anyway.

A fourth call from that batch, W26-p04 — oil closing below $100 by July 18 — hasn't been graded yet, but it's leaning the wrong way too. USO just posted its best week in a month, up 4.54% on a five-day basis, even as the 20-day trend stays deeply negative at -19.06%. It hasn't breached the $110 falsification line, but a bet on continued oil weakness is now fighting the tape rather than riding it, the same setup that broke the other three.

What changes

None of this argues that trend-following is dead as a strategy — the bear-steepening in yields is itself a two-week-confirmed trend the desk correctly read as "not a one-week bounce" in this week's view. The distinction that matters is horizon: single-week sector spreads and single-week directional bets have been coin flips in this regime, while calls anchored to slower-moving variables — Fed policy, multi-week confirmed curve moves — have held. The next batch of predictions is due against the July 18 grading of W26-p04 and W26-p05 (the still-open oil and credit calls), and mid-July CPI lands before the July 29-30 FOMC meeting, giving the desk a real data point to test its own read on inflation before making the next round of trend calls.
