Energy and Health Care lead for a second month — this rotation has structural legs

HF Sinclair's Puget Sound refinery, Anacortes, Washington — energy infrastructure stocks have now led the sector table for two consecutive months. — Walter Siegmund / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.5)
In one sentence
Energy, Health Care, and Financials are the only sectors up on both 5-day and 20-day windows — at two months, this is rotation, not a blip.

For the second consecutive month, three sectors are clearing both the five-day and twenty-day performance bar simultaneously: Energy, Health Care, and Financials. That triple confirmation is the tightest signal the sector return table produces — and two months of it converts what might have been a week's noise into something with structural legs.

The numbers are unambiguous. XLV, the health care ETF, posted +2.37% over five days and +5.73% over twenty — the strongest twenty-day return in the entire sector table. XLE notched +2.45% and +3.07% over the same windows. XLF, tracking financials, was the quieter contributor at +1.40% over five days and +1.45% over twenty, but positive on both. Meanwhile, growth-facing sectors remain in reverse.

The case for each

The energy argument is not complicated. Supply remains tighter than the forward curve suggested heading into the year, and cash flows from integrated majors look durable at current prices. When growth trades feel crowded and duration exposure feels risky, investors gravitate toward assets that generate cash whether or not the next Fed move arrives on schedule. Energy delivers that.

Health care is the more structurally interesting story. The sector carries essentially no currency sensitivity and no meaningful duration exposure — which means it does not move in lockstep with Treasuries the way staples or utilities do during a rates-driven selloff. Demand is inelastic by nature: prescriptions are filled, surgeries are scheduled, insurance premiums are collected regardless of the macro cycle. That combination — real cash flows, minimal cross-asset sensitivity — is what makes XLV's +5.73% twenty-day gain read as a genuine all-weather rotation rather than a tactical refuge trade. Healthcare has no natural adversary in the current environment; it is simply absent from most of the risks that are repricing everything else.

Financials have a simpler macro driver. The three-month-to-ten-year Treasury spread has moved back to +91 basis points — a slope that benefits bank net interest margins and flatters the near-term earnings trajectory of the sector broadly. XLF's gains are tracking that curve normalization closely, making the thesis legible: as long as the curve holds its pitch, the financials bid holds too. (The Rates desk has the fuller picture on what a reinverted-then-normalized curve means at 4.5% — see Ninety-one basis points of normality.)

What breaks the trade

Two months of persistence does not make the rotation bulletproof. Energy reverses quickly if crude comes off on demand pessimism — and there is evidence of growing China skepticism in the base metals complex, a signal worth watching given how tightly commodity currencies have tracked that deterioration. Healthcare's political exposure did not disappear with the 2025 drug pricing negotiations; a CMS reimbursement headline can interrupt multi-week momentum faster than any macro cross-current. And financials remain hostage to the rate environment: any sharp long-end repricing that runs ahead of credit quality will hurt XLF first.

The next meaningful read arrives with Thursday's initial jobless claims and next week's CPI print. If the labor market holds and inflation shows further easing, the macro backdrop underpinning this rotation stays intact. If either data point surprises to the hawkish side, watch the curve flatten — and watch financials give back their gains before the other two do.
