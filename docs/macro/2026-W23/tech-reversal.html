Tech's five-session reversal: digestion or the opening move of distribution?

The Nasdaq MarketSite display at 4 Times Square, New York City — home to the exchange that hosts most of XLK's top holdings. — Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA 3.0
In one sentence
XLK lost nearly all of its 20-day gain in five sessions — and with Communication Services already weakening on both time frames, the question is whether this is a correction or the start of distribution.

Technology was the market's best-performing sector over the prior twenty days. Then five sessions erased most of that. The SPDR Technology Select Sector ETF (XLK) closed the week ended June 6 down 5.61% — almost precisely reversing a +6.25% gain accumulated since mid-May. The speed matters as much as the magnitude: a month of patient accumulation was undone in a single working week.

The catalyst came from two directions at once. Broadcom reported fiscal second-quarter earnings on June 3 that beat consensus — AI semiconductor revenue of $10.8 billion grew 143% year-over-year, and non-GAAP EPS came in at $2.44 versus expectations of $2.39. None of it was enough. CEO Hock Tan guided third-quarter AI chip revenue at $16 billion, below analyst estimates of $17.2 billion, and declined to raise the full-year forecast for AI semiconductor sales. The market, priced for acceleration, read that as deceleration. Broadcom shares fell roughly 14% in after-hours trading Wednesday night. By Thursday afternoon the damage had spread: Marvell dropped around 16%, Micron 13%, AMD and Intel each shed roughly 11%.

Then the macro environment threw its own curveball. Friday morning's nonfarm payrolls showed 172,000 jobs added in May — nearly double Wall Street's forecast of around 90,000. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped five basis points to 4.54%. The dollar index, already firming, extended its weekly gain to +1.17%, bringing DXY to the 100 level. The combination landed hard on high-duration growth assets. "This is a goldilocks labor market that is on firm footing, but without adding an inflationary impulse," said Christopher Hodge of Natixis CIB Americas — a characterisation that, for equities priced for rate cuts, was cold comfort. The Nasdaq fell 4% on Friday, its worst single session since April 2025.

The signal in Communication Services

The XLK move does not sit in isolation, and that matters for interpreting it. Communication Services (XLC) was already trending lower on a longer time frame: down 3.47% for the week, but also down 4.86% over the prior twenty days. Unlike XLK — which had built and then surrendered a meaningful gain — XLC showed consistent weakness on both time frames. The rotation out of growth and high-multiple names was not a purely this-week phenomenon. It was already in motion before Broadcom's earnings and before Friday's payrolls print.

Consumer Discretionary (XLY) adds to the same picture: -4.97% for the week and -4.19% over twenty days. The selloff is not an isolated repricing of semiconductor risk. It is broad-based across the growth and consumer cyclical complex, with the heaviest concentration in the names most sensitive to real rates.

The math on real rates is straightforward. The 10-year at 4.54%, with the dollar firming, does not leave much room for the multiple expansion thesis that underpinned the May rally in XLK. High-multiple technology stocks are long-duration assets in all but name — their earnings are heavily weighted toward the out-years, which means their present value is disproportionately sensitive to discount rate moves. When the 10-year moves up and the dollar firms simultaneously, those valuations face a double headwind. The May rally was built partly on the hope that the Fed was done; Friday's jobs report put a rate hike back on the table, with CME FedWatch showing better than 60% probability of at least one more increase by year-end.

Correction or distribution?

The question the desk is now sitting with is structural. The 20-day return of +6.25% functioned as the evidence for a resumed uptrend — a clean break higher out of the April lows, with momentum and flows confirming. The five-session reversal doesn't yet flip that case. As long as XLK holds near or above the level from which the May rally launched, the technical read is still a correction within a larger uptrend: painful, but not a change of regime.

The alternative reading is harder to dismiss after this week. Distribution — the process by which institutional sellers quietly offload positions to later buyers at elevated prices — typically looks like a series of wide-ranging weeks with inconclusive closes near the high. That is an approximate description of what just occurred. The elevated volume on down days relative to up days during this reversal is worth watching. So is the behaviour of Communication Services: a sector that never confirmed the May rally is rarely a coincidence when the leading sector rolls over.

The line in the sand is the 20-day anchor itself. If XLK recovers and holds above the May launch point, the correction-within-uptrend interpretation survives. If the ETF breaks decisively lower — taking out the levels that defined the May base — the signal shifts from digestion to distribution. The next catalyst lands in roughly two weeks: June CPI, followed by the Federal Reserve meeting on June 17-18. Both data points will reprice real-rate expectations in real time. That is when the pattern resolves.
