Energy gives back 12% in a week as crude retreats

In one sentence
XLE fell 12.0% in five days alongside USO's 12.1% drop, but with USO flat over twenty days the week looks like an unwound rally, while natural gas is a separate, weaker story.

Energy stocks spent the week handing back what crude oil had given them. The Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) fell 12.0% over five trading days, almost exactly in step with the United States Oil Fund (USO), which dropped 12.1%. The size of the fall is easy to misread. Over twenty days USO is up 0.14%, which is flat. The week reads less as the start of a slide than as the undoing of an earlier climb.

An unwind, not a trend

The arithmetic is simple. If USO lost 12.1% this week and is level over the full twenty days, it must have gained roughly 14% in the fifteen sessions before. A fund that rose about 14% and then fell 12% ends up where it began, and that is what the screens show.

XLE has a similar shape with a worse ending. It is down 5.32% over twenty days, so the sector rose by something like 7.6% earlier in the window before this week's drop took it below its starting point. The equity move was larger than the oil move in the way that matters. Oil stocks did not just give back the run-up; they gave back a little more than the run-up.

That distinction changes how the week should be read. A twenty-day decline in crude would point to a market repricing demand or supply for the long haul. A round trip points to positioning: money that arrived on the way up and left on the way down, with no net change in the price. The one-for-one tracking between XLE and USO supports that reading. Equity holders in the sector are trading the commodity, not a separate view of earnings or balance sheets.

The data here cannot say what drove either leg. Neither the rise nor the fall comes with a catalyst in the figures, and this article does not assign one. The shape alone is evidence enough that the week is better described as a reversal than as a breakdown.

Gas tells a different story

Natural gas does not fit the oil narrative. The United States Natural Gas Fund (UNG) gained 2.97% over five days while crude was falling by twelve percent. Over twenty days, however, UNG is down 15.08%.

Run the same arithmetic and the fifteen sessions before this week were a decline of about 17.5%. The recent gain is a small bounce within a larger fall, not a recovery. Oil gave back a rise; gas has so far recovered only a sliver of a drop.

The two funds are moving on separate drivers, which is why the energy label can mislead. A caveat applies to both. USO and UNG hold futures contracts rather than physical barrels or cubic feet, so their returns include the cost of rolling from one contract month to the next. Gas funds in particular can diverge from the spot price. The figures describe the funds, and the underlying commodities may have done slightly better or worse.

What the sector number hides

XLE is dominated by integrated oil companies and producers whose revenues follow crude more than gas, so a twelve percent week in the sector fund alongside a modest gain in gas is not a contradiction. The sector is trading as a leveraged claim on oil. That is also why a flat month in crude can sit beside a five percent loss in the equities: the stocks overshoot in both directions, and this time the overshoot on the way down was bigger.

The week also belongs to a wider pattern in this edition, where technology and oil fell together and the bond market offered no cushion to either.

What to watch

The first test is whether USO holds near its twenty-day starting level. A further leg lower would turn a round trip into a genuine downtrend, and the question would then move to whether XLE's extra weakness persists or closes. For gas, the question is whether the 3% bounce can extend, or whether the larger 15% decline resumes. Next week's sessions will show which of the two energy stories has momentum.
