Communication services rebounds 4.9% but remains down on the month

In one sentence
XLC gained 4.89% in a week when tech lost 8.23%, but it is still down 5.06% over twenty days, which looks more like a rebound than a decoupling.

The communication services sector had the best week in the US market, and it did so while technology fell hard. The Communication Services Select Sector SPDR (XLC) gained 4.89% over five days. The Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) lost 8.23% over the same stretch. The question is whether that gap is a real break between the two groups or a rebound inside a sector that is still falling.

The twenty-day figures point to the second reading. XLC is still down 5.06% over that window.

The arithmetic of a bounce

A 4.89% gain this week and a 5.06% loss over twenty days imply something about the fifteen sessions before the week began. Working backwards, XLC fell roughly 9.5% over that earlier stretch (0.9494 divided by 1.0489 is about 0.905). The week's gain therefore recovered about half of a decline that was already well under way.

That fits a rebound better than a change of regime. A sector that had truly decoupled from tech would be flat or higher over the month, not five points lower.

Relative strength, with limits

The case for something more than a bounce rests on the comparison with technology. Tech's drawdown over the month is about 17%, a move examined elsewhere in this edition. XLC's 5.1% monthly loss is roughly a third of that. Over the month it has behaved like a milder version of the same sell-off, not like an asset class apart.

The composition of the two funds complicates the picture. XLC is built around a handful of very large platform and media companies, and some of those names trade with technology. That overlap argues against a clean separation. The sector fell with tech through most of the month and recovered while tech was still being sold. That is consistent with buyers looking for large, cash-generative businesses outside the hardware and software complex. It does not show that those buyers are leaving technology as a theme. No company-level data was available to test that directly.

The week's other winners suggest the buying was broad. Real estate (XLRE) rose 1.92% and consumer discretionary (XLY) rose 1.91%. Both gains are well short of XLC's. XLC's lead over the next-best sector was nearly three percentage points, which makes it an outlier within the rotation and not simply one of many beneficiaries. The week looks like money moving away from technology into several places, with communications taking the most.

What would change the verdict

The bounce reading would weaken if XLC held its gains on days when XLK falls again, and strengthened if the sector made up the remaining five points of its twenty-day loss while tech stayed lower. Rates matter too. Long-duration equities usually need bond yields to stop rising, and the bond market gave little help to equities this week, as the Rates coverage in this edition describes.

For now the evidence supports a modest conclusion. XLC was the best place to be this week, and it is still a loser over the month. A sector that is down 5% over twenty days and up 5% over five has had a rebound, and a decoupling from tech would need a longer record than one week. Next week's sessions will show whether XLC can hold its gain if technology falls again.
