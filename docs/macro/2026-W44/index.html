EDITORIAL · WEEKLY THESIS · By The Editor

The market is rotating out of growth and into things that pay

Tech and communications are down 10% in a month while health care, staples, utilities and energy lead, and a 4.93% 10-year is doing the work.

Money is rotating out of technology and communications stocks and into health care, consumer staples, utilities and energy, and the cause looks like higher interest rates rather than fear. Over the past 20 trading days the Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) has fallen 10.2% and the Communication Services fund (XLC) has dropped 9.5%. Health care (XLV), staples (XLP) and energy (XLE) each rose about 11% over the same stretch, with utilities (XLU) also gaining.

The 10-year Treasury yield sits near 4.93% and the 30-year is above 5.3%, which makes long-dated growth earnings worth less in today's terms and pulls money toward companies that pay cash now. Credit markets are not showing stress: the high-yield bond fund (HYG) rose 0.49% over five days against 0.40% for the investment-grade fund (LQD). Gold and silver have also fallen, which points to real interest rates rather than a flight to safety as the driver.

The thesis breaks if XLK recovers its 20-day losses, or if the 10-year yield drops below 4.6%, within two weeks. Watch the 10-year yield and the next few sessions of tech trading for which way this goes.

RATES · LONG END · By G. Mehlman

The 10-year at 4.93% and the 30-year above 5.3% keep pressure on duration

Long-dated US Treasury yields are doing most of the work in steepening the curve. The 10-year sits at 4.93% and the 30-year at 5.31%, against 3.97% at the three-month bill, a 96 basis-point gap between the shortest tenor and the 10-year. Most of the term premium sits beyond ten years. The 5-year is at 4.72%, so the 5s-10s step is about 21bp, while the 10s-30s segment adds roughly 38bp.

That back-end premium is the rate against which growth equities are being discounted, which is why long-duration stocks stay sensitive to every move in the long bond. The 10-year is only about 7bp below 5%. A close above that level would likely extend the rotation from long-duration growth toward shorter-dated, cash-generative names. This week, watch the 10-year against 5% and whether the 30-year holds above 5.3%.
