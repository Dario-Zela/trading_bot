RATES · LONG END · By G. Mehlman · 2026-11-01 · 4 min read

The 10-year at 4.93% and the 30-year above 5.3% keep pressure on duration

In one sentence
The curve is steep, with 96bp between the 3-month and the 10-year, and the long end is setting the price of equities.

The US Treasury curve is steep, and the steepness is not confined to the short end. Three-month bills yield 3.97%. The 5-year note yields 4.72%, the 10-year 4.93% and the 30-year 5.31%. That is 96 basis points between the front of the curve and the 10-year benchmark, and a long bond that sits well above the 5% mark. For equity investors the number that matters most is the one at the far end, because that is the rate used to discount distant earnings.

Walking the curve

The curve climbs in three stages of unequal size. From the 3-month bill to the 5-year note is 75 basis points, the largest single leg. From the 5-year to the 10-year is only about 21 basis points. From the 10-year to the 30-year is about 38 basis points.

The segments cover very different stretches of time, so the raw gaps flatter the middle. The 5Y-10Y leg spans five years and adds about 4 basis points per year of maturity. The 10Y-30Y leg spans twenty years and adds under 2 basis points per year. Per year of maturity, the curve is therefore flattening as it lengthens.

The 38 basis points beyond ten years still matter. They are what investors demand for locking money away for two more decades. A 30-year yield above 5.3% says that buyers of the longest bonds want a clear premium for inflation uncertainty, fiscal supply and the chance that the policy path surprises them. How the front end is priced is a separate question, taken up in a steep front end leaves the policy path as the swing factor.

Why the long end prices equities

A share is a claim on cash flows that arrive over many years. The further out the cash flows, the more sensitive the share's value is to the discount rate. That is the basic arithmetic behind the pressure on growth stocks: companies whose earnings are expected late are worth less when the 10-year yields 4.93% and the 30-year yields 5.31%.

This is the setting for the rotation described in the market is rotating out of growth and into things that pay. The drawdown in communication services and technology and the strength of defensive sectors fit a market adjusting to a higher hurdle rate. They do not, on their own, prove that rates are the cause, but the direction of travel is consistent with it.

A 5% 10-year is a psychological line as much as an analytical one. At 4.93% the benchmark is a few basis points from it. A move through 5% would turn a rate that has been rising into a headline level, and it would give income-oriented and defensive assets a more visible rival in Treasuries themselves. A risk-free 5% is a high bar for any equity whose return depends on distant growth.

Other markets suggest the story is about real rates rather than fear. The moves in gold and silver point to real yields, and credit has largely ignored the equity rotation, with high yield outrunning investment grade.

Levels for the week ahead

The 10-year at 4.93% and the 30-year at 5.31% are the two anchors. A close for the 10-year above 5% would extend the pressure on duration and on long-dated equity cash flows. A fade back below 4.9% would suggest the long end is catching its breath without changing the picture.

The shape of the curve matters as well. The 96 basis points between the 3-month and the 10-year, and the 38 basis points between the 10-year and 30-year, show where the market is asking to be paid. If the 10Y-30Y gap widens while the 5Y-10Y step holds near 21 basis points, the premium is building at the far end. That would be a different signal from a broad parallel shift higher. The longer risk is set out in a 5% long end is the tail risk that equity leadership has not priced.

For now the equity market's leadership has moved toward companies that pay out cash today, and the long end is the reason. The test is whether the 10-year can hold below 5% in the coming sessions, and whether the 30-year, already above 5.3%, keeps drifting up.
