Tech rebounds 3.4%, still down 7.2% on the month

In one sentence
Tech's 3.4% weekly bounce reads as mean reversion after a 7.2% monthly slide; a rotation would need renewed outperformance with the 10-year holding above 5%.

A week's bounce can look like a recovery. In technology it is not one yet. The sector's benchmark exchange-traded fund, XLK, gained 3.36% over the last five sessions, and the communication services fund, XLC, added 3.72%. Over 20 sessions, though, XLK is still down 7.19%, the worst of the eleven sectors, and the leadership of the month belongs to other parts of the market.

What led the month

The 20-day table is an unusual mix. Health care is first at +6.87%, followed by consumer discretionary at +6.69%, financials at +5.7% and consumer staples at +5.4%. Two of those are the classic places to hide, and two are the classic places to be when growth is expected to hold up. Technology and, to a lesser degree, communication services (XLC is down 1.48% over the same stretch) sat at the other end.

The pairing of defensives and cyclicals at the top is what makes the month hard to label. It does not describe a market running toward safety, nor one chasing growth. It describes a market that has marked down the long-duration earnings streams at the heart of the technology index while leaving most of the rest alone.

Reading the bounce

The week's move is best read as mean reversion. A sector that has fallen more than 7% in a month tends to attract buyers on any pause in selling, and a 3.4% rebound recovers less than half of the 20-day loss. Nothing in the sector figures suggests that money has changed its mind about where to sit. The leaders over 20 days are still the leaders.

The broader backdrop explains why the bounce carries limited weight. Long-dated Treasury yields have been the pressure point across this week's coverage, with the long end at 5.48% and a curve 103 basis points steep, as detailed in the rates pieces. A higher discount rate bears hardest on companies whose profits sit furthest in the future, which is a fair description of much of XLK. If that is the force that drove the month's decline, a one-week reprieve does not remove it.

What would make it a rotation

A rotation back into technology would look different from this week. It would need two things together: technology outperforming the market again, and the 10-year Treasury yield holding above 5%. The second condition matters most. Leadership that persists while rates are high would show that investors have stopped treating yields as the sector's main risk. A rebound that fades once yields rise again would confirm that this week was a pause in the month's trade rather than the end of it.

Until both conditions appear, the more defensible reading is the simpler one. A strong week for XLK and XLC has not repaired a month in which health care, discretionary, financials and staples did the leading. The next test is the coming sessions' relative performance of the two funds against the broad index, set against where the 10-year closes each day.
