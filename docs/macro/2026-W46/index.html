EDITORIAL · WEEKLY THESIS · By The Editor

The stress is in duration, not in credit

With the 30-year near 5.48%, the week's damage landed on long-duration IG bonds and bullion rather than junk credit, pointing to term premium and real yields, not default fear.

Equities rose across all eleven S&P sectors over the past five days, but the bond and bullion tape tells a less comfortable story. Investment-grade credit (LQD) fell 1.65% over the week, more than high-yield bonds (HYG), which slipped just 0.38%. That 1.27-point gap in favour of junk debt points to investors repricing the cost of holding long-dated money, not a sudden fear of companies defaulting.

The yield curve shows where the pressure sits. The 10-year Treasury closed at 5.15% and the 30-year at 5.48% as of 13 November, leaving the 3-month to 10-year spread at +103 basis points. Gold and silver also sold off, GLD down 4.6% and SLV down 5.8%, even as the dollar index rose 1.09% to 103.41. Bullion usually finds support when the dollar weakens, so its slide against a firmer greenback suggests the move is about real yields and term premium rather than a flight to havens.

The equity bounce looks fragile against that backdrop. Technology is still down 7.2% over 20 days, even as the broader index recovered. The thesis is that the rally holds only if the long end stops cheapening. The falsifier is a rally in long-dated Treasuries accompanied by a softer dollar, which would indicate the week's moves were positioning rather than a repricing of duration. Watch the 30-year yield and the dollar index in the sessions ahead.
