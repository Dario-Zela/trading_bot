EDITORIAL · WEEKLY THESIS · By The Editor

Everything that was crowded fell at once

Tech, metals and energy were liquidated together while a 4.91% 10-year and a firmer dollar tightened conditions; the next two weeks will show whether it was a flush or a tightening.

Technology, precious metals and energy were sold together last week, and even the defensive sectors that usually absorb selling fell. The technology sector ETF (XLK) dropped 12.49% over five days and 18.68% over 20, and every sector was negative over the week. Silver fell 16.22% and gold 9.17%. Consumer staples (XLP) lost 2.05%, utilities (XLU) 3.07% and real estate (XLRE) 4.30%, so no sector offered a clean refuge. The most plausible common driver is tighter financial conditions: the 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.91%, the 30-year at 5.30%, and the dollar index at 103.81, up 1.96% over 20 days.

The read is testable. If this is forced selling, the 10-year should settle back below 5% and the dollar should stop rising, with metals and tech bouncing within one to two weeks. A 10-year move above 5%, or high-yield bonds (HYG) continuing to lag investment-grade bonds (LQD), would instead point to a genuine tightening of conditions rather than a flush.
