US assets took the hit while the long end refused to help

In one sentence
US equities, credit and commodities all weakened as a 4.91% 10-year, a 5.30% 30-year and a firmer dollar tightened financing for a market already deleveraging.

The United States is absorbing a tightening of financial conditions from three directions at once. Long-term borrowing costs are climbing, the dollar is firming, and a market that was already shedding positions has found no buyer willing to step in. The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.91%, nine basis points short of 5%. The 30-year has already gone through it, at 5.30%. Over the past five days, all 11 sectors of the S&P 500 have fallen.

A broad decline of that kind says less about any single company than about the price of money. When every sector falls together, the common factor is usually the discount rate, and that is what the bond market is now setting.

A curve that steepens against the market

The shape of the Treasury curve matters as much as its level. A 30-year yield of 5.30% against a 10-year of 4.91% puts the extra compensation for holding the longest bonds at roughly 39 basis points. Investors are demanding more to lend for three decades than for one, and they are demanding it while equities and credit fall. In a conventional risk-off episode, money runs to the long end and pushes yields down. Here the long end has declined to play that role.

That is the awkward part for a market already deleveraging. Mortgages, long-dated corporate issuance and the discount rates used to value equities are all priced off the long end. A steep curve that keeps rising raises the hurdle for every asset that depends on distant cash flows. It also makes the unwinding of leveraged positions harder to stop, because the usual safe-haven bid is missing. The wider unwind is covered in Everything that was crowded fell at once, and the yield levels themselves in The 10-year sits 9 basis points from 5% and the 30-year is already past it.

The damage across assets

Equities are the most visible casualty. Technology has fallen 18.7% over a month and energy 12.7%. Between them, the two sectors carry much of the index's recent drawdown, and the five-day sweep across all 11 sectors shows the selling has spread beyond them. Credit has weakened too. High yield lagged investment grade by 1.6 points over the week, the pattern expected when financing gets scarcer and lenders reprice the weakest borrowers first. The split is examined in High yield lagged investment grade by 1.6 points in a week.

Commodities, which might have offered a refuge from a rising dollar and rising yields, weakened as well. Energy's slide is largely an oil story, as set out in Energy is down 12.7% in a month and oil is the reason. Gold's failure to cushion the fall is the sharper signal, because a hedge that fails in the one scenario it exists for invites investors to question the rest of their protection (Gold failed as a hedge, which is the real warning).

The dollar adds to the squeeze

The dollar index has risen 1.96% over 20 days. A stronger dollar tightens conditions on its own account. It makes dollar-denominated debt more expensive to service for borrowers outside the US, and it trims the translated earnings of American multinationals. It also pressures commodity prices, which are quoted in dollars. The dollar's 20-day climb of 2% is the quiet tightener covers the currency in detail.

One caution on scope. The data available for this report contains no direct readings from non-US markets, so any claim about spillovers abroad rests only on the dollar and commodity moves. The inference is that a firmer dollar and weaker metals and oil are the channels through which US conditions reach everyone else, a pressure taken up in Emerging markets and commodity exporters face a dollar and metals double hit. Whether foreign bond or equity markets are falling in step cannot be confirmed from the figures here.

What to watch

The obvious threshold is 5% on the 10-year. A close above it would put the benchmark at a level it has tested only intermittently, and would test whether the equity selloff has further to run or has begun to price the new rate. The next readings to check are the 30-year's behaviour around 5.30%, whether the dollar's 20-day gain extends past 2%, and whether high yield continues to underperform investment grade. If long yields stop rising while the dollar holds its gain, the pressure on US assets may ease. If both keep moving, the deleveraging has no obvious floor.
