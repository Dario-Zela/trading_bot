Technology's one-week reign ends in a complete round trip

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang at CES 2025 — the chipmaker briefly lost its title as the world's most valuable company this week as AI-trade sentiment soured. — Pronoia / Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
In one sentence
XLK swung from +5.18% to -5.50% on a 20-day basis in a week, fully erasing its gain as the desk's rating flipped from Bullish to Bearish.

A week ago, technology was the sector everyone wanted to own. This week, it is the one nobody wants to be caught holding. The Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) has fallen 5.48% over the past five trading days and is now down 5.50% on a 20-day basis — a complete erasure of the 5.18% twenty-day gain it carried into last weekend, achieved in barely more than a single trading week. The desk's own rating on the sector has swung from Bullish to Bearish, the sharpest one-week downgrade on its entire coverage table.

This is not a cooling-off. A cooling-off would leave some residue of the prior move. This is a round trip: the sector has given back, almost to the decimal, everything it gained in the month before.

From leaderboard to bear market in seven sessions

The proximate cause was the semiconductor complex, which did not so much correct as fall out of bed. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index shed more than 20% from its late-June record high, marking its worst week since March. Micron Technology fell 13% ahead of its earnings report; Nvidia dropped more than 4% in a single Tuesday session before closing the week down 2.2%. The moves were large enough that Apple — up nearly 23% year-to-date on the back of a lighter capital-spending profile — briefly overtook Nvidia as the world's most valuable company on Friday, touching a $4.88 trillion market cap before Nvidia reclaimed the title by roughly $6 billion at the close. It was a symbolic moment as much as a financial one: money rotating away from the most obvious AI infrastructure bet and toward AI's more capital-light beneficiaries.

The drivers were not a single headline but a pile-up of them. A hawkish Federal Reserve signal on inflation the prior week pushed investors to sell this year's most crowded winners first. Reports that major hyperscale cloud providers are accelerating in-house, custom application-specific chip development — reducing their reliance on third-party silicon — rattled the thesis that Nvidia's data-center dominance is structurally durable. Renewed worry about Chinese competition in AI compute added a geopolitical layer. And with SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI all circling public listings, some of the selling looked like positioning: trimming exposure to richly priced AI names to make room for a fresh supply of AI-adjacent paper.

A sector with no multi-week memory

What makes this move worth dwelling on is not just its size but its company. This is the third XLK-related call the desk has made since mid-May, and all three have been falsified — not by small margins, but by tech moving hard in whichever direction the prediction bet against.

In late May, a bet that energy would outperform technology over two weeks was blown out when XLK returned 19.76% on a 20-day basis against XLE's -5.63%. Then, on June 28, with tech already 3% off its highs, the desk predicted the sector's 20-day return would break below -5% by July 11 as "the growth-narrative unwind continues into early Q3." It didn't — XLK ripped to +5.18% by that date, and a companion prediction that defensive Health Care would reassert leadership over tech was falsified the same day, by an even wider margin.

The uncomfortable footnote is that the June 28 call was directionally right — just early. The -5% twenty-day threshold that failed to arrive by July 11 has now been breached by July 17, at -5.50%. A forecast graded wrong nine days ago would have graded correct today. That is not evidence the desk has cracked tech's code; it is evidence that tech itself currently has no multi-week memory. Bulls who bought the June rally are underwater. Bears who sold ahead of the June 28 call and covered too early missed the payoff by a week and a half. Nothing about this sector — not the desk's calls, not the sector's own price action — has held its shape for more than one turn of the calendar.

What happens next

The immediate test is earnings. Micron reports imminently, and how the market treats its results — whether a beat is sold the way strength was sold this week, or bought as validation that memory pricing has genuinely turned — will say more about sentiment than the number itself. Nvidia's own report, along with any further detail on hyperscaler custom-silicon plans, is the next structural catalyst for the Nvidia-versus-in-house-chips debate. And with the Fed's next decision ten days out, a hold that reads as more hawkish than expected would give this week's risk-off tilt somewhere further to run. Until one of those catalysts produces a move that survives a second week intact, treating this week's reversal — or last week's rally, or the one before that — as the start of a trend would be exactly the mistake the desk's own record just finished making twice.
