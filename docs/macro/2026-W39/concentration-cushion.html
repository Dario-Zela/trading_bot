RISK · CONCENTRATION · By S. Vance · 2026-09-27 · 3 min read

Two stocks are doing a lot of work for the index

The New York Stock Exchange trading floor — nine of eleven S&P sectors are negative for the month even as the headline index holds up. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Tech and communication services are the only sectors positive this month, and the usual defensive ballast — utilities, real estate, financials — is falling hardest, not offsetting the risk.

Look past the index level and the picture gets less comfortable. Nine of the eleven S&P 500 sectors are negative over the past 20 trading days, averaging a loss of roughly 4.5%. The only two holding the tape up are Technology (XLK, +4.06%) and Communication Services (XLC, +1.39%) — the two sectors most exposed to the AI capital-spending story that has carried this market for most of 2026.

That concentration would matter less if the laggards were the usual cyclical suspects — the sectors expected to lag in a growth scare. They aren't. Utilities (XLU) are down 8.50% over 20 days, the worst in the market. Real Estate (XLRE) is off 6.94%, its sixth straight losing week. Financials (XLF) have slid 5.25% even as the yield curve steepens in a way that, on paper, should help bank margins. These three are traditionally where money rotates into when tech leadership wobbles — bond proxies, value plays, rate-sensitive ballast. Right now they're the worst performers in the index, not an offset to it.

A bear-steepener, not a risk-off

The mechanism is a bond-market story more than an equity one. The 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to 5.18% and the 30-year to 5.50%, with the 3-month/10-year spread widening to +111 basis points from +102bp a week earlier — a classic bear-steepener, term-premium and fiscal-supply concerns pushing up long rates while the front end reprices a slower pace of Fed cuts. Utilities, real estate and regional banks are the sectors most mechanically sensitive to that move: higher discount rates hit long-duration cash flows, and Utilities in particular has behaved almost like a levered bond position, moving inversely with the 30-year yield tick for tick this month.

Mega-cap tech and communication-services names, by contrast, have simply shrugged. Growth expectations tied to AI infrastructure spending have proven strong enough this month to absorb a nearly 20-basis-point weekly jump in the 10-year without denting the sector's advance. That resilience is the entire reason the S&P 500 doesn't look worse than it does.

Why the cushion is thin

The risk isn't that tech falters tomorrow — nothing in the data points to that. It's structural: with nine of eleven sectors already underwater and the traditional defensive rotation destinations posting the deepest losses of any group, there is no visible mechanism to catch the index if mega-cap leadership stalls. In a healthier market, weakness in growth sectors gets absorbed by strength in value, defensives or rate-sensitive plays reversing course. Right now those sectors are falling for the same reason — rising long rates — that would be the likely trigger for a tech wobble in the first place. A stumble in AI-driven leadership and a continuation of the bear-steepener would be pulling in the same direction, not offsetting ones.

The next test is whether Technology can keep posting gains if the 30-year pushes toward the 5.60%–6.00% range some desks are now pricing in for the coming quarter. A negative weekly print from XLK alongside still-rising yields would be the first real sign that the absorption is ending — and, on the current sector map, there is nothing obviously waiting to catch the market if it does.
