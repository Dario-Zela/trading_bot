SECTORS · ENERGY · By I. Aresti · 2026-10-18 · 3 min read

Energy follows oil higher and extends a 6% monthly gain

In one sentence
XLE rose 4.9% on the week and 6.1% over twenty days, capturing about half of USO's 9.2% jump, while natural gas fell 3.2% and sat out the rally.

Energy shares rose with crude this week, and the sector now holds the strongest one-month record on the board. The open question is whether stock investors are paying for oil that stays expensive or are still catching up to a price that has already jumped.

The Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) gained 4.86% over five trading days and is up 6.11% over twenty, the best 20-day showing of any sector. The United States Oil Fund (USO), which tracks crude futures, rose 9.15% over the same five days. The two moved in the same direction and on the same days, but not by the same amount.

Half the move

XLE captured a little over half of USO's weekly gain (4.86 against 9.15). That gap is the heart of the pricing question, and it can be read two ways.

The first reading is scepticism. Equities are long-dated claims on cash flow. A producer's value rests on what oil fetches over years, not on this week's front-month contract. A 9% spike in the spot price, if investors expect it to fade, should lift an energy stock by much less than 9%. On this view, XLE's smaller move is the market saying it does not expect today's oil price to last.

The second reading is lag. Energy stocks have already risen 6.11% over twenty days, so some of the oil move was priced earlier. If crude holds near current levels, earnings estimates would have to rise, and the sector could extend its gain as analysts catch up.

The data fit both readings, which is the useful finding. The sector is rewarded for the move but is not treating it as permanent. A single week cannot tell which view is correct. A second week of firm crude would favour the lag reading, and a retracement in oil with XLE holding its gains would suggest the equity market had looked through the spike.

Gas goes its own way

Natural gas did not join in. The United States Natural Gas Fund (UNG) fell 3.18% on the week, even though it is still up 2.40% over twenty days. Gas trades on its own supply, storage and weather factors, and the week's oil rally did not carry over to it. The split is a reminder that "energy" is several markets under one label. Crude drove this week's sector gain, and gas did not contribute.

Where it sits in the tape

The energy gain came in a week when investors favoured safety and commodities over growth, a pattern set out in the week's editorial. Technology had its worst week on the board, while staples and health care led the defensive bid. Energy is an odd member of that group. It is cyclical, yet it behaved as a hedge against the same inflation and supply worries that hurt long-duration assets.

The oil move also reaches beyond equities. Higher crude is the input that matters for the long end of the bond market, where a steep curve has left little room for error. If energy stocks are betting that oil stays high, bond investors are being asked to take the same bet from the other side.

What to watch

The next test is whether crude holds this week's gain. If USO keeps its 9% jump and XLE closes more of the gap, the catch-up reading gains weight. If oil gives back the move while XLE holds, the market had already discounted the spike. Weekly inventory data and the next Treasury auctions, where oil's effect on long-term yields will show, are the near-term checkpoints.
