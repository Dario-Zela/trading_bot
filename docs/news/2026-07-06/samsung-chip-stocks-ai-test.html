Samsung earnings become next test for jittery chip-stock investors

Samsung Town, the company's headquarters complex in Seocho-gu, Seoul. — Oskar Alexanderson / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Samsung's Tuesday earnings, expected to show profit up eighteenfold on AI memory demand, are the next test of whether the chip-stock selloff was a pause or a warning.

Samsung Electronics Co. reports second-quarter earnings on Tuesday, and for once a single corporate print looks capable of moving sentiment across an entire asset class. Global chip stocks have just been through their most violent stretch in years — a rally that produced the best quarter semiconductor shares have ever put up, followed almost immediately by a selloff that tripped a circuit breaker on South Korea's stock exchange. Investors now want to know which version of the story is real.

In one sentence: Samsung's Tuesday earnings, expected to show profit up eighteenfold on AI memory demand, are shaping up as the test of whether the AI chip trade's recent selloff was a healthy pause or the start of something worse.

A rally that outran itself

The numbers behind the swings are startling even by chip-cycle standards. On June 23, the Kospi index fell 10% in a single session, triggering a trading halt, as Samsung and SK Hynix — which together account for roughly 40% of the index's weight, up from about a quarter at the end of last year — shed a combined $290 billion in value. The index had swung 16% over 48 hours earlier in June after Broadcom issued AI chip guidance that fell short of what the market had priced in. On July 2, Samsung and SK Hynix shares each fell more than 9% again as a rout that started on Wall Street spread to Seoul. Then, a report that Anthropic was in talks with Samsung to help develop a custom AI chip — a move that would sideline some of the company's existing U.S. chip partners — hit American memory names directly: SanDisk fell more than 14% in a single session, Western Digital dropped 10%, and Micron slid 5.5%.

None of this happened in a vacuum. Thirty-day volatility across the top 20 global semiconductor stocks has climbed to its highest level since 2020, a reflection of just how much of the AI trade now runs through a handful of memory makers. Samsung and SK Hynix control the supply of the high-bandwidth memory chips that feed AI accelerators, and DRAM prices rose more than 40% in the second quarter alone, with NAND prices up over 50%, according to HSBC. That kind of pricing power is exactly what has powered the rally — and exactly what makes investors nervous it can't last.

What Tuesday's print needs to show

Analysts compiled by Bloomberg expect Samsung to report preliminary operating profit of 84.3 trillion won ($55.1 billion), an eighteenfold increase from a year earlier, on revenue that surged 127% to a record 169 trillion won — a single quarter's profit that would dwarf the company's entire 2025 total. The bar, in other words, is already extraordinarily high.

"A print near the consensus settles the argument in Samsung's favor," said Dave Mazza of Roundhill Financial, capturing the binary nature of what's riding on Tuesday. Citigroup has raised its price target on the stock to 530,000 won from 460,000 won ahead of the release. And despite Samsung shares roughly doubling over the past quarter, the stock still trades at just 5.7 times forward earnings — cheap next to Micron's 7 times and far below the 24 times commanded by the broader semiconductor index, an unusual gap for a company sitting at the center of the AI capex story.

Not every voice is unreservedly bullish. Jian Shi Cortesi of GAM Investment Management noted that "memory stocks have traditionally peaked a few months before memory prices hit their cyclical top" — a reminder that a blowout quarter can also mark the beginning of the end of a pricing cycle, not just confirmation that it still has room to run. Samsung's own guidance will matter as much as the headline profit number: the company has signaled a "meaningful increase" in 2026 capital spending, including roughly $20 billion aimed at advancing 1c-nanometer HBM production, part of a plan to expand high-bandwidth memory capacity by about 50% this year as it chases a 15-20% share of the HBM4 market by the fourth quarter.

The SK Hynix wrinkle

Samsung's earnings arrive days before a second catalyst that could compound the volatility either way. SK Hynix, the world's second-largest memory chipmaker, is set to debut American depositary receipts on the Nasdaq on July 10 under the ticker SKHY, in an offering of up to 17.79 million new shares expected to raise close to $29.5 billion. If it prices near expectations, it would be the largest ADR listing on record, surpassing Alibaba's $21.8 billion New York debut in 2014. Proceeds are earmarked for new fabrication capacity, including the Yongin semiconductor cluster and a packaging plant in Cheongju, plus EUV lithography equipment — spending that itself depends on the same AI memory demand Samsung's results are meant to validate.

That sequencing — Samsung's earnings Tuesday, SK Hynix's Wall Street debut days later — means algorithmic and discretionary positioning in the chip sector has two closely spaced events to trade around, not one. A soft Samsung print, or cautious commentary on pricing beyond the current quarter, could easily bleed into how the SK Hynix listing is received, and vice versa.

What's next

The immediate test is straightforward: does Samsung's operating profit land at or above the 84.3 trillion won consensus, and does management's commentary on HBM pricing and 2026 capacity extend the current cycle or hint that it's peaking. Traders will also be watching guidance on DRAM and NAND contract pricing for the third quarter, the detail that most directly determines whether Micron, SanDisk and Western Digital retrace their recent losses or resume the slide that the Anthropic report set off. From there, the SK Hynix ADR debut on July 10 becomes the next data point — a listing whose reception will say as much about appetite for the AI memory trade as Samsung's numbers do.
