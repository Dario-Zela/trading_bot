AstraZeneca partners with China's Abbisko on lung cancer drug combination

In one sentence
AstraZeneca will test Tagrisso alongside a Chinese biotech's experimental PD-L1 pill, one of several China tie-ups struck without disclosed financial terms.

AstraZeneca has agreed to test one of its best-selling lung cancer drugs alongside an experimental compound from a Shanghai biotech, in a deal that is small in disclosed terms but notable in what it signals about where Western drugmakers are placing their oncology bets.

Abbisko Therapeutics Co. Ltd. said Wednesday it has entered a strategic collaboration with AstraZeneca Plc to jointly study a combination therapy for patients with EGFR-mutated, PD-L1-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) — a subgroup that tends to respond poorly to EGFR-targeted drugs alone. The trial pairs Abbisko's lumipodlin (ABSK043), a first-in-class oral small-molecule PD-L1 inhibitor, with AstraZeneca's Tagrisso (osimertinib), the company's third-generation EGFR-TKI and one of its largest revenue drivers. China's National Medical Products Administration cleared the investigational new drug application on May 20, clearing the way for a multicenter, open-label Phase I/II study that Abbisko will lead, with both firms sharing clinical trial duties.

In one sentence: AstraZeneca will test its blockbuster lung cancer drug Tagrisso alongside a Chinese biotech's experimental pill, the latest in a run of China tie-ups but one disclosed without financial terms.

A gap Tagrisso alone can't close

The clinical logic is straightforward enough. Patients whose tumors carry both an EGFR mutation and high PD-L1 expression — a marker tied to how tumors evade the immune system — tend to get less mileage out of Tagrisso than patients with low or negative PD-L1 levels. Lumipodlin, still an unapproved compound in early-stage trials in China and Australia, is designed to bind PD-L1 and trigger its breakdown inside the cell, with preclinical data described as comparable to approved PD-L1 antibodies. No oral PD-1/PD-L1 small molecule has yet reached market, so the pairing is as much a bet on a novel drug class as on the specific combination.

Part of a pattern, not a one-off

What makes the tie-up worth watching is context rather than content. AstraZeneca has committed to investing $15 billion in China through 2030 and has struck a run of deals with Chinese biotechs this year, including agreements with CSPC Pharmaceutical worth up to $18.5 billion for obesity treatments and up to $1.77 billion for kidney-disease therapies. Pfizer and Bristol Myers Squibb have each put more than $16 billion into Chinese drugmaker collaborations since the start of 2025, according to industry trackers — a scale that puts this week's Abbisko announcement, disclosed with no upfront payment, milestone figures or royalty structure, closer to routine pipeline-tending than to a marquee bet.

Abbisko, listed in Hong Kong with a market capitalization near $859 million, already has one approved drug in China (pimicotinib, for tenosynovial giant cell tumors) and a pipeline of 21 candidates. This is not its first brush with AstraZeneca — the two have previously licensed compounds — though neither company detailed a broader relationship in Wednesday's release.

What to watch

Without financial terms, the deal itself tells markets little beyond confirming that Beijing's regulator is still willing to wave through advanced combination trials involving foreign drugmakers — itself a modest data point on China's openness after periods of friction over drug pricing and IP rules. The real test comes later: whether Phase I/II data, expected only after the study enrolls and reports results, shows the combination clears the response-rate bar that Tagrisso alone does not. Until then, this reads as one line item in AstraZeneca's broader China build-out rather than a standalone catalyst.
