Fed rate-hike odds edge above 50% after Warsh's hawkish turn

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, whose Jackson Hole remarks pushed September rate-hike odds above 50%. — Official Federal Reserve portrait, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Fed funds futures price 55-60% odds of a September rate hike after Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks, with Thursday's CPI now the deciding factor.

Fed funds futures have flipped from a coin-toss against a September rate hike to a coin-toss in favor of one — and the man who moved the needle only gave one speech to do it.

When Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh took the podium at Jackson Hole on August 28th, traders had priced roughly a one-in-three chance of a 25-basis-point hike at the September 15-16 FOMC meeting. By the time he finished, that probability had jumped past 50%. It now sits at roughly 55-60% on the CME's FedWatch tool, with the broader odds of at least one hike before year-end climbing to around 70%.

"We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do. That's our job."

That line, delivered in Wyoming, did the repricing. Warsh acknowledged the economy's underlying strength and conceded that recent CPI and PCE readings had come in better than feared. But he argued a few soft prints don't prove the trend has turned, and reaffirmed the Fed's 2% target — measured by the PCE index — as "firm" and "fixed." Markets read it as a chair unwilling to declare victory, at a fed funds range of 3.50%-3.75% that plenty of officials still consider too loose given headline CPI running at 3.4% year-over-year in July.

The committee isn't unified

Not every voice on the FOMC is leaning the same way. Governor Christopher Waller said on September 3rd that he'd be inclined to support holding rates steady, on one condition: continued evidence of disinflation in the data due before the meeting. "If this continues in the data due over the next two weeks, I would be inclined to support holding the target for the federal funds rate at its current setting," Waller said — a direct bet that Thursday's CPI print comes in soft.

That sets up a genuine swing vote sitting on a genuine swing data point. Economists expect core CPI to rise around 0.2% on the month, with headline inflation ticking down to roughly 3.3% year-over-year from 3.4%. A print in that range or cooler hands Waller's camp the ammunition to argue for a hold. A hot surprise — particularly if it confirms the Cleveland Fed's nowcast showing core PCE reaccelerating toward 3.4%-3.5% — would likely cement the hike camp's majority and validate the market's post-Jackson Hole repricing.

For traders, the practical problem is timing rather than direction. The window between Thursday's data and the September 15-16 decision is narrow enough that repositioning around a single CPI print carries real risk — a hike resets the discount rate used to value every rate-sensitive asset, from long-duration equities to mortgage-backed securities, and a coin-flip Fed leaves little room to fade the outcome cleanly either way.

The next fixed point is the FOMC's rate decision itself on September 16th, alongside an updated dot plot that will show how split the committee really is. Until then, Thursday's CPI is the only data point left standing between a hawkish chair and a wavering governor.
