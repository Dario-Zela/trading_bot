Gauff sets up US Open semi-final against new world No.1 Rybakina

Coco Gauff, whose comeback from match points against Mirra Andreeva set up a US Open semi-final against new world No. 1 Elena Rybakina. — Hameltion / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Gauff saved two match points to beat Andreeva in three sets, setting up a US Open semi-final against Elena Rybakina, tennis's new world No. 1.

Elena Rybakina became the world No. 1 in women's tennis on Sunday night in New York without so much as a champagne toast — she barely had time to process it before turning her attention to the semi-final draw. Coco Gauff, three courts and several hours of nerve either way, had to save two match points just to make sure Rybakina had someone to play.

Rybakina's coronation had been drawn up on a spreadsheet for days: beat Qinwen Zheng in the quarter-final and the ranking points would do the rest, regardless of what happened elsewhere in the draw. She did — 3-6, 6-1, 6-4 — ending Aryna Sabalenka's 99-week run at the top of the WTA rankings. "It's just a number right now and I'm in the semis," Rybakina said afterwards, sounding more relieved than triumphant. "It was a bit of a battle even inside myself. Then I found the way."

Gauff's escape act

The more dramatic tennis, and the story that will dominate Monday's back pages, belonged to Gauff. The No. 4 seed lost the first set to 19-year-old Mirra Andreeva in 26 minutes, then found herself two points from elimination at 5-6 in a second-set tiebreak. She saved both match points — the turning one arriving via a 39-shot rally she closed out with a drop shot — forced a decider, and ran away with it 6-2. Final score: 2-6, 7-6(7), 6-2, in two hours and nineteen minutes that included 12 double faults and 42 unforced errors from the eventual winner.

"Just believing is all I can do," Gauff said. "The game may not always show up, but I can control the belief in myself." It was her sixth win in as many meetings with Andreeva, and her twelfth comeback victory of the season — a pattern that has become something of a signature since her 2023 US Open title, the last time she reached this stage of the tournament.

What it sets up

Gauff and Rybakina now meet in the semi-final with the ranking business already settled — whatever happens on court, Rybakina keeps her No. 1 spot, at least until the next set of rankings is calculated. That takes one layer of jeopardy out of a match that otherwise pits two very different games against each other: Rybakina's serve-and-power blueprint against Gauff's speed, resilience and increasingly reliable knack for finding a way through matches she has no business winning.

The winner advances to Saturday's final, where Jessica Pegula or another semi-finalist from the bottom half awaits. For a tournament that has otherwise followed the seeding sheet with unusual obedience, Gauff's escape from Andreeva is the one result that has left the draw looking less like a formality and more like a tennis match.
