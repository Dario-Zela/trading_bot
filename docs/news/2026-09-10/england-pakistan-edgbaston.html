England seize control of third Pakistan Test at Edgbaston

Edgbaston Cricket Ground, where Pakistan were bowled out for 133 on day one of the third Test. — Tony Hisgett / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Pakistan were bowled out for 133 on day one at Edgbaston, their third sub-40-over collapse in a series England had already won 2-0.

Pakistan's batting fell apart again at Edgbaston on Wednesday, bowled out for 133 in little more than two sessions as England's seam attack ran through a brittle order on the first day of the third and final Test. Josh Tongue took 4 for 42, Ollie Robinson 3 for 15 and Jofra Archer 3 for 25, and by the time Harry Brook and Emilio Gay had added 104 for England's fourth wicket, the hosts were 156 for 4 at stumps — 23 runs ahead with six second-innings wickets in hand.

It reads, on the scorecard, like the decisive session of a series decider. It isn't one. England won the first Test at Headingley by an innings and 103 runs and the second at Lord's by 194 runs, wrapping up the three-match contest with a match to spare. What's being decided at Edgbaston is whether England complete a clean sweep, not who wins the series — a distinction that changes how Wednesday's collapse should be read.

A pattern, not an accident

Pakistan's first-innings totals this series now read 171, 135, 110, 194 and 133. Strip out the one respectable score — 194 in the second innings at Lord's, built around Saud Shakeel's hundred — and the touring side have been bowled out inside 40 overs on three separate occasions in three Tests. That is not a single bad day on a seaming pitch; it is the same failure recurring against the same attack in three different conditions.

Edgbaston does have a reputation for helping the ball move on a fresh morning, and Joe Root had no hesitation putting Pakistan in after winning the toss. But the collapse owed as much to technique and application as to surface. Babar Azam was bowled for seven by a full, straight delivery from Tongue that did relatively little off the pitch. Only Shakeel, with 43 from 47 balls, offered the kind of application that had rescued the tourists at Lord's. Pakistan had also dropped seven players from their squad after that defeat, fielding a much-changed and inexperienced line-up against an attack that has now had a fortnight to find its rhythm.

England's attack, warmed up

For England, the day had a personal landmark buried in the carnage. Robinson's dismissal of Saim Ayub, leg before, was his 100th Test wicket, reached in his 24th match — a rate beaten since the war only by Tony Lock among England bowlers, and arriving with a bowling average of 19.34, reportedly the best of any bowler in over a century of Test cricket at the point of their hundredth wicket. Tongue's 4 for 42 was his third four-wicket haul of the series, a reminder that England's much-discussed seam reserves — Robinson, Tongue, Archer, with Mark Wood and Chris Woakes still in the background — have given Root selection problems of abundance rather than scarcity all summer.

Setup or surprise?

The honest answer sits between the two framings on offer. Edgbaston's pitch did seam and bounce more than a flat, early-September strip might be expected to, and England's attack is genuinely the best in the world at exploiting exactly that. But Pakistan's batting has not needed much help this series: three collapses in three Tests, against the same three seamers, is a selection and technique story as much as a conditions one. A touring top six already missing seven of its squad members offered little of the resistance Pakistan's batting unit at home can produce against spin — and found no substitute method for England's pace in England.

Play resumes Thursday with England well placed to push for a substantial first-innings lead and, from there, a 3-0 sweep. Pakistan's more pressing question extends beyond this match: whether a batting order that has now lasted fewer than 40 overs in three of five completed innings this series can be rebuilt before the away assignments — not just the scoreboard at Edgbaston — get harder.
