Salesforce in talks to buy AI research firm Listen Labs for $2bn

Salesforce Tower in San Francisco, headquarters of the company reportedly in talks to buy Listen Labs for $2bn. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Salesforce is reportedly negotiating to buy AI research startup Listen Labs for $2bn, roughly 4x its January valuation, extending its 2026 M&A spree.

Salesforce is in talks to buy Listen Labs, a three-year-old AI market-research startup, for roughly $2 billion — about four times the $500 million valuation investors put on the company eight months ago, according to reporting first surfaced by Business Insider and confirmed by outlets including PYMNTS and TechCrunch. Salesforce shares slipped nearly 2% on the news.

In one sentence: Salesforce is reportedly negotiating to buy AI research startup Listen Labs for $2bn, roughly 4x its January valuation, extending its 2026 acquisition spree.

Listen Labs, co-founded in 2023 by Florian Jüngermann and Alfred Wahlforss, uses AI moderators to run customer interviews at scale — reaching into a pool of 50 million participants to do the kind of qualitative research that market-research shops have traditionally staffed with humans. Clients include Microsoft, Canva, Anthropic and Sweetgreen. Annualised revenue sits at roughly $30 million, meaning a $2 billion price tag would value the company at close to 67 times sales.

From term sheet to talks

The deal's back-story is unusual even by this year's standards. Listen Labs had a signed $125 million Series C term sheet, led by Menlo Ventures at a $1.5 billion valuation, and walked away from it once Salesforce talks opened up — a move venture investors generally frown on, since scrubbed rounds can spook other suitors if the acquisition falls through. Neither Salesforce nor Listen Labs has commented publicly, and people close to the talks caution the deal could still collapse before signing.

Even if it doesn't, the arithmetic is striking: a company priced at $500 million in January, then $1.5 billion on a term sheet it never signed, now in discussions to fetch $2 billion nine months later. That is less a valuation than a moving target, repriced upward at each stage by whoever showed up with the biggest number.

Part of a pattern, not an outlier

Listen Labs would be at least the sixth Salesforce acquisition disclosed in 2026, following Momentum in February, Cimulate in March, the roughly $8 billion purchase of data-management firm Informatica in November 2025, and June's $3.6 billion deal for Fin, the customer-service AI agent business formerly known as Intercom. Chief executive Marc Benioff has framed the buying spree as building out the full stack beneath Agentforce, the company's AI-agent platform — from raw data (Informatica) to customer-facing agents (Fin) to, potentially, the research layer that tells enterprises what customers actually want (Listen Labs).

The strategic logic holds together. The valuation discipline is harder to defend. A 67x revenue multiple for a company with no public financials and a three-year operating history is the kind of number normally associated with the peak of a hype cycle, not measured strategic buying. Salesforce has the balance sheet to absorb it — the stock's 2% dip suggests investors are uneasy rather than alarmed — but each deal raises the bar for what the next one needs to look like, and pushes further out the point at which any of this AI spending shows up as incremental Agentforce revenue rather than acquired headcount and technology.

What to watch

Salesforce reports fiscal third-quarter earnings in early December, which will be the first real test of whether Agentforce and its recent acquisitions are converting into revenue growth rather than just cost. Whether the Listen Labs deal actually signs — and at what price — will also say something about how much further acquirers are willing to stretch for AI-adjacent assets before the market for them cools.
