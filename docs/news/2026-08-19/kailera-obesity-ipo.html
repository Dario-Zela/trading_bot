Kailera Therapeutics prices record $625m obesity-drug IPO

Nasdaq's Times Square market display, where Kailera Therapeutics began trading under ticker KLRA in April. — bfishadow / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Kailera priced the largest US biopharma IPO on record at $625m — its stock has since given back the pop, with pivotal data still two years away.

Kailera Therapeutics raised $625 million in its Nasdaq debut in April, the largest initial public offering by a US biopharmaceutical company on record. The Waltham, Massachusetts-based obesity drug developer priced 39.06 million shares at $16 apiece — above its original target — and closed its first day up 63%, valuing the two-year-old company at roughly $3.1 billion. Four months on, that valuation has come back to earth, and the gap between the opening-day euphoria and where the stock trades now is itself the story: a live test of how much investors will pay for a seat at the obesity-drug table before a single pivotal trial has read out.

A record built on unproven data

The $625 million raise beat the previous high-water mark for a venture-backed US biotech IPO — Moderna's $604 million debut in 2018 — and dwarfed recent obesity-sector listings such as Acelyrin's $540 million in 2023. Kailera's backers, led by Atlas Venture, Bain Capital Life Sciences and RTW Investments, had already put more than $1 billion into the company privately since its founding in October 2024, including a $400 million Series A and a $600 million Series B. The IPO proceeds were earmarked precisely: $625 million to fund three global Phase 3 trials of injectable ribupatide through the second quarter of 2028, another $150 million to push an oral version into late-stage testing, and $50 million for an earlier-stage candidate, KAI-7535.

What Kailera does not yet have is proof the drug works at the scale the market is pricing in. Ribupatide, also known as KAI-9531, is a GLP-1/GIP dual agonist Kailera licensed from China's Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals in 2024, alongside rights to develop and sell it everywhere except greater China, which Hengrui retains along with a 19.9% equity stake in Kailera. In a Chinese Phase 2 trial, a weekly 8mg injection produced 23.6% weight loss from baseline; an oral formulation topped 12%. Those numbers are competitive with the GLP-1 class leaders, but they come from bridging studies, not the Western population and dosing regimens regulators will ultimately judge. The company's own KaiNETIC Phase 3 program, spanning more than 4,700 patients, only began randomizing participants in January; full data isn't expected until 2028.

Wegovy and Zepbound's shadow

Kailera is walking into a market two companies already dominate. Novo Nordisk's Wegovy and Eli Lilly's Zepbound — along with their diabetes siblings Ozempic and Mounjaro — generated roughly $30 billion in combined quarterly revenue between the two firms earlier this year, and 2026 has brought both companies' oral GLP-1 pills to market, with Novo's version alone topping 3 million prescriptions within five months of launch. CEO Ron Renaud, a veteran of three prior drugmaker sales including Cerevel Therapeutics' acquisition by AbbVie, has argued publicly that no therapeutic category stays a two-player market forever, and that Kailera can differentiate on tolerability and on patients with the most severe obesity. The wider deal market backs the thesis that there's room: Pfizer paid roughly $10 billion for the obesity biotech Metsera late last year after a bidding war with Novo Nordisk, and mid-stage obesity names like Viking Therapeutics and Structure Therapeutics both carry market values above $3 billion despite lacking an approved product.

The valuation-discipline question

The cleanest evidence that the market has started pricing this more soberly sits in Kailera's own chart. Shares that closed their debut session at $26 have since drifted down to around $18, a decline of nearly 29% from the post-IPO high and roughly back toward the $16 offer price — even as the company reported a second-quarter net loss of $111 million against $1.17 billion of cash, funding it says will last into 2028, exactly when the pivotal data lands. Seven sell-side analysts still carry Buy or Strong Buy ratings with an average price target above $42, a gap between Wall Street's optimism and the market's current verdict that is itself worth noting. Any acquirer, meanwhile, would inherit up to $5.725 billion in milestone payments owed to Hengrui if ribupatide succeeds commercially — a structural claim on future profit that complicates the buyout math investors are implicitly betting on.

None of this makes Kailera unique so much as illustrative. A record-breaking raise for a pre-revenue asset with data two years out is a signal about the market's mood, not about the drug's odds — and moods, unlike Phase 3 endpoints, are reversible. The next hard data points are a Phase 2b readout expected in mid-2027 and additional early-phase results before then; until those land, KLRA's share price will keep functioning as a real-time referendum on how much the obesity trade still has left in it.
