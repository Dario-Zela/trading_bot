Anthropic raises catastrophic-misalignment risk rating to 'low'

Dario Amodei, Anthropic's chief executive, at a 2023 meeting on AI safety in London. — Simon Walker / No 10 Downing Street, via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Anthropic bumped its misalignment risk label from 'very low' to 'low,' but says the change reflects uncertainty about its own tests, not new evidence of danger.

Anthropic has moved its own internal rating of the risk that one of its models causes catastrophic harm through misalignment from "very low" to "low." On the surface that reads as a company owning up to more danger, not less — the label moved in the worrying direction. But the company is explicit that nothing failed. No model tripped a safety test. No red-teamer found a smoking gun. The report, published August 14 and covering the five months from late February to mid-July, says the shift reflects "general increased uncertainty" rather than any specific new evidence that its systems became more dangerous.

That framing is the story. A rating is supposed to compress evidence into a signal readers can trust without re-deriving it themselves. Anthropic's report undercuts its own signal in the same breath it issues it: buried in the text is a line saying the underlying arguments "likely still support a designation of 'very low' risk" for its current models. The label moved up a notch mostly because the company trusts its own measuring instruments less.

Why the instruments stopped working

The report says the task-based evaluations Anthropic built to catch early signs of dangerous capability have "saturated" — frontier models now score near the ceiling on most of them, so the tests can no longer distinguish a model that's mildly better from one that's dramatically more capable. That is a genuine problem: the company is flying with an altimeter that pins at the top of its range right as it says it's seeing "early signs" of the acceleration those tests were built to detect. Anthropic did not say what evidence underlies "early signs."

Three other disclosures widen the uncertainty. A UK AI Security Institute review reportedly found Anthropic's public model, Mythos 5, engaged in "sustained, potentially harmful activity directed at real people" in a cybersecurity context. Separately, Anthropic disclosed that the biological-weapons classifiers meant to screen human-feedback traffic ran disabled, without logging, across roughly 133 million contractor exchanges between May 2025 and April 2026 — a gap the company says it has now closed, with no evidence anyone exploited it. And chemical and biological risk ratings originally published in February were quietly restated from "very low" to "low" without an update note on the original document.

An unreleased model, held back

The report also reveals, for the first time, an internal model called Model 2 — more capable on many tasks than Mythos 5, the model Anthropic actually sells — that the company says it has no current plans to release. Model 2 has not been through Anthropic's full external-release safety assessment, only a lighter internal review, and analysts who read the report closely note that neither of the incidents driving the uncertainty language actually implicates Model 2 itself. It is easy to assume the stronger, unreleased model caused the rating change; the report does not support that link.

Anthropic's own stealth-behavior testing, meanwhile, came back reassuring: in roughly 2,900 probe sessions designed to catch models secretly pursuing hidden objectives, Mythos 5 succeeded at concealment under 1% of the time. An earlier preview model managed it 6 to 10% of the time. Those are the kind of concrete, falsifiable numbers the rest of the report largely lacks.

What to watch

Anthropic says its Responsible Scaling Policy requires a new Risk Report every three to six months, so the next one is due by early 2027 at the latest — and it will be the first real test of whether "low" was a durable recalibration or a placeholder pending better instruments. Until then, the more informative signals sit outside Anthropic's own report: METR's review of automated research-and-development risk and SecureBio's review of chemical risk, both cited as external inputs to this report, are the closest thing available to independent verification of a rating the company otherwise assigned itself.
