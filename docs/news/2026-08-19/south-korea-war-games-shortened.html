South Korea shortens joint war games with US at Washington's request

United Nations Command and US Forces Korea personnel at Camp Humphreys during a prior Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise. — Spc. Joshua Holladay / U.S. Army, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Trump ordered the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise halved mid-drill, citing cost and his rapport with Kim Jong Un — Seoul says it wasn't consulted first.

The annual rehearsal for a war on the Korean peninsula usually runs on for the better part of two weeks, with tens of thousands of American and South Korean troops moving through simulated invasions, counteroffensives and command-post drills designed to prove, above all, that Washington's commitment to Seoul's defence is not in doubt. This year it stopped early.

Ulchi Freedom Shield 2026 began on 17 August as scheduled, with around 18,000 South Korean troops and personnel from eleven other United Nations Command member states taking part alongside US forces. It was due to run for eleven days, through 27 August. Instead, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff confirmed this week that the exercise would wrap up on Friday 21 August — roughly half its planned length — after what Seoul described as a "proposal of the US side." The second phase, focused on counteroffensive operations, has been scrapped entirely, along with a partial scaling-back of combined field training.

An order from the top, not the Pentagon

The request did not originate with military planners weighing readiness against cost. It came directly from President Trump, who ordered the Pentagon hours before the exercise began to "substantially reduce" it. In a Truth Social post, he called the drills "costly, with much of these costs paid for by the United States of America (as usual!)" and said they sent a signal that was "totally inappropriate and hostile" toward North Korea, citing his "very good relationship" with Kim Jong Un. He added that the exercise was being trimmed rather than cancelled outright only because "it is too late to cancel."

A Pentagon official confirmed the mechanics of the cut: "Based on direction from the President and Secretary of War, the Department has substantially reduced Exercise Ulchi Freedom Shield while continuing training that enhances tactical capability. Associated live-training events have been reduced, with certain events canceled or converted to simulations."

Trump also tied the decision to an unrelated grievance, telling reporters that Seoul had declined to back a US military operation against Iran despite the American troop presence that protects it from North Korea: "We have 39,000 soldiers over there guarding you from Kim Jong Un, your next door neighbour, and you're not going to help us on a very easy military operation in Iran." The linkage suggests the exercise cut functions, in part, as leverage in a dispute that has nothing to do with the peninsula.

A reversal with no recent precedent

Washington has adjusted the size and cadence of its Korea exercises before — most notably suspending large field drills after the 2018 Trump–Kim summit in Singapore, a move made as part of a stated diplomatic bargain and announced before drills began. What is different this time, analysts note, is that a major combined exercise was scaled back abruptly after it was already underway, without a reciprocal step from Pyongyang and without the kind of formal diplomatic framework that accompanied the 2018 pause. North Korea has not offered any concession in return; a state commentary this week instead accused the allies of using the drills to test AI-enabled and uncrewed warfare systems against it and warned it would respond with "evolved new power."

South Korean officials have been careful in their public language, framing the change as a mutual adjustment of "period and scale" rather than a unilateral American climbdown. But the sequence — an order issued from Washington, implemented over a exercise Seoul had already committed troops to — leaves Seoul absorbing a decision it did not initiate.

What it signals beyond Korea

For six decades, large-scale joint exercises have served a dual purpose: sharpening interoperability between the two militaries and broadcasting resolve to Pyongyang and, implicitly, to Beijing. Cutting the exercise short without a matching move from North Korea reorders that logic, prioritising a bet on personal rapport with Kim over the demonstrative function the drills have traditionally served. Whether that bet pays off in reduced tension on the peninsula, or is read in Pyongyang and Beijing simply as reduced resolve, will only become clear over the coming exercise cycles — the smaller-scale Freedom Shield drills typically held in the spring will be the next test of whether this is a one-off or a lasting recalibration of how Washington signals commitment to South Korea.
