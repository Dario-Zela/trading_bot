Stripe finalises $7bn-plus acquisition of AI router OpenRouter

Stripe co-founder and CEO Patrick Collison, who has framed the OpenRouter deal as part of building "economic infrastructure" for the AI era. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Stripe is paying $7bn+ for AI router OpenRouter — over 5x its valuation from a $113m round just three months ago.

Stripe has agreed to buy OpenRouter, a three-year-old startup that lets developers route AI queries across more than 400 different models through a single connection, for more than $7 billion. The price is the headline: it values OpenRouter at over five times the $1.3 billion it was worth when it raised a Series B round just three months ago, in May.

That kind of markup, on that short a timeline, is the story here as much as the deal itself. It says something about how fast infrastructure incumbents are moving to lock down the plumbing beneath the AI boom — not the flashy foundation models everyone argues about, but the unglamorous layer that decides which model actually answers a given request.

What OpenRouter actually does

OpenRouter's pitch is simple. An app that wants to use AI doesn't have to pick one model and integrate with it directly — it can send a request through OpenRouter, which routes it to whichever of more than 400 models fits the job, the budget, or the latency requirement, and bills for it through one API. Founded by Alex Atallah, previously chief technology officer at the NFT marketplace OpenSea, and Louis Vichy, the company has grown fast: it says it now handles 25 trillion tokens a week and serves roughly 8 million users worldwide, according to figures reported around its May funding round. Sacra, a research firm that tracks private company financials, estimated OpenRouter was on pace for around $50 million in annualised revenue earlier this year.

That May round — $113 million led by CapitalG, Alphabet's growth-investing arm, with participation from Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Menlo Ventures and venture units at ServiceNow, MongoDB, Snowflake and Databricks — valued the company at $1.3 billion, itself more than double its Series A valuation from a year earlier. Three months later, Stripe is paying more than five times that.

Why a payments company wants an AI router

Stripe processes payments — a business that has made it one of the most valuable private companies in the world, alongside SpaceX and OpenAI. It is not, on its face, in the business of routing chatbot queries. But the logic Stripe's leadership has been laying out this year connects the two more directly than it first appears.

In December 2025, Stripe acquired Metronome, a billing platform built for usage-based pricing — the kind of metered, per-token, per-GPU-second billing that AI companies increasingly rely on instead of flat subscriptions. Announcing that deal, co-founder and chief executive Patrick Collison called metered pricing "the native business model for the AI era." At Stripe's Sessions conference this year, the company unveiled an Agentic Commerce Suite letting retailers — Best Buy, Coach and Kate Spade among the early builders — sell products directly inside AI applications rather than through a browser.

OpenRouter slots into that same thesis from a different angle. If Metronome lets Stripe bill for AI usage and the Agentic Commerce Suite lets Stripe process AI-driven purchases, OpenRouter gives Stripe the actual traffic layer — the point where an application decides which model handles a request, and where the money for that request first gets counted. One person close to the deal, cited by TechCrunch, described the ambition as making OpenRouter "the equivalent of Stripe for AI": a neutral gateway that businesses use regardless of which model — OpenAI's, Anthropic's, Google's or anyone else's — ultimately wins out. For a payments company, sitting on that gateway means visibility and a toll booth on a category of commerce that didn't exist five years ago, independent of who wins the underlying model race.

A crowded, and now consolidating, middle layer

OpenRouter was not alone in the AI-gateway business — rivals such as Portkey, LiteLLM, Vercel's AI Gateway and Cloudflare's AI Gateway compete for the same developers who don't want to hard-wire their apps to one model provider. But OpenRouter had emerged as the largest independent player, and its acquisition removes the most visible neutral option from a market where cloud giants and model makers all have their own routing products with their own incentives.

That's the pattern to watch: infrastructure incumbents aren't just investing in the AI labs everyone already follows — OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI — they're buying up the narrower, less visible layers that sit between those labs and the applications actually paying for tokens. A payments processor buying a model router is a bet that whoever owns that connective tissue captures value no matter which foundation model wins, much as credit-card networks made money regardless of which retailer sold the most goods.

Whether that bet pays off depends on something Stripe can't fully control: whether developers keep routing through a neutral middleman once that middleman is owned by a company with its own commercial interests in the transaction. Model providers have long grumbled about intermediaries taking a cut; owned by Stripe, OpenRouter now has a more obvious reason to make peace with — or extract more from — the labs on the other end of its API. The terms of that renegotiation, and whether OpenRouter's user base tolerates it, will be the next thing to watch as the deal closes and Stripe folds the routing layer into its broader AI push.
