Trump to nominate Heidi Overton as next FDA commissioner

FDA headquarters, Building 1, on the White Oak campus in Silver Spring, Maryland. — Wikimedia Commons / U.S. General Services Administration
In one sentence
Trump plans to nominate White House aide Heidi Overton as FDA commissioner, the agency's fourth leader since January 2025 after Marty Makary's resignation over vaccine and pricing conflicts.

President Trump intends to nominate Heidi Overton, a physician currently serving as deputy director of the White House Domestic Policy Council, to lead the Food and Drug Administration, according to Bloomberg and confirmed by three outlets including STAT News and CNN.[^1] If confirmed by the Senate, she would become the fourth person to run the agency since January 2025 — and, more pointedly, the second person Trump has had to nominate for the job in under eighteen months, after his first pick resigned amid open conflict with the White House over vaccine policy and drug pricing.

That churn is the story underneath the story. An FDA commissioner sets the tone for how quickly and how strictly the agency reviews new drugs, biologics and devices — decisions that show up directly in biotech and pharma valuations. Instability at the top has historically been read by investors as a proxy for regulatory unpredictability, and this is the second time in four months the agency has changed hands.

A physician with no agency-running experience

Overton, 37, holds a medical degree and a Ph.D. in clinical investigation, both tied to her training at the University of New Mexico and Johns Hopkins, where she also completed a general surgery residency.[^2] She has spent Trump's second term inside the West Wing rather than inside a regulatory agency: since January 2025 she has been the administration's point person for translating Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s "Make America Healthy Again" priorities into policy language, work that has included redesigning federal dietary guidance, revising the childhood vaccine schedule, and negotiating pricing arrangements with drugmakers including Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk over their weight-loss drugs.[^3]

That résumé cuts two ways. Supporters inside the administration describe her as a policy operator capable of holding together the competing factions around Trump's health agenda — a New York Times profile in July called her the White House's "MAHA gatekeeper," someone MAHA activists themselves have at times accused of slow-walking their priorities, including marijuana rescheduling and stricter nutrition rules.[^4] Critics in the pharmaceutical industry take a different view. One analyst, cited in coverage of the pick, put it bluntly: Overton "has degrees but seemingly no real experience running anything," and industry will regard the choice as concerning for an agency that employs roughly 18,000 people and oversees a fifth of consumer spending.[^5]

Why the last commissioner left

To understand why this nomination matters, it helps to trace how unusual the past eighteen months have been. Sara Brenner, an FDA device-review official, served as acting commissioner from January to April 2025 while Marty Makary's nomination worked through the Senate; Makary was confirmed 56–44 on April 1, 2025.[^6] His tenure lasted just over thirteen months. He resigned in May 2026 after clashes with the White House over flavored vape approvals, mifepristone policy and the agency's direction more broadly, compounded by criticism from public-health figures who accused him of narrowing COVID-19 vaccine recommendations to appease vaccine skeptics without ever formally withdrawing authorized products.[^7] Kyle Diamantas, the FDA's deputy commissioner for human foods, has run the agency in an acting capacity since Makary's departure — a period during which the agency has also lost more than 3,000 staff, including several division heads.[^8]

The White House–HHS fault line

Overton's nomination does not resolve the underlying tension so much as relocate it. She would technically report to Kennedy at HHS, but people close to the process have questioned how that reporting line will function given that Overton, from her West Wing perch, has at points pushed back on Kennedy's more aggressive vaccine positions and sided with agricultural interests over MAHA activists on pesticide policy.[^9] RBC analysts covering the pick described her likely impact on biopharma as "uncertain," noting she is "not a known champion of pro-innovation programs" such as expedited review pathways, "but also not on record opposing them" — a characterization that amounts to a shrug from Wall Street's perspective, not a signal.[^5]

What investors are watching

For biotech and pharma, the practical questions are less about Overton's biography than about what she does with the review backlog, the vape and mifepristone disputes Makary left unresolved, and the pricing framework the administration has already begun negotiating deal-by-deal with individual drugmakers rather than through blanket rulemaking. A commissioner who continues that deal-by-deal approach extends uncertainty for companies not yet at the table; one who moves toward a uniform policy could cut either direction on margins.

Confirmation itself is not guaranteed to be smooth. The Senate health committee that would vet Overton is chaired by Louisiana's Bill Cassidy, who lost a bruising primary this cycle after conflict with Trump — raising open questions about how much friction, if any, he brings to shepherding an administration pick through committee.[^9] A contested or drawn-out hearing process would extend the leadership vacuum industry groups are already flagging. The next concrete marker is the formal nomination paperwork and a hearing date from Cassidy's committee; until then, Diamantas remains acting commissioner, and the FDA's 3,000-person staffing hole remains unaddressed regardless of who ultimately holds the title.
