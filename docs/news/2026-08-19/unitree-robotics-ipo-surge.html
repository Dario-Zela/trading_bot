Unitree Robotics shares surge over 500% in Shanghai IPO debut

A Unitree G1 humanoid robot on display at a trade expo — Sayanesy / Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
In one sentence
Unitree Robotics jumped as much as 629% on its Shanghai debut to a $66bn valuation, roughly 700 times last year's profit.

Shares of Unitree Robotics, the Hangzhou-based maker of backflipping robot dogs and humanoid workers, opened at more than seven times their IPO price on Shanghai's STAR Market on Wednesday, briefly valuing the company at roughly $66 billion before the rally cooled through the session. It closed up 486%, a smaller number than the opening spike but still one of the largest first-day pops in the exchange's history.

The debut caps a run that has made 36-year-old founder Wang Xingxing China's first humanoid-robotics billionaire and turned Unitree into the country's flagship proof that robotics can be both a hype cycle and a real business at the same time. Whether it can stay both is the question the stock will spend the next few months answering.

From lab curiosity to listed company

Wang started Unitree in 2016 after a stint at drone maker DJI, building on a quadruped robot he'd developed for his master's thesis. The company built its early reputation — and roughly 70% of the global quadruped-robot market, by its own account — on robot dogs that could be knocked over, kicked, and still right themselves, a party trick that made for good marketing and, eventually, good margins.

Humanoids came later and grew fast. Unitree shipped five humanoid units in 2023, 412 in 2024, and 5,215 last year — more than any other company globally, according to the company's own disclosures. Revenue rose from 392 million yuan in 2024 to about 1.7 billion yuan ($250 million) in 2025, and the company posted its first annual profit, roughly 600 million yuan ($90 million), up more than sixfold on the year. That combination — real shipments, real revenue, actual profit — is rare in a sector where most humanoid-robot makers, including Figure AI and Boston Dynamics' Hyundai-owned business, are still burning cash.

The IPO itself priced conservatively by the standards of what followed: Unitree sold 10% of its enlarged share capital, raising 6.1 billion yuan (about $904 million) at 150.8 yuan a share, implying a valuation near 61 billion yuan, or about $9 billion. Retail demand for the offering ran to more than 5,500 times the shares on offer, a STAR Market record, according to Forbes and Bloomberg reporting ahead of the listing.

The debut, and the pullback within it

That demand is what produced Wednesday's fireworks. Shares opened at 1,100 yuan, a 629% jump from the IPO price, pushing the market cap to roughly 445 billion yuan — about $66 billion — before the stock gave back some of the gain and finished the day at 883.87 yuan, up 486%. DeepSeek, the Chinese AI lab, had put in 141 million yuan as a strategic investor under a three-year lock-up, alongside Tencent and a roster of state-linked funds including China National Petroleum and the National Council for Social Security Fund — backing that lent the listing both AI-industry credibility and a whiff of national-champion status.

Even at the closed-out $66 billion-adjacent figure, Unitree trades at roughly 700 times last year's net profit — a multiple that only makes sense if investors are pricing in years of humanoid-robot demand that hasn't arrived yet, not the robot-dog business that actually generated the earnings. That gap between today's price and today's profit is the whole story in miniature: a bet on where embodied AI is going, wrapped around a company that happens to already make money, which is more than can be said for most of its category.

A pattern investors know well

STAR Market debuts running hot on day one is closer to house style than surprise. The board's price-discovery mechanism removes the usual first-day limits on gains, and recent listings have leaned into it: chipmaker CXMT surged as much as 531% intraday on its debut earlier this year, and quantum-security firm QuantumCTek holds the exchange's record at 924% back in 2020. Big pops on STAR Market are common; what happens over the following months is more varied, and it's where the CXMT and QuantumCTek comparisons get less flattering — both cooled substantially from their opening-day highs within weeks, even as their underlying businesses kept growing.

Unitree's intraday retreat from 629% to 486% by the close is a small-scale preview of that pattern, and worth watching rather than dismissing: STAR Market stocks are volatile by design, thinly floated (Unitree sold only 10% of its capital), and prone to retail-driven swings that have little to do with quarterly numbers.

The next real data point is Unitree's first post-listing earnings report, likely in the coming months, which will show whether the 2025 profit run continues as humanoid shipments scale — or whether, like plenty of STAR Market debutants before it, the stock spends the rest of the year drifting back down toward the numbers the business can actually support.
