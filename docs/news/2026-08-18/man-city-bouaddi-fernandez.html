Man City move for £85m-rated Bouaddi while still pursuing Fernandez

Ayyoub Bouaddi playing for Morocco against Norway in June 2026, before his £85m-rated move to Manchester City took shape. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Manchester City are chasing Lille's £85m-rated Ayyoub Bouaddi and still pursuing Chelsea's Enzo Fernández to rebuild a midfield stripped of Rodri, Reijnders and Bernardo Silva.

Manchester City's midfield has been gutted this summer, and the club's response is to spend its way back to full strength — fast.

Rodri, the Ballon d'Or winner who anchored City's midfield for six years, is completing a move to Barcelona worth up to £65m. Tijjani Reijnders, signed only last year, is also on his way out. Bernardo Silva has already left for Real Madrid. That is three midfield departures in one window, and new manager Enzo Maresca — who succeeded Pep Guardiola at the end of June — is trying to fill the gap with two of Europe's most sought-after names before the deadline on 1 September.

The teenager City are chasing

The more advanced deal is for Ayyoub Bouaddi, the 18-year-old Lille midfielder who lit up this summer's World Cup for Morocco, the country he switched allegiance to from France in May. Lille value him at around £85m — a startling figure for a player who only turned 18 in October — and Bouaddi has reportedly made clear he wants the move. City are said to have agreed five-year contract terms with him, with talks over a fee entering their final stages.

Lille's manager, Davide Ancelotti, has not hidden his reluctance to sell. "There is interest from so many clubs," he said. "He is a really, really good player and I hope that he is going to stay with us." He added: "He's going to be one of the best midfielders in Europe for the next 10 years" — the kind of endorsement that tends to precede a departure rather than prevent one.

The one that got away — for now

City's other target is further from the finish line. Enzo Fernández, Chelsea's Argentina World Cup winner, has 16 goals and 10 assists to his name from last season and has made clear he wants to leave Stamford Bridge. City tabled a £102.5m bid, short of Chelsea's £120m valuation, and then missed a Friday deadline Chelsea had set for a full-price offer. Chelsea now insist he will not be sold this window or in January.

That is not quite the end of it. City retain the option to return with an improved bid in the window's final fortnight, and Fernández's relationship with Maresca from their time together at Chelsea gives the pursuit some logic beyond the transfer sheet. Asked directly about him this week, Maresca kept it neutral: "It's a player from another club." City have also been linked with Liverpool's Alexis Mac Allister as an alternative should Fernández prove unreachable.

What it says about City's summer

Taken together, the two pursuits are less a transfer story than a repair job. Losing Rodri, Reijnders and Bernardo Silva in a single window would strain any midfield, City's included, and the club's response — chase a generational teenage prospect and a proven Premier League performer at the same time — is as much about hedging as ambition. Whether either deal lands before the window shuts will say a good deal about how City manage a squad rebuild without their long-serving manager for the first time in a decade.

The next marker is 1 September. Bouaddi's move looks the more likely to close first; Fernández's depends on whether Chelsea's resolve holds once City decide how much more they are willing to pay.
