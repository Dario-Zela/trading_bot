Stripe finalises $7bn-plus acquisition of AI gateway OpenRouter

Stripe's headquarters entrance in San Francisco. — HaeB / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Stripe has closed a $7bn+ deal for AI model router OpenRouter — over five times its Series B valuation from three months ago.

Stripe, the payments company that quietly became one of the internet's essential pipes, has finalised a deal to buy OpenRouter, a three-year-old startup that lets developers switch between AI models the way a driver switches lanes. The price: more than $7 billion — over five times the $1.3 billion valuation OpenRouter carried when it raised its Series B just three months ago.

OpenRouter's product is simple to describe and evidently hard to replicate: a single API that routes a developer's request to whichever of more than 400 AI models — from Anthropic's Claude to OpenAI's GPT line and dozens of open-weight alternatives — fits the job and the budget. No separate contracts, no rewriting code every time a better or cheaper model appears. Founder and chief executive Alex Atallah, who previously built OpenSea into the largest NFT marketplace before leaving in 2022, has described the pitch bluntly: OpenRouter is "the equivalent of Stripe for AI." Investors evidently agreed with the analogy before Stripe made it literal — Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Menlo Ventures and Alphabet's CapitalG all backed the May round.

Why a payments company wants a model router

Stripe processes payments for a huge share of the software industry, and an increasing share of that software is AI-native — chatbots billing per token, agents billing per task, apps that need to meter usage across several providers at once. Owning the layer that routes those requests gives Stripe visibility into, and potentially control over, how that usage gets billed. It is less a diversification than a vertical extension: infrastructure for the infrastructure.

The scale of the premium is the more telling number. A 5.4x markup in three months is not the kind of multiple that shows up when a company is merely useful — it shows up when a buyer believes it is looking at a chokepoint. OpenRouter says it serves 8 million users globally, mostly developers, and Stripe's spokesperson has so far said only that the company "does not comment on rumors or speculation," which is corporate-speak for confirmed and not yet ready to talk about integration plans.

Neither company has said what happens to OpenRouter's brand, pricing or independence once the deal closes — an open question for developers who chose the platform partly because it wasn't owned by any single model maker or cloud provider. For anyone building AI-powered tools, including trading systems that lean on multiple model providers for cost and redundancy, the acquisition is worth watching for what it implies about where pricing power in AI infrastructure is heading, and who now holds the routing switch.
