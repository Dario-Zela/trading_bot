Anthropic Q2 revenue hits $11.5bn, up 14x year-on-year, as fall IPO looms

Dario Amodei, Anthropic's co-founder and chief executive, at TechCrunch Disrupt in 2023. — Kimberly White / Getty Images for TechCrunch, via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Anthropic told IPO investors Q2 revenue topped $11.5bn with a first operating profit — unaudited, and short of the profit a $2tn valuation implies.

Anthropic told prospective investors this month that its revenue more than doubled in the second quarter of 2026, crossing $11.5bn, and that the company turned an operating profit for the first time — a claim that, if it holds up, would mark one of the fastest climbs to profitable scale any technology company has managed.

The figures were not announced by Anthropic itself. They come from internal documents shared with investors ahead of a planned autumn stock listing, first reported by Bloomberg News and since corroborated by the Wall Street Journal and multiple outlets including CNBC and Fortune. Anthropic has declined to comment officially, and the company's own disclosures describe the numbers as preliminary — "deliberations are ongoing and the figures could be revised," as Bloomberg's sourcing put it.

Even with that caveat, the trajectory is striking. Anthropic's revenue is reported to have gone from around $787m in the second quarter of 2025 to $4.73bn in the first quarter of this year to more than $11.5bn in the second — a roughly 14-fold year-on-year jump and more than double the prior quarter. The company's annualised run rate, a separate measure derived from a given month's revenue multiplied by twelve, was already put at $47bn back in May.

Where the money is coming from

The growth is not evenly spread. Enterprise adoption of Claude Code, Anthropic's coding assistant, has been the standout driver — the product alone is reported to have reached roughly $8bn in annualised revenue by May, with customers including Netflix, Spotify and Salesforce, and business subscriptions said to have quadrupled since the start of the year. More than 1,000 customers now reportedly spend over $1m a year with Anthropic, doubling in barely two months this spring. That is the pattern behind the topline number: this is not a single blockbuster product but an API and coding business compounding across a widening enterprise customer base.

The positive operating income is the more consequential claim. Frontier AI labs have spent the past three years burning enormous sums on compute and training runs, and investors have generally been asked to take profitability on faith, years out. An operating profit — even a modest, "adjusted" one, which typically excludes items like stock compensation — would be the first hard evidence that the economics of running large language models at scale can turn positive before a company burns through its balance sheet entirely.

The IPO backdrop

The numbers are surfacing now because Anthropic is deep in preparation for a stock market listing, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan Chase working the offering and a confidential filing already lodged with the SEC in June. Investor meetings have been under way since July, and reports this week suggest bankers and existing investors are discussing a listing as soon as September or October at a valuation approaching $2 trillion — roughly double the $965bn price tag set in May's $65bn Series H round, and enough to make it the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX's $1.77 trillion debut in June.

That valuation is where the skepticism concentrates. A $2 trillion price tag, at typical Nasdaq-100 multiples, would imply Anthropic needs to be generating something in the range of $60bn-$80bn in annual net profit to look fairly priced — a figure nowhere close to what even the most bullish reading of this quarter's numbers implies. Amazon, held up as the scale comparison bankers are reportedly using, made $200.6bn in revenue and $62.6bn in net income in its own second quarter this year. Anthropic's reported operating profit, whatever its size, is not the same thing as net income, and the company has not disclosed one.

What to watch

None of this is yet a matter of public record. Anthropic has filed confidentially, which means the figures investors are currently working from — including this quarter's revenue and profit claims — will not be independently auditable until a public S-1 is released, expected in the coming weeks if the September or October timeline holds. That filing, not this week's leaks, is the point at which the $11.5bn claim either becomes a verified data point or gets revised. Rival OpenAI, which had also eyed a fall listing, is now reportedly looking at 2027 instead — leaving Anthropic to make its case to public markets largely on its own.

In one sentence: Anthropic told IPO investors its revenue passed $11.5bn last quarter with its first operating profit, but the numbers are unaudited and the $2tn valuation being floated implies profits it hasn't shown.
