Labour MP suspended amid investigation into Covid loan scheme

Bayo Alaba, Labour MP for Southend East and Rochford, elected in 2024. — Laurie Noble Photography / UK Parliament, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Labour suspended Southend MP Bayo Alaba's whip after he referred himself over a £50,000 Covid Bounce Back Loan taken out by a business he co-owns.

Bayo Alaba, the Labour MP for Southend East and Rochford, has had the whip suspended after referring himself to his own party for investigation over a taxpayer-backed pandemic loan. The move — unusual in that Alaba triggered it himself rather than waiting to be caught — followed questions from The Times about a £50,000 Bounce Back Loan taken out by Alaba Properties Ltd, a business he co-owns.

The loan was the maximum available under the Bounce Back scheme, the emergency lending programme that let small businesses borrow up to £50,000 with minimal checks and a state guarantee covering the full amount if the borrower defaulted. Companies House records show an attempt was made to dissolve Alaba Properties in March 2022 — a company can only be struck off if it has no outstanding debts, so a wind-up attempt while a loan remains unpaid invites scrutiny. Alaba has since said one of two loans he took out has been settled, and insists there was "no attempt to avoid my financial obligations."

Self-referral, then suspension

A Labour spokesperson said the party "expects the highest standards from our elected representatives" and has "robust processes in place" to enforce them, declining further comment while the investigation proceeds. Alaba, elected in 2024 on an 18.9-point swing that unseated two decades of Conservative representation in Southend, has agreed to administrative suspension from the party pending the outcome, and the whip has been withdrawn — meaning he now sits in the Commons as an independent rather than as a Labour MP, though he keeps his seat. The party stressed the process is being run without prejudice, implying no finding of wrongdoing at this stage.

Mike Wood, the shadow Cabinet Office minister, was blunter, telling The Times that Alaba "clearly has questions to answer." Alaba himself has largely declined to elaborate publicly beyond his initial statement.

Not an isolated case

Alaba's is the second British case this year of an MP losing the whip over Bounce Back Loan questions. In May, Reform UK's James McMurdock removed himself from his party's whip after The Sunday Times reported he had borrowed £70,000 across two companies — one of which, by his own turnover, would not plausibly have qualified for the sum. Neither firm appeared to have significant staff or trading history at the time the loans were issued.

The scheme that both cases trace back to was designed for speed, not scrutiny — self-certified turnover figures, no credit checks, money out within days — because the alternative in April 2020 was to watch viable businesses collapse for want of a bridging loan. That trade-off produced roughly £47 billion in lending and, by the National Audit Office's estimates, several billion pounds in losses to fraud and default, much of it never recovered. The scheme's design flaws are well documented; what is less common is seeing them surface in the political class doing the governing rather than the small businesses the scheme was built for.

What happens next

Labour's internal investigation has no published timeline, and there is no indication either case has yet reached the level of a police referral or Insolvency Service inquiry — the routes through which most Bounce Back fraud has actually been pursued. The more immediate marker to watch is whether Alaba is readmitted to the parliamentary party, loses it for good, or — as happened with several MPs suspended over other conduct issues in 2024 — the process simply runs quietly for months without resolution.
