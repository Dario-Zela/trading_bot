Meta goes to trial over child safety claims facing up to $1.4tn in damages

In one sentence
A coalition of state attorneys general opened a landmark federal trial accusing Meta of designing Instagram and Facebook to addict children.

A coalition of state attorneys general opened a landmark federal trial accusing Meta of designing Instagram and Facebook to addict children.

If the states prevail, Meta faces not just massive liability but forced redesign of engagement-maximization features that power its business. The precedent would ripple across the tech sector—TikTok, YouTube, Snapchat all face similar addiction-by-design claims—and could trigger regulatory overhauls in how platforms target minors.

Key facts:
- Coalition of state attorneys general opened a federal trial against Meta, alleging Instagram and Facebook were designed to exploit and addict children
- Potential damages pegged at $1.4 trillion, making this among the largest tech liability claims ever
- Case explicitly modeled on landmark tobacco litigation, suggesting prosecutors believe social media platforms face similar legal exposure for intentional harm design
