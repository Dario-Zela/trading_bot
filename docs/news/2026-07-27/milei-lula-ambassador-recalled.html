Milei calls Lula a 'thief and convict,' Brazil recalls ambassador

Argentine President Javier Milei, whose São Paulo rally speech prompted Brazil to recall its ambassador. — Argentine Presidency / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Milei's 'thief and convict' jab at Lula during a rally for Flávio Bolsonaro triggered Brazil's first-ever ambassador recall from Argentina.

Javier Milei has never hidden his contempt for Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. But on Saturday, at a rally in São Paulo, the Argentine president crossed a line that seven decades of feuds between South America's two largest economies — including years of military dictatorship on both sides of the border — had never crossed. Brazil recalled its ambassador from Buenos Aires.

The occasion was the formal launch of Flávio Bolsonaro's presidential campaign at the Mercado Livre Arena Pacaembu, where the senator and son of the convicted former president Jair Bolsonaro was anointed as the Brazilian right's standard-bearer for October's election. Milei, the guest of honour, did not hold back. Without naming Lula directly, he branded Brazil's leftist incumbent a "thief" and a "convict," and dismissed Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes — who oversaw Jair Bolsonaro's coup conviction and had just denied Milei's request to visit the former president in custody — as "bald trash."

Brazilian diplomats did not mince words in response, saying Milei had "insulted two branches of government, the Brazilian people and Brazilian democracy." Ambassador Julio Bitelli was ordered home for consultations, and Foreign Minister Mauro Vieira summoned Argentina's envoy in Brasília to convey what the ministry called Brazil's "revulsion." Lula, for his part, struck a composed note: he would keep governing "without anyone's interference," he said, adding that "in Brazil, we do not accept anyone sticking their nose where it doesn't belong."

A trade bloc under strain

The insults did not come out of nowhere. Milei has spent recent months telling Radio Mitre that Lula and his allies are "progressive leaders who don't want the ideas of freedom to work," and warning darkly of a "Lula risk" to the region. He has also accused Brazil, Mexico and the US Democratic Party of financing a coordinated campaign against him, pointing to criticism of Argentina during the World Cup as evidence.

Underneath the rhetoric sits a real economic rift. Argentina's tariff deal with Washington in February, which zeroed out duties on some 1,675 products, unsettled Brasília, as has Buenos Aires' bid to join the Trans-Pacific trade pact known as the CPTPP — a bloc that includes Japan, Canada and the UK but not Brazil. Milei skipped a MERCOSUR leaders' summit in June, and after São Paulo he continued on to Peru, Colombia and Ecuador, stitching together what Argentine officials describe as a coalition of the regional right.

That matters for MERCOSUR, the trade bloc built on the presumption that Argentina and Brazil would manage their disagreements at the negotiating table rather than at rival campaign rallies. With Bolsonaro's son now formally in the race against Lula, and Milei openly campaigning for him, the bloc's largest members are drifting toward treating each other as adversaries rather than partners — a dynamic investors will be watching as Brazil's election campaign builds toward October.
