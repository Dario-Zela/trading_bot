ECB holds rates, flags energy-price volatility risk

The European Central Bank's headquarters in Frankfurt, where the Governing Council held its key rates on 23 July. — Daniel Vorndran / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA)
In one sentence
The ECB held rates at 2.25% on 23 July, saying energy prices remain well above pre-conflict levels — a hawkish-leaning pause with a September hike still on the table.

The European Central Bank left borrowing costs unchanged on 23 July, holding its deposit rate at 2.25%, the main refinancing rate at 2.40% and the marginal lending rate at 2.65%. The decision itself surprised no one — markets had priced a hold for weeks. What mattered was the reasoning the Governing Council attached to it: energy prices, it said, are "close to the baseline of the June Eurosystem staff projections and well above the levels recorded prior to the conflict in the Middle East."

That single line does most of the work in explaining why the ECB, having already raised rates once this year, is not yet done tightening — and why any talk of cuts has gone quiet.

Oil, not demand, is setting the pace

The backdrop is a widening energy shock. Fighting around the Strait of Hormuz pushed Brent crude briefly above $100 a barrel earlier in July, and Houthi strikes on the tankers Encelia and Layla in the Red Sea opened a second chokepoint alongside Hormuz — meaning the disruption is no longer one that a single diplomatic breakthrough with Iran could neatly resolve. Eurozone inflation eased to 2.8% in June, but energy costs were still up 8.7% year-on-year, and the ECB's own staff see the headline rate stuck "well above target" into the first half of next year.

President Christine Lagarde was explicit about the mechanism at risk: "The longer energy prices stay high, the more likely they are to drive up broader inflation through indirect and second-round effects." Pressed on whether hawks or doves on the council carried the heavier burden ahead of the next meeting, she offered a line that will likely define the September debate: "The burden of proof is on data." Some governors, she confirmed, had already asked whether a further hike was warranted this month; the council chose unanimously to wait.

The euro did not read the hold as dovish. EUR/USD slipped roughly 0.3% to around 1.1385 on the day, as safe-haven dollar demand tied to the same Middle East tensions outweighed what ING and other desks described as a "hawkish-leaning hold" — a pause with the door to a September hike left deliberately open.

What changes the calculus

The geopolitical variable driving all of this is not static. As this edition went to press on 27 July, oil prices were sliding on signs that the US and Iran had agreed a fragile pause in strikes around Hormuz — the kind of de-escalation that, if it holds, would ease the exact pressure the ECB cited four days earlier. Whether that pause survives matters more to the September 10 decision than any single eurozone data print between now and then.

Until it's clear which way the energy shock breaks, the ECB has effectively parked its policy debate on the price of crude. Two more inflation readings and updated staff projections arrive before the council reconvenes; a durable ceasefire would hand doves their strongest argument yet, while a reopened front at either chokepoint would do the opposite.
