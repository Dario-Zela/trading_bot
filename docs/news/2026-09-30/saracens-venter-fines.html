Saracens face fines over Venter's media no-shows

In one sentence
Saracens face more Premiership Rugby fines after Brendan Venter, re-titled 'head of rugby culture and wine consultant', kept avoiding media duties, following an initial £5,000 sanction.

Saracens are facing further fines from Premiership Rugby because Brendan Venter, their former director of rugby, keeps skipping media duties. The club has already paid £5,000 for his absence from the league's pre-season launch day. Venter is not a player, as some early accounts had it. He is a coach and administrator who has changed job title, and the new title does not seem to have changed his availability to broadcasters.

A convenient new title

Venter "resigned" as director of rugby and took up a new role as "head of rugby culture and wine consultant". Head coach Joe Shaw took over the club's media commitments. Saracens told the league about the change. They described the new job as covering the "culture, values and broader development of the playing and coaching environment".

The league's media rules bind the people who hold specific roles, and the club's reading appears to be that a wine consultant is not one of them. Premiership Rugby has not accepted that reading. It is reportedly pursuing further sanctions, which suggests it thinks the reshuffle was a way around its rules and not a real change of duties.

An old habit

Venter's reluctance is not new. During his first spell at Saracens he collected a series of fines for publicly criticising officials. In December 2010, after a defeat by Racing Metro, he gave a post-match interview in one-word answers and agreed with whatever the interviewer put to him. He later said he was inspired by a scene in the film Mike Bassett: England Manager. It was a protest with a punchline, but it was also a refusal to engage.

What it says about enforcement

Rules on media access are the least dramatic part of a league's governance. Broadcasters pay for access to coaches and players, and the league needs to be able to deliver it. The £5,000 fine is small for a club of Saracens' size. It is a nuisance, not a deterrent, and a club can absorb it more cheaply than it can put a reluctant executive in front of a camera.

There is no sign that the dispute affects Saracens' playing strength. It does show how far a league's leverage extends when a club redesigns a job to get outside the rule. The pressure will now come from the sanction process. Premiership Rugby has to decide whether the wine-consultant title is a genuine reorganisation or an evasion, and whether the fines grow with each missed appearance.
