Huel advert banned for suggesting products could replace all conventional food

In one sentence
The ASA banned a Huel ad in which an ambassador told of an ultramarathon winner who 'only eats Huel', saying it implied replacing all food was appropriate.

A man who, by one account, eats nothing but Huel turned up at an ultramarathon and won it. That anecdote, told in a podcast-style video by the brand's ambassador, has now cost Huel an advert. The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA), Britain's ad regulator, has banned the spot for encouraging or condoning "irresponsible" dietary practices.

The ad

The video ran as a sponsored Instagram post and a YouTube pre-roll ad. It showed Spencer Matthews, the television personality and Huel ambassador, talking to Huel's founder, Julian Hearn. Matthews described meeting a competitor in a 260km race who "only eats Huel". The man, he said, "won it by miles, like literally just left us all in the dirt."

The ASA received four complaints and examined whether the ad was irresponsible.

The ruling

The regulator accepted that the ad did not explicitly tell anyone to swap all their food for Huel. It concluded that viewers could still reasonably take the story as an endorsement. In its reading, the ad implied that replacing all conventional food with the shakes was "nutritionally appropriate" and compatible with elite physical performance.

Huel's defence was that Matthews came across as sceptical throughout. The company said he showed "extreme scepticism and disbelief" and that the anecdote was "a remarkable and unexpected occurrence, not as a recommendation." The Instagram caption also said the product was "not for every meal". The company told BBC News it had complied with the ruling.

The finding turns on the ad's overall impression rather than its literal words. A winning athlete is a strong image, and the ASA judged that it outweighed the caveat.

The triage summary for this piece framed the ruling as one about unsubstantiated claims. The published coverage instead describes a finding of irresponsibility, which is a different breach of the advertising code.

A familiar name at the ASA

The Grocer reports that this is Huel's fifth censure by the ASA in four years. The trade title's headline describes an earlier round as over unauthorised health claims. Detail on the earlier rulings was not available for this article.

The timing is awkward for the company. Founded by Hearn in 2015, Huel sells powders, shakes and bars in what is described as a roughly $5.9bn complete-nutrition market. It is reported to be in the process of being acquired by the French food group Danone. A ruling that its marketing implies total food replacement sits uneasily beside that corporate backdrop.

What next

Bans of this kind carry no fine. The ASA requires the ad not to appear again in its current form and expects the advertiser to take care with future campaigns. Persistent offenders can be referred to Trading Standards. The thing to watch is whether the Danone deal completes and whether its owners tighten the brand's influencer scripts. Ambassador anecdotes are where meal-replacement marketing keeps getting into trouble.
