Scotland's failings persist under new coach

In one sentence
Scotland lost 3-0 to Switzerland on new coach Sébastien Pocognoli's Hampden debut, extending a run of four games without a goal and putting the hire under early scrutiny.

Scotland have a new head coach, a new-look squad and the same problem they had before. On Tuesday night at Hampden Park, Sébastien Pocognoli's side lost 3-0 to Switzerland in the Nations League. It was the Belgian's first home match in charge, and it left Scotland without a goal in four consecutive games.

Pocognoli, 39, was appointed a little over a month ago to succeed Steve Clarke. His record so far is one goalless draw away in Slovenia and one heavy home defeat. That is a small sample. It is also one with no goals in it.

A night that went wrong early

The match turned in the 11th minute. Jack Hendry was sent off for denying Dan Ndoye a clear goal-scoring chance. Ricardo Rodríguez then scored from the free-kick, and Scotland spent the rest of the evening chasing the game with ten men. Nico Elvedi and Zeki Amdouni added goals after the break.

The red card is the easy explanation, and it does account for much of the scoreline. Kieron Bowie was taken off soon afterwards to make tactical room, which is a cost of its own. Luis Binks and Stephen Welsh defended capably in difficult circumstances, and John McGinn won fouls. Scotland still created very little going forward, and that was true before Hendry's dismissal too.

The question is the hire

The goal drought predates Pocognoli, so it cannot be pinned on him alone. That is what makes it awkward for the Scottish FA. Clarke's departure was meant to end a period of stagnation, and the decision to replace him looked easy to defend. Yet the replacement has so far produced the same symptoms: too little threat, too much reliance on individual moments, and a team that struggles to recover once a match slips.

The case for patience is real. A new coach has had little time with the squad and has been dealt one sending-off he did not choose. The case against is equally plain. A changeover was supposed to alter how the team plays, and four games without a goal suggests it has not yet done so. The scrutiny is therefore moving from why Clarke left to why this appointment was made and what it was supposed to fix. Public explanations of the FA's rationale have been thin, and the record is the only evidence to hand.

Skopje next

Scotland travel to North Macedonia on Saturday. It is the sort of fixture that a side in Scotland's position is expected to win, and it offers the first realistic chance of ending the drought. Failing to score there would make it five games without a goal. The conversation about Pocognoli would then move on from bad luck.
