England player ratings and Tuchel's Alexander-Arnold question

In one sentence
Alexander-Arnold's assist in a 2-0 win over ten-man Czechia earned high ratings, but Tuchel's guarded praise leaves England's right-back slot unsettled.

Trent Alexander-Arnold got his chance in Prague on Tuesday and took it. He made one decisive pass in a 2-0 Nations League win over Czechia. Whether that changes anything for Thomas Tuchel's right flank is a separate question, and the England manager was careful not to answer it.

The full-back was recalled on 18 September, his first call-up since the World Cup and his first England start under Tuchel. He had not played for England since 2025. Tuchel's message on recalling him was blunt: "It's an opportunity for Trent", and it was "now time to fight for his place".

One pass, and an early red card

The decisive moment came two minutes after the break. Alexander-Arnold's right-foot delivery found Anthony Gordon for the opening goal, and Harry Kane added the second. Czechia were down to ten men after an early red card for Pavel Sulc, which flatters any attacking rating on the night.

The marks were generous all the same. Sports Illustrated gave him 8.4 and called the delivery "a cross of the highest quality". Goal.com scored him 8 and said that in his hybrid role he was the main architect of England's attacking shape. A more cautious 7 came from JingleTree, which noted he had little to do defensively.

That caveat matters. The question about Alexander-Arnold has never really been whether he can pass. It is what happens when the opposition has the ball and the space behind him is exposed. A side playing with ten men for most of the evening did not test that.

Tuchel was measured afterwards. He told BBC Radio 5 Live: "He did very well. He had an excellent assist. Overall, like everyone, he was professional, top attitude and one decisive action." That is praise for the assist and the attitude, and it stops short of endorsing him as first choice.

Gordon was warmer: "Only a certain number of players have that ability, technique and bravery to make that pass... I've been dying to play with him." Alexander-Arnold himself said: "The manager gave me a chance, now hopefully he is happy."

A position still unsettled

The BBC's analysis argues that it is time for Tuchel to show more trust. It puts the numbers starkly. Alexander-Arnold is reported to be Tuchel's ninth right-back in 24 games, though the tally is the BBC's and depends on how a hybrid role is counted. Whatever the exact figure, a manager who has rotated that many players through one position has not found the answer.

The pattern points to a settled hesitancy rather than a single bad night. Tuchel left Alexander-Arnold out for more than a year while favouring other options. He has now brought him back on probation, not on merit. One assist against ten men does not overturn that approach, and the manager's careful wording suggests it has not.

The counter-argument is that Alexander-Arnold's qualities are scarce. England have creators in abundance, and few of them can switch play or split a defence from deep as he can. Selecting him means building the defence around him, which is the trade-off Tuchel appears reluctant to commit to.

What next

The next Nations League window will show whether the Prague performance was an audition or the start of a run. A start against opposition at full strength, with no red card to help, would be the real test. Until then the right-back position stays open, and Alexander-Arnold is still fighting for it.
