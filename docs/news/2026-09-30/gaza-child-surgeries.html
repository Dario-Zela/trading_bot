Girl undergoes repeated surgeries for infections after Gaza strike

In one sentence
Six-year-old Raseel al-Balawi has had more than 20 surgeries to control infections since a late-July strike hit her tent in Gaza, as the ceasefire falters.

Six-year-old Raseel al-Balawi has had more than 20 operations since an air strike hit her family's tent in Gaza in late July. The surgeries are not a single course of treatment. They are repeated attempts to keep infection in her wounds under control, according to BBC reporting.

Raseel was living in a tent in al-Mawasi, the coastal strip in southern Gaza where many displaced families have sheltered. Witnesses say the tent was struck by an Israeli helicopter. The Israeli military says it has no record of the attack and that it "regrets any harm to civilians".

A wound that keeps returning

Why a child needs so many operations comes down to how injuries are treated in a place where medicine is scarce. A wound that is cleaned and closed can still become infected again. If it does, surgeons must reopen it, remove damaged tissue and try again. Each round demands theatre time, anaesthetic, dressings and antibiotics, and Gaza's health system has struggled to supply all of these consistently.

Al-Mawasi is not a hospital ward, either. Tents offer little protection from dust, heat and unclean water, which all raise the risk of infection. A child discharged after surgery often returns to those conditions.

BBC Eye Investigations followed Raseel through the summer. The team's aim was to show the challenges facing doctors as the ceasefire falters and bombardment continues. The available reporting does not detail her family, her treating hospital or the full extent of her injuries. The BBC's documentary carries that fuller account.

What the case does and does not show

One child's case cannot stand in for a whole conflict, and this one should be read with care. The account of the strike rests on witnesses. The Israeli military says it has no record of the incident, so the circumstances have not been independently established in the reporting available here.

What can be said is narrower. A young child was badly hurt while living in a displacement camp. Her recovery is being measured in operations, not weeks. And the ceasefire that was meant to ease pressure on Gaza's hospitals is, in the BBC's words, faltering.

What to watch

The practical questions are about access and supply. Whether surgical materials, antibiotics and evacuation routes for complex cases reach Gaza will shape outcomes for children like Raseel more than any single announcement. The BBC documentary on her treatment is the next fixed point for a fuller account of her progress.
