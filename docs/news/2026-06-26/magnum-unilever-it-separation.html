Magnum taps Microsoft, SAP and four others to build its own IT stack after Unilever split

A Magnum Classic bar — the flagship product of the newly independent Heartbrand portfolio company. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Magnum Ice Cream named Microsoft, SAP, Salesforce, Kinaxis, Accenture and HCLTech to replace Unilever's shared IT stack — a back-office milestone with minimal trading signal.

The Magnum Ice Cream Company, demerged from Unilever in 2025, has revealed six technology vendors tasked with building the standalone IT systems it needs before its transitional services agreements with its former parent expire at the end of 2027.

The roster covers the full stack. Microsoft and SAP occupy the two heaviest positions — cloud infrastructure and enterprise resource planning respectively, with SAP's deployment consolidating multiple legacy instances onto a single RISE with SAP S/4HANA Cloud. Salesforce takes the CRM role across in-home and out-of-home channels; Kinaxis handles supply chain and sales-and-operations planning. Two systems integrators round out the group: Accenture for enterprise, AI, data and cybersecurity deployment, and HCLTech for infrastructure and end-user service management. The rollout will proceed in waves over eighteen months toward an end-2027 hard deadline.

The Heartbrand portfolio — which stretches from Magnum and Cornetto to Ben & Jerry's, Breyers, Klondike and Popsicle — makes this a non-trivial IT exercise. A global distribution footprint, highly seasonal demand patterns and multiple go-to-market channels mean a clean data foundation matters before any of the AI-assisted forecasting the company is publicly targeting. "None of this works without clean data," Michael Friedlander, CIO of the Americas, observed in an earlier interview. The vendor selection, said Chief Supply Chain and Operations Officer Sandeep Desai, was guided partly by how aggressively each partner is investing in AI capabilities, and partly by a deliberate preference for configuration over heavy customisation — the kind of bespoke legacy systems that make future upgrades slow and expensive.

Reading the signal

For traders tracking Unilever, the announcement is largely a non-event. The demerger was priced in across 2024 and 2025; the transitional services agreements were always understood to have a finite life. Their orderly wind-down — with a credible vendor list and a clearly stated deadline — removes a residual operational overhang rather than creating one.

For Microsoft and SAP, a fresh enterprise customer is a minor positive, but the contract size is not material to either company's quarterly numbers. Kinaxis and HCLTech, smaller by market capitalisation, receive proportionally more from the brand association than from the revenue line.

The story with longer legs is the pattern this illustrates. Large conglomerates historically justified their IT spend partly through shared infrastructure — one ERP, one cloud contract, one helpdesk spread across dozens of brands. As portfolio fragmentation accelerates across consumer staples, the cost of disentanglement is becoming a recurring line item that analysts are only beginning to model properly. Building a replacement stack from scratch, even at Magnum's scale, is a multi-year, almost certainly nine-figure undertaking. The precedent accumulates.

The clock on Magnum's TSA deadline is running. System go-lives and integration milestones over the next eighteen months will be the moments when execution risk either validates or complicates the company's independence story.
