FDA broadens Ionis' Tryngolza label to cover severe hypertriglyceridemia

The FDA's White Oak campus in Silver Spring, Maryland, where drug approvals are processed. — Wikimedia Commons / Public Domain
In one sentence
FDA approved Ionis' Tryngolza for severe hypertriglyceridemia on June 24 — the first specific indication for a condition affecting ~3 million Americans — ahead of a July commercial launch.

The FDA on June 24 cleared a broader use for Ionis Pharmaceuticals' Tryngolza (olezarsen), extending the drug's label to cover adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia — defined by triglyceride levels at or above 500 milligrams per decilitre. The approval arrived ahead of the June 30 target action date and sets up a July commercial launch. Ionis claimed what it called an absolute first: no prior FDA-approved therapy has carried a specific indication for severe hypertriglyceridemia or for reducing the acute pancreatitis risk the condition carries.

The drug is already on the U.S. market — approved in December 2024 for familial chylomicronemia syndrome, a much rarer condition where triglycerides exceed 880 mg/dL. The new label reaches a substantially larger patient pool.

The number that matters most

Roughly 3 million Americans carry a severe hypertriglyceridemia diagnosis, versus a FCS population counted in the low tens of thousands. That gap is why analysts moved their peak-sales models after the Phase 3 data landed: William Blair raised its estimate to $3 billion; Jefferies moved from $1.5 billion to $2.5 billion. Tryngolza generated roughly $102 million in full-year 2025 revenue from the FCS indication alone, with Q1 2026 at $27 million.

The clinical evidence underpinning the expansion is hard to argue with. The Phase 3 CORE and CORE2 studies showed olezarsen — an antisense oligonucleotide targeting apolipoprotein C-III — cut fasting triglycerides by up to 72% versus placebo at six months. More distinctively, the drug reduced acute pancreatitis events by up to 91%, a claim no prior lipid-lowering agent could make in this indication. At twelve months, 86% of patients with baseline data had dropped below the 500 mg/dL threshold. The existing alternatives — fenofibrate, omega-3 fatty acids, Vascepa — reduce triglycerides, but none carried an sHTG-specific label or a pancreatitis endpoint.

The uncertainty, as usual, is on the commercial side. Diagnosed sHTG and treated sHTG are different numbers. Awareness among primary care physicians is uneven; dietary management remains the first intervention; and payer coverage for a branded monthly injectable autoinjector at expected premium pricing is unresolved. Ionis has targeted cash-flow breakeven in 2028, and the sHTG launch is central to getting there.

There is also a competitive clock running. Plozasiran, another APOC3 inhibitor from a separate developer, is in late-stage development for both FCS and sHTG. Ionis has a first-mover advantage — but the window is finite, and the market will want to see what actual uptake looks like once reimbursement pathways are set. The first quarterly revenue report reflecting sHTG sales, likely in late October, will be the first real signal.
