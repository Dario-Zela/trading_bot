US and Gulf states call for full Lebanese disarmament and reaffirm Gaza freedom of movement

Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Bahrain's Foreign Minister Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani depart the GCC ministerial meeting in Manama, June 25, 2026 — Eric Lee / Pool / Reuters
In one sentence
The US and GCC called for full disarmament of Lebanon's non-state armed groups and reaffirmed Gaza non-displacement, with Rubio reassuring Gulf allies over the Iran deal.

The United States and the six members of the Gulf Cooperation Council emerged from a ministerial session in Manama on Wednesday with a joint statement calling for the full disarmament of non-state armed groups in Lebanon and reaffirming that no Palestinian in Gaza would be compelled to leave — or barred from returning.

The meeting, held at The Ritz-Carlton Bahrain and co-chaired by Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Bahrain's Foreign Minister Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani, arrived against an unusually crowded backdrop. The June 17 signing of a US-Iran memorandum of understanding — the first formal de-escalation framework between Washington and Tehran in decades — had left Gulf capitals uneasy about whether their security interests had been adequately considered. Rubio's Manama stopover was, in part, about reassurance.

The Lebanon clause

On Lebanon, the language was pointed. The ministers declared that full Lebanese sovereignty "cannot be achieved as long as non-state armed groups retain military capabilities outside the authority of the Lebanese state" — a formulation that targets Hezbollah without naming it. The statement demanded complete restoration of the Lebanese state's monopoly on force and expressed support for the Lebanese Armed Forces in pursuing that objective.

Critically, the ministers insisted that ongoing US-sponsored Israel-Lebanon negotiations must continue on their own terms, independent of outcomes elsewhere in the region. Progress on a Lebanese framework should not be held hostage to either the Iran nuclear track or the Gaza ceasefire calendar — a positioning that suits both Washington and Riyadh, which have commercial and political reasons to see Lebanon stabilise sooner rather than later.

Gaza and non-displacement

On Gaza, the joint statement reaffirmed support for President Trump's Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict, endorsed by UN Security Council Resolution 2803. The ministers stressed that all non-state armed groups in the territory must be demilitarized as a condition for reconstruction, and that civilian administration should eventually transfer to "an independent, technocratic civil Palestinian committee." GCC member states confirmed participation in a proposed Board of Peace to oversee rebuilding efforts.

The anti-displacement pledge — that no one would be forced to leave Gaza, and those who departed voluntarily would retain the right to return — restated an established position rather than breaking new ground. Its inclusion signals continued Gulf resistance to any plan that could be read as demographic re-engineering of the strip.

Coordination without a timetable

What the statement adds up to is a publicly coordinated posture, not a roadmap. Neither the Lebanon disarmament clause nor the Gaza governance framework carries timelines or enforcement mechanisms. The meeting's immediate political function was to signal to GCC capitals that the US-Iran MOU was not negotiated over their heads. Rubio, on the Strait of Hormuz, was blunt: "No country on Earth has the right to charge for the use of international waterways."

The real test of this alignment comes when US-Iran talks move from the June 17 MOU toward a permanent agreement. How much of the GCC's agenda — from Hezbollah's weapons to Iran's proxy networks — ends up written into that text will determine whether Wednesday's joint statement was architecture or atmosphere.
