Candel Therapeutics appoints AstraZeneca veteran Mark Sims as chief commercial officer

AstraZeneca's global headquarters in Cambridge, UK — where Mark Sims spent eight years building and leading one of the company's largest oncology franchises. — D Wells / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Candel Therapeutics hires AstraZeneca's former EGFRm franchise head as CCO ahead of a planned Q4 2026 BLA filing for its lead prostate cancer therapy.

Candel Therapeutics has recruited Mark Sims as its first Chief Commercial Officer, a hire that reflects less a change in strategy than the arrival of a long-planned moment: the Needham, Massachusetts biotech is moving from trial mode to launch mode, and it needs someone who has done this before.

Sims, who joins effective July 6, spent eight years at AstraZeneca, most recently as Vice President and Global Franchise Head for EGFRm Lung Cancer — one of AstraZeneca's largest oncology franchises. Before that he held senior commercial roles at Novartis and earlier positions at Merck and Idenix Pharmaceuticals. By any measure, the hire is aimed squarely at the prostate cancer market.

The machine being built

Candel's lead programme, aglatimagene besadenovec (CAN-2409), completed a pivotal Phase 3 trial in intermediate- to high-risk localized prostate cancer and carries both Fast Track and Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy designation from the FDA — a rare pairing that can shorten review timelines. The company plans to file a Biologics License Application in the fourth quarter of this year, positioning potential approval and launch for 2027.

CEO Paul Peter Tak, speaking at the announcement, cited the data, the regulatory designations, and the BLA timeline as the context for Sims' appointment — the scaffolding, in other words, is already standing.

The commercial infrastructure around it has been going up in parallel. Candel signed a commercialization agreement with EVERSANA earlier this year to handle U.S. launch logistics, and in February secured a \$100 million royalty funding deal with RTW Investments tied to potential aglatimagene approval. The company reported cash of roughly \$217 million, which management says covers operations into the first quarter of 2028 — comfortably past a plausible approval window. The addressable population, by Candel's own estimate, is around 65,000 U.S. patients annually who are candidates for radiotherapy-based curative treatment.

Sims' mandate is to build on that foundation: strategy and launch readiness for the prostate cancer indication, while the EVERSANA partnership handles field infrastructure. It is the kind of division of labour a small-cap biotech uses to preserve cash while still fielding a credible commercial team.

The broader pipeline

Aglatimagene is not Candel's only asset, but it is the one with a clock on it. The company is also advancing a Phase 3 NSCLC programme and linoserpaturev (CAN-3110) in recurrent high-grade glioma, a notoriously difficult indication. Those programmes add optionality rather than near-term revenue — the prostate cancer BLA is what the market will be watching.

The appointment of a CCO with Sims' pedigree — and AstraZeneca's EGFRm franchise is not a small operation — signals that Candel is taking launch readiness seriously enough to hire accordingly. Whether that translates to a commercial product depends on the FDA. The Q4 2026 BLA filing is the next concrete milestone to track.
