Barclays upgrades Keurig Dr Pepper, sees $6 of hidden value in breakup plan

Keurig Dr Pepper logo. The company plans to separate into two publicly traded entities by early 2027. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Barclays lifted KDP to Overweight with a $36 target, saying its planned split into Beverage Co. and Global Coffee Co. is now credible enough to buy.

Keurig Dr Pepper stock got a lift from Barclays on Thursday, when analyst Lauren Lieberman upgraded the shares to Overweight from Equal Weight and raised her price target by $6 to $36 — implying roughly 15% upside from Wednesday's close of $31.39.

The move reverses a downgrade Barclays issued in September 2024, when Lieberman flagged concerns about governance, elevated leverage, and the complexity of the company's plan to fold in JDE Peet's and spin out a standalone coffee business. Nine months on, she wrote, "several pieces of the puzzle have fallen into place," reducing the risk around a trade that had been difficult to size with confidence. Barclays pointed specifically to the resolution of private credit financing arrangements and greater clarity on the mechanics of the planned separation as the factors that shifted the calculus.

Two companies by early 2027

The strategic backdrop matters here. KDP completed its acquisition of JDE Peet's in April 2026, financed through roughly $9 billion of new long-term debt, $8.5 billion of equity, and the assumption of approximately $5 billion of existing bonds — a transaction that temporarily pushed combined net leverage to around 4.5 times EBITDA. The plan is to unwind that overhang by splitting the company into two publicly traded entities: a North American Beverage Co. anchored by Dr Pepper, Snapple, and related brands, and a Global Coffee Co. combining the Keurig and JDE Peet's businesses. Separation is targeted for early 2027.

Barclays characterised KDP as "a rare valuation opportunity within the consumer staples sector," noting that its blended EV/EBITDA and P/E frameworks point to an intrinsic value of $35 to $37 per share. The firm forecasts adjusted earnings per share of $2.30 for 2026, rising to $2.52 in 2027, and sees a $400 million synergy program from the JDE Peet's integration as a further cushion. Target leverage ratios at separation — 3.5 to 4.0 times for Beverage Co. and 3.75 to 4.25 times for Global Coffee Co. — remain consistent with investment-grade profiles, Lieberman noted.

One complication has surfaced in the interim. Rafa Oliveira, who had been designated to lead Global Coffee Co., will leave at the end of July to become CEO of Heineken — removing a named executive the market had begun to associate with the separated entity. KDP's board, with chairwoman Pamela Patsley leading the process, has opened a search for a replacement. CEO Tim Cofer will oversee the coffee business in the interim while also preparing to head Beverage Co. after the split.

KDP shares are up roughly 14% year-to-date and carry a dividend yield of around 2.9%, with the next quarterly payment of $0.23 per share due July 10. The next formal catalysts are the CEO search resolution for Global Coffee Co. and any management update on leverage progression — the two remaining variables Barclays flags as the key distance between the current share price and its $36 target.
