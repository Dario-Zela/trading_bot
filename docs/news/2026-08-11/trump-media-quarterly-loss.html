Trump Media posts $238m loss as crypto treasury slides

Trump Media's bitcoin treasury, once pitched as strategic upside, became the primary driver of a $238m quarterly loss. — Jorge Franganillo / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Trump Media posted a $238.1m quarterly loss as its bitcoin and crypto token holdings were marked down $190.4m, prompting a retreat from its crypto treasury strategy.

Trump Media & Technology Group, the company behind Truth Social, reported a net loss of $238.1 million for the second quarter, a result driven almost entirely by the collapsing value of the cryptocurrency it has spent the past year accumulating.

The headline number dwarfs the $20 million loss the company posted in the same quarter a year earlier, even as revenue rose 89% year-over-year to $1.7 million on the back of its Truth+ streaming service and a new Truth API product. The gap between a $1.7 million top line and a $238 million loss is the story: $190.4 million of the shortfall came from unrealized markdowns on digital assets, pledged tokens and equity securities the company holds on its balance sheet rather than from its actual media operations.

Most of that damage traces to bitcoin. Trump Media held roughly 9,477 BTC worth $557.1 million as of June 30, after buying a total of 11,542 coins at an average price near $118,522 as part of a treasury strategy launched last year with plans to deploy up to $2.5 billion into crypto. As bitcoin's price slid through the quarter, so did the value marked on the company's books — first-half losses on digital assets alone reached $360.6 million. The company also held Cronos (CRO) tokens acquired in anticipation of a planned treasury venture with Crypto.com.

A retreat from the crypto pivot

That venture is now dead. On August 7, Trump Media, Crypto.com and SPAC partner Yorkville Acquisition Corp mutually scrapped plans to build a publicly traded CRO treasury vehicle, with interim chief executive Kevin McGurn citing a saturated market for digital-asset treasury companies rather than regulatory concern. "We wanted to get focused," McGurn said, framing the retreat as a return to the company's core media business — Truth Social, Truth+ and a pending merger with fusion-energy firm TAE Technologies — after a year spent branching into crypto and online betting. DJT shares fell 7.8% on the news of the unwound deal and slipped further after the earnings release, closing near $9.39, valuing the company at roughly $2.8 billion against trailing twelve-month losses exceeding $530 million.

The episode is a clean illustration of how corporate crypto treasuries transmit volatility rather than absorbing it. Companies that stack bitcoin as a hedge or a growth story inherit its price swings on the same balance sheet as their operating business, and when the asset drops, the loss shows up next to the revenue line regardless of how the core business is performing. Trump Media's next test comes when it reports third-quarter results, by which point the market will want to see whether a leaner, non-crypto treasury policy actually holds — or whether more of the balance sheet gets marked down before it does.
