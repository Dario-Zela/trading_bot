Tencent shares jump as WorkBuddy AI assistant sparks turnaround hopes

In one sentence
Tencent shares rose 3%+ on WorkBuddy's rapid adoption, but the company has disclosed no revenue or user numbers to back the AI turnaround story.

Tencent shares climbed more than 3% this week, a small but pointed rally for a stock that has otherwise had a miserable year — down around 27% in 2026 as gaming jitters and a rotation into AI hardware names hammered Hong Kong-listed tech. The trigger was WorkBuddy, a workplace AI agent that has, almost by surprise, become the most-used product of its kind in China.

WorkBuddy launched in March as part of Tencent's Hunyuan model family, designed to take a single instruction — build a deck, analyse a spreadsheet, draft a report — and carry it out across multiple steps rather than answering one question at a time. By June, according to research firm Analysys, it topped China's desktop AI office-agent rankings with nearly 21 million monthly visits, more than double its nearest rival. Monthly visits had grown 831% in its first month alone. Tencent has since pushed it out as a standalone app on iOS, Android and HarmonyOS, and internally elevated it to the same strategic tier as QQ and WeChat — the two products that built the company.

A number investors can't check

What's notable is what hasn't been disclosed. Tencent has not published revenue tied to WorkBuddy, subscription or seat counts, or any data on how usage converts into paying enterprise customers. The 21-million-visit figure is a traffic metric, not a monetisation one, and it comes from a third-party research firm rather than Tencent's own reporting. On this week's earnings call — due Wednesday — investors are hoping for a first real look at those numbers. Until then, the stock move has been a bet on momentum, not a verdict on economics.

Catching up, not pulling ahead

The backdrop explains why investors are so ready to grab onto a good story. Tencent has spent much of 2026 seen as a laggard in China's AI race, watching ByteDance and Alibaba — the latter now pushing its own paid office-assistant tier through the Qwen app — capture more of the enthusiasm. Tencent's argument for catching up rests less on model quality than on distribution: WeChat alone counts roughly a billion users and four million mini-programs, a moat rivals would need years to replicate. That's a real advantage, but it's also the same argument Tencent has made about past products that never became the growth engine WorkBuddy is now cast as.

The pattern is a familiar one for mega-cap tech: a feature launch with strong early adoption gets read as proof of an AI turnaround, even before there's a dollar figure attached to it. That doesn't make the rally wrong — WorkBuddy's growth curve is genuinely steep — but it does make it fragile. Wednesday's earnings call is the first test of whether the traction shows up in numbers Tencent is willing to put in a filing, rather than ones a research firm estimated from app traffic.
