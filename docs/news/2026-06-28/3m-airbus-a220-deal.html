3M locks in long-term Airbus A220 supply deal as narrow-body demand holds

An Airbus A220-300 in house livery makes a low pass at Toulouse-Blagnac airport — the aircraft 3M has now committed to supply with thermal and acoustic insulation materials. — Romain COUPY / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
3M wins long-term Airbus A220 insulation supply deal, anchoring revenue to a narrow-body platform where Boeing currently has no certificated rival.

3M Company signed a long-term supply agreement with Airbus on June 23, securing a seat on one of commercial aviation's more defensible platforms. Under the deal, 3M will provide thermal and acoustic insulation materials for the A220 — a 100-to-150-seat narrow-body that Boeing cannot currently match with a purpose-built alternative.

The thermal materials are designed to improve operational efficiency, while the acoustic insulation reduces engine and airframe noise throughout the cabin. No contract duration or financial value was disclosed. What the two companies did confirm is that this agreement extends an existing relationship spanning multiple Airbus programmes beyond the A220.

"Our long-term agreement with Airbus reflects the value of deep collaboration in bringing advanced materials science to the future of aviation." — Eric Forbes, VP of Aerospace and Defense, 3M

The A220's production math

The A220 backlog stands at close to 1,000 firm orders — worth over $40 billion at list prices — so 3M is locking into a production queue rather than a product cycle. The constraint is on Airbus's side: the company now targets 12 aircraft per month in 2026, down from an earlier goal of 14, with the shortfall attributed to component scarcity and labour bottlenecks familiar across post-pandemic aerospace.

That slower cadence, however, does not drain the order book — it delays when work lands. Airbus delivered 93 A220-family aircraft in 2025, a programme record, and the platform has become the default option in the 100-to-150-seat segment that Boeing has not addressed with a certificated aircraft. The 737 MAX 7 sits a class larger; below it, Boeing offers nothing in current production.

3M's industrial crosscurrents

For 3M, the Airbus deal is a measured positive in a year when the company is working to rebuild investor confidence. CEO Bill Brown flagged in June that he expects organic growth "solidly above 3%" in Q2, with roughly 60% of the portfolio showing positive momentum in Q1. Full-year guidance targets adjusted organic sales growth of around 3% and adjusted EPS of $8.50–$8.70.

Goldman Sachs reinstated coverage on June 15 with a Buy rating and a $190 price target, characterising 3M as an "intriguing self-help story" with room for valuation re-rating if remaining liability overhangs clear. MMM shares edged down 0.85% on the day of the Airbus announcement — a muted reaction that reflects scale rather than scepticism.

The A220 contract adds revenue visibility in aerospace and extends 3M's materials footprint across Airbus's narrow-body lineup, but it does not shift 2026 numbers in any material way. The payoff accrues over years, paced by Airbus's ability to close the gap between its order book and its build rate. The next read on that will come with Airbus's Q2 delivery figures, expected in early July.
