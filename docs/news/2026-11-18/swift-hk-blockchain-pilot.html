Swift, Hong Kong and major banks test blockchain cross-border payments

In one sentence
Swift, the HKMA and large banks are testing tokenised-deposit settlement across borders, an incremental step that matters only if it moves from pilot to live volume.

A pilot coordinated by Swift, the messaging network that carries most cross-border bank payments, is testing blockchain rails for settlement, with Hong Kong authorities and large banks taking part. The reports describe a test rather than a live service. Volumes, dates and outcomes have not been published.

What was announced

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and Swift announced a new phase of cooperation on 16 November, according to trade and crypto outlets including Cointelegraph. The banks named are Bank of China (Hong Kong), Standard Chartered (Hong Kong) and HSBC. The plan is to study linking the settlement layer of the HKMA's Project Ensemble to Swift's blockchain-based ledger. The aim is multi-currency cross-border payments using tokenised deposits, which are ordinary bank balances represented on a blockchain.

Ensemble began its pilot in November 2025, settling in Hong Kong dollars through the existing real-time gross settlement system. A later update allowed banks to pay with tokenised deposits at any hour, settled in digital forms of central bank money.

Part of a longer run of tests

The new phase follows several earlier steps on the Swift side:

  • HSBC and Ant International ran a cross-border tokenised deposit transfer between Singapore and Hong Kong over Swift's network. It used ISO 20022 messaging and was billed as the first such case on the Swift network.
  • In July 2026, Swift said its ledger was ready for initial use. It was preparing a pilot with 17 major banks, including Citi, DBS and HSBC.
  • UOB and HSBC completed live Hong Kong dollar cross-border transactions on the Swift ledger. UOB, the first Singapore-headquartered bank on the platform, said it planned Singapore dollar and US dollar transactions in September.

That history shapes how the latest news should be read. The Ensemble link is a feasibility study of connecting two existing projects, not a launch. Live transactions have already happened on the Swift ledger, but in limited form and between a few institutions.

What is unconfirmed

The sources are mostly crypto and payments trade outlets. No primary HKMA or Swift document turned up in searches. Bank counts also differ between reports: 12 banks for Ensemble's transaction work and 17 for Swift's pilot, which may be different groups. None of the reports gives a timeline or any measure of success.

Some coverage links the news to particular crypto tokens. That is one outlet's framing and has no support in the announcement as described.

What would make it bigger

On the evidence so far, this is an incremental step in tokenised interbank payments. It becomes a larger story if testing turns into live settlement at volume, using central bank money, across several currencies. The markers to watch are a published timeline from the HKMA or Swift, a count of participating banks, and any report of live multi-currency transactions between the two systems.
