Rubio says recent tech outages are 'completely unrelated'

In one sentence
The US Secretary of State has dismissed any link between a run of recent major tech outages, but the outages and his reasoning remain unspecified in the available reporting.

The US Secretary of State has told the public that a run of recent major technology outages is "completely unrelated", according to a BBC Technology report. The denial is unusual. Marco Rubio's brief is foreign policy, not data-centre reliability, and officials rarely volunteer that separate failures are only coincidence unless someone has asked whether they are connected.

What is known

The reported facts are thin. Rubio's phrase, "completely unrelated", comes from the BBC's headline and summary. The Tribune could not retrieve the original report or the full context of his remarks. The outages themselves are described only as "a run of recent major tech outages". This article cannot name the affected companies, give dates or durations, or say what question prompted the answer.

One caution is needed. A search for the quote turned up nothing from this week. It returned coverage of an earlier episode: in February 2024, when AT&T's mobile network failed across the US, Rubio, then a senator, warned that a Chinese cyberattack would be "100 times worse". AT&T attributed that outage to an incorrect process applied during a network expansion, not an attack. That is a different event with a different role for Rubio, and it should not be read onto the present story. It does show how quickly large outages prompt questions about hostile action.

Why the denial matters

A statement that outages are unrelated can carry weight in a few ways.

  • Shared cause. If the failures traced to one cloud provider or a common piece of software, businesses would want to know, and so would regulators with an interest in concentration risk.
  • Security. A denial from the State Department could be read as ruling out a hostile state, which would make it a statement about attribution as much as engineering.
  • Official confidence. A senior official can only say "unrelated" with confidence if technical staff have told them so. The public has not seen that analysis.

None of these can be tested without the specifics. Absent a named list of outages and a stated basis for Rubio's view, his remark is an assertion, not evidence. Independent outage trackers and the affected companies' own incident reports are the usual ways to check such a claim, and they typically publish post-incident analyses within days or weeks.

What to watch

The test is whether the affected providers publish root-cause reports that point to separate, ordinary faults such as configuration changes or software updates. If they do, Rubio's characterisation will stand. If two or more point to the same upstream supplier, the denial will look premature. The Tribune will update this piece once the BBC's original report and the named incidents can be confirmed.
