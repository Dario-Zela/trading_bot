Shell starts Malaysian gas output and names a new CIO; energy shares slip

In one sentence
Shell has started gas production from Malaysia's Rosmari and Marjoram fields and named a new CIO, while energy shares slipped in afternoon trading for reasons not specified.

Shell has brought two gas fields off the coast of Sarawak, Malaysia, into production and has named a new chief information officer. Energy shares were lower in the afternoon session, with the oil majors among the biggest movers. The startup and the appointment were announced separately, and nothing in the available reporting ties either one to the share-price move.

The fields

Rosmari and Marjoram are deepwater sour-gas discoveries made in 2014. They lie roughly 220 km offshore Sarawak. Shell operates the project with an 80% interest, and Petronas Carigali holds the other 20%. The partners took a final investment decision in September 2022.

The development is compact in concept. A subsea tieback and an unmanned wellhead platform feed a 207-km pipeline to an onshore processing plant at Bintulu. The platform was designed to run on solar power. Earlier project descriptions put design capacity at about 800 million standard cubic feet a day. The gas is meant to keep the Petronas LNG complex at Bintulu supplied, which makes the project a supply-maintenance exercise more than a new export stream.

The startup date and initial volumes were not in the material available for this report, so how quickly output reaches that design rate is not yet known. Shell's Malaysia chair said earlier this year that the company hoped for first gas towards the end of 2026, so the timing is in line with the guidance.

The appointment

Shell has also named a new chief information officer. The name and start date were not available at the time of writing. Robbert Van Rutten, previously the company's chief information security officer, took the group CIO job in July 2023, so a new appointment may mean a change at group level or in one of the divisional roles. The report does not say which.

Such appointments rarely move a share price. The exception is when a new technology chief arrives alongside a shift in digital or cost strategy. Nothing in the announcement points that way.

The tape

Energy stocks slipped in the afternoon. The discovery report, from MT Newswires, gave no index level, percentage move or crude benchmark, and named no companies. Without those figures the move cannot be attributed. A fall in crude, a company-specific event or a rotation out of the sector would each fit what was reported. A single Malaysian startup of this size would not plausibly explain a sector-wide decline.

What to watch

The useful data points are the ramp-up. Shell and Petronas will need to show output approaching the design rate, and Bintulu's feedgas supply will show whether the fields offset declines elsewhere in Malaysia's gas system. On the personnel side, the name and remit of the new CIO will show whether this is a routine succession or something more.
