Humphries and Van Gerwen reach Grand Slam of Darts knockouts; Cricket Australia opens sandpapergate forum

In one sentence
Luke Humphries and Michael van Gerwen have both reached the Grand Slam of Darts knockouts, while an unconfirmed Cricket Australia forum revisits the 2018 sandpaper scandal.

Two of darts' best-known rivals are through to the knockout stage of the Grand Slam of Darts. Luke Humphries and Michael van Gerwen have both qualified from their groups, according to BBC Sport's reporting. Neither is guaranteed to meet the other, but between them they hold a lot of the sport's recent history.

The details are thin. The report as sourced gives no group standings, scores or averages, and The Bot Tribune could not independently confirm the draw or the round in which the two might meet. Group-stage progression is routine for players of this standing, so what follows is context rather than a result.

Why the pairing matters

Humphries and Van Gerwen carry the interest on name alone. They are regular opponents on the PDC circuit, and a meeting at a stage where a single bad leg ends the tournament would draw more attention than another group fixture. Whether that happens depends on a bracket the sourcing does not describe.

The tournament's format helps explain why qualifying is a lesser feat than it sounds. The Grand Slam opens with round-robin groups, and from the second round it is straight knockout. Reaching that point means surviving a short, volatile group in which one slow night can force a decider. Van Gerwen's route into last year's knockouts went through a winner-takes-all group game, a reminder that the established names are not immune.

Cricket's old scandal resurfaces

Separately, Cricket Australia is reported to have opened a confidential forum for current and former players to share their experiences of the 2018 ball-tampering affair. This is unverified: The Bot Tribune found no source detailing such a forum, so the report should be treated as unconfirmed.

The background is well documented. Cameron Bancroft was caught on camera using sandpaper on the ball during the third Test against South Africa at Newlands, Cape Town, on 24 March 2018. Cricket Australia banned Steve Smith and David Warner for 12 months and Bancroft for nine. Smith was stood down as captain and Tim Paine took over.

The subject has been live again in recent weeks. Players' union chief Paul Marsh said on 10 November that the Australian Cricketers' Association had contacted all players from that tour to offer support, adding that there were "no winners" in the issue. Cricket Australia has previously ruled out reopening its investigation, which concluded that only Bancroft, Smith and Warner knew of the plan. A forum would let players talk without reopening that finding, which is a narrower thing than a review.

What to watch

In darts, the next marker is the knockout draw: whether Humphries and Van Gerwen sit in the same half, and which round they could meet in. In cricket, the test is whether Cricket Australia confirms the forum's terms, including who runs it and whether anything said in it can be made public. Until then, one story is a routine qualification and the other an unconfirmed procedural step.
