Barclays sees more upside in Lilly and AbbVie as AstraZeneca rallies

In one sentence
Barclays targets imply about 9% more upside for Lilly and 14% for AbbVie after big gains, but with no stated catalyst the call says more about valuation than news.

A broker has told clients that two of the year's best-performing large drugmakers can still go higher. Barclays has set price targets for Eli Lilly and AbbVie that imply further gains, according to figures circulated by TIKR. The note appears to be a routine target call rather than a response to new trial data, an earnings change or a shift in guidance. Its interest lies in the question it raises: whether the big-pharma rally has run its course.

What Barclays is saying

Lilly shares have risen about 39% over the past year, per TIKR. Barclays' target implies roughly another 9% from here, a modest margin against the size of the year's move. AbbVie has gained about 27% over the same period, and the 14% upside figure is attached to Barclays' view. The attribution of that number is not clear in the material available, and neither figure has been checked against the note itself.

The arithmetic matters. A 9% implied gain on a stock that has climbed nearly 40% is a target that assumes the re-rating is largely done. For AbbVie the implied gain is larger, 14%, on a smaller prior rise. Read together, the two targets say Barclays sees more room in the laggard than in the leader. That is an inference from the percentages, not something the bank is reported to have said.

No catalyst has been cited. A target revision without one is usually a statement about valuation and earnings estimates rolling forward, not about news.

AstraZeneca's month

The same sector is producing other prompts to reassess. A Zacks piece asks whether AstraZeneca, said to be up 12% in a month, is a buy, sell or hold. The 12% figure has not been verified against price data. Zacks' "buy, sell or hold" articles are a recurring screener format rather than original reporting, and the one-line summary refers to a "valuation debate" without naming an analyst, a target, a multiple or a cause for the move. Without a trial readout, deal or guidance change behind it, a one-month rise says only that buyers were active.

Other pharma news this week was company-specific: GSK won FDA approval for its lung cancer drug Nelkarmi, the kind of event that does move a share price on its own.

The test

The case for caution is simple. When a sector has risen sharply and the next target implies single-digit or low-double-digit upside, the margin for disappointment shrinks. The case for the bulls is that a target above the current price from a large broker is still a target above the current price. Neither side has new evidence in this episode.

What would settle it is not another price target. It is the next earnings reports and any trial readouts or guidance updates from the companies, which would show whether estimates are rising fast enough to justify prices already up by a quarter to two-fifths. Until then, the Barclays call is a data point on sentiment, not on fundamentals.
