Waymo pulls driverless taxis from US freeways

In one sentence
Waymo did suspend freeway rides across its US markets in May and recalled 3,871 cars in June, but service began returning in late July; a fresh withdrawal is unconfirmed.

The headline on the BBC item, that Waymo has taken its driverless taxis off freeways across the US, describes something that did happen. It did not happen this week. Waymo suspended freeway rides in May 2026 and has been bringing them back since late July. If the BBC report is a fresh one, it is not a new withdrawal. This article rests on reports from other outlets, which could be checked. The BBC original could not be, so the framing and date of that piece remain unconfirmed.

What happened

In late May, Waymo paused freeway service in San Francisco, Los Angeles, Phoenix and Miami. The company said it wanted to improve how its cars respond in construction zones, and it told TechCrunch it was folding "recent technical learnings into our software". Reports at the time also cited flooded roads. Surface-street service carried on. Reports of the pause varied: some also mentioned a pause in Atlanta operations.

A viral video of a Waymo driving through traffic cones on 19 May preceded the pause, according to one aggregator. In June the company went further. It recalled 3,871 vehicles after some failed to recognise freeway construction closures, including incidents in Phoenix, AZFamily reported. The recall covered Jaguar-built cars made between March 2022 and May 2026 and was voluntary. It was a software fix, not a hardware one.

So the broad claim holds up in one sense: for about two months, the company's whole freeway offering was switched off. Waymo had only begun offering highway rides to the public in late 2025, so the service was young.

Back on the road, slowly

On 29 July, Waymo began restoring freeway routes, starting in Phoenix. Engadget and Gizmodo reported that Miami, Los Angeles and the San Francisco Bay Area were due to follow. Riders join an interest list in the app and are notified once approved. Even then, the system sends them onto a freeway only when it judges the route suitable. One timeline puts the restart in June, but most outlets date it to late July.

This search did not establish where each city stands as of today, and no source checked here confirms a fresh nationwide withdrawal in November. If one has occurred, it would be a second reversal, and the BBC would need to say what triggered it.

Why freeways are hard

Freeways look easier than cities: no pedestrians, no junctions. But they punish mistakes. Speeds are high, so a car that misreads a coned-off lane has little time to recover, and construction layouts change from week to week in ways a map cannot capture. The recall reason, failure to recognise closures, points to exactly that weakness. That is an inference from the recall wording, not a statement from Waymo.

What to watch

Three things would settle the matter: a Waymo statement on current freeway availability by city, any further filing with US safety regulators, and the BBC's own sourcing and date. Until those are checked, the safe summary is a temporary, software-driven pause that has been partly lifted, not a permanent retreat.
