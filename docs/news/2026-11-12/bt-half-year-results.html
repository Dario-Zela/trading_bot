BT reports higher half-year earnings

In one sentence
BT's half-year EBITDA rose about 1% to roughly £4.1bn and the interim dividend rose to 2.5p, but weak first-half cash flow leaves the valuation case resting on full-year delivery.

BT Group has reported a small rise in half-year earnings, and the numbers support the modest reading. Adjusted EBITDA for the six months to 30 September rose about 1% to roughly £4.1bn on revenue of about £8.75bn, also up about 1%. The results were published on 5 November. Whether that changes the valuation case is a separate question, and the figures so far suggest it does not.

What the print showed

The revenue line is the more notable of the two. One report describes it as BT's first increase in two and a half years. The company credited lower staff costs and cost-transformation savings for more than offsetting the continuing decline in legacy voice. That is cost-led growth in profit. It is useful, but it is not the same as an expanding customer base.

The interim dividend rose 2% to 2.5p a share. Full-year guidance was held, including normalised free cash flow of about £2bn for the year, excluding the effects of the TalkTalk transaction.

Where the gloss comes off

Cash is the weak spot. One report puts underlying free cash flow in the half at £236m, down 59%, with net debt at £21.0bn. A second source describes the £236m as normalised free cash flow, about £300m lower than a year earlier on a continuing-operations basis. The two accounts use different definitions, so the like-for-like comparison needs checking against the primary release. The first half is typically the weaker period for cash, which makes the full-year £2bn target the number that matters.

Broadband remains a drag. BT lost 366,000 lines in the half, and about 800,000 are still expected for the full year. Openreach now passes more than 24 million premises with full fibre, and BT expects to reach its 25 million build target in March 2027. Heavy build spending is the reason cash generation lags profit.

One offsetting item is pensions. A triennial review lets BT pay out up to £1.5bn this year, rising to £2bn by 2030, without triggering extra cash injections from the company. That removes one overhang on the cash outlook.

The valuation question

Shares rose in early trading, by 5.6% according to one report. A move of that size on a 1% rise in EBITDA suggests the market was responding to reassurance on cash, the dividend and guidance rather than to the earnings figure itself. Hargreaves Lansdown's headline described the results as ahead of expectations, which fits that reading.

The valuation case still rests on the build programme tapering and cash conversion improving as capital spending falls. A half in which free cash flow dropped sharply and broadband lines kept leaking does not yet show that. The signal that would change the argument is the full-year cash outturn against the £2bn guide, reported with the full-year results, alongside progress toward the March 2027 fibre target.

Figures here are drawn from secondary reports of the results; the company's own release is the authority on definitions.
