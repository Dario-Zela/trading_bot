Israeli strike on Gaza hospital kills at least five, civil defence says

In one sentence
A strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis killed at least six, Gaza civil defence says; Israel says it targeted Hamas militants inside, and neither account is independently verified.

A strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, in southern Gaza, killed at least six people on Thursday, according to Gaza's civil defence service. The toll was first reported as five and was revised upward as the day went on. The Israeli military says it was aiming at Hamas fighters it claims were operating inside the facility. The hospital's own account and the military's account of the target cannot both be right, and neither has been independently verified.

What is known

Civil defence spokesman Mahmoud Bassal said the strike hit the hospital's surgical department and caused extensive damage. About 20 people were wounded. Reuters, citing the civil defence service, reported that the dead included a surgeon, whom medics named as Rezik Abushira. Al Jazeera reported the strike as hitting an intensive care unit. That is a different part of the building from the one Bassal described, and the discrepancy has not been resolved in the accounts available.

The early reports said five dead. Later reports from Reuters and AFP put the figure at six. Both rest on the civil defence service, which operates under the Hamas-run authorities in Gaza. Its figures are widely used by international agencies, but the wire services reporting this strike have not independently confirmed them.

The Israeli account

The Israeli military said it targeted a senior Hamas militant and other members of the group's armed wing, whom it said were operating inside the hospital. It had not responded when the first reports appeared. It has not published evidence for the claim, and the available reports carry no independent confirmation of who was inside the targeted building. A Hamas spokesperson called the strike a "fully-fledged war crime."

That dispute is the legally significant part of the story. Under the laws of war, hospitals lose their protection only if they are used for acts harmful to the enemy, and even then an attack must be proportionate and preceded by a warning where possible. Whether the military's claim holds, and whether those conditions were met, would have to be established by evidence that has not yet appeared.

A ceasefire under strain

The strike came during a ceasefire that began in October 2025. Gaza's health officials say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed in Israeli attacks since the deal was agreed. Israel says four of its soldiers have been killed over the same period. Al Jazeera also reported 13 people killed across Gaza on Wednesday, including in a strike on a tent camp.

Nasser Hospital has been hit before. It was a main medical centre in the south during the war, and in August 2025 a double strike on the complex killed 20 people, including five journalists. That earlier episode drew wide criticism from aid agencies and several governments.

What to watch

The next markers are an Israeli military statement naming those it says it targeted, and any independent verification of the building hit and its use. The World Health Organization and UN agencies typically comment on strikes on medical sites within days. How they describe the hospital's operating status will decide whether this stays a single incident or becomes another case in the larger argument over attacks on Gaza's health facilities.
