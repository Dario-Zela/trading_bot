Acquitted 'China spy' says he was a fall guy for the security services, and naive

In one sentence
Chris Cash, whose China spying case collapsed before trial, tells the BBC he was a convenient fall guy for the security services, while admitting he was unprofessional and naive.

A former parliamentary researcher who was charged with spying for China has told the BBC that he was a "convenient fall guy" for the police and security services. He also says he was "unprofessional and naive". Chris Cash gave the interview on 11 November. It reopens a case that ended without a trial verdict on the evidence, and that left unanswered questions about how the state builds national security prosecutions.

What Cash says

Cash was arrested in March 2023 on suspicion of breaching the Official Secrets Act. He and his friend Christopher Berry were later charged with espionage. The Crown Prosecution Service dropped the case in autumn 2025, before it reached a jury, after the Director of Public Prosecutions said it could not proceed. Some outlets describe him as acquitted. The more precise description is that the prosecution collapsed.

His central claim is that the authorities needed a case. Cash suggests the police and security services wanted to put espionage threats from foreign states "on the map", and that he was a convenient person to do it with. He also argues the case served as a warning to others. These are his characterisations. The BBC reports no admission from the services that supports them, and no statement from them appears in the account of the interview.

The concession

Cash does not present himself as a bystander. He says it was "unprofessional and naive" to send "gossiping" texts to Berry. The BBC reviewed 6,273 Signal messages exchanged between January 2022 and March 2023. Cash marked 12 of them "off the record" or "very off the record".

In one exchange, Berry described a meeting with a senior Chinese official. Cash replied that Berry had been "elite captured". He says he meant it as a joke and did not see the risk. He also named Berry as a referee for a parliamentary pass, which was granted. According to the BBC's account, there is no evidence that Cash was paid by, or sympathetic to, the Chinese Communist Party.

Cash says much of what the pair discussed was publicly available. He has given the BBC a detailed spreadsheet of links and references to support that. The security services alleged that the messages passed information to people linked to the Chinese state.

Why the case fell

The prosecution did not fail on the content of the messages alone. The case depended on showing the material was useful to an enemy. The country's top prosecutor said it collapsed because government witnesses would not state that China was classed as a national security threat at the relevant time. The Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, said she was "very disappointed". Kemi Badenoch said the affair "stinks of a cover-up". The government has denied interfering. Berry has denied wrongdoing and said he was "unfairly subjected to a trial by media". China has called the allegations "pure fabrication and malicious slander".

What to watch

Cash's account is one side of a case that was never tested before a jury, so it cannot settle whether the evidence was thin. The test is whether the CPS, the Home Office or the security services respond on the record to the fall-guy claim, or whether any parliamentary committee asks about how the case was brought and dropped. Without a response, the interview adds to the existing argument about the handling of the case and settles nothing.
