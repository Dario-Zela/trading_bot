Poundland gets more time for a rescue as MPs question owner's payouts

In one sentence
Poundland is set for a short extension to find a rescue, while MPs say owner Gordon Brothers took millions from the chain on a 'knife-edge'; the firm disputes the claim.

Poundland, the discount chain with about 600 stores and 12,000 staff, looks set to get more time to find a buyer. Its US owner, Gordon Brothers, has told MPs it supports an extension of the legal protection that keeps creditors at bay. The same day, the chair of the Commons business committee accused the owner of taking "millions out of Poundland" while the business was on a "knife-edge". Gordon Brothers disputes the charge.

The extension had not been confirmed by the court when reports were filed. Management was due to ask the High Court on Thursday morning for a two-week extension of creditor protection. Some accounts describe the same request as 10 working days. Without protection, Poundland would probably call in administrators, and Alix Partners is already lined up for the job.

A chain with sales but little cash

Poundland filed a notice of intention to appoint administrators about two weeks before the hearing. Sources say sales are up on last year, but cash covers only a short period. Gordon Brothers has released a further £10m for stock purchases, which is understood to support the extension. One outlet puts the release at £9m, so the exact sum is unsettled.

Gordon Brothers bought Poundland from Pepco for a nominal £1 in June 2025. Shareholders took on £30m of debt in the deal. Pepco still holds £7.5m of it and is reported to be willing to wait for repayment if that helps save the business.

Who gets paid first

The argument is over that debt. Gordon Brothers is in talks with three potential bidders for the whole chain. It says it can raise more by breaking Poundland up and selling stock and store parcels to rival retailers. The bids are understood to be worth less than the £30m.

Two have been named:

  • Martin Property Group, a family-owned Northern Irish firm, backs a management-led rescue that includes the Dealz chain in Ireland. Its first bid was rejected as worth no more than half of £30m, with under £7m upfront. Another report puts its upfront offer at £5m.
  • Xyra Group, a little-known finance company with no retail experience, is reportedly offering about £12m upfront and close to £30m in total. Another report puts the upfront sum at £12.5m.

A rescue that repays the owner's loan in full looks more attractive to Gordon Brothers than a cheaper one that keeps more stores open. That is the tension the MPs are probing.

The MPs' case

Liam Byrne, who chairs the committee, said the owner had taken millions out over the past 17 months. He said he was "deeply concerned that Gordon Brothers' behaviour may have jeopardised thousands of jobs on Britain's high streets". He added that Gordon Brothers "refused to say it will prioritise saving jobs" over its own returns. He also suggested that Poundland, which appears to be recovering, may have been cut off from its own funds just before the Christmas trading period.

One report puts the sum at up to £20m. Neither that figure nor the "millions" claim has been tested in public. Gordon Brothers' European head, Mark Newton-Jones, gave nearly two hours of private evidence on Wednesday. Poundland's management, led by Barry Williams, gave evidence separately. The firm has defended its stewardship in a letter to the committee. It says Poundland remains "challenged and vulnerable to any potential downturn in future trading".

What to watch

The first test is the High Court's decision on the extension. After that, the question is whether any bidder can match the owner's £30m debt or persuade Gordon Brothers to accept less. If no deal emerges, administration moves back to the front. The committee has so far heard evidence only in private. Its next step, and any government move on the owner's legal obligations, will show whether the extraction claim leads anywhere beyond the hearing room.
