New York building collapse kills at least two

In one sentence
At least two people died and 17 were hurt when a Brooklyn residential building collapsed beside an excavation site, as rescuers searched and investigators began probing the cause.

A partly occupied residential building in the Flatbush neighbourhood of Brooklyn collapsed on Wednesday afternoon, killing at least two people and injuring at least 17. Rescuers were still searching the rubble overnight, and officials said they did not know how many people remained unaccounted for. The cause has not been established.

The collapse

The failure came shortly after 4pm, at 1321 Flatbush Avenue near Bedford Avenue. CBS New York reported that police described the structure as a three-storey residential building that was occupied at the time. The neighbouring building at 1323 Flatbush Avenue lost a side wall, which left stairwells and apartments exposed to the street.

Next to both, at 2625 Bedford Avenue, lay a vacant lot under construction. The Department of Buildings (DOB) said plans for a new three-storey building there had been approved and that excavation was under way before the collapse.

Of the injured, four are in critical condition and 13 are stable. Seven of the injured are children. One firefighter suffered minor injuries. The ages of the dead have not been released.

More than 250 firefighters and paramedics responded. Chief of Department John Esposito said the nearest fire company arrived in under a minute and pulled people out of the adjacent building. Crews then worked by hand, breaking through walls and using search dogs, among dangerous voids and unstable wreckage. One survivor was brought out at about 6.15pm. Flatbush Avenue was closed between Clarendon Road and Bedford Avenue.

"We know that we're not finished here, and we will likely not be finished for quite some time," Fire Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore said. Mayor Zohran Mamdani called the operation difficult and fast-moving and said it was "exactly what the FDNY trains for".

The question of cause

Officials have not said what brought the building down. Attention has turned to the excavation beside it. Glenn Corbett, a fire science professor at John Jay College who is not involved in the investigation, said deep digging next to an existing structure can destabilise it if the right precautions are not taken. That is general engineering context, not a finding about this site.

The record on the site is mixed. City records show that in April the DOB issued a violation to a demolition contractor, saying the building previously on the lot had been demolished despite a stop-work order. The violation is now listed as resolved, and the notice does not explain why the order was issued. Nothing published so far links that episode to Wednesday's collapse, and it is not yet clear whether it bears on the excavation or on the buildings next door.

The Brooklyn District Attorney's office is working with the DOB and police to determine whether any criminal conduct contributed. The DOB commissioner and investigators were sent to help with recovery and to carry out structural stability checks on nearby buildings.

What comes next

The first task is the search itself. Crews are expected to be on site for several days, and the final toll will not be clear until officials establish who was inside the two buildings and on the construction site. The Red Cross and the city opened a reception centre at the Flatbush YMCA for displaced residents and families, and anyone worried about a relative can call 311 or, from outside the city, 212-639-9675.

The investigation will then turn to permits, the demolition history, excavation methods and any inspections carried out on the site. The April violation and the question of what the stop-work order covered are the obvious early lines of inquiry. Whether the findings point to a failure of the site's own safety measures or of city oversight will decide whether the collapse prompts wider questions about construction near occupied buildings.
