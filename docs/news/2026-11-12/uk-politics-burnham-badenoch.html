Westminster roundup: Burnham on a warehouse inquiry and the census, Badenoch under attack

In one sentence
Inquiry evidence reportedly has Burnham flagging a Manchester warehouse's safety, while his census remarks and attacks on Badenoch remain unconfirmed Westminster talk.

Three separate Westminster stories landed on the same day, each involving a leading figure in British politics, and each reported so far by a single outlet. Taken together they show how much of the political week is made of remarks and testimony that have not yet become decisions.

Burnham and the warehouse

The most substantial thread is evidence to an inquiry, reported by the Guardian, that Andy Burnham, the Greater Manchester mayor, raised safety concerns about a Manchester warehouse the report describes as a "monster". The detail available at the time of writing is thin. The inquiry's name, the dates of any warnings, the recipients and Burnham's own words have not been independently confirmed, and searches of other coverage turned up nothing that matches the account.

That gap decides how far the story travels. As it stands, it is a regional matter for Greater Manchester residents. It becomes a national one only if the inquiry's documents show that Burnham flagged specific risks, to named bodies, and that nothing was done. That would bear directly on his standing, because his authority rests on a record of running the region. It could cut either way: evidence that he warned early reflects well on him, while evidence that he warned and then let the matter drop does not. Until the inquiry's published bundle settles what he raised, when and with whom, the claim cannot be read either way.

The census question

Burnham also signalled support, according to BBC Politics, for more ethnicity options on the next census. The background is better documented. The 2021 census in England and Wales used 18 tick-box categories plus a write-in option under "Other". The government has confirmed that a further census will take place in 2031.

The Office for National Statistics is already consulting on the standard. A 2026 consultation is considering multi-select responses, expanded options within the Mixed group, which currently offer only categories beginning "White and…", and new response options more broadly. The Royal Statistical Society has responded to it.

So a mayor's endorsement adds political weight but no decision. The ONS and ministers, not a regional politician, will settle the 2031 question, and no change to the questions has been reported.

Badenoch under pressure

The third thread is the Conservative leader, Kemi Badenoch, who is reported to face criticism from a double amputee marathon runner whose achievement she had cited in an earlier remark. Bridget Phillipson, the Education Secretary, separately attacked the Conservative record on LGBT+ issues. The remark itself, its date and the runner's name were not available to verify, so the substance of the exchange cannot yet be described. It looks like a two-front Westminster row with little reach beyond the parties involved.

What to watch

The warehouse thread is the one with consequences. The inquiry's published evidence will show whether Burnham's concerns were raised and ignored or simply recorded. On the census, the ONS consultation outcome is the point at which a signal could become policy.
