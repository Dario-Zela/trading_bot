AI rally lifts Nasdaq 100 by 2.8% and European shares to a record ahead of Nvidia's results

In one sentence
US tech and, reportedly, European shares rallied on AI optimism, led by CoreWeave and Nvidia, ahead of Nvidia's November 17 earnings report; the headline figures are single-sourced.

US technology shares jumped on Thursday on renewed enthusiasm for artificial intelligence, and European equities were reported to have closed at a record. Both moves came days before Nvidia reports quarterly results. The market is rewarding AI-linked companies in advance of the one report most likely to test them.

The figures need a caveat. The 2.8% gain in the Nasdaq 100 comes from a single Benzinga headline, and The Bot Tribune could not match it against an exchange or index-provider close. The European record rests on a Reuters headline that gives no index name, level or percentage gain. Neither has been independently confirmed here, and the numbers below should be read with that in mind.

The move

According to Benzinga, the Nasdaq 100 rose 2.8%, with CoreWeave and Nvidia named as leaders. A gain of that size in a single session is unusual for a large-cap index. Daily moves in the Nasdaq 100 are typically a fraction of that, so a 2.8% day implies broad buying across the AI complex rather than a few stocks doing the work.

CoreWeave is an informative leader. The cloud provider rents out Nvidia-based computing capacity and was added to the Nasdaq-100 index in June, so it now moves a benchmark as well as its own shares. It also reports a large contracted backlog: one summary of its August results put the figure at $104bn on quarterly revenue of $2.58bn. A company whose revenue depends on customers' AI spending, and on a steady supply of Nvidia chips, tends to react strongly to anything that touches either.

Europe joins in

Reuters reported that European shares closed at a record, attributing the rise to AI optimism and naming Siemens and CoreWeave as drivers. Siemens is an industrial group, not a chipmaker, and its inclusion points to a different channel for the AI trade: electrification, automation and data-centre infrastructure. The size of Siemens's move was not given. If the record holds up on checking, it would suggest the trade is spreading beyond US megacaps. One day's close cannot establish that.

The earnings test

Nvidia confirmed in an October 28 press release that it will report fiscal third-quarter results on Tuesday, November 17, after the US close, with a call at 2pm Pacific. The quarter ended October 25. That corrects the framing in some circulating items that cast November 12 itself as earnings day. It is the run-up, not the report.

The pattern matters because a rally ahead of results prices in a good outcome. If the market has already bid up Nvidia and its suppliers, an in-line quarter can disappoint, and only a clear beat on revenue and forward guidance would justify the move. Consensus figures were not available to this desk, so how high the bar has been set cannot be stated.

The stakes reach beyond AI specialists. Nvidia is among the largest single-stock weights in the S&P 500, so its results move broad index funds and the target-date funds in many US retirement plans. A current weight figure was not checked against an index provider and is left out.

What to watch

The first data point is the November 17 report, specifically data-centre revenue and the guidance for the next quarter. Reaction in CoreWeave and in European industrials such as Siemens would show whether the spread of the AI trade is durable. Pressure on Nvidia's leadership, covered separately in this edition, adds a governance question to the usual numbers.
