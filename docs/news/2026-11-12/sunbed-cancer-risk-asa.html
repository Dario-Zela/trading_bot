Sunbed firms must not downplay cancer risk, says ad watchdog

In one sentence
The ad regulator has again told tanning businesses they may not imply sunbeds are safe, given the WHO cancer agency's Group 1 carcinogen classification.

Britain's advertising regulator has again made clear that tanning businesses may not play down the cancer risk of sunbeds, a product the World Health Organization's cancer agency ranks alongside tobacco smoke and asbestos as a known cause of cancer. The reasoning is narrow and procedural. It rests on a broader principle: a commercial message cannot reassure consumers about something the evidence says is hazardous.

A note on sourcing: the specific rulings this piece is built around could not be independently matched to a published adjudication dated 12 November. The detail below draws on the Advertising Standards Authority's (ASA) published guidance and on its rulings reported earlier in 2026.

The classification behind the rules

In July 2009 the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) moved UV-emitting tanning devices into Group 1, its highest-risk category, meaning there is sufficient evidence that they cause cancer in humans. Group 1 describes the strength of the evidence, not the size of the risk. It also contains many agents with very different potencies.

UK law has followed that direction. The Sunbeds (Regulation) Act 2010, in force since 2011, bans under-18s from using sunbeds. It does not restrict adult use, which is where advertising comes in.

What the ASA polices

The ASA enforces the CAP Code, the advertising industry's self-regulatory rulebook. It cannot fine firms, but it can require misleading ads to be amended or withdrawn. Its guidance says sunbed ads must not claim or imply that use is safe, harmless or without risk, and should avoid health or medical claims, explicit or implied, about the effects of UV tanning.

The pattern of recent cases shows how that works in practice. In a batch of rulings reported in January 2026, the ASA upheld complaints against five tanning businesses. One firm's claims of "Safe, Responsible Tanning" and "Your safety is our top priority" were found problematic, with the regulator noting that NHS advice is cautious and warns that sunbed UV raises the risk of skin cancer. Others were criticised for implying health benefits, including vitamin D and skin-condition claims. A tanning accelerator marketed as the "safest tanning cream" was judged likely to leave consumers believing it avoided the health risks of tanning.

The complaint is rarely that a firm denies cancer outright. An earlier case involving the chain Indigo Sun, in 2014, did turn on a claim that sunbeds do not cause cancer. More often the issue is softer: "safe" used as a brand promise, or a wellness benefit offered in place of a risk warning.

The industry's position

The Sunbed Association, the trade body, has argued that the ASA and WHO rely on "outdated data", according to a BBC report on tanning firms' social media marketing. The same report says the association nonetheless urges members not to make medical claims in their ads. The ASA's position, resting on NHS and Cancer Research UK advice that there is no safe or healthy way to tan with UV, is unchanged.

What to watch

Because the ASA's remedy is withdrawal rather than penalty, its leverage lies in repeat scrutiny and in referral of persistent offenders. The measure of the latest ruling will be whether tanning firms' social media wording changes, and whether the regulator's next batch of cases finds fewer "safe tanning" claims than this one.
