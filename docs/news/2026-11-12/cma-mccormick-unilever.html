CMA raises concerns over McCormick's Unilever food deal

In one sentence
Barron's reports the UK competition regulator has raised concerns about McCormick's Unilever food deal, but the CMA's own notice and the sauce-mix detail are unverified.

The UK competition regulator has raised concerns about McCormick's deal to take over most of Unilever's food business, according to a report in Barron's. The headline points at sauce mixes. The Competition and Markets Authority's own notice was not located in checking, so the specifics of what it objects to, and in which categories, remain unconfirmed.

What is known

The CMA has been looking at the transaction for months. It invited comments from 21 July to 5 August, then launched a formal Phase 1 inquiry on 16 September. Its Phase 1 decision deadline was 11 November, the day before this article. Coverage in the trade press puts the value at about $44.8bn, though other outlets quote different figures (one cites £34bn, another $65bn), which probably reflects differing definitions of the perimeter and of the structure.

Phase 1 is a screening exercise. The authority asks whether a merger could produce a "substantial lessening of competition" in any UK market. It has three broad outcomes: clear the deal, accept proposed fixes, or refer it to a Phase 2 investigation by an independent panel. A statement of concerns at this stage usually gives the parties a window to offer undertakings, such as selling a brand, before a reference is made.

Where the overlap lies

The earlier coverage centred on condiments rather than sauce mixes. Combining McCormick's French's mustard with Unilever's Colman's raised an overlap that Unilever moved to pre-empt by proposing to spin Colman's into an independent entity. Reports also noted that the deal would put the world's two biggest mayonnaise brands, Hellmann's and McCormick's own, under one owner.

Sauce mixes are a different shelf. Whether the CMA has found an overlap there, and on what market definition, is the part the Barron's headline adds, and it is the part that could not be checked against a CMA document. Until that text is found, the sauce-mix detail rests on a single source.

What the US did

The US Federal Trade Commission granted early termination of its Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period on 5 October, so the American review is not an obstacle. Senator Elizabeth Warren separately wrote that the deal could give the combined group more power over prices, citing supplier concentration in pantry staples. Her letter has no legal effect on the CMA's process.

What to watch

The CMA's case page for the McCormick/Unilever merger inquiry is the primary source. It should show whether the authority has published a decision, which product markets it names, and how long the parties have to offer remedies. If it refers the deal to Phase 2, the timetable stretches by months and the transaction would be tested against a more demanding standard. If the parties offer a divestment that the CMA accepts, the deal could clear with conditions. Either way, the outcome will indicate how the regulator treats consolidation among branded condiments and spices, where a few large owners hold many of the shelf brands.
