Biogen upgraded to buy ahead of Alzheimer's drug data

In one sentence
Truist upgraded Biogen to Buy ahead of today's detailed Alzheimer's data, even though the topline results, out since May, already missed their main goal.

Biogen shares got a vote of confidence Tuesday from Truist Securities, which upgraded the stock to Buy from Hold and lifted its price target to $235 from $190 — timed to land hours before the company presents detailed data on an experimental Alzheimer's drug at the Alzheimer's Association International Conference in Amsterdam. The trouble, for anyone reading the upgrade as a simple green light, is that the headline numbers from that study are already public. And they weren't clean.

The drug is diranersen, known in trial documents as BIIB080, an antisense therapy designed to suppress production of tau, the protein that tangles inside neurons as Alzheimer's progresses. Topline results from its Phase 2 CELIA study, released May 14, showed the drug missed its primary endpoint: a statistically meaningful dose-response on the Clinical Dementia Rating–Sum of Boxes scale, the standard yardstick for cognitive and functional decline, after 76 weeks of treatment. That is the headline a cautious reader should hold onto before the rest of the story.

Betting on the fine print

What kept analysts interested is what came alongside the miss. PET and cerebrospinal-fluid measurements showed diranersen reduced tau pathology across every dose tested, and clinical decline slowed on secondary cognitive measures — most notably at the lowest dose studied, 60 milligrams every six months. Safety was broadly consistent with earlier-phase data, though serious adverse events rose at the highest dose. Biogen has already said it plans to advance the drug into Phase 3 registration trials regardless.

Truist's bet is that Tuesday's fuller data cut — session materials, subgroup breakdowns, the numbers behind the topline summary — will read more favorably than the May headline suggested, enough to convince a market that priced in disappointment. The firm was explicit that this is a pipeline call, not an earnings call: it said it does not view Biogen's second-quarter results, due July 29, as a major catalyst on their own. Truist also pointed to two other readouts as supporting the higher price target — a Phase 3 result for the lupus drug litifilimab expected in the fourth quarter, and one for felzartamab, in antibody-mediated organ rejection, expected in mid-2027 — assigning them probability-adjusted peak sales of $750 million and $500 million respectively.

Biogen shares, which have traded in a range of roughly $115 to $202 over the past year, rose on the news. But an upgrade built on a data readout that already missed its main goal is a wager on interpretation as much as substance: that investors and, eventually, regulators will weight the biomarker and secondary-endpoint signals more heavily than the primary miss. That is a real possibility in a field starved of tau-targeting successes — and also exactly the kind of judgment call that can look prescient or premature within a single trading session.

The immediate test came Tuesday afternoon, when Biogen's full CELIA presentation was scheduled at AAIC. The next comes July 29, with second-quarter earnings that Truist itself has already downgraded to a non-event.
