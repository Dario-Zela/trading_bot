SAP beats third-quarter estimates and affirms 2026 guidance

In one sentence
SAP's Q3 revenue passed €10bn for the first time, backlog growth held at 26%, and it now expects cloud revenue near the top of its 2026 range.

SAP reported third-quarter results on 21 October that cleared analyst expectations. It also leaned toward the upper end of its cloud-revenue guidance. That is slightly more than the "affirmed" in the headline. Europe's largest software company is a standard barometer for enterprise software demand on the continent. On this evidence the demand looks intact.

The numbers, and which currency

The dollar figures in circulation come from a secondary outlet. Benzinga put adjusted EPS at $2.18 against a consensus of about $2.03, and sales at $11.82bn against $11.49bn. SAP reports in euros, so those are conversions, and the company's own statement is the reference.

In euros, SAP's quarterly statement shows total revenue of €10.3bn, up 12% at constant currencies. It was the first quarter in which SAP's revenue passed €10bn. Cloud revenue rose 26% (25% at constant currencies) to about €6.7bn. Cloud ERP Suite revenue rose 29% (28% at constant currencies). IFRS operating profit rose 18%.

The $11.82bn sales figure is consistent with €10.3bn at an exchange rate near $1.15 to the euro. The dollar "beat" therefore partly reflects how the estimate and the result were converted. The euro-denominated comparison is the cleaner one, and the pre-release dollar consensus on Benzinga's quote page, $11.49bn, matches the figure in the triage notes. A reader should treat the EPS comparison with the same caution. SAP's headline profit measures are in euros, and a dollar "adjusted EPS" depends on the data provider's conversion method.

Backlog and guidance

The backlog is a better demand signal than the quarter's revenue. Current cloud backlog, the contracted cloud revenue due within twelve months, reached €24.1bn. That is up 28% as reported and 26% at constant currencies. It stood at €22.9bn after the second quarter, when growth was 27% reported and 26% at constant currencies. Constant-currency growth has therefore held at 26%.

On guidance, SAP now expects 2026 cloud revenue toward the upper end of its €25.8bn–€26.2bn range at constant currencies. The range itself was not raised, so "affirmed" is defensible, but the shift to the top of the range is the more informative detail. I did not verify the operating-profit outlook against the primary statement. In July, SAP trimmed that outlook by €0.1bn to reflect the Dremio and Prior Labs acquisitions.

SAP's statement says constant-currency backlog growth should slow slightly this year, from 25% in 2025. Management told analysts the fourth quarter, its largest bookings period, should see cloud revenue growth moderate to about 23%. That is a deceleration from 25% this quarter, though from a high base.

What it says about demand

The read-through is modest. A beat of this size is common for a large-cap software company, and the same figures carry a built-in slowdown for the final quarter. What stands out is the consistency: backlog growth in the mid-20s, a cloud business now close to two-thirds of revenue, and a guidance range that has drifted up rather than down. For European software, that is more reassuring than a single beat.

The next data point is the fourth-quarter result, due early next year. It will show whether the year-end bookings season delivers the roughly 23% cloud growth management has pencilled in, and whether the backlog holds at 26% growth at constant currencies.
