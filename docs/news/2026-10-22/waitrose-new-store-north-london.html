Waitrose opens first new supermarket in eight years

In one sentence
The John Lewis Partnership's Waitrose is opening its first new supermarket in eight years, in north London, ending a pause spent refurbishing existing stores.

The north London postcode of NW2 is about to get a Waitrose. The John Lewis Partnership's grocer is opening a new supermarket there, its first in eight years. For a chain that built its name on the well-heeled high street, it ends a long stretch spent refitting what it already had.

The store is reported to be around 18,000 sq ft, a full-size supermarket rather than one of the compact "Little Waitrose" convenience shops that have made up much of the chain's recent footprint. Details such as the exact address and opening date could not be confirmed ahead of filing, and Waitrose should be asked for both.

Why the pause matters

Waitrose has spent the intervening years tending its estate rather than growing it. The grocer has said it plans to refurbish some 80 stores through to 2028, and to start opening new supermarkets again for the first time in almost a decade. Its recent north London activity has been a rebuild rather than a launch: the John Barnes shop on Finchley Road reopened in August 2024 as a test of a new store concept, the second of its kind after Sudbury, which opened in November 2023.

A fresh site in NW2 suggests those trials have satisfied management that the format is worth taking to new ground. That is an inference from the sequence of events, not something the company has stated.

A second full-line supermarket is also planned, at Brabazon in Bristol, expected in 2027. Taken together the two sites mark a modest but real change of direction.

A small item, with a limit

There is little here for investors. The John Lewis Partnership is employee-owned and has no listed shares, so a single opening carries no direct market consequence. Its interest is as a read on a mature grocery sector, where the big chains have mostly been closing or reshaping stores rather than building them, and where a premium brand deciding to add floor space says something about its confidence in the customer.

What to watch next is the opening date itself, and whether the Brabazon scheme in Bristol keeps to its 2027 timetable. Two openings in as many years would make a pattern of what is, for now, a single store in a London postcode.
