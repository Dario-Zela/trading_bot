SpaceX jumps 10% as talk of a Tesla merger revives

In one sentence
SpaceX rose about 10% as the AI trade returned and Tesla merger talk revived, but no deal, filing or board decision has been cited.

SpaceX shares rose about 10% in a single session as investors returned to artificial-intelligence stocks, and the move has revived an old question: whether Elon Musk's companies are heading for a combination, or whether the market is simply pricing a story. Reports cited by the BBC and Benzinga describe talk of a SpaceX–Tesla tie-up. The material available cites no deal, no filing and no board decision.

What has been said, and what has not

The merger idea is not new. Musk has said combining companies cannot be discussed on an earnings call and would need a formal process, which stops short of ruling it out. Analyst commentary has gone further: Dan Ives of Wedbush has floated a combination as early as 2027, with the chip-fabrication project as a bridge between the two companies. That is one analyst's scenario, not company guidance.

The link to the merger talk is 'Terafab', a chip-fab ambition attributed to Musk's companies. Reports have put a Tesla commitment to the project at about $16.8bn, but estimates of total cost differ widely, from roughly $25bn to $55bn, and the scale and timeline are not settled in the reporting reviewed here. SpaceX's own stock-offering filing, according to one account, describes Terafab as a general framework without binding terms, and says neither Tesla nor its other named partner is obliged to stay in it. A framework that either side can walk away from is a thin foundation for a merger thesis.

Strategy or speculation

The case for strategy is that the pieces fit on paper. Tesla needs vast quantities of advanced chips for driving software and robotics. SpaceX, through its AI interests, needs compute. A shared fab would tie the two supply chains together, and a merger would formalise that.

The case for speculation is in the sequence. The shares moved because the AI trade came back, and the merger talk followed the price rather than preceding it. A 10% day for a heavily retail-held stock is consistent with sector momentum alone. Nothing in the reporting shows a new fact about the companies themselves.

There are also practical obstacles. Both companies are among the most richly valued in the US market, with large retail shareholder bases. Any exchange ratio would need independent board review, and Tesla's minority holders would be entitled to scrutiny of any related-party terms given Musk's stakes on both sides. None of this makes a deal impossible. It makes it slow, and visible in filings long before it is complete.

What to watch

If talks are real, the evidence will be documentary: a special committee at either company, a regulatory filing, or a change in how Terafab commitments are described. Tesla's third-quarter earnings, due alongside Meta's this week, give Musk a platform to say more, or to decline again. Until then, semiconductor and AI-infrastructure investors have a headline to trade, not a transaction to reprice.
