Tories suspend former candidate over contract-killing claims

In one sentence
The Conservatives have suspended Jack Ross, a former local candidate, after an Al Jazeera film appeared to show him discussing hired killings; he denies it and Police Scotland is reviewing the footage.

The Conservative Party has suspended a former local election candidate after an Al Jazeera undercover documentary appeared to show him discussing the price of hiring contract killers. The man is Jack Ross, chief executive of the campaign group Turning Point UK (TPUK). He denies the programme's findings. Police Scotland is assessing the footage, and the party's own inquiry has only just begun.

What the documentary alleges

The film, Contract Killers and the Far Right, was reported by the Guardian on 21–22 October. It says Ross, apparently filmed by an undercover reporter, asked what the reporter wanted done and then set out options running from assault to killing, with prices. One report put the sums discussed at up to £50,000. The Guardian account says he offered to "broker" the services of a Glasgow-based man described as having alleged links to the Ulster Volunteer Force, a loyalist paramilitary group. That man is not named. The same man is said to have offered killings, including a hit-and-run made to look like drink-driving.

Some of the quoted remarks are blunt. Ross is reported as saying: "They can shoot someone. They can kill someone." He is also said to have told the reporter he would need to "keep my name out of it" if there was "lashback", and to have warned that journalists could hand recordings to police. The film further alleges that he was paid by Zimbabwean interests to organise a staged rally in favour of lifting sanctions.

Sources disagree on when the footage was recorded. One account says 2022, another that the investigation began in 2021, when a reporter joined TPUK as a videographer. No account found for this article says the programme shows a killing being commissioned or carried out.

Suspension and denial

A Conservative spokesperson said: "Mr Ross's membership of the Conservative party has been suspended with immediate effect pending an investigation." Ross stood as a Conservative in local elections. The reports do not say which ward or year, and that detail matters for judging what vetting the party did at the time.

Al Jazeera reports that Ross "completely" denies its findings and says nothing he told the undercover reporter was meant seriously. The Guardian approached him through TPUK. No response from the organisation is included in the version of the article available to this writer.

Police Scotland said it is aware of the documentary and will review and assess it "for any criminality". That is a statement about assessment only. It is not an investigation announced, a suspect named or a charge brought.

The wider cast

The Guardian also reports on TPUK's chief operating officer, Nick Tenconi, who is filmed outlining a plan to use football hooligans to stir up trouble. He attended the 2022 Conservative conference with Ross. There is no suggestion in the material reviewed that the party knew of any of this at the time, but the overlap will draw questions about who was admitted to its events and on whose invitation.

What the case does and does not show

The suspension is a holding measure. Parties commonly use it when allegations are serious and the facts are unsettled, and it implies no finding. The case rests, so far, on one broadcaster's covert filming, a published denial that the words were serious, and a police review at its earliest stage. Talk of a plot, as opposed to talk, is not supported by anything public.

The vetting question is real but narrower than it may look. Ross was a local candidate whose alleged conduct was filmed after, or around, his candidacy. Whether the party could have known depends on dates not yet established.

Two things will settle how large the story becomes: whether Police Scotland moves from review to investigation, and what the Conservatives' inquiry concludes about Ross and his association with the party. Neither has a published timetable.
