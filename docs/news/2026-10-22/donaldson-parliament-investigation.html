Jeffrey Donaldson investigated over alleged rape inside Parliament

In one sentence
The Met is investigating an unproven allegation that jailed ex-DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson raped a woman in Parliament in 2016; no arrests or charges so far.

The Metropolitan Police are investigating an allegation that Jeffrey Donaldson, the former leader of the Democratic Unionist Party, raped a woman inside the Palace of Westminster. The allegation is unproven and no charges have been brought. It reaches the capital's police at a point when Donaldson is already in prison for other offences.

What the Met has said

The force said it received a report on 13 October from the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI). It describes an alleged rape in 2016 involving one victim, who is being supported by specialist officers. The Met called the inquiry early-stage. No arrests have been made and enquiries are continuing.

Its statement did not name Donaldson. The link comes from media reporting. A woman from Northern Ireland was reportedly interviewed by PSNI officers, who then passed their information to London. Because the alleged offence is said to have happened on the parliamentary estate, the Met rather than the PSNI would lead any investigation.

The Belfast Telegraph reported in July that a woman said Donaldson sexually assaulted her in the DUP's parliamentary offices after a tour in 2016. She said he tried to kiss her and put his hand up her skirt. The BBC later reported that the same woman also alleges he raped her. Outlets differ slightly on details, and the allegation has not been tested in any court.

The existing case

Donaldson, who led the DUP until he resigned in March 2024, was convicted in June of historical sex offences against two girls. The offences ran from 1985 to 2008. He was convicted of 18 offences: one count of rape, 13 of indecent assault and four of gross indecency. He was sentenced to 15 years. He has been stripped of his knighthood and removed from the Privy Council. His solicitor has said an appeal has been lodged.

The new allegation is separate from those convictions. Any new prosecution would have to be decided on its own evidence, and he is presumed innocent of it.

Why the setting matters

Westminster has spent years trying to repair how it handles complaints of misconduct. It created an independent complaints and grievance scheme after the 2017 revelations, and it has been criticised since for being slow and opaque. A serious sexual allegation said to have taken place in an MP's workplace is a test of that machinery, even though this one is going through the police and not the parliamentary process.

The period also matters. The alleged incident dates from 2016, when Donaldson was a sitting MP. Parliament will presumably be asked what records, such as pass logs and CCTV, still exist from that time. Nothing in the public reporting says what evidence the Met has.

What to watch

The next step is whether the Met moves from an early-stage inquiry to interviewing Donaldson, who is in custody, and then passes a file to the Crown Prosecution Service. Separately, the outcome of his appeal against the June convictions is still pending. Neither has a published timetable.
