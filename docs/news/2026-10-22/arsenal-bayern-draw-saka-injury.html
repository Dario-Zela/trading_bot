Rice earns Arsenal a draw at Bayern, but Saka injury 'does not look good'

In one sentence
Declan Rice's 82nd-minute goal earned Arsenal a 2-2 draw at Bayern, but an early injury to Bukayo Saka, which 'does not look good', is the bigger worry.

Arsenal left Munich with a point, a late equaliser from Declan Rice and a worry. Bukayo Saka went off injured, and the early word on the winger, as relayed in the BBC Sport report, is that it "does not look good". The match finished 2-2 at the Allianz Arena on Wednesday night in the Champions League. Arsenal can be pleased with the draw, and Saka's injury is the larger concern.

A game that swung twice

Bayern were the more threatening side for long spells, and one match report noted they will feel they should have won. Arsenal still took the lead first. Eberechi Eze, on as a substitute for the injured Saka, collected Martin Ødegaard's punt in behind and scored in the 25th minute. Alphonso Davies levelled before half-time, in the 35th.

The second half belonged to Harry Kane. Bayern's captain had a shot blocked by a handball from Arsenal's Timber, and he converted the penalty himself in the 61st minute. Kane has scored against Arsenal in this competition before, from the spot at the Emirates in April 2024. He did not need many chances to hurt them again.

Rice finds the answer

Arsenal were behind with eight minutes left when Rice struck in the 82nd. Reports credit the build-up to Noni Madueke and Viktor Gyökeres. The goal rescued a point against a side that had looked likelier to take all three, and it keeps Arsenal in the mix in the league phase.

A point in Munich is a good result on its own terms. This one cost Arsenal their right winger.

The Saka question

Saka's withdrawal came before Eze's goal, so the injury happened early. The source says it "does not look good", but no diagnosis or timescale had been published when this piece was written. The nature and length of the injury are unconfirmed and should be checked against the club's next update.

The cover is the question. Eze scored after replacing Saka, which shows he can fill the role. But Saka has been among Arsenal's most dependable attackers for years, and a long absence would stretch a squad that is also playing across several competitions. Madueke and Gyökeres both featured in the equaliser, so Arsenal have options in attack. Replacing Saka's output over a run of games is a different test from replacing him for 70 minutes.

What next

The immediate catalyst is a medical assessment of Saka, which should settle whether this is a matter of days or months. Arsenal's next fixtures will show how Mikel Arteta rearranges the right side without him.
