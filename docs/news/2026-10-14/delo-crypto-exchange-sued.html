Crypto exchange founded by Reform donor Ben Delo sued for £20m

In one sentence
A £20m claim has reportedly been brought against BitMEX, co-founded by Reform UK's biggest donor Ben Delo, but the claimant, court and grounds are not yet established.

A £20m legal claim has been brought against a crypto exchange co-founded by Ben Delo, who is also the largest single donor to Reform UK. The Bot Tribune has not been able to establish who is suing, in which court, or on what grounds. The claim is therefore reported here as it was flagged, and the details that matter most remain open.

What is known

The exchange is understood to be BitMEX, which Delo co-founded in 2014 with Arthur Hayes and Samuel Reed. Searches this session turned up no filing, judgment or press statement describing a £20m claim. A Guardian politics item was the only pointer. Nothing below should be read as describing the substance of the claim.

A separate report from July 2026, carried by an aggregator and attributed to CryptoBriefing, said BitMEX was sued for $40.7m within hours of announcing that it would close. That figure is in dollars, and the report names neither court nor claimant. It has not been checked against the original, and it cannot be assumed to be the same case.

The company's position

BitMEX's operator, HDR Global Trading, said in July that the exchange would close on 23 September after a strategic review. Delo no longer runs it. If that date held, the defendant is a platform that has already wound down, which bears on how any judgment could be enforced and on whether the claim concerns past trading, customer funds or something else. The closure has not been independently confirmed here.

The company has history in court. In January 2021 BitMEX's owners pleaded guilty in the US to Bank Secrecy Act violations and agreed to a $100m penalty. Delo pleaded guilty in his own case in 2022 and was pardoned by President Trump in 2025. These facts come from earlier coverage and memory rather than primary documents, and the penalty figure in particular should be confirmed before it is relied on.

The political angle

Delo's link to Reform UK is what gives the claim public interest. Reports in September 2026 put his latest gift at £36m, described as the largest in British political history. It follows £4m given earlier in the year, according to Electoral Commission data cited by Decrypt. Parliament's corruption watchdog is reported to be investigating the payment. One crypto-industry outlet put Delo's share of Reform's second-quarter 2026 donations at about 75%.

A civil claim against a company he co-founded does not by itself say anything about his donations. The two matters are legally distinct. They would converge only if the claim alleged conduct that bore on how the donated money was made, or if its outcome affected his finances. Nothing yet published suggests either.

What to watch

The first test is the court record. A claim of this size would be lodged in the High Court or an equivalent forum, and the claim form would name the parties and the cause of action. Any BitMEX or HDR statement, and any comment from Reform UK or Delo, would help show whether the claim is live or historical. The corruption watchdog's inquiry into the £36m gift is a separate track, and its outcome would matter to the political story regardless of how the claim ends.
