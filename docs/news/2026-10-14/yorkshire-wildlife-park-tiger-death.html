Man killed by tiger at Yorkshire Wildlife Park named; family pay tribute

In one sentence
William Chapman, 23, a former child actor, died after entering a tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park, and police and council health and safety investigations are under way.

A 23-year-old man who died after entering the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park has been named as William Chapman. His family have paid tribute to him. The park near Doncaster is one of the best-known animal attractions in the north of England. The attack there has raised the question of how safely big cats are kept where the public can walk up to them.

What happened

The attack took place on Saturday 10 October, shortly after 1.30pm. South Yorkshire Police said they received reports of a man entering a tiger enclosure and that the tiger then attacked him. He was pronounced dead at the scene. Police later confirmed he was visiting the park alone. They asked the public not to share video of the incident, which had already circulated online.

ITV News reported that he was apparently filming the animal when he was killed. The tiger is an Amur tiger called Altai. The park said Altai is "safe and well and will not be euthanised". It closed the following day while staff received support, and it expressed sympathy to the man's family and loved ones.

Local reports described a screen being put up around the enclosure and said shots were fired. Those accounts could not be verified, and neither police nor the park had confirmed them in the coverage reviewed.

The man and his family

Chapman had been a child actor. He attended the Jackie Palmer Stage School in High Wycombe, Buckinghamshire, and the school said it was deeply saddened by the loss of its former student. According to IMDb, he appeared in ITV's May Contain Nuts and in the BBC series True Stories.

His family described a warm, lively person. They also said he had long suffered significant mental illness and had been prescribed strong medication, and that the coroner will decide what part this played in his death. The coroner's findings will carry weight here. A visitor who deliberately enters an enclosure raises different questions from one who is let in by a failed barrier or an unlocked gate.

The safety questions

A health and safety investigation has been launched by the City of Doncaster Council, alongside the police inquiry. Local reporting says Chapman is believed to be the first member of the public killed by a tiger in UK history. That would make the case unusual. Zookeepers have died in earlier incidents, among them a keeper killed by a tiger at Hamerton Zoo in Cambridgeshire in 2013, but fatalities among visitors are almost unheard of.

That rarity cuts two ways. It suggests the barriers and rules at UK parks usually work. It also means the sector has little precedent for a visitor reaching an enclosure. The questions are practical ones. How the man got in has not been made public, and that answer will decide whether this was a gap in the perimeter, in supervision, or in neither. Investigators will also look at whether the park's response afterwards met the standards expected of a licensed zoo.

The politics have been muted so far. Doncaster MPs expressed sadness, and Ed Miliband sent condolences to the family. Neither has called for changes to the rules.

What comes next

The police inquiry and the council's health and safety investigation are continuing, and the coroner will open an inquest. The park's licence conditions and any findings on the enclosure's design are the likeliest places for changes to emerge. Altai's survival means the animal will stay at the centre of the park's decisions on whether and how the enclosure reopens.
