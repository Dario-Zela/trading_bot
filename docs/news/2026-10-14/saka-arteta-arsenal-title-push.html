Saka responds to Arteta's challenge as Arsenal lean on his 'huge impact'

In one sentence
Told by Arteta to aim for two or three goals a game, an unwell Saka came off the bench to assist and score in Arsenal's 3-0 Champions League win over Lille.

Arsenal's manager spent the build-up to a Champions League night telling Bukayo Saka to think bigger. The winger answered in about half an hour of football. Saka was ill and left on the bench against Lille, then came on, set up one goal and scored another in a 3-0 win at the Emirates.

The challenge

Mikel Arteta's message before the game was about ambition rather than arithmetic. Saka's best return is 20 goals in a season, and Arteta wants him to stop treating that as the ceiling. He said the winger should aim for "two or three goals" in each game. Arteta added that he does not expect that literally every week, only that Saka should walk out believing it is possible.

The prompt was a run of chances that did not go in. Reports have Saka missing a late one-on-one against Leeds goalkeeper James Trafford, after earlier misses against Sunderland and Napoli. He still leads Arsenal's Premier League scoring with three goals from the opening six matches. The complaint is about conversion, not about his output.

The response

Saka was not fit to start because of illness. He came on, assisted Eberechi Eze's goal, then finished Arsenal's third with a one-touch effort. Jurrien Timber had opened the scoring. The goal took Saka to four in nine games this season.

Arteta's verdict afterwards was brisk. Saka "came on and made a huge impact and delivered with real quality", he said, and "could have had another four or five goals by now, easily". He also described a deliberate positional tweak to bring Saka closer to goal. "That is where we need to get him," he said.

There was a second tactical note. Viktor Gyökeres had only 12 touches before being replaced, while Kai Havertz looked a more natural fit up front. Noni Madueke supplied Timber's assist but had a mixed night. Arsenal moved into a share of the Champions League lead, while Lille, beaten earlier by Real Betis, remain without a point.

What it means

This is a morale story more than a tactical revolution. Arteta publicly asked for more, got it within a day, and could point to the same player's misses as proof that the chances are there. The framing also shows how much of the early-season attack runs through one player. Arsenal's title push leans on Saka for goals as well as creativity, and a manager who says he should be on four or five more goals is acknowledging how much he expects from him.

Two caveats apply. One game against a side with no points is a thin test of a new mindset. And Arteta's "two or three" is a motivational figure, not a target he expects to be met. The measure will be whether the positional shift keeps Saka in shooting range against better opposition, and whether the Leeds-style misses turn into goals when the league resumes.
