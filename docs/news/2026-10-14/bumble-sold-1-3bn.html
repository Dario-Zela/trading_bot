Bumble sold for $1.3bn in a deal pitched as a 'bet on the future of humanity'

In one sentence
Bumble agreed to a $1.3bn all-cash takeover by Ithaca Holdings at $6.05 a share, a 138% premium to a depressed price, with the buyers calling it a 'bet on the future of humanity'.

Bumble, the Austin-based dating app, has agreed to be taken private by Ithaca Holdings in an all-cash deal that its announcement values at $1.3bn. The buyers' co-founders, Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman, framed it as more than a dating-app trade. "This investment is a bet on the future of humanity," they said jointly in the company's statement. The arithmetic is more modest than the phrase.

The price

Shareholders will receive $6.05 per Class A share. That is about 138% above the unaffected close on 9 October and about 119% above the 30-day volume-weighted average price. The premium looks large because the starting point was so low. Before news of talks broke, Bumble's market value was around $389m, and the shares had fallen nearly 29% this year, according to Reuters.

Reuters and the Wall Street Journal reported on 12 October that talks were under way at roughly $1bn. The shares rose about 50% that day, to around $3.81. The final price of $6.05 is well above even that run-up, so holders who sold into Monday's jump left money on the table.

The $1.3bn headline needs a caveat. The company's release does not say whether it is an equity value or an enterprise value, which would include debt. It discloses no debt figures. A buyer paying $1.3bn for the equity is making a different bet from one paying $1.3bn for the whole capital structure. Until the information statement and the Schedule 13E-3 filing appear, that question stays open.

The process

An independent special committee recommended the deal, and the board approved it unanimously, with founder Whitney Wolfe Herd recusing herself. Affiliates of Blackstone and Wolfe Herd then gave written consent, which supplies the required shareholder vote. Ordinary shareholders will not get one. They will receive a Schedule 14C information statement instead. Reuters reported that Wolfe Herd is expected to roll at least part of her stake into the new company.

"This transaction with Ithaca represents the best outcome for our shareholders," said Ann Mather, chair of the board and of the special committee.

Ithaca is a recently formed firm. Gupta and Hegeman will become Bumble's co-CEOs after closing, and the company will remain based in Austin. No closing date has been given, and the deal still needs regulatory approvals.

Does the pitch hold up?

The numbers describe a distressed asset being repriced, not a civilisational wager. Online dating is crowded and growth has slowed. Reuters notes weaker engagement, particularly among Gen Z users tired of "endless swiping", and says larger rival Match Group, owner of Tinder and Hinge, is under similar pressure. Bumble had explored a sale earlier this year. Wolfe Herd, who founded the app in 2014, stepped down as chief executive in January 2024 and returned in March 2025. She has been rebuilding the product around AI-driven features.

A private owner can pursue that rebuild without quarterly scrutiny of user numbers, and that is the practical case for the deal. The buyers have not published the strategy behind the rhetoric. Nor has anyone shown how a 138% premium on a depressed share price becomes a return.

The next data points are the 14C and 13E-3 filings. They should show the debt load, the rollover terms and the financing. Those will say more about the bet than the quote does.
