Heathrow chief says skipping a third runway would be a 'huge missed opportunity'

In one sentence
Heathrow's chief calls skipping a third runway a 'huge missed opportunity', but the remark is advocacy from an interested party and brings no new decision, date or figure.

Heathrow's chief executive has said that failing to build a third runway would be a "huge missed opportunity". The line comes from the person with the most to gain from the project, and the reported remarks add no new figure, date or decision.

The airport is the UK's busiest. It has pursued a third runway, the Northwest Runway, for well over a decade. In June 2025 the government gave expansion conditional support, which moved the scheme past the earlier "no" stage. It has not reached final approval.

What the remark does and doesn't change

The headline is advocacy. It restates the case Heathrow has made throughout the process: that capacity is the constraint, and that the cost of not building falls on the wider economy. The airport's interest is plain, and the claim should be read that way.

Nothing in the remark alters the formal position. The scheme still has conditions to meet and approvals to secure. A chief executive's framing does not move any of them. Until a planning ruling, a funding structure or a regulatory settlement arrives, the statement is a marker of intent rather than a development.

Why the question stays live

The runway matters beyond one airport because it is a test of whether the UK can deliver major infrastructure against local, environmental and fiscal objections. Each of those objections has been raised against Heathrow before:

  • Local: noise and air quality for communities under the flight paths.
  • Environmental: whether more capacity can be squared with aviation emissions targets.
  • Fiscal: who carries the cost and risk of a multi-billion-pound build, and what it does to airline charges.

There is also a competitive angle. Expansion at Heathrow bears on the position of regional and other London airports, which have their own growth cases and would see the balance of traffic shift.

The government has tied the project to its growth agenda, which raises the political cost of delay. It does not remove the conditions attached to its support.

What to watch

The chief's comments become news only if they are followed by a decision. The markers to track are any planning or consenting step on the Northwest Runway, a published funding and charging framework, and any change in the conditions the government attached to its backing. Absent those, the case for a third runway remains where it has stood for years: argued loudly by those who would build it, and still unfinished in the approval process.
