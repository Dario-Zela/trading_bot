Man Utd: Martinez launches scathing attack on the board

In one sentence
Lisandro Martínez criticised Manchester United's board over summer investment and named Jason Wilcox after a 2-0 Champions League loss at Atlético Madrid, with no club response reported yet.

Lisandro Martínez has publicly criticised Manchester United's board, and the criticism is specific. The defender named a lack of summer investment and one senior executive. He spoke after United lost 2-0 at Atlético Madrid in the Champions League on Tuesday.

The row did not happen at Old Trafford, as an early summary of the story suggested. It followed a defeat in the Spanish capital. It leaves United winless in five games in all competitions, after their worst start to a league season in 40 years, according to reports of the BBC's coverage.

What he said

Martínez's central complaint was about recruitment. He said he "100 percent" expected more investment in the squad over the summer, given that United finished third in the Premier League last season. His reasoning is that a top-three side should have been backed to build on that, not left to stand still.

He also named Jason Wilcox, the club's technical director, saying he expects more from him. The criticism is therefore tied to a named role and a concrete decision, summer spending, and not just general discontent.

He defended the manager, Michael Carrick, arguing: "You can't keep changing, changing, changing the manager." The implication is that the instability sits above the dugout, with the people who hire and fund it.

The quotes here come from secondary reports of an interview. This article has not seen the full transcript, and outlets differ on how hard the language landed. Some call it a "blistering attack" on the hierarchy, while others frame it mainly as a complaint about investment and managerial turnover.

Reaction

Wayne Rooney, the club's record scorer, thought Martínez had overstepped: "I think he's maybe gone a bit too far, and that should have stayed internal." Some outlets suggest the outburst could damage the player's standing at the club.

Contract timing adds a sharper edge. Martínez has a year left on his deal and, the BBC noted, has yet to agree an extension. A player in that position who criticises the board is either making a point on principle or positioning himself, and the available reporting does not say which.

What to watch

The governance question depends on the club's response, and no official reply has been reported so far. Whether United discipline Martínez, ignore him or engage with his points will show how the board handles dissent after a poor run. His contract talks and the next round of results will show whether the dispute spreads beyond one player.
