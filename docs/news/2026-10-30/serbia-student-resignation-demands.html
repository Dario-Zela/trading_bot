Student-backed election winners demand sweeping resignations in Serbia

In one sentence
Days after winning Serbia's snap election, the student-backed list is demanding that senior police, intelligence and judicial officials resign and that graft during Vučić's tenure be investigated.

Serbia's student movement, four days after winning a parliamentary election, has moved from celebrating to making demands. On Thursday it called for the resignation of senior officials in the police, the intelligence services and the judiciary. It also asked for investigations into alleged corruption during the 14 years of Aleksandar Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) in power. The demands, reported by Reuters and NBC News, come before a new parliament has met.

From a railway station to the ballot box

The movement began on 1 November 2024, when the concrete canopy of the railway station in Novi Sad collapsed and killed 16 people. The disaster was widely blamed on corruption in public works. A call for a transparent investigation grew into months of student-led demonstrations and then into a demand for early elections.

The protests had already cost one government its head. Prime Minister Miloš Vučević resigned in January 2025, and Đuro Macut later formed a new cabinet. The students said at the time that a single resignation was not the point.

The result

The snap election was held on 25 October. Preliminary figures from the Republic Electoral Commission gave the Student List 52.65 per cent of the vote, or about 2.25 million ballots. The Vučić-led coalition took about 1.67 million. Turnout was close to 70 per cent, which reports described as the highest in a parliamentary election since the breakup of Yugoslavia.

Seat counts differ between outlets and are not final. Projections put the Student List at roughly 135 to 140 of 250 seats, and one report put the SNS on 104. The remaining seats go to ethnic-minority parties. Parliament cannot convene until the count is certified. The list is led by the cardiologist Ilija Srdanović, and it is not yet clear who would be prime minister.

Vučić conceded within hours of the polls closing. "It is clear that we have lost these elections, the Student List has won," he said in a televised speech. He has since struck a harder note. He called the result the product of a "revolution" backed by European countries and the "US deep state", and he accused the winners of "revolutionary terror" after people stormed the Informer TV station. That channel had run coverage branding students as enemies of the state.

Leverage before power

The demands reach into institutions that do not answer to parliament in the way a ministry does. Police chiefs, intelligence heads and senior prosecutors were appointed under the old order, and they will be central to any corruption inquiry. A representative of the movement also urged the Supreme Public Prosecutor to ensure prosecutors act lawfully, impartially and swiftly in the cases that have drawn most public attention.

The pressure is already showing results at the public broadcaster. Reuters-linked reporting says the head of RTS has resigned since the vote. The movement's own 2025 list of demands had called for the departure of RTS and RTV directors and editors.

The approach carries a risk for the students. Pressing officials to go before taking office could look like the politicisation of the courts and police that the movement says it opposes. Officials who refuse to leave would also test how much authority a government has before it is sworn in.

The EU question

Serbia has been an EU candidate since 2012 and opened accession talks in 2014, but progress has stalled over concerns about the rule of law and media freedom. Ursula von der Leyen said the EU "stand[s] ready to support reforms on the rule of law, media freedom." That offer matches the movement's agenda closely, although the students have so far framed their programme around corruption rather than accession.

The next markers are the certification of the final results, the convening of parliament and the choice of a prime minister. After that comes whether the officials named in the students' demands leave voluntarily or have to be removed.
