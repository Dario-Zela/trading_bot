Farage faces two rows: a guard's rape charge and a Lib Dem MP's shove allegation

In one sentence
A guard who protected Farage on the spring campaign has been jailed for rape, while a separate row over an alleged shove of Lib Dem MP Caroline Voaden resurfaced.

A man who provided security for Nigel Farage on the spring campaign trail has been jailed for rape. On the same day, a separate row over whether Farage shoved a Liberal Democrat MP in the Commons reached the party leaders' front benches. The two stories are unrelated, but they land on the same man and on the same question about his party: how carefully Reform UK checks the people around its leader, and how it answers when the checks are questioned.

One correction to the early framing is needed. Initial reports described the guard as "facing" a rape charge. According to the Guardian's report, as reproduced by ua.news, he has since been tried, convicted and sentenced. The Guardian is the only source for the conviction that this newsroom could reach, and the account below rests on that reproduction rather than on a court record.

The guard

The man is Ryan Dinnage. He was charged in 2025 and found guilty in June of raping a woman who was asleep in a caravan at the Royal Windsor Horse Show. Thames Valley Police said the offence took place in the early hours of 16 May 2022. The victim reported it the same day and Dinnage was arrested the next. He was sentenced on 30 October to eight and a half years in prison.

The offence predates his work for Reform by nearly four years. The charge, though, was already laid in 2025, well before the party's campaign for the April and May 2026 local elections. Dinnage was seen with Farage at least four times in that campaign: in Llandudno, Waterlooville, Worthing and Downham Market.

Reform has confirmed that Dinnage provided security for Farage. It says his services were supplied by a third-party company, which assured the party that he had been vetted, and that it stopped using him "as soon as it became aware of the matter". It did not answer the Guardian's further questions about its relationship with the contractor or whether it would take action against it. A Labour spokesperson has asked Farage to explain when Reform learned of the charge and what it will do to prevent a repeat.

Those unanswered points are the substance of the story. Outsourcing security is ordinary practice for campaigns, and a party can fairly say it relied on its supplier. But an assurance of vetting is only as good as the checks behind it. A pending criminal charge is the sort of thing a basic check might miss, because it can fall short of a conviction. What is unclear is whether anyone at the party or the firm asked. It is also unclear when Reform learned of the case, and the party has so far declined to say.

The shove

The second row is older and murkier. Caroline Voaden, a Liberal Democrat MP, says that at the state opening of Parliament in May Farage told her to let him pass and, when she did not, took hold of her arm or shoulder and pushed her aside. She says she nearly fell into a colleague or the wall, and that a colleague stepped in because she seemed about to fall down the stairs. Max Wilkinson, a Lib Dem MP, is understood to have intervened.

Voaden says she complained to the serjeant at arms, who she believes asked Farage to apologise, and raised it with the Speaker. She says nothing came of either, and that she is still waiting for an apology.

The matter resurfaced this week. On Wednesday Farage said he had skipped John Healey's budget speech to "avoid conflict", citing a "physical altercation" with another MP in May and a seat reservation that had been moved. Commons staff later removed the reservation because it lacked the proper date stamp. Voaden replied that Farage knows his version is untrue.

Reform's response is a flat denial. A Reform source said the Lib Dem account is wrong and that Lib Dem MPs were the aggressive ones. Reform sources also say Commons video contradicts Voaden; the Lib Dems say the incident happened before the footage begins. Daisy Cooper, the Lib Dem deputy leader, said she did not witness it but understood a female MP was shoved and a male colleague challenged Farage. Robert Jenrick, Reform's shadow chancellor, called the dispute "pathetic" and declined to engage with Cooper.

The shove itself has not been independently established. There is one complainant, one denial and a disputed video, and no formal outcome has been reported.

What ties them together

The two episodes differ in gravity. One is a conviction for a serious crime by a contractor, with the question being what Reform knew and when. The other is a contested account of a few seconds in a Commons corridor. Yet both come down to process. In the first, the test is whether the party's vetting of those guarding its leader was real. In the second, it is whether the Commons' own complaints machinery can settle a dispute between members, since the serjeant at arms and the Speaker have been involved and no resolution has emerged.

What to watch is concrete. Reform has yet to say when it learned of Dinnage's charge or whether it will review the contractor, and Labour has put those questions on the record. In the Commons, the question is whether the Speaker's office or the standards system takes up Voaden's complaint, and whether the video Reform cites is published in full.
