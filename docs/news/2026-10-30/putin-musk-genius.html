Putin calls Musk a 'real genius' after talks involving SpaceX satellites

In one sentence
Putin called Musk 'a real genius' and said Russia is in close contact with him over satellite coordination, but no terms or deal have been reported and SpaceX has not commented.

Vladimir Putin has called Elon Musk "a genius, a real genius" and said Russia is in "close contact" with the SpaceX founder over satellites. The remarks, reported on 29 October, bring back an old question: how neutral is Starlink, the network at the centre of Ukraine's battlefield communications, when its owner is on speaking terms with the Kremlin?

What Putin said

Speaking on Wednesday at the Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow, Putin said Russia was in close contact with American partners, Musk among them. The quotes come from the BBC's translation, as relayed by other outlets. Putin said coordination was needed so that Russian and Starlink satellites do not "interfere with each other in orbit."

The reports give no terms, no participants beyond Putin's own account, and no outcome. SpaceX did not respond to a request for comment. Nothing published so far shows a commercial or political arrangement. The substance, as reported, is orbital coordination, a technical matter for any operator sharing crowded low-Earth orbit. It is not a deal. The BBC original has not been checked directly.

The context for the coordination is Rassvet, Russia's planned satellite internet network. The Russian embassy in South Africa has called it "Russia's Starlink alternative" and said it marks only the first steps. Online trackers show a couple of dozen Rassvet satellites in orbit. SpaceX says it has launched about 13,000 Starlink satellites.

Starlink's awkward position

SpaceX switched Starlink on in Ukraine in February 2022. The Ukrainian military came to rely on it for command links, drones and artillery coordination. In 2022 Musk declined a Ukrainian request to extend coverage towards Crimea, saying US sanctions required government approval and that activation risked escalation. A corrected account of the episode says coverage near Crimea had already been geofenced and that he refused a later request.

Since then SpaceX has tightened its terms. In 2023 it restricted licensing to block direct military use on weapons systems. Russian forces were reported to have obtained terminals through third-country resellers, a route SpaceX said it worked with Ukraine and the US to close by May 2024. In February 2026, according to Wikipedia's account, Starlink moved to whitelisted terminals, which cut off unauthorised Russian use.

That record explains why Putin's praise draws attention. Musk has admitted that SpaceX deliberately caused the Starlink problems the Ukrainian military suffered last week. Reports say a speed cap hit strike and interceptor drones, and that full access was restored by 25 October after Trump reportedly asked Musk to lift the limit. Neither SpaceX nor the administration has taken responsibility for the disruption. Against that background, a public show of warmth from Putin sits uncomfortably, whatever the technical subject.

Sanctions and what to watch

A sanctions question exists only if the contacts go beyond orbital deconfliction. Starlink service and hardware are subject to US export controls and sanctions rules, which is the framework Musk himself cited in 2022. Coordinating orbits does not obviously engage them. Any supply of terminals, services or launch capacity to Russian entities would. No such arrangement has been reported.

The diplomatic climate is also shifting. US-Russia ties appear to be warming, with Trump recently announcing a deal to import Russian diesel and waiving certain sanctions. Musk is a major political ally and financial backer of Trump.

The next markers are a SpaceX or Musk response, any US government comment on the contacts, and whether the Kremlin names a date or format for further talks. The Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov was quoted as saying no date had been set for separate talks, which would depend on several factors including the Iran conflict.
