Is the double drop from the Premier League becoming more common?

In one sentence
Five clubs have been relegated from the Premier League and then straight down again, and two of them, Luton and Leicester, have done it in the past two seasons.

Relegation from the Premier League is supposed to be a bad year followed by a reset. Parachute payments cushion the fall, the squad is still better than most of the Championship, and the usual story is a quick return. A different outcome, a second drop straight away into the third tier, has been rare. It is now less rare.

A club relegated from the top flight can only fall again from the Championship, and that is what makes the double drop so uncommon. Five clubs have done it in the Premier League era, and two of them have done it in the past two seasons.

The count

The first was Swindon Town, who went down from the Premier League in 1993-94 and dropped from the second tier to the third for 1995-96. Opta's analysts credit them as the first such case. It then took until 2012-13 for another club to follow.

Wolves were relegated from the Premier League in 2011-12 and from the Championship a year later. Sunderland followed after 2016-17, dropping to League One in 2017-18. Luton Town went down from the Premier League in 2023-24 and were relegated again on the last day of 2024-25, losing 5-3 at West Bromwich Albion and going down on goal difference.

Leicester City are the latest. Relegated from the Premier League in 2025, they were confirmed in League One in April 2026 after a 2-2 draw with Hull City. They had won two of their 20 Championship matches in 2026. The drop came ten years after the club's 5,000-1 title win. Reports differ on whether a points deduction for financial breaches played a part, so that detail is unconfirmed.

That makes five cases in about 33 seasons. Three came in the 14 seasons from 2012-13 onwards, and two in the last two. A single case is not a trend, and five cases is still small. But the earlier gaps were 19 years and then four, and the latest was a single year.

Why it may be getting harder

The cases differ, so no single cause fits them all. Several point the same way, though. Opta has argued that the gap between the Premier League and the Championship is widening, and the revenue figures back that up. One estimate has Leicester's income falling by about half in the Championship compared with the Premier League. A club that has built a top-flight wage bill can find that cost base outlasting the income that paid for it. Financial rules sharpen the problem, because a club that overspent to survive in the top flight has less room to rebuild.

There is a counterweight. Sunderland spent four seasons in League One before returning to the second tier in 2022-23, so falling twice is not a one-way ticket. Swindon and Wolves both recovered quickly.

Southampton complicate the picture. They were relegated in 2023, came back through the play-offs in 2024 and went straight down again in 2025. That is a yo-yo rather than a double drop, and it hurts in a different way. The Premier League's three-down format and its financial pull are producing awkward cycles at both ends of the table.

What to watch

The test comes in the 2026-27 season. Leicester are in League One, where the question is whether they can follow Wolves back up or follow Sunderland into a long stay. Luton, a year ahead of them in the third tier, are the nearest comparison. The clubs relegated from the Premier League next May will show whether a sixth case follows soon.
