Fed's Warsh builds out team as Warren presses on Bowman's bank ties

Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman, whose appearance at a Bank of America client dinner during the FOMC blackout period drew a Senate request for an inspector general review. — Federal Reserve / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Warsh named ex-BoE chief Mervyn King and a Bessent Treasury aide to his Fed team as Warren sought a watchdog probe of Bowman's blackout-period bank dinner.

Kevin Warsh is three weeks into the Fed chairmanship and already remaking the institution in his own image — thinner statements, an outside referee for how the central bank talks to markets, and a Treasury hand close at his side. None of that has stopped a separate reckoning over how the Fed's own officials talk to Wall Street.

Warsh named Mervyn King, who ran the Bank of England through the 2008 crisis, to co-chair a new communications task force, one of five review panels Warsh unveiled at his first FOMC meeting last month. The move follows through on a pattern he'd already set: the June policy statement ran 132 words, down from 341 in April, and Warsh skipped the Fed's quarterly dot-plot exercise entirely, arguing markets have grown too reliant on Fed hand-holding. "We're not asking for de Tocqueville to come to America," Warsh told reporters at the ECB's Sintra forum, "but sometimes we need a foreigner to sort of see things clearly." King, 78, is known from his BoE years for a style that was intellectually sharp but often deliberately withheld — not obviously a template for simplicity. Warsh has not said what the task force will recommend or when, only that further staffing news is coming next week.

A Treasury hand joins the building

Warsh also brought in Samantha Schwab, principal deputy chief of staff to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, as a senior adviser. Schwab, who had been part of the transition "sherpa" team that helped install Warsh, joined Treasury last year after a stint in the first Trump White House. Pairing a Bessent staffer with the Fed chair's office does not by itself change policy, but it tightens a channel between two institutions that are supposed to operate at arm's length — one worth watching as Warsh's task forces start producing recommendations that touch fiscal as well as monetary terrain.

The blackout-period dinner

While Warsh builds his bench, one of his colleagues is facing an ethics complaint. Senators Elizabeth Warren, Jack Reed and Chris Van Hollen have asked Fed Inspector General Michael Horowitz to investigate Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman over a private, invitation-only dinner Bank of America hosted for clients — held, the Wall Street Journal reported, immediately after Warsh's first FOMC meeting and during the blackout period in which officials are barred from discussing monetary policy with anyone positioned to profit from it. Bowman says she stayed off policy; reporting suggests she touched on the Standing Repo Facility and other Fed topics anyway. Former Philadelphia Fed president Patrick Harker put the concern plainly: "If Bank of America is putting their dinner on, it's giving them a commercial advantage, right?" It is the second such letter this year targeting Fed officials' access to industry.

The throughline is a Fed under Warsh trying to look disciplined from the podium while its personnel choices and old habits keep testing that discipline from the side. Watch for the task force's remaining members, due next week, and for whether Horowitz's office opens a formal inquiry into Bowman.
