Glencore's Suresh Vadnagra named next CEO of Northern Star

The Super Pit gold mine at Kalgoorlie-Boulder, part of Northern Star's flagship KCGM operation in Western Australia. — Rr2000 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
Northern Star has named Glencore operations veteran Suresh Vadnagra as CEO, effective October 5, amid activist pressure from Elliott and repeated Kalgoorlie output downgrades.

Northern Star Resources, Australia's second-largest gold producer, has picked a Glencore operations executive to run the company at a moment when investors are questioning both its output and its board. Suresh Vadnagra, currently head of Glencore's global nickel and zinc industrial assets, will take over as managing director and chief executive on October 5, succeeding Stuart Tonkin after roughly a decade in the job.

Vadnagra's résumé is built on fixing mines rather than dealing them. At Glencore he oversees more than 25 operations from Switzerland; before that he was chief technical and projects officer at Newcrest, and earlier still he held senior roles at BHP, Iluka Resources and MMG — including the operations job at Las Bambas in Peru, where he steered one of the world's ten largest copper mines through its ramp-up to stable production. "I am very excited to be joining Northern Star at a time of significant opportunity for the Company and its portfolio of high-quality, long-life assets," he said in the company's statement. Chief financial officer Ryan Gurner has been named deputy CEO and will run the company on an interim basis until Vadnagra arrives.

An activist at the gate

The reshuffle lands three-quarters of the way through a rough year for Northern Star's shareholders. The stock is down roughly 23% in 2026, hurt both by softer gold prices and by repeated guidance cuts at its flagship Kalgoorlie Consolidated Gold Mines (KCGM) operation: full-year production targets were trimmed from an original 1.7–1.85 million ounces to 1.6–1.7 million in January, and again since, as the mill struggled to sustain throughput. A processing-plant expansion at KCGM is still on track for commissioning in early FY27, the company says, which would ease the bottleneck.

Into that gap stepped Elliott Investment Management, which last month disclosed a stake north of A$1 billion — roughly 3–4% of the company — and pressed for a board overhaul, citing what it called persistent underperformance. Elliott had also floated a sale of the company, a proposal Northern Star rejected. The board's response has now gone beyond the CEO chair: Michael Ashforth is set to succeed Michael Chaney as chairman following the annual general meeting in November, giving Northern Star a new chief executive and a new chairman within the same cycle. Shares rose about 5% on the announcement.

For a company whose central problem has been converting a large ore body into consistent tonnes at a predictable cost, hiring an executive whose career has been spent doing exactly that at Glencore and MMG reads as a deliberate signal — mine planning and cost control over dealmaking. Vadnagra's arrival in October will coincide with the KCGM expansion's final run-up to commissioning, the clearest near-term test of whether Northern Star can convert his operational reputation into an end to the guidance downgrades.
