BP, TotalEnergies and Eni deepen emerging-market energy bets

In one sentence
BP deepened its India tie-up with ONGC as TotalEnergies bought into Abu Dhabi's Bab gas project and Eni sanctioned a $5.1bn Angola field — majors chase emerging-market scale.

The world's Western oil majors have spent the past two years explaining to shareholders why growth increasingly means going somewhere other than the North Sea or the Gulf of Mexico. This week supplied three fresh data points, from three different companies, on three different continents — and all of them point the same direction.

BP widens its India footprint

On 25 June, BP and India's Oil and Natural Gas Corporation signed a technical services contract extending their partnership from the Mumbai High field — India's largest oilfield — across the entire Western Offshore Basin, a 43-block area that is ONGC's most prolific hydrocarbon-producing region. The signing took place with India's oil minister, Hardeep Singh Puri, and the ministry's secretary, Neeraj Mittal, both in the room, underlining how much political weight New Delhi is putting behind the deal.

The commercial structure is straightforward: BP is paid a fixed fee for the first two years, then a performance-based fee tied to incremental production after that. ONGC keeps full ownership and operational control — BP is a technical adviser, not a partner with equity in the ground, deploying well-optimisation and reservoir-surveillance expertise rather than capital.

"Building on the encouraging outcomes at Mumbai High, this expanded collaboration will support improved recovery, greater efficiency and sustained production growth," said Arun Kumar Singh, ONGC's chairman and chief executive. BP India's chairman, Kartikeya Dube, framed it as strengthening "India's energy security" — language that echoes New Delhi's own push to shrink its heavy reliance on imported crude. The original Mumbai High arrangement, running since February 2025, moderated a natural production decline at the ageing field and delivered some growth purely through better well management, according to both companies — evidently enough of a track record to justify the expansion.

Abu Dhabi and Angola tell the same story

The India deal did not happen in isolation. TotalEnergies has taken a 10% stake in Abu Dhabi's Bab Gas Cap concession, joining a consortium led by ADNOC (60%) alongside CNPC, Japan's JODCO/INPEX, China's ZhenHua and South Korea's GS Energy. The project, operated by ADNOC Onshore, targets 1.5 billion cubic feet of gas a day and feeds into the emirate's expanding LNG ambitions, including the Ruwais LNG project where TotalEnergies also holds a 10% interest. One of the consortium's other members, holding an equal 10% slice, is BP.

Meanwhile Eni, through Azule Energy — its 50-50 joint venture with, again, BP — has taken the final investment decision on the Greater PAJ project off Angola, a $5.1 billion development that will be the country's first integrated cross-block offshore project. It knits together five fields, Palas, Astraea and Juno in Block 31 and Urano and Dione in the adjacent Block 31/21, via 17 wells feeding a new floating production vessel rated at 95,000 barrels a day. Sonangol and Equinor hold minority stakes; first oil is targeted for the first half of 2029, against roughly 252 million barrels of combined reserves.

The common thread

Taken individually, each of these is a routine industry announcement — a service contract, a minority stake, a sanctioned project. Taken together, and with BP appearing as a named or silent partner in all three, they describe a deliberate pattern: European majors facing flat-to-declining demand at home and rising political and cost pressure on the energy transition are redeploying capital and technical muscle toward India, the Gulf and West Africa, where reserves are large, governments are courting foreign expertise, and growth is still available. What to watch next: whether ONGC extends the BP model to further Indian basins, how quickly the Bab concession ramps toward its 1.5 bcf/d target, and whether Greater PAJ stays on schedule for first oil in 2029 as Angola tries to reverse a decade of declining output.
