International dividend ETF AVDE outpaces US markets with 23.6% gain

The Frankfurt Stock Exchange, home to some of AVDE's largest European holdings — Pythagomath / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
AVDE's 23.6% one-year gain owes more to a weaker dollar than stock-picking, and its 2026 lead over the S&P 500 has already narrowed.

The Avantis International Equity ETF, ticker AVDE, has returned 23.6% over the past year — a margin wide enough that even investors who don't own it are asking why. The fund holds roughly 300 developed-market stocks outside the US, from ASML in the Netherlands to HSBC in the UK to Toyota in Japan, weighted toward companies with cheaper valuations and higher profitability. Over the same twelve months, the S&P 500 has posted a solidly positive but noticeably smaller return, on the order of 9 to 11%.

That gap looks less like an anomaly and more like a pattern once the broader index numbers are lined up. The MSCI All Country World ex-USA Index beat the S&P 500 by double-digit percentage points in 2025, and the rally carried into 2026, pulling flows into international ETFs generally. AVDE is a beneficiary of that trend, not an outlier within it.

A currency story more than a stock-picking one

The clearest driver is the dollar. The ICE US Dollar Index fell roughly 11% over the trailing year and is down more than 2% so far in 2026, after a 9.4% slide in 2025. A weaker dollar mechanically inflates the value of foreign earnings and dividends once converted back into dollars, which flatters funds like AVDE independent of what their underlying holdings actually did in local-currency terms. Add in a mild value-and-profitability tilt — the same factor bet Avantis runs domestically — and the case for AVDE looks more like being positioned correctly for a point in the dollar cycle than a manager finding stocks nobody else saw. The portfolio's 2.62% dividend yield, with three-year dividend growth averaging near 14%, gives it a steady income floor, but that yield predates the past year's currency tailwind and isn't itself the source of the 23.6% headline number.

The timing detail worth watching: AVDE's own year-to-date return, 9.9%, sits much closer to the S&P 500's than its trailing twelve-month figure suggests. Most of the outperformance was banked in 2025, when the dollar's decline was steepest. That doesn't kill the structural-shift case — sustained dollar weakness and a re-rating of ex-US equities after years of US dominance is a theme several strategists expect to persist — but it does mean 2026 alone has not repeated last year's gap, at least not yet.

Whether the rotation continues depends less on stock selection than on the Federal Reserve. A dollar that stabilizes or strengthens would remove the tailwind AVDE has ridden since 2025, while further rate cuts would likely extend it. The next Fed meeting, and the dollar index's trajectory into year-end, will say more about whether this is a genuine reallocation away from US equities or a currency cycle finishing its run.
