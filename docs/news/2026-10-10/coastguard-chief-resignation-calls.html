Coastguard agency chief faces calls to resign over 'misleading' pay-row claims

In one sentence
Union and MP pressure is mounting on Maritime and Coastguard Agency chief Virginia McVea after a scrapped plan to end hourly pay for rescue officers and a disputed survey claim.

The head of the Maritime and Coastguard Agency (MCA), Virginia McVea, is under pressure from a trade union and at least two MPs to consider her position. The pressure follows a row over pay for the part-time rescue officers who respond to coastal emergencies. The dispute began as an argument over money. It has become an argument over whether the agency's leadership gave Parliament and its own staff an accurate account of what its workforce wanted.

How the row began

The MCA relies on Coastguard Rescue Officers (CROs), who are on call to attend cliff, shoreline and mud rescues. They have long been treated as volunteers, though they were paid roughly £11 an hour for call-outs and training. A Court of Appeal ruling held that CROs are in fact workers, not volunteers, because they are paid for on-call time. The MCA said that judgment meant it had to change how the service operates, and it planned to stop the hourly payments.

The plan met resistance from the GMB union and from MPs. A GMB survey reported in July suggested that 37% of Welsh coastguards might cut their hours under the changes. The government later stepped back. Maritime Minister Keir Mather confirmed in the Commons that the MCA would not proceed with ending hourly pay. Coastguard campaigners described the reversal as a "huge win".

The 'misleading' allegation

The reversal did not end the matter, because attention moved to how the original plan was defended. Joe Robertson, the MP for Isle of Wight East, wrote in the Spectator that McVea told MPs the Court of Appeal had mandated the decision. He wrote that she also said it matched "what CROs are telling us they want". He said a figure of 93% that she cited from an agency survey was one "we now know to be wrong", and by a wide margin.

That is one MP's characterisation. The search coverage reviewed for this article shows no independent finding that she misled anyone, and no formal inquiry. The MCA's stated position is that its decision followed from a legal judgment.

Alison Hume, the MP for Scarborough and Whitby, said McVea had vigorously defended the decision and implied it had the backing of most CROs. She urged McVea to "review her position in the light of this debacle".

The union's response

GMB members have formally expressed a "lack of confidence in the leadership of the Maritime and Coastguard Agency under chief executive Virginia McVea". The union has asked the agency's board for an independent review. A later report said coastguard rescue teams at 580 stations had signed up to the no-confidence vote.

The union's case rests on trust as much as pay. If staff believe the leadership misrepresented their views to ministers and MPs, the pay question can be settled while the relationship stays damaged.

What to watch

The stakes are limited to one agency and its chief, but they are real. The coverage found does not establish that the disputed claims touch safety-critical staffing, although the GMB has warned of a risk of an exodus of rescue officers. The next points are whether the board commissions the independent review the union wants and whether McVea responds publicly to the survey criticism. I found no report that she has resigned, and nothing dated after August.
