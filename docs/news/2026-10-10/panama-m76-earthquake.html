PANAMA · QUAKE · By C. Tanaka · 2026-10-10 · 4 min read

Magnitude 7.6 earthquake strikes off Panama's Pacific coast

In one sentence
A USGS-reported magnitude 7.6 quake off Panama damaged buildings in central provinces with no confirmed deaths; the Panama Canal's status has not been publicly stated.

A powerful earthquake struck off Panama's Pacific coast on Friday, shaking the whole of a country of about 4.5 million people, damaging buildings in its central provinces and briefly setting off a tsunami warning across the eastern Pacific. As of the latest reports there were no confirmed deaths. Whether the Panama Canal, which carries a meaningful share of world container and energy trade, was affected had not been stated by the canal authority or the main outlets covering the quake.

What happened

The US Geological Survey (USGS) put the quake at magnitude 7.6, having first measured it at 8.0. Some outlets give 7.7 in their body text, so the magnitude is not settled across coverage. France 24 reported the shock was detected at 1756 GMT on 9 October. The epicentre was roughly 10–12 km from Pitaloza Arriba, a remote community near the country's southern tip. The depth was reported as 10 km in some accounts and 12 km in others. Either way it was shallow, which generally means stronger shaking at the surface.

Several aftershocks followed, including events of magnitude 6.0 by one account, and 5.8 and 5.5 by another.

The shaking was felt about 200 km away in Panama City, where buildings swayed and residents ran into the streets. It was also felt in parts of neighbouring Colombia.

Damage and the human toll

Damage appears concentrated in the central provinces. Omar Smith of the National Civil Protection Service told the broadcaster Telemetro Reporta that the harm was mainly in Veraguas, Herrera and Los Santos, and that none had been reported in the capital. Local footage cited by Euronews showed a cathedral collapsing and a hotel facade falling onto a car in Penonomé. ABC News cited photos and videos of collapsed buildings, damaged vehicles and debris in streets.

Rene Garcia, head of emergency operations at the National Civil Protection System, told AFP there were no reported fatalities so far. At least two people were reportedly injured. President José Raúl Mulino urged calm on X: "What happened should not be used to cause alarm or disorder."

These are early figures. In the first hours after a large quake, counts of the dead and injured from remote areas often rise, and reports of structural damage are patchy.

The tsunami scare

The US Tsunami Warning System initially showed potential hazardous waves along much of the Pacific coast of Central and South America. France 24 reported that it said waves of up to three metres could reach the Panama coastline within hours. Ynet reported the threat was extended as far as Hawaii. The warning was later lifted, and no tsunami damage has been reported.

The canal question

The quake's distance from the waterway is the first thing to weigh. The epicentre lay in the south-central part of the country, offshore of the Azuero region, while the canal runs across the isthmus's narrowest stretch near Panama City and Colón. Panama City, nearest to the canal's Pacific entrance, saw shaking but no reported damage.

None of the coverage reviewed for this article mentions the canal's status, and a search found no statement from the Panama Canal Authority on the quake. That absence is not evidence of disruption, but it is not confirmation of normal operations either.

There is some precedent. A magnitude 6.2 quake near the Costa Rica border in 2019 was felt in Panama City, and port operations continued. A larger, shallower event is a different test, and the authority would be expected to inspect the locks, which are the most sensitive structures, before saying more.

The canal is also already under strain from other causes. A report dated 2 August 2026 described limited operations in some services tied to a national energy crisis, with around 40 vessels transiting daily. Any quake-related pause would land on a system with little slack.

What to watch

Three things will settle the matter. The first is a statement from the Panama Canal Authority on lock inspections and transit schedules. The second is the USGS event page, which may revise magnitude and depth again. The third is the civil protection toll from Veraguas, Herrera and Los Santos, where the worst damage appears to lie and where the final count of the dead and injured is likely to emerge.
