Labour holds Holborn as Greens, Reform and Lib Dem leaders face pressure

In one sentence
Labour held Starmer's old seat with 45% to the Greens' 33%, but a 13-point swing to the Greens and Labour's lowest majority there since 1983 undercut claims of a left-wing return.

Labour held Keir Starmer's old seat of Holborn and St Pancras on Thursday. It did so with its smallest majority there since the constituency was created in 1983, and against the leader of the Green Party in person. The figures point to a comfortable hold that was also a warning.

Sagal Abdi-Wali, the leader of Camden Council, took 12,292 votes, or 45 per cent. Zack Polanski, the Greens' leader, took 8,928, or 33 per cent. The majority was 3,364, or 12.4 per cent. Reports put the swing from Labour to the Greens at 13 per cent. Only four parties kept their £500 deposits; eleven candidates lost theirs.

What the numbers say

Some in Labour have framed the result as left-wing voters coming home after the Starmer years. Reporting on the count attributes no such claim to a named source, so it stays an unsourced party line. The vote shares do not support it either.

At the 2024 general election, Starmer held the seat with 48.9 per cent. The independent Andrew Feinstein was second on 18.9 per cent and the Greens third on 10.4 per cent, according to pre-poll coverage. Against that baseline Labour's share fell by roughly four points, to 45 per cent. The Green share more than tripled. A by-election is not a general election, and the field changed: the incumbent left, and the Greens put their national leader on the ballot. Even so, a party that gains about 23 points on its previous showing is not a party in retreat.

The fairer reading is that Labour held its core and the Greens consolidated much of the progressive vote that had been scattered in 2024. Some of that vote may have gone to Feinstein then, but the available reports do not say where it went this time. That is a guess, not a finding. Labour won because the opposition to it was split and because Abdi-Wali was a local figure with a council record. It did not win because left-wing voters had returned.

Polanski's position

Polanski has said he will stay on as Green leader, and said he does not think standing was a mistake. He described the contest as "an opportunity to present Green Party values". A party spokesperson said: "We are here to stay and just getting started." The spokesperson added that voters were sending a message to the government on rent controls, "privatised water rip-off" and "justice for Palestinians".

Second place with a third of the vote in a seat the Greens have never come near is a strong result by historic standards. It still fell short of what a leader who chose to fight the seat needed. Some reports describe the loss as his toughest moment in the job. One summary also says he was challenged over whether Israel has a right to exist. I could not confirm who asked it, where, or what was said, so it is left out here.

Pressure elsewhere

The night also bore on other leaders. Reform UK and the Liberal Democrats finished behind the Conservatives' Ewan Cameron, who came third. I could not find their vote shares.

A report of a senior Liberal Democrat quitting the frontbench over Ed Davey's leadership could not be verified. Searches turned up no named MP, date or stated grounds, so no weight should be put on it yet.

The Farage question is better documented. Nigel Farage resigned his Clacton seat and won it back in August on 63 per cent, with turnout of 44.7 per cent. Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats, the Greens and Restore Britain all declined to stand. His return automatically reopened the standards inquiry into a £5m donation from Christopher Harborne. Farage and Richard Tice deny wrongdoing. Parties have said they would contest any second by-election that followed an adverse finding. Harriet Harman has called such a finding "almost inevitable". No second contest is scheduled, and the parties' preparations amount to statements of intent.

What to watch

The test of the "returning left" claim will come in the polls and in the next contest where Labour and the Greens meet head to head. If Labour's share holds in national polling while the Greens' also rises, the Holborn result was a split opposition. If Labour's share falls toward the Greens' in similar urban seats, the 13-point swing is the more telling number. Any second Clacton contest would turn on the conclusions of the standards commissioner, Daniel Greenberg.
