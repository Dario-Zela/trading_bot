Man City 115-charges whistleblower to remain in witness protection

In one sentence
Rui Pinto, whose leaks triggered the Premier League's case against Man City, says Portugal is ending his witness protection on 10 October, days after City were reportedly found guilty.

The whistleblower whose leaked documents set off the Premier League's case against Manchester City says he is about to lose the state protection he has lived under since 2020. That runs against the premise of the headline this piece was commissioned under, which had him "remaining" in witness protection. On the reporting available, the dispute is over whether he will be allowed to remain.

Rui Pinto, the Portuguese hacker behind the Football Leaks files, says he was told his place in Portugal's witness protection programme will end on 10 October. His lawyers say that means losing a confidential residence and any guarantee of police protection. Reports differ on whether he was notified last week or earlier this week. The Portuguese Ministry of Justice had not responded when his legal team issued its statement, so the decision rests on Pinto's account alone.

What Pinto says

According to his lawyers, the decision was taken without the prior hearing the law requires. They describe his situation as "critical" and point to threats and an assault in Lisbon last April. They say his disclosures implicated "powerful figures with vast resources", and that he is now more exposed than at any point since he entered the programme in August 2020.

They say they will file an administrative appeal with suspensive effect, which would in principle pause the termination while it is heard. They have also asked the Ministry of Justice to reconsider, to disclose its reassessment of the risk to him, and to let him be heard. One outlet reports a fundraiser aimed at relocating him to an undisclosed location, with a €3m target. That figure appears in only one report and is unconfirmed.

The verdict behind it

The Premier League charged City in February 2023 after a four-year investigation that grew out of Pinto's files. The charges cover alleged breaches of the league's financial rules across nine seasons from 2009 to 2018, and alleged failure to co-operate with the inquiry. The total is widely given as 115, though some reports count 114.

In late September an independent commission reportedly found City guilty of the financial-rules charges and most of the co-operation charges. Reports disagree on the exact date, and no sanction has been made public in the coverage reviewed. City have denied wrongdoing throughout, said "the club is innocent", and lodged a formal appeal on 1 October. A hearing is expected within 12 weeks.

The Arteta quote

The "conscience clear" remark attributed to Mikel Arteta could not be traced. Arteta manages Arsenal, not City, so it would be a rival's comment rather than the club's. His recorded remarks on the case are more guarded. He has said it is "something for the authorities to manage, it's not for us". After a 2-2 draw with City in September he said, "I have been there before, I was there for four years" and "I have all the information", which Pep Guardiola asked him to clarify. Neither is a conscience-clear line, and the quote is left out here.

What next

Two clocks are running. Pinto's appeal against the end of his protection is the immediate one, and whether it carries suspensive effect will decide whether he is still covered after 10 October. City's appeal, due to be heard within roughly 12 weeks, will decide whether the verdict stands and what sanctions follow. Arsenal, who finished behind City in the title races of the period, are the obvious rival to watch if penalties are imposed.
