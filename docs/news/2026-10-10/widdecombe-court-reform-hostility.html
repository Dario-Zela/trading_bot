Court told Widdecombe suspect had 'particular hostility to Reform'

In one sentence
Prosecutors told the Old Bailey that Joshua Kerry, charged with murdering Ann Widdecombe, held a 'particular hostility to Reform'; he also faces a terrorism charge, and trial is set for June 2027.

Prosecutors have told the Old Bailey that the man accused of murdering Ann Widdecombe harboured a "particular hostility to Reform", the party the former Conservative minister joined and later spoke for. The remark, reported by the BBC and others on Friday, is the first public attempt by the Crown to tie the killing to a political motive. It comes after the accused was separately charged with a terrorism offence.

The case so far

Widdecombe, 78, was found dead at her home in Haytor, Devon, on the morning of 9 July. Her gardener found her on the kitchen floor after she missed a pre-arranged online interview. The attack is believed to have taken place the previous day.

Joshua Kerry, 28, from Rotherham, is charged with her murder. Prosecutors told an earlier hearing that she was struck 21 times on the head with a hammer. He was remanded in custody in July, when he appeared at Westminster Magistrates' Court and then the Old Bailey. Reports of those hearings say he confirmed only his name, date of birth and address.

Kerry was first arrested on suspicion of murder and then re-arrested on suspicion of terrorism offences. At that stage Laurence Taylor, head of Counter Terrorism Policing, said detectives were still examining whether there was a political or terrorist motivation.

What changed

That question now has a partial answer. Counter Terrorism Policing has announced a further charge of preparing terrorist acts, relating to alleged activity between 31 May 2024 and 8 July 2026. Reports describe the alleged target of that count as Nigel Farage, the Reform UK leader. The hearing on Friday was where prosecutors set out their reading of motive, including the "particular hostility to Reform" line.

The wording matters, and so does its limit. A prosecution description of a defendant's outlook is an allegation, not a finding. Nothing reported so far indicates that Kerry has entered pleas or answered the motive claim in court. The murder charge and the terrorism charge are distinct, and the terrorism count is where an alleged political purpose carries legal weight. Under UK law, an offence can carry a terrorist connection when it is committed for the purpose of advancing a political, religious, racial or ideological cause. That can affect how a sentence is set if there is a conviction.

What comes next

A provisional trial date has been set for 8 June 2027, with further preparatory hearings before then. Reports differ on the exact date of the next listing, so it is best treated as unconfirmed here. Two things will show how far the motive claim holds. One is whether Kerry's plea on the terrorism count differs from his plea on the murder charge. The other is whether the Crown puts forward evidence beyond its description of his views, for example the material said to underpin the allegation of preparatory activity over two years. Until then, the claim is the prosecution's account and nothing more.
