Boots has a new owner

In one sentence
Canada's Weston family, through Wittington Investments, has agreed a deal worth about £6.7bn including debt for Boots UK and Ireland, with completion expected in early 2027.

Boots, the high street pharmacy chain, is to change hands for the second time in two years. Wittington Investments, the holding company of Canada's Weston family, has agreed to buy the UK and Irish business from its private-equity owner, Sycamore Partners, and the Pessina family. The deal was announced on 7 October.

The headline price is about £6.7bn, or roughly $8.9bn, including debt, according to trade reports. One outlet puts it at "$9bn", which looks like rounding. The figure is enterprise value, so it is not what the sellers take home in cash. The reports are from trade and business outlets. This desk found no BBC article to check them against.

What is being sold

The perimeter covers Boots UK and Ireland, Boots Opticians, the No7 beauty brand and the franchised operations. Sycamore and the Pessina family are keeping other assets from the former Walgreens Boots Alliance group, including stakes in Farmacias Benavides and Alliance Healthcare Deutschland.

Fairfax Financial, a Toronto-based holding company, is partnering on the purchase. Wittington is to hold operational control once the deal closes. Galen Weston, Wittington's chairman, is to become chairman of Boots.

The path to this sale was short. Sycamore agreed in 2025 to take Walgreens private in a deal valued at up to $23.7bn including debt, with roughly $10bn in equity. Boots was then set up as a standalone group, registered and headquartered in the UK. One analysis says the price fell by about $1.1bn after a rival bidder, Sigma, withdrew in June. That is a single secondary account, and the price movement has not been confirmed by either side.

What is not yet known

The reports do not say what the new owners plan for the estate. No figures have been published on store closures, pricing or headcount. Any claim about the effect on staff or customers is therefore speculation. The nearest guide is the buyer's stated posture: coverage has framed Wittington as a long-term family owner rather than a financial investor with an exit date. That is a description of the buyer, not a commitment on stores or jobs.

Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approval. Until then Boots continues to trade under its current management.

What to watch

Three things will settle the open questions. The first is the regulatory filings, which should set out the formal terms and any competition review. The second is the first statement from Wittington and Fairfax on investment, the store estate and the pharmacy and opticians businesses. The third is the first-quarter 2027 completion date, which is when Mr Weston takes the chair and the new owners' plans can be tested against what they said beforehand.
