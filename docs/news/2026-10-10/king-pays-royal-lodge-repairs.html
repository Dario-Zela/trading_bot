King pays Andrew's £1.5m Royal Lodge repair bill

In one sentence
The King is reported to have paid Andrew's £1.5m Royal Lodge repair bill from private funds, but whether it is a gift or a loan, and who benefits, remains unconfirmed.

The King has paid a £1.5m bill for repairs to Royal Lodge, the Windsor Great Park mansion that Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, formerly the Duke of York, left earlier this year. The payment has put the cost of one disgraced royal's home back on the agenda. Secondary reprints of BBC reporting say it comes from the King's private income, not from public money.

The Tribune could not reach the BBC page directly. The account here rests on those reprints and on other royal reporting that matches them.

What the bill is

The sum is a dilapidations charge, the sort a landlord raises when a tenant leaves a property in worse condition than the lease requires. Reports put the original bill at about £1.8m. About £302,000 of compensation to which Andrew was entitled under his lease terms was deducted, which left roughly £1.5m for the Crown Estate. The Crown Estate owns the property and its surplus goes to the Treasury, so unpaid repairs would have been a public cost.

Andrew left the house in February. That was during a wave of renewed scrutiny of his links to the convicted US sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. He has consistently denied wrongdoing in relation to Epstein.

Private money, but whose benefit?

The money is understood to be the King's own. Reports say it does not come from the Duchy of Lancaster, the royal estate whose income funds the monarch's private expenses. That answers the narrow question of whether taxpayers are covering the bill, but it leaves a second one open: who benefits. A private payment relieves Andrew of a debt he would otherwise have faced. It also spares the Crown Estate from having to pursue a former tenant who is also the King's brother.

The terms are unclear. One account describes the money as a loan from the King's private income, and others describe it as a payment. Whether Andrew repays it is said to be a private matter. The Crown Estate's own statement names the "former tenant" as the payer. That wording does not confirm that the money came from the King.

Palace insiders reportedly say the arrangement is unconnected to Andrew's judicial review. The Tribune has not seen that claim confirmed on the record.

What to watch

The test is disclosure. The Crown Estate's annual report would show whether the dilapidations claim was settled in full. Any confirmation from Buckingham Palace on whether the payment is a loan or a gift would settle the question that matters most to those following royal finances. Until then, the precise terms rest on unnamed sources.
