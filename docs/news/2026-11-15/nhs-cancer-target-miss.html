NHS England on track to miss key cancer treatment target by 10,000 patients

In one sentence
A reported 10,000-patient miss on a cancer target is unverified, but official data show 62-day performance near 72% against an 85% standard unmet since 2015.

NHS England is heading for a significant miss on a headline cancer waiting-time target, according to a Guardian report citing performance data. The paper's figure is a shortfall of about 10,000 patients. The report's own text was not available for this article, and the 10,000 figure could not be matched to any published NHS England or charity analysis. It is therefore attributed to the Guardian and not independently confirmed. What the published statistics do show is a cancer pathway that is improving slowly and still falling short of every one of its targets.

What the published data show

Three standards govern the pathway. The Faster Diagnosis Standard (FDS) says patients referred urgently for suspected cancer should be told within 28 days whether they have the disease. The 31-day standard covers the wait from a decision to treat to the start of treatment. The 62-day standard covers the wait from urgent referral to first treatment.

Cancer Research UK's analysis of the latest release, published on 12 October 2026, puts August 2026 performance at:

  • FDS: 78.6%, against an 80% target — about 3,900 patients short.
  • 31-day: 92.1%, against 96%, a target not met since December 2020 — about 2,100 short.
  • 62-day: 71.9%, against 85%, a target the charity says has not been met since December 2015 — about 3,700 short.

The charity's page labels the 62-day figure as July in one place, while its shortfall estimate refers to August. NHS England's own provisional July release gives 71.2% for that month, up from 69.9% in June. The two sources disagree on the month, so the exact figure for any single month should be checked against the primary release.

About 1,400 patients, 8.9% of those treated, waited more than 104 days from urgent referral to treatment in August.

A target missed before it was set

The 85% standard is the long-term goal. NHS planning guidance set interim steps of 75% by March 2026 and 80% by March 2027. Cancer Research UK reports that the 75% interim target was missed, with performance averaging 70.1% across October 2025 to March 2026.

The arithmetic of the next milestone is demanding. Reaching 80% by March 2027 from roughly 70–72% means gaining eight to ten percentage points in about four months of reporting, against recent gains of one to two points. That is an inference from the published figures and not a finding from the Guardian's data. Which target the 10,000 figure refers to, and over what period, is not established here.

The pattern also matters for the question the Guardian story raises: whether this is a one-quarter wobble or a stall. The monthly shortfalls above are all in the thousands, and annualised they would reach tens of thousands. A 10,000-patient miss over a quarter or a part-year would sit comfortably within that range. This is an observation about scale. It does not confirm the Guardian's calculation.

The government's commitment

The National Cancer Plan for England, published in February 2026, commits to meeting all cancer waiting-time standards by 2029. Cancer Research UK has said that meeting them will likely require more investment in capacity. Its own projection, as relayed in search summaries of the charity's work and not checked against the original report, has 62-day performance reaching only 77.8% by March 2029. If that is right, the 2029 commitment would be missed on the 62-day standard even if the current rate of improvement held.

The 62-day standard was widened in October 2023, and the 31-day standard now includes subsequent treatments as well as first ones. Both changes complicate comparisons with older years.

What to watch

The next monthly Cancer Waiting Times release from NHS England is the test. It will show whether performance is moving toward the 80% March 2027 milestone or has levelled off near 72%. Any official confirmation of the Guardian's 10,000 figure, including the target and period it refers to, would need to come from NHS England's statistics or the Department of Health and Social Care.
