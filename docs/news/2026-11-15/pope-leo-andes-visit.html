Pope Leo speaks of community 'suffering' on visit to remote Andes town

In one sentence
Pope Leo spoke of a remote Andean community's 'suffering' on his South America tour, but the town, his words and any specifics remain unconfirmed beyond a BBC headline.

Pope Leo XIV has spoken of a community's "suffering" during a visit to a remote town in the Andes, according to a BBC World headline. The details of what he said, and where, have not been independently confirmed. The only reporting available to this desk consists of the headline and pre-trip coverage of his itinerary, so this article sets out what can be established about the visit and what cannot.

The trip

Leo, elected in May 2025, is making a long tour of South America this month that takes in Peru and Argentina. NCR Online has described it as his longest journey yet, involving 14 flights and some 19,000 miles. Peru has a particular place in his story: he spent years there as a missionary and bishop before becoming pope, and CNN has framed the visit as a homecoming.

Pre-trip reporting by NCR said the pope would travel by military helicopter on 14 November to the Andean mountain town of Santa Cruz de Succhabamba. This desk found no report confirming that the stop took place, and the BBC headline does not name the town. Whether the two are the same place is not established. The itinerary also included a stop in Cusco on 15 November, at about 3,400 metres, which Vatican News has described as brief but intense.

What is known about the setting

The broader context is documented, if general. Church sources describe remote Andean and Amazonian parishes where the Church is often the only organisation on the ground, and where there can be more than 15,000 Catholics for each priest. Leo's record in rural Peru, including years of work among poor communities during disasters and a pandemic, is the backdrop against which any remark about local "suffering" will be read.

What the pope actually said is a separate matter. No quotation is reproduced here because none could be verified. The BBC's wording, "community 'suffering'", suggests a phrase used in a homily or address, but the text, the speaker's meaning and any specific grievance, such as poverty, migration out of the highlands or the effects of mining or climate, have not been checked. The framing of the visit as one about poverty and displacement is an editorial gloss, not a sourced finding.

What to watch

The Vatican's published texts of the pope's addresses, usually released within hours, will settle what was said and to whom. The next test is whether the language is followed by anything concrete, such as a church-backed aid programme or an appeal to the Peruvian government. Without that, the remark stays at the level of pastoral language, important to the people who heard it but not yet a policy story.
