Zelensky says Washington floated Donbas for Nato membership

In one sentence
Zelensky says US negotiators floated Ukraine ceding Donbas in return for NATO membership; Washington has not publicly confirmed it and Kyiv says it won't give up territory.

Ukraine's president says American negotiators have, for the first time, linked NATO membership for his country to a withdrawal from the Donbas. The claim, made public on Saturday, rests so far on Volodymyr Zelensky's account alone. The US has not confirmed it in the reporting available, and the terms, if there are any, have not been set out.

Speaking after two days of US-brokered talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi, Zelensky said the Americans had put a hypothetical to Kyiv. He quoted them as asking: "What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?" He called the conversation "extremely difficult" and said NATO was "back on the agenda".

What Kyiv says it was asked

The significance lies in the linkage. Ukrainian membership of the alliance has been a Russian red line for years, and Vladimir Putin cited it as a reason for the 2022 invasion. During the earlier peace diplomacy of late 2025, the American ideas that Zelensky described were different in kind. He then spoke of a demilitarised "free economic zone" in Donetsk, which he called unworkable, and offered to drop Ukraine's NATO bid in return for legally binding Western guarantees while rejecting any cession of territory. Washington was then reported to be offering guarantees and EU prospects as the substitute for NATO membership. What Zelensky describes now runs the other way: membership offered as the price of land.

He was careful about what Kyiv would do with it. He said Ukraine was not ready to give up territory. But if Western countries confirmed "specific security guarantees" from NATO and the EU, he would consult Ukrainian society, adding that he has no right to decide alone. With a comprehensive package — guarantees, a strong and well-funded army, and money for reconstruction — he said he would put the question to a national referendum. That is a conditional opening, not an acceptance.

The account is also thin. It does not say who raised the idea, at what level, or whether it was a formal proposal or a probing question in the room. Zelensky's office released the remarks; no American official has been quoted confirming or disputing them. The reporting available includes no response from Washington to the NATO-for-Donbas claim itself.

The diplomatic backdrop

The Abu Dhabi round ended on Wednesday without a major breakthrough. The two sides did agree a prisoner exchange of more than 1,000 people, with final numbers still under discussion. Europe sat in the talks with American and Ukrainian officials but not with Russia.

The US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Zelensky in Ireland on Thursday and told him all sides agreed Europe should have a seat at future peace talks. They were due to raise an energy ceasefire with Putin in Moscow, with no progress reported. Kushner, in comments reported by CNN, said only that "several new ideas emerged" and were being explored. He did not mention NATO.

EU leaders have agreed that Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, together with EU institutions, will represent Europe at the next talks. Ursula von der Leyen said the EU would soon propose a 22nd sanctions package and address Ukraine's 2027 financing gap, adding that the "path to peace" would not be easy.

Moscow's position and the battlefield

Russia has insisted that Ukraine renounce NATO membership and surrender the part of the Donbas it still holds. A formula that offers one in return for the other would collide with the first demand while appearing to meet the second, which is why it is hard to read as a Russian-friendly compromise. Moscow also wants a broad European security agreement settled before the terms of the war, a sequence Zelensky rejects.

The fighting continues in parallel. Putin proposed a local humanitarian ceasefire in Kostiantynivka; Zelensky dismissed it, saying Moscow wants Ukraine to leave so that Russia can occupy the city. He said Ukraine had listed seven or eight other frontline settlements for possible truces, but that Russia prefers to treat them as military targets. Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians. On an energy ceasefire, Zelensky said Putin "personally does not support" one. He said Russia is "betting everything" on this winter's campaign.

What to watch

Three things will show whether the claim carries weight. The first is whether Washington, through Witkoff, Kushner or the White House, confirms, qualifies or disowns the idea. The second is whether other NATO members, who would have to agree to any accession, say anything about it. The third is Moscow's reaction. The next negotiating round, with the E5 and EU institutions at the table, is where any such framework would have to be tested. Until then it is a claim by one party to the talks about what another said.
