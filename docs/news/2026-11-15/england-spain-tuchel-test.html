England face Spain in a first check on Tuchel's post-World Cup direction

In one sentence
England's Madrid game against Spain is a competitive Nations League tie, not a friendly, and a second look at the errors that cost them 3-2 at Wembley in September.

England play Spain in Madrid tonight, and the fixture is not the friendly the pre-match billing suggested. It is a competitive Nations League game, the last league-phase match in Group A3, and it comes seven weeks after Spain beat England 3-2 at Wembley. Kick-off at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano is 7.45pm BST. Thomas Tuchel's side are therefore taking a second test against the world champions, not a first.

That changes what the night can show. A friendly lets a manager experiment at no cost. This game carries group points, and with them a place in the March 2027 quarter-finals.

What September showed

The Wembley game on 26 September was the first meeting since Spain won the World Cup, less than ten weeks earlier, and England were still absorbing their semi-final defeat by Argentina. Spain were unbeaten in 39 games.

England were behind after two minutes. Marc Guéhi lost the ball deep in his own half and Lamine Yamal scored. Anthony Gordon equalised in the 36th minute and Harry Kane headed England in front within a four-minute spell. Spain levelled through Alex Baena in the 60th, after a misplaced pass from Nico O'Reilly. Mikel Oyarzabal lofted the winner over James Trafford in the 74th. Kane had a second-half penalty hit the crossbar after his standing foot slipped.

Tuchel called it "the worst start possible" and argued that Spain created only one goal of their own, the third, while the first two came from individual errors. He said England could not yet produce consistently for 90 minutes. Kane said England would have won "on another day".

What to look for in Madrid

The pattern of that night is the useful thing to test. England competed well in open play and lost through errors in the first third and in the build-up. The first and second Spanish goals came from giveaways in England's own half. Whether Tuchel's side can pass out of pressure against a team that presses well and punishes errors is a style question, and it is the one the result will answer.

Squad selection is the other measure. Tuchel has used the autumn to rotate, and Trafford, O'Reilly and Gordon all featured against Spain in September. No confirmed line-up or squad list for Madrid was available at the time of writing, so any claim about how far the personnel have changed since the World Cup rests on the team sheet.

The group table is also unclear. Reports published at different points in the campaign give conflicting points totals for England, Spain and Croatia, and the standings should be checked before kick-off. The group also includes the Czech Republic.

What's next

The result decides who advances from Group A3, and England need to know where they stand before the draw for the quarter-finals. The Nations League continues in March 2027. For Tuchel, a second defeat by Spain would put pressure on England's defensive structure. A win or draw would suggest the September errors were a one-off and not a fault in the system.
