Wales stun All Blacks in one of their greatest wins; England turn to New Zealand next

In one sentence
Wales beat New Zealand 35-28 in Cardiff for their first win over the All Blacks since 1953, leaving a rattled New Zealand to face England next.

Wales beat New Zealand 35-28 at the Principality Stadium on Saturday, their first win over the All Blacks since 1953. It ended a wait of 73 years and 30-odd meetings. The result came in the Nations Championship, and it turns an autumn that had looked like a bruising schedule for Wales into one with a real talking point.

The 'one of the greatest wins' framing, which began as an unverified claim in the BBC Sport headline, holds up on the facts. A side that lost 52-26 to the same opponents a year ago, and that bookmakers reportedly gave around a 4 per cent chance, scored five tries against them in front of 55,152 people in Cardiff.

How it was won

Wales started quickly, with two early tries, and New Zealand dragged the game level by half-time. The decisive stretch came after the break, when Wales added two more tries and then defended through the closing minutes. Taine Plumtree's turnovers inside the Welsh 22 were part of the All Blacks' late pressure, and Wales still held out.

The tries came from Tomos Williams, captain Dewi Lake, Archie Griffin, Eddie James and Louis Rees-Zammit. Tom Costelow converted all five. For New Zealand, Cortez Ratima, Wallace Sititi, Fehi Fineanganofo and Peter Lakai scored, and Richie Mo'unga kicked four conversions.

New Zealand's tries kept them in touch, so the margin flatters Wales slightly. The visitors were never more than a score or two away, which is why the closing defence mattered so much. Lake's post-match line was simple: of course the team believed.

What it changes for Wales

Results like this tend to be measured against the record book first. Wales had lost to New Zealand in every meeting since 1953, and the last time they met in Cardiff the margin was 29 points in the All Blacks' favour. A one-off victory does not repair the wider decline in Welsh rugby, and one afternoon says little about the weeks ahead. But it gives a squad built around young backs and a new captain something concrete: they have now beaten the best side in the sport's history, at home, in a Test that mattered.

It also lifts Wales' position in the Nations Championship table, though the full standings were not verified for this piece.

England next

For New Zealand, the defeat lands just before a fixture against England. England beat the All Blacks 33-19 at Twickenham last November, coming back from 12-0 down. They now face a New Zealand side stung by the loss in Cardiff.

The real question for the All Blacks is whether Cardiff was a bad day or a pattern. Stuff has already asked how Wales exposed their flaws, and England will have studied the same tape. Their next Test will show whether New Zealand have answers.
