UBS raises ASML target, reiterates Buy

In one sentence
UBS has raised its ASML target and kept its Buy rating, a routine move that signals continued broker support for European chip equipment but gives no figures to weigh.

UBS has raised its price target on ASML Holding and kept its Buy rating, according to an aggregator report. The note is routine. It matters mainly as a reading on how brokers see the Dutch lithography maker, which supplies the machines used to print advanced chips and is the largest European semiconductor-equipment stock.

What is known, and what is not

The only item behind this brief is the headline carried by fiscal.ai, an aggregator. It gives no new target figure, no prior target and no share price, and a primary UBS note or wire report confirming the figures could not be found. This article therefore states none of them.

Searches turned up no confirmed UBS action on ASML dated this month. The most recent move found was in May 2026, when press reports said UBS lifted its target to €1,900 from €1,600 and named ASML its top euro-area chip pick. In April, UBS and Deutsche Bank had both raised targets from €1,500 to €1,600 after first-quarter results. Those are earlier notes, and they should not be read as the figures behind this one.

Why a routine raise still gets read

ASML sits at the centre of the chip-equipment supply chain. Its order book is watched as a proxy for how much chipmakers plan to spend on capacity, so broker changes on the stock tend to be read across European technology and semiconductor-equipment names. UBS has been a consistent bull: it moved to Buy in September 2025 with a €750 target and raised it to €940 in October 2025, on the strength of AI-led demand, according to Investing.com reports.

A raised target with an unchanged rating is the most common kind of broker note. It says the analyst's valuation has moved up, usually because of higher earnings forecasts or a roll-forward in the valuation period. It does not say the demand outlook has changed. Absent the note's reasoning, the event adds little to the market picture beyond confirming that a large bank remains constructive.

What would change the read

The note would matter more if it cited a shift in the outlook for chip-equipment orders, for example higher expected spending on extreme-ultraviolet tools or a change in China exposure. Neither is visible in the headline. The next hard data point is ASML's fourth-quarter results in January, when its bookings and 2027 guidance will test whether brokers' targets are being earned.
