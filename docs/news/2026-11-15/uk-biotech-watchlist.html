UK biotech names to watch in November

In one sentence
A recent screen of UK biotech names flags Hikma, Oxford Biomedica and Genus, but only Oxford Biomedica's August guidance cut gives the list a dated catalyst.

A stock-screen list is not news, and this one comes with a caveat: the most recent Simply Wall St screen of British biotech names that could be verified is dated 1 October 2026, not November. The three companies it flagged are still worth a look. None has a confirmed November catalyst, and the only dated corporate event found is Oxford Biomedica's profit warning, which came in August.

The screen

Simply Wall St, an investor-focused analysis platform, listed three UK-listed names in its early-October piece. It gave no share prices, only market values and revenue splits. The figures below are the platform's own and have not been independently checked.

Hikma Pharmaceuticals (LSE:HIK) is valued at roughly £3.4bn. Its revenue splits into injectables of about $1.4bn, US generics (Hikma Rx) of $1.0bn and branded medicines of $914m. The screen points to high-value complex generics, specialty injectables and the Xellia acquisition as the drivers of higher-margin growth.

Oxford Biomedica (LSE:OXB) is valued at £632m. Manufacturing services bring in about £88m and development work about £60m. The screen's watch-point is how quickly contracted work converts into paid manufacturing runs.

Genus (LSE:GNS) is valued at roughly £1.4bn, with pig genetics (PIC) at about £355.8m and cattle genetics (ABS) at about £299.8m. The screen flags a shift toward plant-based diets and alternative proteins as the main risk to demand for animal genetics. Genus is an animal-genetics business and sits at the edge of any biotech definition.

The one dated catalyst

Oxford Biomedica is the name with recent news attached. In August it cut its 2026 revenue guidance by 17%, to £180–200m from £220–240m. It cited client programme deferrals, delayed timelines, a change in a large client's procurement strategy and a later-than-expected ramp-up at its Durham, North Carolina site. Shares fell roughly 18% on the day, and one broker noted that the new guidance implied EBITDA of about £9.5m against consensus of about £21.9m.

The company still reported half-year revenue up about 9% to roughly £80m and kept its 2027 growth guidance of 25–30%. That makes the screen's October watch-point, conversion of contracted work into billable runs, the right test. A £632m valuation after a guidance cut leaves little room for further slippage.

What to watch

No November trading updates from Hikma or Genus turned up in searches. The next hard data points are each company's own regulatory announcements. For Oxford Biomedica, the question is whether deferred client programmes return on the timetable that supports its 2027 guidance. A list of tickers is not a reason to buy, and a screen built on trailing revenue splits says little about any of these three before those updates arrive.
