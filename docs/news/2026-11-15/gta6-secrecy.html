Even the original GTA creators haven't played GTA 6

In one sentence
Rockstar's secrecy around GTA 6, due on 19 November, is so tight that even the creators of the original 1997 game have not played it.

In an industry that leaks like a colander, Rockstar Games has managed something close to a vow of silence. With Grand Theft Auto VI due on 19 November, the people who made the very first Grand Theft Auto have not played the sequel, according to BBC Technology's reporting. It is a small detail, but it shows how tightly the launch is being held.

Four days to go

The game was originally pencilled in for May 2026 before Rockstar pushed it back to this week. The delay did nothing to loosen the studio's grip. Few releases in entertainment are as heavily anticipated, and few are guarded as closely.

The lineage matters here. The first Grand Theft Auto arrived in 1997 from DMA Design, a Scottish studio whose founders included David Jones. It was a top-down game of traffic, mayhem and a notably flexible attitude to the rules of the road. Nearly three decades on, the series is a global franchise, and the people who started it are in the same position as everyone else: waiting for launch day.

That is less odd than it sounds. DMA Design was long ago absorbed into what became Rockstar North, and the founders have moved on. Being an original creator confers no access to the current build. Rockstar's secrecy does not make an exception for sentiment.

The departed

The wall around the game also stands where a key voice once sat. Dan Houser, who co-wrote the Rockstar-era GTA games, left the company in 2020. The sequel is therefore the first mainline entry in years to be finished without him, a fact that adds to the curiosity about what the finished game will feel like. Whether it will read as a continuation or a break is something no outsider can yet judge.

What is riding on it

The audience for this story is mostly players and a narrow band of investors. Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar's parent, has a great deal of its near-term outlook tied to the release, and a studio that has already delayed once has every reason to keep the final product sealed until it ships. Secrecy also serves marketing: every unanswered question is a reason to talk about the game.

The approach has a cost, which is that expectations have been left to inflate with little evidence to anchor them. A game this hyped has little room to be merely good.

The first real test arrives on 19 November, when reviews and player reactions replace speculation. Until then, the people who made the original are queuing with everyone else.
