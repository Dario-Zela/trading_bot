Treasury yields climb as traders price Fed hikes in December and January

Citi now expects a 25bp Fed hike in December after hot September CPI, as traders price tightening in December and January and Treasury yields rise, awkwardly for Trump.

Treasury yields have climbed as traders shift from pricing Federal Reserve rate cuts to pricing rate increases. Markets are now positioned for tightening at both the December and January policy meetings, and that repricing is what is pushing yields higher. Citi has taken the most direct view, expecting a 25 basis point hike in December after September's consumer price index came in hotter than expected.

The shift matters because the easing that policymakers and the White House had been counting on is now in doubt. Higher expected policy rates feed through to mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs and the cost of government financing, and they tend to weigh on equity valuations. The move is politically awkward for Trump, who has pressed for lower rates.

The next inflation readings and the December meeting will show whether the repricing holds. A hike at that meeting would reverse the easing path markets had priced for the coming months.
