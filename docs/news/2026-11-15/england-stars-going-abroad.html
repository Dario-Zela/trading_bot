Why England's star names are going abroad

In one sentence
English footballers are leaving for foreign leagues at about five times the old rate, while rugby's selection rule makes the same move a gamble with a player's England place.

England's footballers and rugby players are leaving for foreign leagues, and the two sports are handling it in opposite ways. In football the move abroad has become a trend with a track record. In rugby it runs into a selection rule that costs players their international careers.

This is a trend piece, not a breaking one. The reporting behind it comes from secondary outlets, and no single new event today could be confirmed. The figures below are as reported by those outlets and have not been checked against primary data.

Football: from trickle to flood

For years English players who left for Europe were curiosities. An ESPN analysis of the trend, headlined as the trickle of homegrown talent leaving England and Scotland becoming a flood, put the average at about three moves a year between 2000 and 2017. It puts the figure at about 15 a year since.

Harry Kane and Jude Bellingham are the usual exhibits. Coverage of their moves argues that success abroad has shown English players that a career outside the Premier League is possible. Planet Football has argued that English youngsters abroad offer hope at a critical time for the national game, on the theory that different coaching and a different pace of play are an education. The wage question is less flattering. Premier League money has long kept English talent at home, so a rise in departures suggests that either the gap is narrowing or players are weighing other things above it.

Rugby: a rule, not just a market

The rugby version is sharper because it is a matter of governance. The Rugby Football Union formalised a policy in 2010, taking effect after the 2011 World Cup, of excluding overseas-based players from England selection. Fewer English players moved abroad as a result.

The rule has been tested since the 2023 World Cup. Reports said five England internationals announced moves to France. Another report, from Ruck, said eight England players had confirmed moves overseas for a coming season and that head coach Steve Borthwick was frustrated. Will Carling, the former captain, asked publicly why players would walk away from playing for their country.

Borthwick has pressed for more flexibility under the policy's "exceptional circumstances" clause. The RFU's reported answer is that the policy stands.

What it means

The two cases point to different pressures. English football cannot stop its players leaving, and increasingly does not try. English rugby can, because it controls the shirt, and the cost of that control is a smaller pool when French clubs bid. If departures keep rising, the RFU will have to choose between the rule and its best players, and the answer will say more about how English sport is governed than any single transfer.

The next test is the 2027 World Cup cycle, when the selection policy meets a squad list that may include fewer of the players Borthwick wants.
