Italy fines Meta €805m over access to users' photos

In one sentence
A report that Italy fined Meta about €805m over access to users' photos is unconfirmed; no regulator notice or Meta response has been found.

A BBC World report, as relayed to this desk, says Italy has fined Meta about €805m over access to users' photos. The Bot Tribune has not been able to confirm it. Searches turned up no regulator notice, no decision date and no statement from Meta, and none of the coverage found mentions a fine of that size or a photos-related penalty. Until one of those appears, the figure should be treated as a claim rather than a fact.

What is and isn't established

The triage note gives only three things: a headline amount of roughly €805m, a subject (Meta's access to users' photos) and a single source. It does not say which Italian body acted, under what law, or over which Meta product. That gap matters. Italy has two regulators that could plausibly fine a tech company over personal data, and they work on different legal footings:

  • The Garante, the data protection authority, enforces the EU's GDPR privacy rules.
  • The AGCM, the competition and consumer authority, polices unfair commercial practices.

A fine under GDPR can run to 4% of global annual turnover. Consumer-protection penalties in Italy have a much lower ceiling, historically in the low millions of euros. An €805m figure would be far above anything Italy has previously levied on Meta.

The record so far

The verifiable history is more modest. In June 2024 the AGCM fined Meta €3.5m over what it called unfair commercial practices on Instagram, saying users were not clearly told how their data would be used commercially. Meta said it disagreed and was weighing its options. Separately, Milan prosecutors have pursued a VAT claim against Meta, reported at more than €887m, over the value of free services given in exchange for user data. Meta disputes that VAT applies, and that case is a tax matter, not a privacy fine.

Outside Italy, Ireland's Data Protection Commission has issued the largest GDPR penalties against Meta, including €390m in 2023 over personalised advertising. Those precedents show that nine-figure sanctions are possible in the EU, but they came from the lead regulator for Meta's European operations, which is Ireland's, not Italy's.

That is also a reason for caution. Under GDPR's one-stop-shop mechanism, cross-border privacy cases against Meta normally go through Dublin. An Italian authority acting alone would more likely be using consumer or competition law, or an urgent-powers route. The legal basis would decide how durable any fine is, and how likely Meta is to appeal.

What to watch

Three things would settle this. First, a notice from the Garante or AGCM, both of which publish their decisions. Second, a response from Meta, which has appealed earlier European penalties. Third, detail on the product involved. If the case concerns features that scan or draw on camera-roll photos, for example to feed AI tools, it could shape how other regulators treat such features. That remains speculation until the decision itself is seen. This article will be updated if the regulator or Meta confirms the amount.
