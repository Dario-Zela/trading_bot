South African police fear serial killer after seven women found dead

A South African Police Service patrol vehicle; SAPS has formed a multidisciplinary unit to investigate the Ekurhuleni killings. — Dickelbers / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Police in Ekurhuleni, South Africa are probing whether one killer or several are behind seven women's deaths in two months, as Ramaphosa vows action.

In the East Rand towns southeast of Johannesburg, the discovery of a body in tall grass or on waste ground has, this year, stopped being an isolated tragedy and become something communities dread as a pattern. Since mid-July, the bodies of seven women have been found across the City of Ekurhuleni metropolitan area — most recently on September 15, when a seventh victim was located, prompting South African police to concede publicly that the similarities between the cases are "sufficiently concerning" to warrant a formal warning to residents.

Police have stopped short of declaring a serial killer is at large. Spokesperson Athlenda Mathe told reporters that investigators are "still investigating whether we are dealing with one perpetrator or a group of men" behind the killings, and that no formal link between all seven cases has been confirmed. But the pattern is difficult to ignore: five of the bodies were recovered within a single week in early September, in and around Kwa-Thema, Olifantsontein and the Kempton Park area, roughly 34 kilometres apart at their widest span. Several of the women were found partially undressed with signs of sexual assault; one body was in an advanced state of decomposition.

Who the victims were

The first known victim, a 37-year-old woman, was found bound with cable ties on July 15; a suspect was arrested two days later, though police have not said whether that arrest bears on the later killings. The most recent identified victim, 38-year-old Elizabeth "Tsontso" Moselakgomo, disappeared after leaving home for an afternoon run and was found dead days later. Her death, in particular, galvanised local runners' groups, who held memorial jogs in Ekurhuleni this week in her memory and in protest at the danger women face doing something as ordinary as exercising outdoors.

The response from Pretoria

President Cyril Ramaphosa said he had been briefed by the police minister and the acting national police commissioner, Firoz Cachalia, who called the discoveries "deeply disturbing." Ramaphosa directed police to prioritise the investigation, saying the killings were "causing fear and uncertainty in our communities and especially among women" and pledging that "no stone will be left unturned in bringing those responsible to justice." SAPS has since set up a multidisciplinary team to examine forensic and behavioural links between the cases, and has urged women in the affected areas to take precautions, including avoiding jogging alone.

A crisis with a number attached

What distinguishes Ekurhuleni from many crime stories is the backdrop against which it is unfolding. South Africa recorded 569 women murdered between April and June 2026 alone, alongside more than 9,000 reported rapes in the same quarter, according to police statistics. Parliament put the country's femicide rate at nine per 100,000 women — roughly five times the global average — and the government formally declared gender-based violence and femicide a national disaster in late 2025. Against that scale, seven deaths in two months is a fraction of the toll, but the possibility of a single, active predator has cut through in a way that routine statistics do not. Javu Baloyi of the Commission for Gender Equality argued that area-specific safety measures — better lighting, patrols, emergency infrastructure — should not have needed a body count to arrive.

What happens next hinges on forensic work: ballistic, DNA and pattern-of-injury comparisons across the seven cases that police say are still underway. A formal statement on whether one perpetrator, a group, or unrelated crimes are responsible is the next concrete marker to watch for, alongside whether the multidisciplinary unit widens its net to further, as-yet-unlinked cases in the wider Gauteng province.
