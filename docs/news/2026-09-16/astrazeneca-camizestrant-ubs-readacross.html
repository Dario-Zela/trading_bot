AstraZeneca's camizestrant miss keeps pressure on oncology pipeline

AstraZeneca's global headquarters and R&D centre in Cambridge, UK. — D Wells / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
AstraZeneca's camizestrant missed its main first-line breast-cancer goal, narrowing its use to biomarker-tested patients and reinforcing Guardant Health's diagnostic foothold, per UBS.

AstraZeneca's camizestrant, a breast-cancer pill the company once pencilled in for more than $5 billion in peak annual sales, failed to clear its main hurdle in a closely watched trial — the third oral SERD in its class to lose a first-line Phase 3 study. The result, disclosed late on September 11, trims the drug's near-term commercial ceiling and adds to a run of pipeline setbacks that has left AstraZeneca shares down close to a fifth over six months, even as one diagnostics partner, Guardant Health, comes out looking better positioned.

The SERENA-4 trial paired camizestrant — sold as Etcamah — with the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib and tested it head-to-head against anastrozole plus palbociclib in 1,371 patients starting treatment for advanced, hormone-receptor-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer who had not yet had systemic therapy. The camizestrant arm showed a numerical edge in progression-free survival, but it missed statistical significance. Susan Galbraith, AstraZeneca's oncology R&D chief, called the outcome disappointing while steering attention to the drug's existing approval.

"We are disappointed by the SERENA-4 outcome," Galbraith said, adding that the company would focus on "maximising the number of patients who can benefit from Etcamah today based on SERENA-6."

That SERENA-6 approval is narrower by design: camizestrant is cleared for patients who develop an ESR1 mutation on first-line endocrine therapy, detected via blood test before scans show disease progression. In that trial, switching to camizestrant on an ESR1 signal cut the risk of progression or death by around 55%, with median progression-free survival of roughly 16 months versus 9.2 months on standard therapy. SERENA-4's miss doesn't touch that indication — but it does mean camizestrant's growth now rests more narrowly on biomarker-driven prescribing rather than blanket first-line use, with the CAMBRIA adjuvant trials, enrolling roughly 10,000 patients, as the next major swing factor.

Why Guardant gets the read-across

That narrowing is the mechanism UBS analysts have pointed to in flagging Guardant Health as a relative beneficiary. Guardant's blood-based Guardant360 CDx is the FDA-approved companion diagnostic built specifically to catch the ESR1 mutations that trigger a camizestrant switch — the test AstraZeneca's SERENA-6 data validated as clinically useful before radiographic progression. A first-line win in SERENA-4 would have let doctors prescribe camizestrant upfront without needing a mutation test at all; its absence keeps the drug's commercial path running directly through Guardant's diagnostic. UBS has held a Buy rating on Guardant, built substantially on the SERENA-6 read-through and the broader argument that routine ctDNA monitoring — where Guardant leads on testing volume — becomes more, not less, embedded in cancer care as targeted therapies proliferate. Guardant shares have traded near 52-week highs this month, a contrast with AstraZeneca's more turbulent run, which also includes July's Wainua Phase 3 failure and a bispecific antibody, volrustomig, missing its mark against Keytruda in lung cancer.

AstraZeneca stock actually recovered on September 14, rising over 2% in London, as strong Enhertu lung-cancer data offset the SERENA-4 disappointment — a reminder that the company's roughly 20-Phase-3-readout year cuts both ways. Oncology still drove $6.8 billion in first-quarter sales, up 16% at constant currency, and the SERENA-4 result narrows rather than eliminates camizestrant's opportunity.

The next checkpoints are the CAMBRIA-1 and CAMBRIA-2 adjuvant readouts, which will determine whether camizestrant earns a role in early-stage, curative-intent breast cancer rather than just the metastatic, biomarker-selected setting — the difference between a niche drug and one that can still approach its original sales ambitions.
