Shell strikes $715m Constellation power deal and expands US LNG position

In one sentence
Shell is selling a $715m New England power plant to Constellation, buying a Pennsylvania gas plant and a new US LNG supply deal, as its shares hit fresh highs.

Shell has agreed to sell its stake in a 609-megawatt New England power plant to Constellation Energy for $715 million, while simultaneously buying a smaller gas-fired generator in Pennsylvania and locking in a new multi-year US liquefied natural gas supply deal with a European trading partner — three moves in a fortnight that amount to a fairly assertive reshuffling of Shell's American energy footprint.

The headline transaction is a swap, not a straight sale. Constellation, the Baltimore-based nuclear and power giant, is paying $715 million in cash for Shell Energy North America's full stake in the Rhode Island State Energy Center, a gas-fired plant that has fed the ISO New England grid since 2002. Constellation expects the deal — worth roughly $580 million after first-year tax benefits — to be immediately accretive to earnings and to clear its 10% unlevered return threshold. On the same day, Shell announced it would acquire Hunlock Creek Generating, a 169-megawatt Pennsylvania gas plant, from Riverview Power Holdings, a unit of trading house Castleton Commodities International. Both deals are subject to regulatory approval and are expected to close in the first quarter of 2027.

Trading New England exposure for a seat in PJM

The logic is geographic. Hunlock Creek — a 125-megawatt combined-cycle unit paired with a 44-megawatt peaker — sits inside PJM Interconnection, the largest wholesale power market in the country, spanning 13 states and Washington, DC, and serving more than 65 million customers. Shell said the mix of baseload and flexible peaking capacity would sharpen its ability to respond to swings in power demand and support its trading and optimization desk in that market. Trading a single large New England asset for a smaller but more flexible PJM position reads less like retreat and more like a bet on where US power-price volatility, and the arbitrage that comes with it, will concentrate next.

LNG book grows alongside the power swap

Shell has also struck a new long-term LNG sale and purchase agreement with MET International, the trading arm of Swiss-based MET Group, to supply US-sourced LNG to European customers over a multi-year period. Volumes and duration were not disclosed, though the deal builds on a 10-year, 800,000-tonne-a-year contract the two companies signed in 2024, priced at roughly 119% of Henry Hub plus about $2.70 per million British thermal units. Taken together with the power moves, it points to Shell leaning further into US gas — both as fuel for export and as generation capacity to trade around — at a moment when it has room to spend.

That room is showing up in the share price. Shell's ADR touched a fresh 52-week high of $97.34 in New York on September 14, and the London-listed stock has been outperforming both the FTSE 100 and the wider energy sector through the month, helped by a $3.5 billion buyback programme, a Jefferies “buy” rating with a 4,500p target, and second-quarter adjusted earnings of $9.8 billion on operating cash flow of $21.4 billion. Deploying capital into US gas infrastructure while the shares are already near highs is not the profile of a company under pressure to conserve cash — it is closer to a board comfortable spending into strength. The two power deals close in the first quarter of 2027, when regulatory sign-off and the next earnings print will show whether the market keeps rewarding the pace.
