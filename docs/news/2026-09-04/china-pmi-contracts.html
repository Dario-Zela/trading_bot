China factory activity contracts for second straight month

An automotive manufacturing plant in Wenzhou, China — a snapshot of the industrial base at the center of the PMI reading. — ShaunCG / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
China's official factory PMI stayed below 50 for a second month in August, though the contraction eased and no stimulus has been announced.

China's factories shrank for a second consecutive month in August, but the contraction eased rather than deepened — a nuance that matters for anyone betting on when, or whether, Beijing steps in with fresh stimulus.

The official manufacturing purchasing managers' index came in at 49.8, up from 49.2 in July and ahead of the 49.6-49.7 economists had penciled in, according to National Bureau of Statistics data released on August 31. Readings below 50 signal contraction; the gauge has now spent two straight months there after a four-month run of expansion ended in July. NBS statistician Huo Lihui said 16 of the 21 industries surveyed posted month-on-month gains, and pointed to fading disruption from summer heat and flooding as one driver.

The numbers underneath the headline

The sub-indices tell a more encouraging story than the topline. New orders jumped to 50.6 from 48.5, crossing back into expansion, and new export orders rose to 50.1 from 49.6. Both readings suggest August's factory malaise was shallower than July's. Construction was the weak spot, dropping to 46.9 as heavy rain and typhoons hit building sites in several provinces.

The picture gets murkier alongside China's other gauges. The non-manufacturing PMI, covering services and construction, held flat at 49.0 — also in contraction, and unmoved from July. That means both halves of the economy the NBS tracks are now sub-50 simultaneously, something that hasn't happened since early in the year. Meanwhile the privately compiled RatingDog manufacturing PMI (the survey formerly branded Caixin, which weights smaller and export-oriented firms more heavily) told a different story entirely: 51.5 in August, up from 50.9, its strongest reading in three months, driven by a fifteenth straight month of new-order growth. The gap between an official survey stuck below the line and a private one comfortably above it is itself a signal — large state-linked firms and small exporters are currently living in different economies.

No stimulus, no urgency — yet

What hasn't moved is policy. Beijing has announced no broad stimulus package despite two months of official contraction, and economists tracking the PBoC expect only targeted, incremental easing rather than a sweeping response, with any adjustment more likely in late Q3 or Q4. Second-quarter GDP growth had already slowed to 4.3%, below forecast, with the property sector's ongoing slump still weighing on domestic demand.

For markets, the muted response matters most in commodities. China accounts for roughly 55-60% of global copper demand, and traders had been watching August's data for signs of restocking; instead, firms' own-inventory readings fell, suggesting manufacturers remain reluctant to rebuild stock even as new orders pick up. The next test comes with September PMI data due around October 1, and with it a clearer read on whether August's improvement was the start of a stabilization or a weather-driven blip.
