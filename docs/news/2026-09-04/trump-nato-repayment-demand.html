Trump demands Europe repay US 'hundreds of billions' spent on Ukraine and NATO

President Donald Trump, whose Truth Social posts demanded European repayment for past Ukraine and NATO aid. — Daniel Torok / White House, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Trump said the US will seek repayment from Europe for past Ukraine and NATO aid, citing disputed 'hundreds of billions' in spending.

President Donald Trump said on Wednesday that Washington will seek repayment from European allies for military aid sent to Ukraine under his predecessor, reframing years of NATO burden-sharing arguments as a retroactive debt claim.

"Hundreds of Billions of Dollars was given to Ukraine and NATO, free of charge, that Europe would have paid for — If they were only asked, but we will be asking for that money, though somewhat belatedly!" Trump wrote on Truth Social. He added that "the Biden Administration gave far more Munitions away to Ukraine, at no cost to them, whatsoever, than we have used in Iran," and, elsewhere, that Kyiv had received "$300 billion worth of ammunition" under President Joe Biden.

The figures are contested. Atlantic Council tallies put total US military aid to Ukraine under Biden at roughly $131 billion, most of it committed in 2022, while President Volodymyr Zelensky has previously put the number closer to $100 billion. European governments, by the same accounting, have collectively provided more than $227 billion in aid to Ukraine — already exceeding Washington's contribution. Trump has cited figures ranging from $300 billion to $500 billion in different statements, without disclosing a source.

From cost-sharing to a bill

The demand marks a shift in tone rather than mechanism. Since July 2025, the US and NATO have run the Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List, a scheme brokered by Trump and NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte under which European allies and Canada — not the United States — pay for American-made weapons shipped to Ukraine going forward. It has raised roughly $6.7 billion in a year, funding Patriot interceptors and other high-end munitions. That structure already answers the burden-sharing complaint Trump has made since his first term.

What is new is the claim on the past: an assertion that Europe owes Washington for aid disbursed years ago, before PURL existed and before Trump returned to office. No repayment mechanism, timeline, or legal basis was specified in the post.

The Iran backdrop

Trump's message arrived amid open frustration that European and NATO governments have not joined the US military campaign against Iran, now in its seventh month and reported to include roughly 100 American strikes since September 1. Trump used the same post to reject reports of depleted US munitions stockpiles, insisting the US holds "virtually unlimited amounts of Mid to High Grade Ammunition" and that Washington is now stockpiling rather than exporting matériel — "we are taking them for ourselves, the USA, rather than selling them to others, but the sales to Allies will soon again, begin."

The juxtaposition — a demand for money tied to a warning about arms supply — has been read by some Ukraine watchers as pressure aimed less at recovering funds than at extracting deeper European commitment to US priorities beyond Ukraine, including Iran.

What to watch

No European government has yet responded formally to the repayment demand, and no US agency has outlined how such a claim would be pursued — through NATO channels, bilateral negotiation, or offset against future PURL contributions. The absence of a mechanism, paired with disputed figures, makes near-term enforcement unlikely. The more consequential test will be whether the rhetoric bleeds into upcoming NATO defense-spending discussions or the next PURL funding round, where allies have so far treated forward payment, not retroactive debt, as the price of the alliance.
