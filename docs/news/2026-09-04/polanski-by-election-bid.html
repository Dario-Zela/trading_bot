Green leader Zack Polanski to contest Starmer's former Commons seat

Zack Polanski, leader of the Green Party of England and Wales, who will contest the Holborn and St Pancras by-election. — London Assembly / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0)
In one sentence
Green leader Zack Polanski will contest the by-election in Keir Starmer's former seat of Holborn and St Pancras, testing Labour's grip on its left flank.

Zack Polanski, leader of the Green Party of England and Wales, will stand in the by-election for Holborn and St Pancras — the north London seat Sir Keir Starmer represented for eleven years before resigning from the Commons on 1 September, six weeks after losing the confidence of his own MPs and stepping down as prime minister.

"I have lived and worked in this community over many years. I know it well, and I love it."

That was Polanski's line on announcing his candidacy. He followed it with a sharper one aimed at his former Labour neighbours: "Labour stands up for corporations and big money. I'll stand up for people and our communities."

A seat that looks safer than it is

On paper, Holborn and St Pancras is not obvious Green territory. Starmer held it with a majority above 11,500 at the 2024 general election. But the topline number flattered Labour: an independent candidate campaigning against the Gaza war took nearly 19 percent of the vote, and the Green candidate took more than 10 percent, in a constituency that skews young, renter-heavy and left of the Labour leadership on foreign policy and public ownership. Strip out the incumbency effect of a sitting prime minister and the seat looks more like the kind of dense, university-adjacent patch where Greens have been making inroads across English cities.

Labour's candidate is due to be chosen by nomination, with a deadline set for noon on Friday; Camden council leader Sagal Abdi-Wali has been named as a possible frontrunner. The party will not want this to become a referendum on its record in office, but a resignation by-election in the former prime minister's own seat is difficult to frame as anything else.

Testing the Polanski effect

Polanski has led the Greens for just under a year, since September 2025, and the party has had a livelier twelve months than usual for a fourth-place outfit. It won the Gorton and Denton by-election in Greater Manchester earlier this year — a rare parliamentary gain from Labour — and national polling has put the Greens around the 20 percent mark, roughly level with the two established parties in some surveys. Polanski's own personal ratings have dipped somewhat after criticism of his comments on policing during a stabbing incident, but the party's underlying momentum, built on an explicitly "eco-populist" pitch that pairs climate policy with wealth taxes and anti-austerity economics, has held up.

A win in Holborn and St Pancras would be Polanski's most symbolically loaded yet: not just a Labour seat, but the departing prime minister's own patch, contested by the leader of the party trying hardest to pull disaffected left-leaning Labour voters away for good. A narrow loss with a sharply reduced Labour majority would tell much the same story more quietly.

None of this moves markets on its own — a single by-election, however symbolic, is a domestic political event, not an economic one. But it is a proxy for something investors do watch: whether Starmer's successor in government can hold the coalition of 2024 voters together without the man who built it on the ballot paper. The result lands once polling closes, expected within weeks; the immediate marker to watch before then is who Labour selects as its own candidate by Friday's noon deadline.
