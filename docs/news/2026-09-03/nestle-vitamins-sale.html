Nestlé to sell mainstream vitamins arm for $1bn as it chases premium wellness

Nestlé's headquarters in Vevey, Switzerland, where CEO Philipp Navratil is reshaping the portfolio around coffee, petcare and premium nutrition. — Nestlé / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Nestlé is selling Nature's Bounty and six other mainstream vitamin brands to PE firm Yellow Wood for $1bn, betting its future on pricier, science-led supplements instead.

Nestlé is getting out of the vitamin aisle's crowded middle shelf. The Swiss food giant has agreed to sell its mainstream vitamins, minerals and supplements business — seven brands including Nature's Bounty, Puritan's Pride and the electrolyte-drink maker Nuun — to private equity firm Yellow Wood Partners for $1 billion, or roughly CHF 0.8 billion at current exchange rates.

The unit, which Nestlé calls its Holistic Health platform, also includes Osteo Bi-Flex, Ester-C, Gard and Sisu, plus a US private-label supplements operation and the factories, packaging and distribution network that support them. It generated $1.2 billion in sales last year and operates mostly in the US, with smaller footprints in Canada and China. Nature's Bounty alone is the number-two vitamins brand in the US by Nestlé's reckoning, sold in more than a fifth of American households over a 50-year run. The deal needs regulatory clearance and is expected to close in the first half of 2027.

What Nestlé is keeping tells the story. Chief executive Philipp Navratil said the company is "focusing resources where we have the strongest competitive advantage," pointing to Solgar and Pure Encapsulations — pricier, clinically-marketed supplement lines — as where Nestlé sees room to grow. Mainstream vitamins, by contrast, "require a different approach under dedicated ownership," in his words: a polite way of saying the category has become a low-margin commodity business better suited to a private equity owner focused on cost discipline than to a company chasing premium growth.

A pattern, not an outlier

The sale extends a broader overhaul Navratil has pushed since taking over as CEO, organising Nestlé around four pillars — coffee, petcare, nutrition and health, and food and snacking — while cutting 16,000 jobs, about 6% of the workforce, in pursuit of roughly CHF 3 billion in savings by 2027. Nestlé has also agreed to fold its water business into a joint venture worth close to €3 billion and is weighing an exit from ice cream. The vitamins sale fits the same logic: shed the categories where Nestlé competes on shelf space and price, keep the ones where it can charge a premium for science.

For Yellow Wood, the deal is its sixth carve-out from a major consumer company since 2019, following prior purchases from Bayer, Reckitt, Unilever and Haleon; the firm already runs more than 40 household names including Chapstick, Suave and Q-tips. Partner Dana Schmaltz said the Nestlé brands sit in "high growth sectors including hydration, gut health and immunity," while partner Tad Yanagi pointed to the firm's playbook of applying its "Consumer Operating DNA model" to accelerate growth — private equity shorthand for stripping out overhead and running the brands leaner than a diversified conglomerate can.

Nestlé shares dipped modestly, trading around CHF 77.80 in Zurich the day after the announcement, a sign investors see the move as incremental rather than transformative. The bigger test comes when the water joint venture and any ice cream sale are finalised — together they would mark the most substantial reshaping of Nestlé's portfolio in years, and the next data point on whether Navratil's premium-first bet is paying off arrives with full-year results in early 2027.
