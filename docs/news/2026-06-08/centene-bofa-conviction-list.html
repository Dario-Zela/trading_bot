BofA adds Centene to highest-conviction list as institutional consensus on managed care firms up

Centene Plaza, the company's headquarters in Clayton, Missouri, near St. Louis. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
BofA's US 1 List addition of Centene — paired with a Barclays price target raise and 72 hedge fund holders — marks a broad institutional bet on managed care's Medicaid recovery.

Bank of America handed Centene Corporation (NYSE: CNC) one of its more coveted designations on May 27, placing the managed care insurer on the firm's US 1 List — its highest-conviction roster of stocks expected to outperform. The move came a day after Barclays separately lifted its price target on Centene to $75 from $63, maintaining an Overweight rating and noting that share price moves across the managed care sector following first-quarter earnings "appear sustainable." The convergence was not coincidental.

Centene was already sitting on a notably broad institutional base. As of Q1 2026, 72 hedge funds held the stock — one of the widest ownership footprints in managed care — and it ranks among the top ten equities held by members of Congress. BofA's addition does not arrive at a blank slate; it reinforces a consensus already in formation. Notably, BofA made the change as a direct swap within managed care: at the same time Centene joined the US 1 List, Cigna was removed. That rotation signals a deliberate preference for government-program exposure over commercial book, particularly as inflationary pressures continue to compress commercial payer margins.

The numbers that earned the call

Centene's Q1 2026 results gave analysts something concrete to act on. The company posted adjusted earnings per share of $3.37 — comfortably ahead of the $2.20–$2.30 consensus — on revenue of $49.94 billion, roughly $2.4 billion above forecasts. The headline beat mattered less than the trajectory underneath it. Consolidated health benefits ratio came in at 87.3%, with the Medicaid segment showing a 50-basis-point year-on-year improvement to 93.1%. That figure is the one investors had been watching.

Medicaid margins have been the central anxiety for managed care since 2023. The end of pandemic-era continuous enrollment protections triggered a wave of eligibility redeterminations that stripped membership and reset remaining beneficiaries toward higher-acuity, costlier patients. Centene, as the largest Medicaid managed care organization in the US, absorbed that pain at scale. The Q1 data suggests the worst is clearing.

CEO Sarah London told investors the quarter reflected "excellent progress within the Medicaid business," attributing gains to efforts to "modernize and standardize processes to better manage medical cost trend." Management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to above $3.40, up from the above-$3.00 floor set in February, and lifted premium and service revenue guidance by $1 billion to a $171–$175 billion range. Cash flow from operations for the quarter was $4.4 billion.

The forward question is straightforward: Centene's second-quarter results, expected in late July, will be the first test of whether Q1 momentum was structural or seasonal. A second consecutive improvement in Medicaid HBR would cement the thesis; any reversal would put the BofA conviction call under immediate pressure.
