Musk hails ASML as Europe's greatest company ahead of SpaceX Terafab manufacturing pitch

ASML's corporate headquarters in Veldhoven, Netherlands — the sole source of EUV lithography machines used to manufacture leading-edge chips worldwide. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Musk calls ASML Europe's greatest company ahead of a closed conference pitch for Terafab, SpaceX's $119bn chip-factory venture — but no deal is announced.

Elon Musk took to X on Saturday to call ASML "arguably the greatest company in Europe," adding that it "should be treasured and supported." The comment arrived ahead of a closed virtual discussion he is scheduled to hold this week with ASML chief executive Christophe Fouquet at the Dutch company's annual technology conference — an event restricted to employees and partners.

The venue is not incidental. ASML, headquartered in Veldhoven in the southern Netherlands, holds what amounts to a manufacturing monopoly on extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines — bus-sized devices that use precisely focused light to etch transistors at the nanometre scale onto silicon wafers. No other company makes them. Without EUV, it is effectively impossible to produce the most advanced chips that AI workloads depend on.

Musk's appearance at the conference is billed around SpaceX's Terafab concept, the company's plan to build a network of giant semiconductor fabrication complexes in Grimes County, Texas. The project — a three-way venture with Tesla and xAI, with Intel contributing its 14A process node — carries a reported initial price tag of $55 billion and a full build-out cost of up to $119 billion. Eighty percent of Terafab's projected output is earmarked for radiation-hardened chips destined for SpaceX's planned orbital satellite data centres: servers in low Earth orbit, running on solar power.

Musk himself has distilled the logic bluntly: "We either build the Terafab, or we don't have the chips, and we need the chips, so we build the Terafab."

Flattery with a purpose

The praise sits in an obvious strategic context. Any leading-edge chip facility on the scale Musk is describing would require EUV machines by the dozen — which means ASML is, for practical purposes, indispensable to the Terafab roadmap. Courting the company's workforce and leadership before pitching the vision in detail is straightforward positioning.

What the announcement does not contain is any formal agreement, supply contract, or partnership announcement. The Benzinga report that first surfaced the exchange carries no figures on machine orders, delivery timelines, or financing arrangements between SpaceX and ASML. The discussion this week remains private, and the conference is not open to press.

ASML's machines currently run at roughly $380 million apiece for the most advanced high-NA EUV models. A fab at Terafab's projected scale would represent one of the largest single customers the Dutch company has ever seen — which makes Musk's praise feel less like an editorial opinion and more like a warm-up act.

The week's discussion will say little publicly; the conference releases no transcripts. What would move the needle is evidence of a supply agreement, a construction permit for the Grimes County site, or an update on the SpaceX IPO process — currently pricing the company at an implied $1.75 trillion. Until then, this is executive flattery with a legible motive, not a supply-chain shift.
