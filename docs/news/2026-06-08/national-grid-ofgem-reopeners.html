National Grid files 25 tactical proposals with Ofgem to reshape its multi-billion five-year investment plan

National Grid high-voltage pylons crossing the Coln Valley, England — part of the 22,000-pylon network now subject to a £4.5bn upgrade bid. — Wikimedia Commons / geograph.org.uk
In one sentence
National Grid asked Ofgem to approve £4.5bn in additional transmission investment via 25 re-opener filings, with the regulator's verdict shaping NGG's return profile through the RIIO-T3 period.

National Grid filed 25 transmission investment proposals with Ofgem on June 3, seeking regulatory approval for roughly £4.5 billion in new spending across England and Wales — a sum that, if cleared, would materially reshape the company's capital programme under the RIIO-T3 price control.

The filings were made under Ofgem's "re-openers" framework, a mechanism that allows network companies to revisit their funding arrangements mid-period when demand or network conditions have moved beyond the assumptions baked into the original price control settlement. For National Grid Electricity Transmission, the driver is clear enough: the energy system it was designed to serve when RIIO-T3 was set is not the one it operates today.

What £4.5bn buys

The 25 proposals span connections for new low-carbon generation capacity, grid access for major industrial users and data centres, and reinforcements aimed at keeping pace with rising electricity demand. Grid connection delays have become, in the words of one assessment, "one of the biggest barriers to the delivery of clean energy projects" — a constraint that gives the submission political as well as commercial weight.

Rob Salter-Church, regulation director at National Grid Electricity Transmission, was direct about the impetus: "Britain's energy system is changing rapidly, and the transmission network must keep pace."

The £4.5 billion request sits inside a larger £70 billion, five-year investment envelope that National Grid has laid out across its UK and US operations. The re-openers process is, in effect, the mechanism by which that headline figure gets tested: each proposal must clear Ofgem's scrutiny on necessity, design, and cost-efficiency before funding is confirmed. Ambition on paper does not automatically translate into allowed expenditure.

Ofgem will now consult on the applications — a process that involves reviewing project justifications, soliciting third-party responses, and weighing consumer bill implications before issuing determinations. The regulator has signalled it expects investments to demonstrate long-term cost reduction and consumer value, language that puts the burden squarely on National Grid to show its spending is efficient, not merely large.

For NGG shareholders, the consultation represents a discrete earnings variable: if Ofgem disallows or scales back a material portion of the £4.5 billion, the company's return profile over the remaining RIIO-T3 period contracts accordingly. Full approval would validate National Grid's capital deployment strategy and underpin the dividend assumptions currently embedded in consensus forecasts. The company has not specified when it expects Ofgem to conclude its review, but National Grid's next results update will be the first formal venue to gauge how the regulator is receiving the filings.
