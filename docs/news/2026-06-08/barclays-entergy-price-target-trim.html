Barclays clips Entergy price target to $119 while holding its overweight on the regulated utility

Entergy's New Orleans headquarters tower in the city's Central Business District — Wikimedia Commons / Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0
In one sentence
Barclays trimmed its Entergy price target 4% to $119 on June 3, keeping its Overweight intact, with the June 9 Investor Day flagged as the next meaningful catalyst.

Barclays' equity desk made a quiet housekeeping adjustment to its Entergy Corp. (NYSE: ETR) model on June 3, trimming the price target by five dollars — from $124 to $119 — while keeping its Overweight rating unchanged. The action drew little market fanfare, which is appropriate: at a 4% reduction with no accompanying rating move, it is the kind of recalibration that signals model upkeep rather than thesis revision.

The revised target sits roughly three dollars above the Street consensus of around $116, based on an average across seventeen analyst estimates, and still implies meaningful upside from ETR's recent trading range in the low-to-mid $110s.

A soft patch since Q1

The timing points to first-quarter results as the proximate cause. Entergy reported adjusted EPS of $0.86 for Q1 2026 — a period Barclays later described as producing "lackluster" stock performance despite industrial sales growth of 15% year over year, buoyed by rising data-centre load. The headline print came in below some market expectations, and shares have lagged since.

Barclays did not retreat on the fundamental view. The bank described Entergy shares as "undervalued" and said they "provide strong value at the current level" — language that indicates a firm recalibrating its model rather than its conviction. For comparison: Truist made a similar incremental move on May 29, lowering its target from $130 to $127 while maintaining a Buy rating.

Entergy operates five regulated vertically integrated utilities across Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Texas, serving roughly three million customers from a generation mix spanning gas, nuclear, hydro, solar, and coal. Regulated utilities rarely prompt dramatic analyst pivots — rate cases, capital plans, and load growth are the levers, and they turn slowly.

The forward story centres on Entergy's $57 billion, four-year capital programme, a plan enlarged substantially by a March agreement with Meta Platforms for a Louisiana data-centre campus requiring an estimated $15 billion in infrastructure investment. Management raised its 2029 adjusted EPS target to $6.40, projecting 12% growth in 2030, and still sees a pipeline of seven to twelve gigawatts of potential data-centre load sitting outside the current plan.

June 9 as the next read

The more significant near-term event is Entergy's 2026 Investor Day, scheduled for June 9 in New York City. CEO Drew Marsh and the executive team are set to lay out the company's growth roadmap through 2030. Presentation materials will be posted before market open; a live webcast begins at 1 p.m. ET.

Barclays flagged the event explicitly as a positive catalyst — a signal that the bank expects updated load commitments or a refreshed capital plan to close the gap between the current share price and its revised $119 target. The Investor Day will provide the first formal look at whether management can translate a 7-to-12 GW data-centre pipeline into hard numbers. That is the figure to watch on Monday afternoon.
