HSBC targets 70 Asia IPO mandates as Hong Kong dealmaking recovers

HSBC's headquarters in Central, Hong Kong — the bank is rebuilding its equity capital markets presence in the city where it generates nearly a third of its global pre-tax profit. — Wikimedia Commons / Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike
In one sentence
HSBC has assembled 70 Asia IPO mandates, including 40 in Hong Kong, as the market targets a record HK$350 billion in 2026 listings.

HSBC is pressing into Hong Kong's resurgent IPO market with a scale that signals genuine strategic conviction rather than opportunism. The bank has assembled around 70 IPO mandates across Asia — roughly 40 of them in Hong Kong alone — a stark contrast to the five Hong Kong IPO mandates it held through all of 2025.

The effort is being driven from the top. CEO Georges Elhedery has been meeting clients directly and recording tailored video messages to key Greater China customers, part of a campaign to re-engage approximately 400 major accounts across buyout firms, hedge funds, and large corporates. The bank has also added more than a dozen investment bankers to its China business over the past year, drawing recruits from JPMorgan Chase and Goldman Sachs.

The pipeline includes Club Med's potential US$500 million public offering — where HSBC is working alongside BNP Paribas and JPMorgan — and a possible share sale for Linkerbot, a Chinese robotics startup. Neither deal is finalised, but the breadth of the pipeline reflects how sharply the bank's posture has shifted since Elhedery took the helm in 2024.

A recovering market, and a bank playing catch-up

The strategic rationale is straightforward: Hong Kong's IPO market is recovering faster than most expected. In the first quarter of 2026, the exchange raised HK$109.9 billion across 40 new listings — a 489% increase in funds raised year-on-year and the strongest first quarter in five years. Hong Kong ranked first globally in IPO proceeds for the period. KPMG projects full-year 2026 volume at approximately HK$350 billion, equivalent to roughly US$43–45 billion at current rates. Much of Q1's momentum came from A+H listings — dual offerings on both the mainland and Hong Kong — which accounted for 15 of the quarter's deals compared to just one in Q1 2025.

HSBC has a particular stake in this recovery. Hong Kong generated US$9.6 billion in pre-tax profit for the group in 2025, close to one-third of the bank's total. Missing out on capital markets activity in its home territory is not a rounding error.

That gap is visible in the league tables. HSBC currently sits 12th in Hong Kong share-sale rankings. Morgan Stanley and China International Capital Corp. lead. The bank's recent record includes a high-profile setback: it was passed over for a lead role on A.S. Watson Group's dual London-Hong Kong listing, a deal valued at more than US$2 billion, which went to Goldman Sachs and UBS instead.

Elhedery has responded by repositioning the bank's equity capital markets focus away from Europe and the United States and toward Asia and the Middle East — a structural shift rather than a tactical one. The 70-mandate figure is the early output of that repositioning.

The central question, which the league table position keeps asking, is whether mandate count translates into mandate quality. A pipeline is not revenue. Whether HSBC can convert its rebuilding push into completed transactions — and whether the broader US$43 billion volume projection holds as trade tensions and rate uncertainty persist — will be clearest in the second half of the year, when several of the larger deals on the pipeline are expected to price.
