BP opens the tap on a trillion-cubic-foot gas prize in Azerbaijan

The Sangachal Terminal south of Baku receives oil and gas from BP-operated offshore fields; ACG's new gas wells will flow through existing pipelines to the same facility. — Wikimedia Commons / CC BY 4.0
In one sentence
BP has launched the first gas production at Azerbaijan's ACG block, tapping 4–6 Tcf of reserves, while taking over the Babek field to deepen its Caspian gas bet.

BP's Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli block in the Caspian Sea has pumped oil since 1997. As of June 1, it is doing something new: producing gas. The company has started commercial extraction of non-associated gas — gas sitting in dedicated formations rather than coming up dissolved in crude — from the field for the first time, tapping reservoirs BP estimates hold around 4 trillion cubic feet of recoverable reserves, with a potential upside to 6 Tcf.

The wells target the Qirmaki Upper and Lower Sand formations from the West Chirag platform, with gas and condensate flowing through existing subsea pipelines to the Sangachal Terminal on the Caspian coast south of Baku — sidestepping the need for new export infrastructure. "This is a big day for Azerbaijan and for the ACG co-venturers," said Gio Cristofoli, BP's regional president for Azerbaijan, Georgia and Türkiye. "ACG has a long and successful history and now, nearly three decades into oil production, the field continues to hold potential to deliver value for the nation and its investors as it starts this new chapter."

For now, volumes are measured: initial output from ACG's gas wells is estimated at roughly 100 million cubic metres in 2026 — a rounding error in European supply terms. A second deep-formation well is planned for 2027, with output potentially reaching 2 billion cubic metres per year by 2028.

Adding Babek to the book

Simultaneously, BP is assuming operatorship of the Babek offshore gas field, currently run by SOCAR, Azerbaijan's state energy company. The transition was expected to be formalised on June 1. Babek is a distinct structure with its own scale: estimated reserves of around 400 billion cubic metres of gas and 80 million tonnes of condensate. Combined, the two moves materially extend BP's upstream gas position in the South Caucasus and keep it on track toward its stated regional target of 250,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day by 2027.

The ACG consortium — in which BP holds a 30.37% operating stake alongside SOCAR, MOL, INPEX, ExxonMobil, TPAO and ONGC Videsh — has always been primarily an oil story. The NAG development is a structural shift: a field that has been declining in oil output since its 885,000-barrel-per-day peak in 2010 is now opening a second resource chapter rather than simply being managed for run-off.

The European dimension

The timing is not incidental to European gas markets. Azerbaijan currently supplies roughly 5% of EU total gas demand via the 3,500-kilometre Southern Gas Corridor, which feeds 12 European countries. Exports reached 12.9 billion cubic metres in 2024, up from 8.1 bcm in 2021, and new supply agreements with Austria and Germany came online this year. The Trans Adriatic Pipeline, the corridor's western leg, is adding 1.2 bcm per year of capacity in 2026.

New Caspian output will not reshape European gas curves overnight — ACG's ramp-up alone would not register materially until 2028, and Babek is years from production. But both developments reinforce Azerbaijan's positioning as the EU's preferred non-Russian pipeline supplier, a narrative that carries strategic weight while the continent monitors any easing of Russian gas diplomacy. The clearest near-term gauge will be BP's next quarterly results, where the first ACG gas production figures are expected to appear.
