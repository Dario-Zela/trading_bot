EU extends individual Russia sanctions for 36 months, drops Usmanov and Fridman

Alisher Usmanov, whose removal from the EU sanctions list was pushed by France, pictured at the 2013 World Fencing Championships in Budapest. — Marie-Lan Nguyen / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0)
In one sentence
The EU extended its Russia sanctions regime to 2029 but dropped oligarchs Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman from the list, angering Kyiv.

The European Union has renewed the backbone of its Russia sanctions regime for three years instead of the usual six months — and paid for that stability by striking two of Russia's richest men off the list.

Ambassadors from the 27 member states agreed on Tuesday to extend asset freezes and travel bans on roughly 3,000 individuals and entities tied to Russia's invasion of Ukraine until 22 September 2029, EU officials said. In the same package, metals and telecoms magnate Alisher Usmanov and banking billionaire Mikhail Fridman were removed from the list, ending sanctions both men have carried since the weeks after Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.

A trade, not a thaw

The deal followed two weeks of talks that, by several accounts, pushed the bloc's sanctions machinery — which requires unanimity — close to collapse. France pressed hardest for Usmanov's removal, tying it to the release of two French nationals, Anass Derraz and Martin Ryan, held in Azerbaijan on bribery and espionage charges respectively; Paris cited only "national security" reasons publicly. Fridman's delisting followed a separate push after Slovakia threatened to veto renewal altogether, with Luxembourg arguing the two oligarchs should be treated alike. Fridman has separately filed a $16 billion claim over assets frozen since 2022.

Latvia held out longest, warning the move would ease pressure on Moscow at a sensitive moment, but ultimately abstained rather than block consensus — clearing the way for the rest of the list, some 3,000 names, to roll over for three years, a term EU officials framed as strengthening "the durability and predictability of the sanctions framework."

Kyiv's objection

Ukraine had lobbied openly against the delistings. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the outcome "suicidal and counterproductive," writing that "in Moscow, they celebrate, because they got what they wanted: a sense of impunity, humiliation of the European Union, and division among Europeans." Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga labelled the move "shameful and unjustifiable" and urged member states to impose their own national sanctions on the pair in place of the EU-wide measure.

The practical stakes are sizeable. Usmanov, worth an estimated $18.4 billion, once co-owned Arsenal and until this year held Everton's stadium naming rights through his USM Holdings. Fridman, worth roughly $10 billion, had already resigned from the board of investment group LetterOne and sold his stakes in Alfa Bank and Alfa Insurance under sanctions pressure; unfreezing now reopens the question of what remains of those holdings to reclaim.

Watch for individual member states — Ukraine has specifically named the Baltic states as candidates — to layer national sanctions on top of the EU delisting, and for further legal claims from other frozen oligarchs citing the Fridman precedent before the next scheduled review in 2029.
