Far-right activist films himself slashing Channel migrant dinghy with rescue worker aboard

Daniel Thomas, known online as Danny Tommo, filming at a Patriot Platform protest in Southampton — Indigo Nolan / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 4.0)
In one sentence
Livestreaming to 100,000 viewers, far-right activist Daniel Thomas knifed an empty Channel migrant dinghy with a French rescue worker still aboard, prompting a police probe.

A dinghy that had just carried roughly 60 asylum seekers across the Channel — after a crossing attempt of more than 30 hours, one of the longest on record — had barely been emptied by rescuers on Tuesday afternoon when Daniel Thomas pulled his speedboat alongside it, pulled out a knife, and began stabbing the pontoon.

Thomas, a far-right activist who goes by Danny Tommo online, livestreamed the whole episode to roughly 100,000 viewers on YouTube. The footage shows him leaning from his boat and slashing repeatedly at the inflatable's side as he shouts "This is England." A member of the French rescue team remained on board the dinghy as he did it.

A pursuit that began the day before

The boat had set out from near Vierville-sur-Mer in Normandy, picking up around a dozen more passengers along the Côte Fleurie before turning north — a longer and more dangerous route than the usual Calais-area crossings, and one French and British officials have previously treated as an isolated tactic rather than an emerging pattern. Thomas spotted the vessel early and tracked it by speedboat, broadcasting its position with Starlink equipment as it struggled north.

By the time HM Coastguard tasked two RNLI lifeboats and the French coastguard responded, the dinghy was, in the words of one rescue official, "in a very bad way, and very low in the water, with just a single small outboard motor." Rather than intervene early, crews chose to escort it. The roughly 60 people aboard — after what the RNLI believes may be the longest small-boat crossing time on record — were taken off safely. It was only after the transfer that Thomas's boat closed in on the now-empty dinghy, with the French rescue worker still standing on it.

Police scrutiny, and a pattern of confrontation

Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary said it was "aware of a video circulating online relating to damage being caused to a boat in the English Channel on the afternoon of 22 September" and was making inquiries. Deliberately damaging a vessel, campaigners and legal commentators have noted, can in principle carry serious criminal-damage or endangerment charges, particularly with another person still aboard.

Thomas is a familiar figure to police already. A former British Army recruit discharged after a training injury, he served two years in prison after pleading guilty in 2016 to an attempted kidnapping at knifepoint. He later became a close associate of English Defence League founder Tommy Robinson before founding Patriot Platform this summer, a group that has staged confrontational protests outside asylum accommodation in Dover and Portsmouth. In August he pleaded guilty to one charge of racially abusive behaviour linked to attempts to film asylum seekers at the Port of Dover, while contesting five further charges. In April he had bought a small rigid-hulled inflatable boat, naming it Patriot and declaring he would use it to "police" the Channel; within weeks he had sunk it.

Anti-racism campaigners reacted with alarm to Tuesday's footage. Stand Up to Racism called Thomas "a violent far right thug," adding: "We're going to stop him and his hate." The RNLI has previously said it makes no apology for rescuing anyone in danger at sea, regardless of migration status, and has faced years of criticism from anti-immigration campaigners for doing so.

What's next

Hampshire police have not said whether Thomas will face charges, and the force's inquiry is still at an early stage. The incident lands as Channel crossings remain a defining flashpoint of British politics ahead of the autumn party conference season, and as Patriot Platform's protests in Dover and Portsmouth continue to draw counter-demonstrations. Whether prosecutors treat the slashing as a one-off stunt or as evidence for the more serious charges some campaigners are demanding will be the next marker to watch.
