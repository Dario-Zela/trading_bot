Overnight Russian missile and drone strikes hit Ukrainian regions as leaders gather at UN

The UN General Assembly hall in New York, where Zelensky addressed the general debate on September 23 — Patrick Gruban / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Russia struck Dnipropetrovsk and Chernihiv overnight with over 200 drones and missiles as Zelensky addressed the UN General Assembly in New York.

Russia launched another large-scale missile and drone barrage against Ukraine overnight into September 23, striking industrial sites and energy infrastructure across several regions just as world leaders gathered in New York for the UN General Assembly's high-level session — the annual gathering where, for four years running, the war has been a fixture of the agenda without ever quite being resolved by it.

Ukraine's air force said Russian forces fired 212 drones, four cruise missiles and an unspecified number of Oniks anti-ship and ballistic missiles overnight. The heaviest damage landed in the Dnipropetrovsk region, where strikes hit Dnipro, Kryvyi Rih — President Volodymyr Zelensky's hometown — and Pavlohrad simultaneously. A ballistic missile struck the Interpipe steelmaking complex in Dnipro, killing four workers and wounding seven; officials in the city separately reported three dead and six wounded as missiles, drones and artillery tore into warehouses and business premises. In Chernihiv, an attack on power infrastructure knocked out electricity to nearly 100,000 residents. Russia said its own air defenses had shot down 297 Ukrainian drones overnight, in Moscow's telling a retaliatory tally rather than an admission of anything.

A familiar pairing

The timing was not incidental. Zelensky arrived in New York for a week of roughly twenty meetings, including a planned sit-down with Donald Trump, and used the strikes as the backdrop for his UN address, telling delegates that achieving peace requires "joint efforts" and that Russia was signaling readiness to widen the war rather than end it. It is a rhythm that has repeated across successive Assembly sessions: Kyiv arrives at the world's most visible diplomatic stage while Moscow underlines, with drones over Dnipropetrovsk, that the war continues regardless of who is speaking in the hall.

Zelensky said a deal to strengthen Ukraine's drone defenses was ready to be signed in New York. That urgency is not rhetorical. Ukrainian officials have said the country received only about a third as many Western air-defense interceptors in 2026 as over the same period last year, even as Russian sortie sizes have grown. Washington and Kyiv are separately negotiating a license for Ukraine to produce its own Patriot interceptors domestically — a process US officials have acknowledged would take years to yield meaningful volume, offering no relief for the immediate shortfall.

The diplomatic undertow

Amid the barrage, there were signs of movement short of resolution. French President Emmanuel Macron and Trump were reported to have agreed to work toward a moratorium on strikes targeting energy networks and civilian infrastructure specifically — a narrower ambition than a ceasefire, but one that would matter for a Ukrainian power grid now entering its fifth winter of Russian targeting. Germany announced an urgent transfer of additional Patriot batteries. None of it altered the overnight picture on the ground.

What happens next is less about the Assembly floor than the calendar behind it: whether the energy-infrastructure moratorium Macron and Trump discussed produces anything binding, and whether the Patriot deal Zelensky said was ready in New York actually gets signed before winter deliveries become the more urgent test of Ukraine's air defenses.
