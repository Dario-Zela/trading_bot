AstraZeneca shares seen up to 48% undervalued on Enhertu progress and EU backing

AstraZeneca's Academy House headquarters at the Cambridge Biomedical Campus — D Wells / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Simply Wall St's models put AstraZeneca up to 48-50% undervalued even as Enhertu racks up EU approvals and strong trial data, though a SERENA-4 miss shows the pipeline isn't flawless.

AstraZeneca shares are changing hands around 12,450p on the London Stock Exchange, near record territory. By Simply Wall St's own cash-flow arithmetic, they still shouldn't be.

The research platform's discounted cash flow models have shown AstraZeneca trading below fair value for most of 2026, with the gap widening rather than closing as the year has gone on. A September 11 valuation note pegged shares as trading up to 50% below some intrinsic-value estimates and roughly 34% below the average analyst price target — the upper end of a range that has crept from the high 30s toward 48-50% since the summer, even as the shares themselves have risen.

"The share price still sits below what this cash flow based valuation suggests." — Simply Wall St

The pipeline case

The undervaluation argument leans heavily on Enhertu, AstraZeneca's antibody-drug conjugate developed with Daiichi Sankyo, which has had a run of good news. On September 18 the EU's drugs regulator, the CHMP, recommended approval of Enhertu as an adjuvant treatment for HER2-positive early breast cancer patients with residual disease after surgery — a new use that AstraZeneca calls the first such regimen added to that setting in more than a decade. Earlier in September, Enhertu combined with pertuzumab won EU approval as a first-line treatment for HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, after the DESTINY-Breast09 trial showed the combination cut the risk of disease progression or death by 44% against standard chemotherapy, triggering a $100m milestone payment to Daiichi Sankyo.

On the same CHMP list was gefurulimab, branded Klygefa, a self-administered antibody recommended for approval in generalised myasthenia gravis — the second AstraZeneca drug to clear the EU committee this month.

Separately, at the World Conference on Lung Cancer in Seoul, AstraZeneca presented Phase III data showing Enhertu monotherapy extended median progression-free survival to 14.3 months in HER2-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer, against 8.3 months for chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab — a 37% reduction in the risk of progression or death, with a response rate of 70% versus 44.5%. Grade 3-plus side effects, including lung inflammation in one in five patients, remain the trade-off regulators and doctors will keep watching.

Not everything has gone AstraZeneca's way

The pipeline isn't without its stumbles. Camizestrant, sold as Etcamah, missed the primary endpoint of the SERENA-4 trial in first-line breast cancer this month, briefly denting the shares before they recovered — the drug's existing, narrower approval for ESR1-mutant advanced breast cancer was unaffected, so the setback narrows an opportunity rather than closing it.

Reading the valuation gap with care

Analyst screens flagging "undervalued" pharma stocks are a routine feature of the sector — Simply Wall St's own models have called AstraZeneca cheap at 37%, 38%, 43% and now closer to 48-50% undervalued at various points this year, largely because the DCF inputs shift as new trial data and revenue assumptions roll in, not because the underlying gap is fixed. What's harder to dispute is the direction: a steady accumulation of regulatory wins across oncology and immunology, arriving faster than the market has moved the share price. Simply Wall St's own conclusion is the right caveat — the model assumes AstraZeneca can convert pipeline momentum into sustained cash generation, and that conversion, not the DCF output, is the real test.

What's next

European Commission sign-off on both Enhertu's adjuvant breast-cancer use and Klygefa typically follows a CHMP recommendation within roughly two months, putting formal approval in view before year-end. AstraZeneca reports full-year results in early February, which will show whether Enhertu's expanding label set is translating into the revenue growth the valuation models are betting on.
