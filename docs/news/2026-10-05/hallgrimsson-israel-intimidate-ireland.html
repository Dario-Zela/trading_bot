Hallgrímsson says Israel 'tried to intimidate' Ireland amid spitting row

In one sentence
Ireland boss Heimir Hallgrímsson said Israel tried to intimidate his side in a 1-1 Nations League draw marred by an alleged half-time spitting incident involving a member of his staff.

Ireland manager Heimir Hallgrímsson said Israel tried to intimidate his side from the first whistle in a bad-tempered Nations League draw on Sunday. The match was overshadowed by an allegation that a member of Ireland's staff was spat at during the interval.

The game finished 1-1 at the TSC Arena in Bačka Topola, Serbia, a neutral venue where it was played behind closed doors. Troy Parrott put Ireland ahead with a first-half penalty. Dor Peretz equalised for Israel.

The row at half-time

The flashpoint came as the teams left the pitch at the break. The Irish Times reported that Damien Doyle, Ireland's head of athletic performance, alleged an Israel player had spat on him. On television pictures, a voice can be heard saying "he spat on me" as the players headed down the tunnel.

Hallgrímsson did not dwell on the incident itself, but he was blunt about the wider pattern of the match.

"I felt like they were trying to intimidate us from kick-off but I give a lot of credit to the players to stay calm."

His praise for his players' composure is the notable part. A game with this much friction can easily produce cards, and Ireland avoided the kind of reaction that would have changed the result.

Israel's response

Israel's head coach, Ran Ben-Shimon, said he "didn't see anything". He explained he was in the dressing room with his staff when the incident was alleged to have happened. Asked about the physical treatment of Ireland midfielder Jason Molumby, he said it was "nothing personal" and that his team does not instruct players to injure opponents.

The accounts do not conflict as directly as the headlines suggest. Ben-Shimon is not disputing that the tunnel exchange took place. He is saying he was not there to see it. The allegation rests on Doyle's account and on the audio picked up by the broadcast. No finding has been published, and Israel has not been reported to have responded to the specific claim beyond the manager's comments.

Why the tension

These are the second meeting between the sides in a matter of weeks. Ireland won the first, 3-0 in Hungary last month. Both fixtures have been staged away from Israel and carry the weight of the wider political climate around the Gaza conflict. The Football Association of Ireland is also reported to be looking into alleged racial abuse of Adam Idah by Israel supporters at the first game.

Whether UEFA opens disciplinary proceedings over the half-time allegation is the next thing to watch. The governing body typically asks for match-delegate and referee reports before deciding whether to charge anyone. Any evidence from the tunnel footage would be central to that.
