Green Party adopts policy equating Zionism with racism; Israel bars leader Polanski

In one sentence
Israel barred Green leader Zack Polanski after conference voted 1,022–813 to equate Zionism with racism, a motion he missed and four of five Green MPs opposed.

The Green Party of England and Wales has voted at its autumn conference to adopt a policy declaring Zionism to be racism. Israel's government responded within hours by barring the party's leader, Zack Polanski, from entering the country. Polanski did not vote for the motion. The consequences have landed mainly on him and on a party already split over it.

The vote

Delegates in Brighton passed the motion on 4 October by 1,022 votes to 813, a margin of about 56% to 44%. As reported, the text equates Zionism with racism, backs a Palestinian state "from the river to the sea" and legitimises armed struggle against Israel. The conference also rejected an amendment that would have protected members from disciplinary action over synagogue affiliations.

Polanski, who is Jewish, gave his conference speech on Friday and then left to campaign in a London by-election, so he was absent for the vote. Before leaving he said that Zionism which amounts to the displacement of Palestinians is "clearly racist". That is a narrower formulation than the one delegates adopted. It also leaves him associated with a policy he neither wrote nor voted on.

Israel's response

Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar declared Polanski barred from entering Israel. The foreign ministry called the motion "a clear antisemitic text" under the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance definition, because it denies the Jewish people's right to self-determination. It described the vote as "a stain on the history of the United Kingdom".

The ministry's statement tied the ban directly to the conference vote, so it reads as a reaction to the policy and not a separate decision. Polanski has no government role, so the ban is a gesture. It signals how Israel treats the language, and the party that adopted it, as outside acceptable debate.

A party divided

Opposition inside the party was public. Four of the Greens' five MPs spoke against the motion. Former co-leaders Adrian Ramsay and Carla Denyer voted against it. Ramsay said he was "deeply saddened" and that the position "doesn't reflect long-standing Green principles".

Outside the party, the Board of Deputies, the Community Security Trust and the Jewish Leadership Council warned that the Greens had become the first electorally significant UK party in living memory to adopt what appears to be an overtly antisemitic platform. Labour's Bridget Phillipson called the vote "shameful".

The vote exposes a gap between the membership, whose conference delegates are among its most committed activists, and the parliamentary party that has to defend the policy to voters. Polanski has built the party's recent growth on a left-populist pitch to Labour defectors. Delegates may share his sympathy for Palestinians, but he now has to answer for wording that most of his MPs rejected.

What to watch

The immediate question is whether the leadership tries to reinterpret or soften the policy, which would risk a clash with the membership that passed it. Polanski has not, in the reporting so far, addressed the ban in detail. Whether the four dissenting MPs keep their objections on the record, and how the policy plays in the London by-election he left conference to fight, will show how far the damage reaches.
