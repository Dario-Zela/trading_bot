Daughter of murdered MP says politicians are still at risk five years on

In one sentence
Five years after Sir David Amess's murder, his daughter Katie says threats to MPs are rising, as a government review and her High Court claim test what went wrong.

Five years after Sir David Amess was stabbed to death at a constituency surgery, his daughter says the people who hold such surgeries are no safer. Katie Amess told the BBC's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg that her father's killing "wasn't an isolated incident, we are seeing increased threats towards members of parliament". She described the years since as "absolutely devastating and heartbreaking" and said: "It's a very, very real situation that we're living with."

The anniversary falls on 15 October. Sir David, the Conservative MP for Southend West, was 69 when he was attacked at Belfairs Methodist Church in Leigh-on-Sea, Essex, in 2021. His killer, Ali Harbi Ali, then 26, had been referred to the government's Prevent counter-extremism programme years earlier.

What the record shows

Ms Amess's interview is mostly a restatement of concern, not a new disclosure. The specific failings she points to are already on the public record, and the question is what has been done about them.

The most concrete is Prevent. Ali was referred in 2014, and his case was closed in 2016. A later review, reported in February 2025, found that closure premature, with assessments it called "problematic". Ali has said he was able to appear compliant when dealing with the programme while continuing to plot in secret. Ms Amess has also suggested her father should have been told of potential threats that had been reported.

She has pursued answers through two routes. She has called for a public inquiry into the safety of politicians, so that there are, in her words, "the full facts to hand, full openness, honesty, and transparency". She has also filed a personal injury claim at the High Court against Essex Police and the Home Office. Her lawyers say she wants to "ensure proper accountability and learning" from the murder.

Review under way

The government has so far answered with a review rather than an inquiry. Sir Robert Buckland KC, a former justice secretary, was appointed to examine missed opportunities that might have prevented the murder. He is due to report within six months and to recommend ways of reducing risk. The BBC report of the interview did not say whether he has yet published anything.

The reporting available does not set out a detailed list of unfixed security gaps or previously unreported incidents. Ms Amess's argument rests on the broader trend of rising threats to politicians. The legal claim and the Buckland review are the two places where concrete failings are likely to be tested.

What to watch

The next markers are the Buckland review's findings and any government response to the call for a public inquiry. The High Court claim is a further route to disclosure of how Prevent and Essex Police handled the case, and the outcome of each could give the anniversary debate more substance than it has had so far.
