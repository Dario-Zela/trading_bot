HSBC lifts Apple target, betting on a stronger product cycle

Apple Park, the company's Cupertino headquarters — HSBC's upgrade bets on an installed base of roughly 2.5 billion active devices. — Arne Müseler / arne-mueseler.com / CC BY-SA 3.0
In one sentence
HSBC upgraded Apple to Buy and raised its target to $366, but the bank is only now catching up to an AI-and-iPhone story the market had already priced in.

HSBC has swung from cautious to bullish on Apple, upgrading the stock to Buy from Hold and lifting its price target by 41 percent, from $260 to $366. The call, published Friday by analyst Nicolas Cote-Colisson, arrives days after Apple shares touched an all-time high near $335 — meaning HSBC is now chasing a rally it spent most of 2026 sitting out.

In one sentence: HSBC upgraded Apple to Buy and raised its target to $366, but the bank is only now catching up to an AI-and-iPhone story the market had already priced in.

The new target implies roughly 10 percent upside from Thursday's close, with HSBC's "blue sky" scenario pointing to another $31 a share beyond that. It is also well above the Street's average target of roughly $312–$327, though still short of the $400 high end among the 111 analysts tracked by major data providers — most of whom already rate the stock Buy.

A capital-efficiency argument, dressed as an AI call

What sets HSBC's note apart from the standard AI-tailwind pitch is its framing. Cote-Colisson had spent this year preferring hyperscalers and memory-chip makers, the companies capturing the immediate AI infrastructure boom, while keeping Apple on Hold. The pivot now rests on a comparison of capital spending: Apple is projected to plow only about 2.5 percent of 2026 sales into capex, against roughly 39 percent for the hyperscalers building AI data centers. HSBC's read is that Apple can sit out that spending arms race entirely and still monetize AI by pushing new features to its installed base of roughly 2.5 billion active devices through a revamped Apple Intelligence and an agentic Siri — unveiled at June's WWDC with visual intelligence and cross-app context awareness — rather than by building infrastructure itself.

Betting on phones not yet on shelves

The rest of the thesis is a wager on an unreleased product slate: the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max this fall, an iPhone Air expected next April, and a book-style foldable iPhone that HSBC calls the most significant device in the pipeline. On the strength of that lineup, the bank raised its 2027–28 group revenue forecasts by 7 to 9 percent, lifted iPhone sales estimates by 11 to 13 percent, and now projects iPhone unit growth of about 21 percent in 2026 and 11.6 percent in 2027. None of those products has shipped, and none of those figures can be checked against actual sales for months.

What the upgrade actually signals

Strip away the framing and the ingredients are familiar: a stronger iPhone cycle, on-device AI monetized through services, a large installed base. Wall Street has been running that playbook on Apple for over a year, which is one reason the stock was already at a record before HSBC moved. What is new is less the thesis than the messenger — a bank that had explicitly preferred the infrastructure side of the AI trade is now conceding Apple's low-capex model looks better, not worse, than the hyperscalers' spending spree. Whether that reasoning holds will show up first in Apple's fiscal third-quarter results, due July 30, where iPhone sales and Services growth will offer the first real test of whether the product cycle HSBC is betting on has actually begun.
