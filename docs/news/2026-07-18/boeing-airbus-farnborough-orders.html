Boeing and Airbus race to lock in narrowbody orders before Farnborough

A Boeing 737 MAX-8, the model at the center of Boeing's pitch to lessor SMBC Aviation Capital. — Colin Brown Photography / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
SMBC Aviation Capital is weighing a ~100-jet order between Boeing and Airbus ahead of Farnborough, with both makers already behind on delivery targets.

Boeing and Airbus are both trying to close the same deal before the same deadline. The prize is a narrowbody order from SMBC Aviation Capital, the Dublin-based leasing giant, and both manufacturers are pushing to get it signed before the Farnborough International Airshow opens outside London next week — the industry's traditional cutoff for splashy pre-show announcements.

The shape of the competition is straightforward. Boeing is in advanced talks to sell SMBC around 100 737 MAX jets. Airbus is chasing a commitment of similar size for the A320neo, the MAX's direct rival. Bloomberg, which first reported the parallel talks, cautioned that order sizes and the timing of any announcement could still shift before the show opens on July 21 — lessor negotiations often run to the wire, and sometimes past it.

SMBC is not a name most flight passengers know, but it is one of the industry's more consequential buyers. Jointly owned by Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, Sumitomo Mitsui Finance and Leasing, Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group and Sumitomo Corporation, the Dublin lessor already carries commitments for 175 A320neo-family jets and 83 737 MAXs. It is also mid-way through absorbing Air Lease Corporation, a $7.4 billion deal that will make the combined firm the world's second-largest aircraft lessor, with a fleet north of 1,500 aircraft once it closes. Lessors like SMBC do not fly the planes themselves; they buy in bulk and rent them to airlines, which makes their order decisions a reasonable proxy for where the industry expects demand — and delivery risk — to sit years out.

Booked solid into the 2030s

The urgency on both sides traces back to the same constraint: neither manufacturer can build fast enough. Boeing is lifting 737 MAX output from 42 to 47 aircraft a month, with an eventual target of 53 by the end of 2026, but a wiring fault forced a delivery pause earlier this year and left 25 aircraft needing rework. Engine supply — CFM International's capacity to produce LEAP engines — remains the tighter bottleneck than the airframe line itself.

Airbus has it worse. Persistent shortfalls from Pratt & Whitney on the A320neo's geared turbofan engine pushed back Airbus's long-standing target of building 75 jets a month to the end of 2027, a delay chief executive Guillaume Faury called "very painful and unsatisfactory." Airbus filed a damages claim against Pratt & Whitney in March, and its deliveries ran roughly 20% behind last year's pace in early 2026. According to lease-data provider IBA, narrowbody production slots on both the MAX and the neo are largely spoken for until around 2033.

That backdrop makes SMBC's timing legible less as showmanship than as positioning. Locking in an order now, ahead of a show where rivals such as Avolon, Flynas and Riyadh Air are also expected to announce commitments, secures a place in the queue before it gets longer. Whichever manufacturer wins will do so largely on delivery-slot arithmetic rather than list price.

Farnborough opens July 21. Whether SMBC signs with Boeing, Airbus, or splits the order between both — as lessors sometimes do to hedge manufacturer risk — should become clear within days.
