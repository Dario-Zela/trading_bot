TSMC lifts US chip investment to a record $265bn, Trump says

TSMC's Fab 21 complex under construction in Phoenix, Arizona — the site at the center of the company's expanded $265bn US commitment. — Hunter Trick / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
TSMC is adding $100bn to its Arizona build-out, lifting its US commitment to $265bn — yet investors sold the stock on the news, not bought it.

TSMC is putting another $100 billion into its Arizona chip complex, taking the Taiwanese company's total US manufacturing commitment to $265 billion — by far the largest foreign corporate investment ever made in the United States. Chairman and CEO C.C. Wei disclosed the figure on TSMC's second-quarter earnings call on July 16, and President Trump posted about it on Truth Social within hours, calling TSMC "the largest Leading Edge Semiconductor Chip Manufacturer in the World" and framing the number as proof his trade policy is working.

The new money is earmarked for what Wei described as "probably additional four more fabs" — a mix of front-end plants for 2-nanometer-and-below production and the advanced packaging facilities that AI chips need — which would bring TSMC's Arizona footprint to twelve buildings in total. Wei was careful to say the construction pace isn't fixed; it will track customer demand rather than a preset schedule.

The number itself has climbed steadily. TSMC pledged $65 billion for its first Arizona fabs, expanded that to $165 billion in March 2025, and has now added $100 billion more. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick said the expansion will support tens of thousands of jobs; the White House cast it as a headline win for reshoring the chip supply chain that makes everything from AI accelerators to fighter jets.

A record that spooked the market

What's notable is how Wall Street took the news. TSMC also reported record quarterly revenue of $40.2 billion and raised its full-year growth forecast to more than 40%, yet the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 4.3% the same day and TSMC's own post-earnings rally reversed. Investors focused less on the headline number and more on the cost: building 2nm capacity in Arizona rather than Taiwan is expected to dilute TSMC's gross margin by three to four percentage points. Some of the sell-off reflects broader nerves about whether AI-driven chip demand is outrunning itself — but the reaction underscores that a bigger US pledge isn't free, and someone eventually pays for it in margin.

The China question still hangs over it

The $265 billion figure also needs context. It arrives five months after Washington and Taipei struck a trade deal in February that cut US tariffs on Taiwanese goods to 15% in exchange for Taiwan committing to more than $250 billion in US investment across chips and energy. TSMC's Arizona spending alone now exceeds that entire economy-wide pledge — a sign the company is leaning further into the US than the deal strictly required, even as its most advanced chips remain barred from sale to China under existing Commerce Department export rules, and Taipei weighs tightening its own curbs further. Reshoring production doesn't resolve that tension; it just shifts where the manufacturing sits while the China question stays open.

The next concrete marker is equipment installation at TSMC's Fab 21 Phase 2 site, due to begin in the third quarter, with volume production targeted for 2027. Until then, the $265 billion is a commitment on paper — real capital, but tested by whether AI chip demand still looks the way it does today when the fabs are ready to run.
