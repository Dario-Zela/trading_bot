Ninth Circuit says Kalshi's sports contracts are gambling, setting up Supreme Court fight

In one sentence
A unanimous panel found Kalshi's sports event contracts are gambling rather than federally preempted swaps, splitting with the Third Circuit and teeing up a likely Supreme Court review.

A unanimous panel found Kalshi's sports event contracts are gambling rather than federally preempted swaps, splitting with the Third Circuit and teeing up a likely Supreme Court review.

The ruling threatens a key fintech business model and signals that prediction markets may face fragmented state-by-state regulation rather than federal oversight. The Supreme Court decision will likely define whether alternative assets are governed by states or federal regulators, with broad implications for derivatives platforms.

Key facts:
- Ninth Circuit unanimously ruled that Kalshi's sports event contracts are gambling under state law, not federally preempted financial swaps.
- The decision directly contradicts a Third Circuit ruling favoring Kalshi, creating a circuit split that virtually guarantees Supreme Court review.
- If upheld, states could impose gambling regulations—including licensing requirements, taxes, and consumer protections—on prediction market platforms.
