Stabbing on Flydubai flight to Israel: Netanyahu says too early to determine motive

In one sentence
A Flydubai co-pilot stabbed the captain on a Dubai–Tel Aviv flight; Netanyahu says motive is unknown as the airline suspends Israel flights and Israel plans to vet pilots.

A Flydubai passenger jet bound for Tel Aviv was diverted to Saudi Arabia on Wednesday after the co-pilot stabbed the captain in the cockpit, according to Israeli officials. The captain survived. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said it was too early to say why it happened, and the airline has suspended its flights to and from Israel while the case is investigated.

What happened

Flight FZ1073, a Boeing 737 Max 8 carrying 174 people, was about two hours out of Dubai when the attack took place. Netanyahu said the captain, Smit Machchhar, an Indian national, was stabbed and seriously wounded. He "fought back, resisted, opened the cockpit door, and enabled passengers and crew to overpower the attacker," Netanyahu said, calling him "a true hero."

Passengers' accounts, as relayed by Israeli officials and media, suggest the aircraft was in real trouble. One passenger, Yaniv Hayun, said he entered the cockpit and "grabbed the controls and pulled them back." Another, Asaf Rajuan, described running forward "to save your daughter's life, your family's life." US broadcasters reported that the jet lost thousands of feet of altitude. The aircraft landed at Tabuk in north-western Saudi Arabia, where authorities arrested the co-pilot.

Motive unknown

Netanyahu told CBS News that the attacker appeared to have meant to crash the plane. On motive, he said he did not know. He suggested the account of the passenger who entered the cockpit looked like an attempted suicide. Asked whether it might be a mental-health episode rather than terrorism, he said: "It could be, I don't know, we'll find out soon." An Omani official told CBS the suspect was born in Oman and holds Omani citizenship.

The official language is cautious on both sides. Flydubai called the incident an "altercation in the cockpit." The UAE's General Civil Aviation Authority said only that the flight "experienced a security incident that was brought under control." Nobody has yet publicly claimed that the attack was ideological, and the reporting so far does not settle the question. Past cases of pilots deliberately crashing aircraft, such as Germanwings in 2015, were attributed to the pilot's own state of mind and not to politics. That precedent is a reason to hold off on drawing conclusions, not a finding about this case.

Why the corridor matters

The Dubai–Tel Aviv route is one of the most visible products of the 2020 Abraham Accords, which normalised relations between Israel and the UAE. Direct flights were among the first practical benefits, linking two business hubs and carrying tourists, traders and investors. Whatever the motive turns out to be, the suspension removes a flagship link, at least for now.

Israel has also said it will vet the identities of all pilots before flights are allowed to land in the country. That is a tougher requirement than the routine one. It shifts security checks onto the crew, and a pilot is usually the last person an airline's screening is designed to doubt. Applied to Gulf carriers, it could add friction and cost to a route that depends on being easy to use.

The commercial stakes are less clear than the symbolic ones. Passenger numbers and the share of Gulf-Israeli trade that depends on this route are not in the early reporting, so any claim of damage to investor confidence is for now an inference. The test will be how quickly the investigation settles the question of motive. A personal crisis by one crew member would call for different remedies than a coordinated threat, and the response from regulators and insurers would differ accordingly.

What to watch

The first marker is the Saudi and Omani findings on the co-pilot, including any medical or background evidence. The second is whether Flydubai resumes Israel flights and under what vetting terms. Until the cause is known, the route's future depends on that answer.
