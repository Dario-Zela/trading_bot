Rival clubs call for Man City to be punished this season

In one sentence
Premier League clubs want any Man City sanction settled this season, but an appeal and a separate sanctions hearing mean the 115-charges saga has no firm end date.

The Premier League's long-running case against Manchester City has reached the point where its rivals want a date. Clubs are pressing for any sanction to be settled before the end of this season, so that a penalty could take effect from 2027-28 rather than being lost to further delay.

The pressure follows a ruling reported on 29 September. An independent commission is said to have found City guilty of almost all of the 115 charges, which cover alleged financial breaches between 2009 and 2018. NBC News reported the charges as including inflated sponsorship figures and undisclosed supplementary payments to staff. City said it was "disappointed and surprised", maintains its innocence and says it will appeal.

Guilt is not sanction

The verdict does not settle what happens next. Penalties are to be decided separately, and the range floated in the press runs from points deductions to something far harsher. Nothing has been imposed. An appeal would send the findings to a fresh panel and could reopen questions the league thought it had closed.

That is the source of the rivals' impatience. One club executive was quoted as saying: "We have all waited far too long, and it is high time that this whole thing came to an end." The same source said many clubs felt it would be "unacceptable, after all this time, for it to go beyond the end of the season."

The charges were first laid in February 2023. A few more months is, in that context, a modest request. It is also a pointed one.

A test of the league, not just City

The league's own rules offer some comfort to the impatient. Reports suggest the sanctions stage could close by the end of January, with any appeal against the penalty facing similar time limits. If that holds, a decision would land well before next summer.

But deadlines in this saga have a habit of being elastic, and City has the resources and the inclination to test every procedural door. The club has also argued before that the league's rules were unfairly constructed. A court challenge on process cannot be ruled out, and it would reset the clock.

That is the real tension. The clubs want sporting justice delivered within a season. The league is bound by legal process that moves at its own pace, and a penalty rushed through is a penalty that gets overturned. Neither side wants that outcome, though the rivals would take the risk more readily than the league.

The ruling has a second consequence. Some reports say it invites compensation claims from other clubs, which would add a civil front to a case that has so far been fought through the league's own tribunal.

What the market is watching

For the league's commercial partners and broadcasters, the issue is uncertainty rather than the outcome. A champion-calibre club with an unresolved cloud over its status complicates sponsorship and valuation talk, and bookmakers have no clean way to price a points deduction that may or may not arrive.

The next marker is the sanctions hearing and whether City files its appeal on schedule. If the January target for closing the case slips, the rivals' demand for a decision this season will be the first thing to go.
