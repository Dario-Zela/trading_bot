Vape prices rise as new UK duty takes effect

In one sentence
A £2.20-per-10ml duty on vaping liquid starts today in the UK, raising legal prices and testing whether revenue arrives or illicit supply fills the gap.

A new excise duty on vaping liquid takes effect in the UK today, adding a fixed charge to every bottle and pod of e-liquid sold legally. The tax is the first of its kind on vapes in Britain. It opens a price gap between suppliers who pay it and those who do not.

The Markets desk could not find a BBC figure for the price change. The numbers below come from HMRC's published rate and trade summaries, not from measured shelf prices.

What the duty does

Vaping Products Duty is charged at £2.20 per 10ml of liquid, or 22p per millilitre. HMRC says it is paid by manufacturers, importers and approved warehousekeepers. It is levied on volume rather than nicotine strength. A 0mg shortfill is therefore taxed the same as a 20mg nicotine-salt pod.

VAT is charged on top of the duty-inclusive price. Retail guides put the effective increase at about £2.64 per 10ml. That figure is a retailer-side calculation, not an official one. Devices, coils and empty refillable pods are outside the scope of the duty, so hardware prices should not move for this reason.

A 10ml bottle that sold for £3 to £5 before the duty is therefore likely to cost materially more. The duty is a flat charge, so the proportional rise is largest on the cheapest liquid. The desk has no verified before-and-after retail price series. Any claim of a specific percentage rise is unconfirmed.

The illicit-trade question

The policy's aim is to reduce the affordability and appeal of vaping, particularly to young people and non-smokers. Treasury expects it to raise more than £550m a year by 2030-31.

That forecast depends on legal volumes holding up. A flat per-millilitre charge pushes buyers who are sensitive to price toward untaxed product. No baseline for current illicit penetration was available to the desk, so the size of that shift cannot be estimated.

HMRC's counter is traceability. A duty stamps scheme has begun, with stamps starting to appear on retail packaging. They become mandatory for all vaping products on the market from 1 April 2027. HMRC argues that tobacco controls introduced alongside strong enforcement have reduced or held the illicit market's size. Vapes are a newer and more fragmented market than cigarettes, so that precedent may not carry over cleanly.

The gap between the October start and the April 2027 stamp deadline is the period in which unstamped product is hardest to tell from compliant product.

What to watch

For equity investors the direct exposure is small. The duty was announced in advance, and a rise that was long expected is unlikely to move consumer-staples or pharma stocks on its own. It would matter if legal volumes fell sharply or if illicit trade grew faster than regulators expect.

The first usable evidence is likely to be HMRC's receipts data in the coming months, set against the Treasury's £550m trajectory. Retail price surveys and trading-standards seizure reports will show whether the legal-illicit gap is widening. The 1 April 2027 stamp deadline is the next fixed date.
