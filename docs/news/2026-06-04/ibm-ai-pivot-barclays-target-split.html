IBM's 'stark message' and a bullish Barclays price target leave the AI services pivot unresolved

In one sentence
IBM's flat guidance despite a Q1 beat signalled that AI monetisation is harder than promised, while Barclays' $350 target backs the software-stickiness floor.

IBM beat Wall Street's first-quarter estimates by a comfortable margin in April — revenue of $15.92 billion, up 9.5% year-on-year, adjusted EPS of $1.91 against an expected $1.81 — and then watched its stock slide 6% after the bell anyway. The reason: management held full-year guidance flat. More than 5% constant-currency revenue growth and a $1 billion free cash flow increase, neither figure revised upward despite the Q1 outperformance. In a market that has spent two years pricing in AI transformation upside, leaving the ceiling where it was carries its own signal.

Analysts described the tone on the earnings call as a notable departure from the bullish AI framing that has defined IBM's investor communications since the generative-AI boom began. Consulting — the segment where AI projects actually touch client workflows — grew 1% in Q1. The company's generative-AI consulting pipeline exceeds $6 billion, and roughly 30% of the consulting backlog now carries an AI label. The gap between those two data points is the story: signed contracts exist, recognized revenue does not yet follow at scale. Integration costs from IBM's $11 billion Confluent acquisition are also pressing near-term margins.

Arvind Krishna acknowledged as much at IBM Think 2026 in May. "This is no longer about pilots," he told the conference. IBM's own research found that only about 25% of enterprise AI initiatives deliver expected ROI, and just 16% have scaled company-wide. The candor is useful, even if it makes the equity case harder to construct. Software — nearly half of IBM's revenue and the majority of its profit, growing 11% to $7.05 billion in Q1 — remains the cleaner story. Infrastructure showed a 15% jump, with the new z17 mainframe surging 51%.

Where Barclays sees the floor

One week after the earnings-driven selldown, Barclays launched coverage of IBM with an Overweight rating and a $350 twelve-month price target — against a stock that had fallen roughly 21.6% year-to-date and was trading near $232. Analyst Raimo Lenschow's base case rests not on AI consulting conversion but on the stickiness of IBM's infrastructure software installed at banks, insurers, and government agencies: clients who rarely rip out middleware once it's embedded. Barclays forecasts mid-single-digit organic revenue growth and continued margin expansion on that foundation alone. A $449 bull case layers in quantum computing optionality — IBM holds more than $1 billion in quantum-related contracts — though Lenschow's note is explicit that the $350 target survives even if the quantum bet never pays.

The IBM stock jumped 9% on the Barclays call, a move that partly reflects how far sentiment had retreated. The unresolved question is structural: the AI services pivot requires consulting backlog to convert into recurring software revenue, and the timeline for that conversion remains opaque. IBM's Q2 earnings on 22 July will be the first real test of whether consulting growth can accelerate toward the low-to-mid single digits management has guided. Whether Salesforce, SAP, and Oracle deliver similarly measured language on their own calls this quarter matters as much as the IBM-specific data — a pattern would shift the sector-wide valuation calculus for enterprise AI.
