AstraZeneca liver cancer trial shows positive survival signal, opening a potential approval pathway

AstraZeneca's Cambridge headquarters, where the EMERALD-3 liver cancer data now frames a regulatory strategy conversation with global agencies — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
AstraZeneca's EMERALD-3 trial met its PFS endpoint in embolization-eligible liver cancer, with an OS trend prompting regulatory discussions in a high-unmet-need market.

A Phase III study in embolization-eligible hepatocellular carcinoma has produced results that AstraZeneca says justify initiating regulatory discussions: the company's Imfinzi and Imjudo combination, added to lenvatinib and transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), cut the risk of disease progression or death by 30% compared with TACE alone.

The data were presented on 1 June at the American Society of Clinical Oncology annual meeting in Chicago, drawing attention to an earlier disease setting where standard of care has barely shifted in two decades.

AstraZeneca's four-drug combination met its primary endpoint in the EMERALD-3 trial, showing a 30% reduction in disease progression risk versus TACE alone in embolization-eligible liver cancer — though the overall survival benefit remains a trend, not yet statistically significant.

An earlier battleground

Hepatocellular carcinoma accounts for more than 80% of all primary liver cancer diagnoses. Most patients present at advanced stages, but a meaningful subset — those with unresectable disease who nonetheless retain preserved liver function — remain candidates for embolization, a technique in which chemotherapy is delivered directly into hepatic arteries to deprive tumors of blood supply. This intermediate-stage population has had limited access to systemic therapy; TACE has been standard of care since the early 2000s, and efforts to layer in targeted agents have, until recently, produced mostly neutral results.

"Early-stage HCC hasn't seen any shift and adoption of new systemic therapy for decades," one expert commentary noted at the ASCO presentation — a characterization that frames the EMERALD-3 study as an attempt to close a conspicuous gap.

AstraZeneca already holds approvals for Imfinzi (durvalumab, a PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitor) in advanced, unresectable HCC — as a monotherapy in Japan and the EU, and in combination with Imjudo (tremelimumab, an anti-CTLA-4 antibody) in several major markets. EMERALD-3 targets patients upstream of that: those whose tumors cannot be surgically removed but whose disease is still confined enough to qualify for embolization and who have received no prior systemic therapy.

What the numbers show — and what they don't yet

The EMERALD-3 trial enrolled 760 patients across 171 centres in 22 countries, randomised 1:1:1 between three arms: the STRIDE regimen (a single priming dose of tremelimumab 300 mg plus durvalumab 1,500 mg every four weeks) combined with lenvatinib and TACE; STRIDE plus TACE without lenvatinib; or TACE alone. The quadruplet combination was the primary comparator.

On the primary endpoint — progression-free survival — the four-drug arm delivered a median of 13 months against 9.8 months for TACE alone. AstraZeneca described the result as statistically significant and clinically meaningful.

The overall survival picture is more cautious. At the pre-specified interim analysis, the quadruplet arm showed a favorable trend: a two-year OS rate of 66.9% versus 61.5% for TACE alone, translating to a hazard ratio of 0.84 (95% CI 0.65–1.09; p=0.1814). That HR is directionally encouraging, but the p-value falls well short of statistical significance, and AstraZeneca was explicit that the trial will continue collecting OS data — the final analysis on survival remains outstanding.

Dr Ghassan Abou-Alfa of Memorial Sloan Kettering, principal investigator for EMERALD-3, called the dual immunotherapy approach "a meaningful advance for embolization-eligible liver cancer patients." Susan Galbraith, AstraZeneca's executive vice president for oncology research and development, said the data showed that "bringing dual immunotherapy earlier with TACE and lenvatinib improves outcomes in earlier-stage liver cancer."

No new safety signals were identified; the tolerability profile was consistent with known drug-class effects.

The regulatory and commercial path ahead

AstraZeneca said it would "have discussions" with regulators globally on potential regulatory submissions — language that signals intent without committing to a timeline or jurisdiction. Whether the PFS data alone will support a label in embolization-eligible HCC depends in part on agency posture: the FDA and EMA have, in some oncology settings, accepted PFS as a primary endpoint for accelerated or conditional approvals, though in liver cancer the regulatory record is mixed. How mature the OS read becomes before formal dossiers are submitted will likely be the determining variable.

Commercially, the franchise behind EMERALD-3 is already substantial. Imfinzi generated approximately $6 billion in 2025 sales — growth of roughly 28% year-on-year — while Imjudo contributed around $346 million at 23% growth. An HCC approval in a new patient population would extend the Imfinzi label into a setting where, until now, systemic treatment has been essentially unavailable.

The broader market context supports the ambition: the global HCC treatment market is estimated at around $5.2 billion in 2026, with several forecasters projecting it to approach $9.4 billion by 2033 as immunotherapy penetration widens and earlier lines of treatment are addressed.

The next concrete catalyst will be AstraZeneca's formal regulatory interactions — with the FDA and EMA chief among them — which should clarify whether the EMERALD-3 PFS data can anchor a submission or whether the agency will hold out for a statistically significant OS read. The trial remains ongoing, and survival data continue to mature.
