HSBC raises Broadcom target on custom AI chip demand; Cisco and Alibaba also flagged

Broadcom's India office in Hyderabad; the company holds an estimated 70% share of the custom AI accelerator design market. — Fostera12 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
HSBC raised Broadcom's target to $600, projecting $100B in custom AI chip revenue for FY2027 — 26% above Street consensus — on deals with Google, Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI.

HSBC raised its Broadcom price target from $450 to $600 on June 2, a 33% revision that puts the investment bank's estimates firmly at odds with Wall Street consensus. The firm, led by analyst Frank Lee, kept its Buy rating and lifted its fiscal 2026 ASIC revenue estimate to $46 billion — 23% above the Street — and its fiscal 2027 figure to $100.2 billion, 26% above consensus.

The thesis rests on a maturing pipeline of custom AI accelerator contracts. Google's TPU v7 chips are beginning to ship, under a supply agreement that runs through 2031. Meta's MTIA accelerator is ramping. Anthropic has signed a multi-year agreement with deployments starting in the second half of fiscal 2026, and OpenAI is expected to begin taking volume in fiscal 2027. "We expect ASIC revenue momentum to start materially ramping through 2H FY26 into FY27 and beyond," Lee wrote.

Broadcom's own guidance already signalled the direction. CEO Hock Tan told investors the company expects AI semiconductor revenue of $10.7 billion in Q2 — a figure that, if met, would represent 140% growth year over year.

The stock has responded: AVGO is up roughly 95% over the past year and trading near all-time highs around $488. Whether the HSBC revisions prove prescient depends substantially on manufacturing execution. HSBC projects Broadcom will require 260,000 CoWoS packaging wafers in fiscal 2026, rising to 480,000 in fiscal 2027. TSMC's advanced packaging capacity has been a structural constraint on AI chip output since 2024; Broadcom currently draws an estimated 15% of global CoWoS supply, competing directly with Nvidia, which holds roughly 60%. Any shortfall at the packaging stage would compress delivery timelines regardless of design wins.

Three plays on the same theme

The HSBC note arrives as a broader analyst consensus is shaping up around the idea that AI capital expenditure will stay elevated longer than near-term models suggest — but that it will flow unevenly. A Zacks summary this week named Broadcom, Cisco, and Alibaba as distinct focus stocks under that thesis, each capturing a different layer of the infrastructure stack.

Cisco's angle is networking. The company has raised its expected fiscal 2026 AI infrastructure orders from $5 billion to $9 billion, with $2.1 billion booked from hyperscalers in Q3 alone. Revenue recognition from those orders — now guided to $4 billion for the full year — is the number to watch.

Alibaba represents a more complex read. The Chinese internet company announced a RMB380 billion ($52 billion) commitment to AI and cloud infrastructure over three years and reported cloud revenue up 38%, with AI-related products now accounting for roughly 30% of external cloud revenue. But US-China trade friction remains a material overlay on any valuation work, and negative free cash flow — RMB46.6 billion for the fiscal year — indicates that the spending cycle has yet to produce proportionate returns.

For Broadcom specifically, the next hard data point is Q2 earnings, which will either validate or complicate HSBC's aggressive revenue projections. A consensus-beating print on AI semiconductor revenue, combined with forward guidance above the $28.6 billion Q3 consensus estimate, would give Lee's $100 billion FY27 thesis its first real footing.
