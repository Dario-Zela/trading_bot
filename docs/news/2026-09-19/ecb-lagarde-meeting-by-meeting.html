ECB's Lagarde rules out near-term cut, says hikes will be 'meeting by meeting'

ECB president Christine Lagarde, who said in Dublin that a near-term rate cut is "very unlikely at the moment." — European Union, 2023 / European Parliament (CC BY 4.0)
In one sentence
Lagarde ruled out a near-term ECB rate cut after this month's hike to 2.50%, insisting decisions stay strictly "meeting by meeting."

Christine Lagarde spent Thursday's meeting calling a rate rise a "no brainer." Speaking in Dublin a week later, the European Central Bank president made sure nobody mistook that confidence for an appetite to reverse course.

In an interview with RTÉ at Dublin Castle on September 18th, Lagarde said the ECB would keep deciding rates "meeting by meeting," and that cutting borrowing costs anytime soon "is very unlikely at the moment." It was a pointed message to markets that had been probing for signs of relief after the ECB's deposit rate reached 2.50% — its second increase this year, following a hike in June and a pause in July.

Why the ECB is still hiking

The move to 2.50% was driven by inflation that has proven stickier than the Governing Council expected. Eurozone inflation hit 3.3% in August, with energy prices — inflated by the ongoing war involving Iran and its effect on oil markets — climbing 14.3% on the year. The ECB left its 2026 inflation forecast at 3.0% but pushed its 2027 and 2028 projections higher, to 2.5% and 2.1% respectively, both still above the bank's 2% target. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, is now seen averaging 2.5% this year.

Lagarde described the September vote as unanimous, and told reporters at the time that the increase was straightforward given the data in front of the Council. In Dublin, she added that the euro area's growth picture had also brightened: "growth is a bit more promising than we thought," she said, while adding that policymakers are "not seeing second-round effects yet" — the wage-price spiral dynamic that would force a more aggressive response.

No pre-commitment, in either direction

That combination — firmer growth, inflation still running hot, but no sign of a self-reinforcing spiral — is what leaves the ECB unwilling to lock in a path. Lagarde said the Council would keep weighing three factors at each meeting: the inflation outlook, underlying inflation, and how monetary policy is transmitting into the real economy. Any further increase, she said, "will depend on the future."

Markets had priced some chance of the ECB pivoting toward cuts once rates crossed 2.5%, treating the level as a plausible ceiling. Lagarde's Dublin remarks closed that door for now without opening a new one toward further tightening — a deliberately narrow message. Currency traders shrugged; the euro's reaction was muted, with analysts at FXStreet noting her comments scored well below her historical average for market impact, since she largely restated the position from the September 10th press conference rather than breaking new ground.

What's next

The next scheduled Governing Council meeting will be the first real test of the "meeting by meeting" pledge, with updated inflation and growth projections likely to hinge on how far Middle East energy costs keep feeding through to consumer prices. Until then, the ECB's message to markets is simple: 2.50% is not obviously the top, and it is certainly not yet the pivot.
