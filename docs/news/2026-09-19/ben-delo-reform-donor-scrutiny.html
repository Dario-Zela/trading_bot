Reform donor Ben Delo's £350k loan to bullying-linked academic under scrutiny

Ben Delo speaking at a Bitcoin industry conference in London, October 2018. — Anne Schwarz Photography / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
A Reform UK mega-donor's £350k loan to a writer found by a judge to have bullied her opponent online has raised questions about his vetting of causes he backs.

Ben Delo's rise from Bitcoin exchange founder to Reform UK's single biggest financial backer has, until now, been a story about numbers: £4m in the spring, another £4m in early September, then £36m — the largest donation to a British political party in history. This week the story acquired a different kind of number: £350,000, the size of a personal loan Delo made to the writer and former academic Nina Power, whose conduct a High Court judge later found included the "whiff of threat and antisemitism."

The loan, first reported by the Guardian, was made on commercial terms — secured against a London property Power owns, with a repayment schedule — weeks before a 2023 defamation trial in which Power and her co-claimant, the writer Daniel DC Miller, sued the performance artist Luke Turner. They alleged he had wrongly branded them antisemitic; he counter-sued for harassment. Power's conditional fee arrangement with her lawyers had just collapsed, leaving her without funding days before the case opened.

In one sentence: A major Reform UK donor's £350k loan to a writer found by a judge to have bullied and menaced her opponent online has put his charitable vetting under scrutiny.

What the judge actually found

Both sides lost. Mr Justice Collins Rice ruled that Turner's online following was too small for Power and Miller's libel claim to clear the "serious harm" bar the law requires. But the harassment counter-claim fared little better for Turner: the judge found Power and Miller had deployed the "full lexicon of Twitter trolling" against him, including posts carrying "the whiff of threat and antisemitism which they themselves can fairly be held to have created" — but ruled the conduct "too occasional, intangible, diffuse and unconsolidated" to meet harassment's higher, quasi-criminal threshold. She also found no overt antisemitism in their original claims against Turner. Costs followed the losses: Turner was awarded roughly £1m, some 80% of the total, and Power was declared bankrupt.

It is understood Delo did not, and does not, believe Power and Miller held the antisemitic views Turner alleged, and says he would never knowingly fund anyone who did. Both Power and Delo declined to comment further to the Guardian.

Why a Reform donor's ledger matters

Delo, a Sheffield-born mathematician who co-founded the crypto exchange BitMEX and was pardoned by Donald Trump in 2025 over a US anti-money-laundering conviction, has built a philanthropic profile alongside his political one — a Giving Pledge signature, an autism charity, a maths institute fellowship. He has also been a known backer of the Free Speech Union, the Toby Young-led campaign group, and of causes broadly badged anti-woke. The Power loan sits at the intersection of the two: a private, commercial transaction that happens to have funded one side of a case a judge found distinctly unflattering to that side's conduct.

None of this implicates Delo in the underlying dispute — the loan was collateralised, not a gift, and long predates his emergence as Reform's patron. But as the party's finances become increasingly dependent on one man's cheque book, journalists and rivals alike will keep asking who else has been in his address book, and what, if anything, he checked before signing.

What's next

Reform UK has not commented on the loan, and neither Delo nor Power has addressed the vetting question directly. With Delo's £36m gift still working through the Electoral Commission's disclosure process, expect further digging into the donor's wider network before that filing is finalised.
