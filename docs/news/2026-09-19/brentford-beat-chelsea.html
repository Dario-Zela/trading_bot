Brentford beat Chelsea 3-0, stay unbeaten in the Premier League

Brentford striker Igor Thiago, who scored in the 3-0 win, arriving at the Gtech Community Stadium. — Timmy96 / Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
In one sentence
Brentford thrashed Chelsea 3-0 to reach third in the Premier League, extending their unbeaten start to five games as Chelsea's winless run reached three matches.

Brentford beat Chelsea 3-0 at the Gtech Community Stadium on Friday night, a result that says less about Brentford's talent — considerable as it is — than about how far Chelsea have drifted from the club that finished the last two seasons pushing for Europe.

The scoreline flattered no one. Brentford were patient and largely unthreatening for an hour, then scored three times in twenty-odd minutes as Chelsea's defending fell apart. Jaidon Anthony opened the scoring in the 61st minute from a set piece, converting after Chelsea failed to deal with a corner. Igor Thiago doubled the lead soon after, and Fábio Carvalho added a third in stoppage time to turn a comfortable win into a rout.

"It was a really good evening — it's important that you enjoy evenings like this because you work so hard," said Brentford manager Keith Andrews afterwards. "The second goal was crucial obviously. The first goal was from a set piece."

A record five games in the making

The win moves Brentford to third in the Premier League on nine points from five matches — two wins and three draws — three points behind co-leaders Arsenal and Manchester City. It is the club's best start to a top-flight campaign, the first time Brentford have gone unbeaten through their opening five league games.

Defender Jannik Schuster, who helped keep the clean sheet, credited a change in approach after the interval: "When the second half began we played it more directly forward and we scored after that, so I'm just happy. I don't care about my performance — I just want to help the team, get the clean sheet and the three points."

Chelsea's stall continues

For Chelsea, the defeat extends a winless run to three league matches and drops them to seventh with seven points from five games — already six adrift of the pace at the top. Manager Enzo Maresca fielded a side featuring the first league starts for two experienced additions, Jordan Henderson and Danny Welbeck, a selection that did little to steady a defence that had already looked shaky before Friday's collapse.

The pattern is becoming familiar: Chelsea competitive for spells, then undone by moments of individual and collective carelessness — the kind of set-piece marking that gifted Anthony his opener being the clearest example. Three matches without a win is not yet a crisis, but for a club that has spent heavily to close the gap on the league's top tier, it is the sort of run that invites scrutiny.

What's next

Brentford travel to face one of the two co-leaders before October, a fixture that will test whether their start reflects genuine title-race credentials or a favourable run of opposition. Chelsea, meanwhile, have a fortnight before their next league fixture to address defensive lapses that have now cost them results in back-to-back matches — the sort of gap a top-four campaign cannot easily absorb.
