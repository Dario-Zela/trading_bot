FTSE 100 slips Friday but on track for best week since July

The London Stock Exchange building in Paternoster Square, City of London. — London Stock Exchange / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
The FTSE 100 fell 0.6% Friday on bank and energy weakness but was still headed for its strongest week since late July.

London's blue-chip index gave back some ground on Friday, but the week it leaves behind is the best the FTSE 100 has had since late July.

The index fell 0.6% to 10,751.57 points by mid-morning trading, pulled down by banks and energy stocks after Thursday's 1.2% surge had briefly lifted it to a one-week high. Lloyds dropped 1.2%, HSBC slipped 0.6%, and the wider banking sector lost 0.7%. Energy names fell 0.8% as Brent crude extended a third straight day of declines, down $1.65 to $103.17 a barrel, easing some of the supply anxiety that has gripped oil markets since Strait of Hormuz shipping tightened — only four tankers transited the strait on Thursday, against a typical rate of sixteen over ten days.

Even with Friday's retreat, the FTSE 100 was on track for its biggest weekly rise since late July. The midcap FTSE 250, more exposed to the domestic economy, was headed for its sharpest weekly gain since early August.

A week of central bank noise

The gains came in a week thick with monetary policy decisions. The Bank of England held its rate at 3.75% on Thursday, but the vote split 6-3, with three MPC members pushing for an immediate quarter-point increase. Governor Andrew Bailey signalled the door remains open to tightening if inflation pressure persists — a stance Barclays and J.P. Morgan read as consistent with a hike in November. The Federal Reserve raised its own rate by 25 basis points to a 3.75%-4.00% range, and the Bank of Japan lifted rates to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years. Against that backdrop, British consumer spending data surprised to the upside in August, even as households pulled back on fuel purchases.

Rotation, not retreat

Friday's weakness looked more like sector rotation than a broad loss of nerve. Telecoms were the day's worst performer, down 4.3%, dragged lower by Airtel Africa's 8.8% slide after reports the group was scaling back the size of a planned share offering. Precious metals miners moved the other way, up 1.6% as gold continued its climb. Among individual names, Softcat fell 2.8% to the bottom of the FTSE 250 after unveiling a $1.05 billion deal to acquire the US IT reseller GDT — a reminder that Friday's index-level move obscured plenty of stock-specific noise underneath.

That pattern — a soft headline number built on a narrow set of sore spots — is why the week's overall gain matters more than the day's dip. Banks and energy account for a large share of the FTSE 100's weighting, so when both wobble at once the index print looks worse than the average constituent's week actually was. Strip those two sectors out and the rally that has carried the index toward its best week since July looks considerably more intact.

What's next

The near-term test is whether oil keeps falling. A sustained retreat in crude would help offset the inflation risk that pushed three BoE members to vote for a hike this week, but it also weighs directly on the FTSE's heavily-weighted energy majors. Barclays and J.P. Morgan's call for a November BoE move puts the next Monetary Policy Committee meeting squarely in view, and any further easing in Hormuz shipping tensions — or a flare-up — will move both the crude price and the index in short order.
