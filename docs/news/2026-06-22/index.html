HEALTH · FDA · By Dr. P. Aaronson

GSK and Spero win FDA approval for the first oral carbapenem antibiotic

The FDA's approval of Utebzi marks the first time a carbapenem-class antibiotic—previously confined to intravenous delivery—is available in pill form, reshaping treatment options for serious urinary-tract infections and opening a new commercial lane for GSK.

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved Utebzi (tebipenem pivoxil hydrobromide), an oral antibiotic for complicated urinary tract infections in adults. GSK plc and Spero Therapeutics developed the drug jointly—the first oral antibiotic in the carbapenem class. Carbapenems, typically reserved for severe infections, have historically required hospital-based intravenous administration.

Complicated UTIs account for roughly 250,000 hospitalizations annually in the United States. An oral option could reshape treatment patterns by eliminating the traditional choice between hospital-based IV therapy and treatment delays. The approval expands a narrow category: oral antibiotics potent enough for serious infections.

The shift reflects a strategic recalibration in antibiotic development—bringing a hospital-dependent drug class into outpatient and community settings. Adoption patterns will indicate whether carbapenems, now accessible orally, gain traction beyond UTIs as resistance to existing antibiotics continues to mount.
