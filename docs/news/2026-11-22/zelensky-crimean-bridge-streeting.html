Zelensky calls Crimean Bridge strikes a 'fair response' as he meets Streeting

In one sentence
Zelensky calls Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge a 'fair response' to Russian attacks, and UK defence secretary Wes Streeting has publicly welcomed them.

Ukraine's president has described his country's strikes on the Crimean Bridge as a "fair response" to Russian attacks, and the remark came as he held talks with the UK's defence secretary, Wes Streeting. The timing matters. Kyiv is telling a close ally, in person, how it wants the most symbolic target of the war to be read.

The accounts available differ on dates. The strikes themselves, and Streeting's public welcome for them, date from 17 November. This article could not confirm the date of the meeting reported under the BBC headline, or the exact context of the "fair response" wording, which Zelensky has also used about Ukraine's long-range campaign since June.

What was hit

The bridge carries road and rail traffic between Russia and occupied Crimea, and has been a recurring target since the 2022 attack on it. Ukrainian accounts say the latest operation was the product of weeks of preparation. The Security Service of Ukraine said it spent a month working with the Defence Forces to destroy Russian air defences around Kerch before the direct attack.

According to Ukrainian reporting, at least 15 strikes near Kerch brought down an arch and part of the roadway span, closing the crossing to road traffic. This was the second hit on the bridge in 24 hours. Kyiv's General Staff said the collapse halted road traffic to Crimea. These figures come from Ukrainian officials and have not been independently verified. No casualty figures were available.

The bridge was not the only target. Ukraine says it has hit at least six bridges on a Russian logistics route since early October, affecting links from Russia's Rostov region to occupied southern Ukraine. The Kerch strike looks less like a one-off gesture than the culmination of a campaign against supply lines.

How Kyiv frames it

Zelensky's argument is one of reciprocity. Russia, he said, has struck Ukraine's bridges and infrastructure many times, and Ukraine is answering in kind: "I believe this is fair." Reuters reported him saying the operation was a response to Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure. The framing is deliberate. Strikes on civilian-use infrastructure invite questions from allies who fund and arm Ukraine, and casting them as proportionate retaliation pre-empts those questions.

The bridge also carries military weight. Crimea is a hub for Russian forces in the south, and cutting or degrading the road link forces more traffic onto other routes. That is the logic of the wider bridge campaign.

London's response

Streeting, speaking in Brussels on 17 November ahead of a Ukraine Defence Contact Group meeting, offered no reservations. He credited "Ukrainian ingenuity and Ukraine's absolute determination to defend itself against Russian aggression" and said it had been "a terrible year for Vladimir Putin", with Russian losses "unacceptably high" and little territory gained in return.

He said the UK was preparing new measures to strengthen Ukrainian air defences, though no size, type or timing was given. He also said he expected several allies to announce fresh aid at the Brussels meeting, which aims to help Ukraine through the winter and begin planning military support for 2027. On diplomacy, he said any peace agreement must align with Kyiv's interests and cannot be decided without Ukraine.

So the messaging appears to be landing, at least in London. A British minister endorsing a strike on the bridge is a sign that Kyiv's retaliation framing carries weight with a G7 partner. It is not evidence that every ally agrees. Public support from the UK has tended to run ahead of some other capitals on questions of deep strikes.

What to watch

Three things will show how far this goes. The first is whether the UK puts a number and a date on its air defence package, since Ukraine's ability to hold its own skies is what limits Russian retaliation against Ukrainian infrastructure. The second is whether other allies at the contact group echo Streeting's tone or hedge. The third is how quickly Russia repairs or bypasses the span, which will show whether the strikes changed the logistics picture in the south or only the symbolism.
