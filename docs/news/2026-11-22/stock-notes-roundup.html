Stock notes: Anthropic's earlier slide, Vodafone, UK dividend screens and JELD-WEN

In one sentence
A five-week-old tech sell-off page is being offered as today's news on Anthropic shares, and the valuation items alongside it are model outputs or advice rather than verified news.

A web page dated 15 October 2026 is circulating as if it were today's news about Anthropic shares. It is not today's news. The link is five weeks old, and the claim attached to it may not be checkable at all.

The stale sell-off

The cited page, a syndicated report on a global technology sell-off, carries a 15 October date in its URL. Its slug reads "global tech selloff deepens as enthusiasm for AI stocks wobbles". That refers to AI stocks in general. It does not name Anthropic.

The pitch also contains a more basic problem. Anthropic is not known to be publicly listed, so "Anthropic shares" would need a primary source before anyone writes the phrase as fact. Possible explanations include a loose headline, a reference to listed companies with exposure to the company, or a secondary-market valuation. None of these is established. The triage note itself concedes that today's market moves could not be verified.

What can be said is narrow. Technology stocks sold off in mid-October on doubts about AI enthusiasm, according to that page's own framing. Nothing in hand shows what markets did on 22 November. A fresh, same-day report from the BBC or another primary outlet would be needed to treat this as a live story.

Vodafone and the dividend screens

The remaining items are valuation commentary rather than news.

A Motley Fool explainer offers takeaways on Vodafone for long-term investors, following the company's recent restructuring. The specifics of that restructuring were not supplied, so there is no hook here beyond general advice to hold for the long term.

Simply Wall St's November screen of British dividend stocks says some names trade as much as 43% below cash-flow value. That figure is an output of the platform's own discounted cash flow model, not an observed price or an analyst target. Its inputs were not checked. A discounted cash flow estimate moves with its assumptions about growth and discount rates, so a wide gap to "value" says as much about the model as about the shares. The company names were not reviewed and are not listed here.

JELD-WEN

The same platform says its fair value estimate for JELD-WEN, the building-products maker, has fallen as analysts turn more cautious. No old or new estimate, size of revision or date was supplied. Without them the note records a model recalculating after lower analyst forecasts. It does not point to a company or sector event.

What ties them together

All four items rest on valuation claims that cannot be verified from what was provided. One is a stale link, two are model outputs and one is an explainer without a hook. The test is the same each time: a dated, primary source. For Anthropic, that means a same-day market report, or confirmation that a tradable security exists. For the screens and JELD-WEN, it means the underlying article figures and the analyst revisions behind them. Until those arrive, none of the four supports a claim about where markets stand today.
