England face the All Blacks looking to end their boom-bust cycle; Wales lose to Australia

In one sentence
England faced a wounded All Blacks side at Twickenham on 21 November, seeking to show their swings between good and bad form can be steadied, while Wales lost to Australia.

England go into the match against New Zealand with a question that has hung over Steve Borthwick's side for a year: which England turns up? The team was poor in the Six Nations, then good, then poor again. Against the All Blacks at Twickenham on Saturday 21 November (2.10pm kick-off), they had a chance to show the swings can be flattened.

This piece was assembled from pre-match reporting. It does not contain the result of the England game, and it gives no score for Wales's defeat by Australia, which could not be confirmed.

A cycle of boom and bust

The evidence for the label is easy to list. England had their worst Six Nations campaign earlier this year. Since then they have won four Tests in a row, according to AFP's preview. That run includes a victory over Australia in the autumn opener, reported as 45-22, and a 13-try win over Japan in which Will Joseph scored five tries, equalling the England record for a single match.

The pattern is not new. Last November England beat New Zealand 33-19 at Twickenham, their first home win over the All Blacks since 2012, and went on a ten-match winning run. The year before, they lost 24-22 to the same opponents after a late Marcus Smith penalty hit the post. The distance between England's best and worst has been wide for some time.

The selection

Borthwick has named what the Irish Times called the strongest available team, with nine changes from the Japan game. Fin Smith returns at fly-half and Ben Curry starts at openside. Ollie Chessum, Seb Atkinson and George Furbank come back from injury. Maro Itoje captains the side.

Joseph keeps his place on the left wing after the Japan game, in place of the injured Immanuel Feyi-Waboso. Fin Baxter, Jamie George and Joe Heyes start in the front row.

Borthwick framed the occasion in terms of emotion. "Emotion is a key part of the game," he said, adding: "To play against New Zealand, you have to go that bit further." Eddie Jones, who coached England until 2022, predicted a "violent" response from the All Blacks.

The visitors arrive stung. New Zealand lost 35-28 to Wales in Cardiff the week before, which the AFP report described as their first defeat by Wales in 73 years. A few weeks ago the All Blacks would have been overwhelming favourites. Several previews now make England narrow favourites, though Planet Rugby's pick was by only three points.

Wales's harder autumn

That Cardiff result is the best moment of Wales's autumn so far. The defeat by Australia that followed leaves them with a harder run. Specific details of the Australia match, including the score and venue, were not available in the sources checked for this piece.

What to watch

The England result is the next data point. A win would extend the winning run to five and would make the case that the six-month recovery is real. A loss, coming after the Six Nations, would give the boom-bust label more weight. Previews said the outcome also affects who reaches the Nations Championship final.
