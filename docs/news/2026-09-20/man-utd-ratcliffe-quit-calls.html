Man Utd Muslim Supporters' Club calls on Ratcliffe to quit over 'colonised' remarks

Sir Jim Ratcliffe, Manchester United's INEOS co-owner, who controls the club's football operations. — Science History Institute / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
Man Utd's Muslim Supporters' Club demanded Ratcliffe's exit after he doubled down, rather than retreated, on his 'colonised by immigrants' remarks in a BBC interview.

Manchester United's Muslim Supporters' Club has renewed its call for Sir Jim Ratcliffe to leave the club, after the INEOS billionaire used a BBC interview to reaffirm rather than walk back remarks about immigration that first caused an uproar in February.

In the BBC's 'Big Boss' interview this week, Ratcliffe said "nobody's tough enough to deal with the immigration problem, nobody's tough enough to deal with the benefits problem," and argued the country "is on the slide" as a result — adding, for good measure, that Britain remains "good, honest folk" who "won two world wars." It was the same interview in which he said he had "no confidence in the UK" and warned of a possible winter gas shortfall.

The comments land seven months after Ratcliffe told Sky News the UK had been "colonised by immigrants, really, hasn't it?" — a remark that drew condemnation from Prime Minister Keir Starmer and forced an apology "if" the language had caused offence. That February apology was always a conditional one, and Wednesday's interview made clear it was not a retreat from the underlying view.

'Deeply disappointing'

Manchester United Muslim Supporters' Club (MUMSC), which had already criticised the February remarks, called the new comments "deeply disappointing and completely unacceptable." The group argued the club's strength comes from a workforce and fanbase drawn from "different countries, cultures, ethnicities, religions and backgrounds," and escalated from criticism to an outright demand for change: "The Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe must go."

The statement framed the issue as bigger than results on the pitch — "bigger than one defeat, bigger than one manager, bigger than one season" — but the timing is hardly flattering. United have been knocked out of the League Cup by Brighton and lost to Manchester City this season, and fans have already unfurled banners reading "the way you treat us is a disgrace" at recent matches.

Leverage, tested

Ratcliffe holds a 27.7% stake in United and, under his 2024 deal with the Glazer family, controls football operations with two board seats — enough influence to shape the club's direction, not enough to be forced out by a supporters' group alone. The Glazers retain majority ownership and have shown no appetite for a sale. MUMSC's demand is a test of whether fan pressure can move an ownership structure that has weathered years of it, from the Green and Gold protests to this year's immigration row, largely undisturbed.

What happens next likely depends less on Old Trafford than on Westminster: Starmer has already weighed in once, and a co-owner of England's best-supported club doubling down on immigration rhetoric, rather than distancing himself from it, is the kind of story that tends to draw political comment before it draws boardroom consequences.
