ASML's 11,000% run sparks chip-equipment maturation debate as fund managers build new stakes

ASML's headquarters campus in Veldhoven, Netherlands — home to the sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet lithography machines for leading-edge chip production worldwide. — A ansems / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
In one sentence
ASML's ~11,000% 15-year run is drawing fresh institutional money—Coatue built a $655M stake—even as a 48x earnings multiple raises the cost of any cycle misstep.

Few industrial companies can claim to have quietly doubled the returns of any big-name tech stock over a decade and a half, but ASML — the Dutch monopoly that makes the only machines capable of printing leading-edge chips — has done precisely that. Long-run holders who bought around mid-2011 are looking at roughly 11,000% in total returns; the company's Veldhoven campus has become one of the most valuable pieces of industrial real estate on earth. That historical run is now prompting a pointed question in semiconductor circles: is this the maturation inflection that ends every supercycle, or is there still a long way to go?

The company gave investors little reason to panic in its April earnings release. First-quarter 2026 revenue came in at €8.8 billion, up 13.2% year-on-year, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to €36–40 billion — implying up to 22% annual growth. TSMC's own capital expenditure plan for 2026, set at $52–56 billion, runs almost entirely through ASML's order books. Demand for High-NA EUV machines — the next-generation tools priced at $350–400 million each — is queuing up for high-volume manufacturing adoption in 2027–28, with the biggest capex wave from newly filling cleanrooms pencilled in for 2028–29. On paper, the cycle looks extended, not exhausted.

The $655 million signal

Billionaire hedge fund manager Philippe Laffont seems to have read the same set of results. Coatue Management's 13F filing, submitted to the SEC on 15 May, disclosed a brand-new $655.4 million stake in ASML — 496,234 shares acquired in the first quarter. The position arrived alongside a 79% increase in Coatue's Applied Materials holding, and a further trim of Nvidia: the eleventh reduction in twelve quarters. The pattern is hard to misread. Laffont is rotating down the semiconductor supply chain, away from dominant chip designers and toward the equipment makers who supply the picks and shovels, treating ASML's EUV monopoly as a structural chokepoint in the AI infrastructure buildout.

UBS, for what it's worth, agrees: the Swiss bank declared ASML the top chip stock in Europe, and 22 of 28 sell-side analysts covering the company maintain a Strong Buy rating.

The maturation case

Yet the counter-argument is not easily dismissed. ASML now trades at roughly 46–48 times trailing earnings — well above its 15-year average — which means any slip in order timing, margin delivery, or guidance credibility gives the market a straightforward reason to de-rate. Jefferies flagged the tension explicitly after Q1: even as earnings estimates drift higher, the multiple could compress enough to offset the fundamental improvement in the share price.

The China exposure adds a second vector of risk. Chinese customers accounted for 33% of 2025 revenues; that share is expected to fall toward 20% under current export-control assumptions, and proposed US legislation that would extend restrictions to older DUV tools could force a guidance reset entirely. High-NA EUV, meanwhile, carries higher unit revenue but lower initial margins, as manufacturing costs are still being optimised.

What actually ends a supercycle is rarely one clean data point. It tends to be the gradual accumulation of lengthening delivery cadences, margin pressure on new tool generations, and a customer base that has largely completed its current node migration. The bear case is that ASML is approaching that inflection; the bull case — which Laffont's $655 million is now effectively underwriting — is that High-NA EUV represents an entirely new upgrade cycle with a peak still years away.

The clearest test arrives in ASML's Q2 2026 earnings report: order intake and gross-margin guidance on High-NA shipments will either sustain the bull narrative or give the maturation debate its sharpest edge yet.
