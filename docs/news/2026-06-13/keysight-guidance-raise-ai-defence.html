Keysight Technologies surges on guidance raise as AI and defence test-equipment orders converge

A Keysight InfiniiVision oscilloscope; demand for precision measurement instruments is being driven simultaneously by AI datacentre and defence electronics customers — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Keysight's record $2bn order quarter — up 56% year on year — signals defence electronics and AI datacentre buildouts are accelerating simultaneously, competing for the same test equipment.

Measurement equipment rarely makes headlines. Keysight Technologies' fiscal second quarter changed that. The Santa Rosa, California company — spun off from Agilent in 2014 and best known for oscilloscopes, spectrum analysers, and network emulators — reported orders of $2.05 billion in the three months to April, up 56% year on year and a record in the company's history. Revenue came in at $1.72 billion, up 31%, with adjusted earnings per share of $2.58, up 52%. Management raised the full-year revenue growth outlook to the "high 20s" percentage range, against a prior assumption of just above 20%.

The stock has roughly doubled over the past twelve months, hitting a 52-week high of $370. Its gains after the Q2 print extended a move that began in February when first-quarter results arrived with a 26% single-session jump.

The numbers are notable less for their magnitude than for what is driving them: defence electronics and AI datacentre infrastructure — two spending streams that analysts had assumed would follow different cycles — are both pressing on the same production bottleneck simultaneously.

Two customers, one constraint

Keysight's aerospace, defence and government segment grew 24% year on year to $373 million in the quarter, with CEO Satish Dhanasekaran citing "broad-based global momentum led by Europe" and particular strength in radar and electromagnetic spectrum operations — the contested radio frequencies that modern conflict now revolves around. A newly disclosed NATO contract to upgrade radar target generators and electronic-warfare threat simulators illustrated what Dhanasekaran described as defence modernisation operating at scale rather than in isolated pilots.

AI datacentre customers arrived with comparable urgency. Keysight's AI-related revenue in the first two fiscal quarters of 2026 ran between $500 million and $600 million — roughly matching the entire AI line for fiscal 2025. The demand is coming from hyperscalers validating 1.6-terabit Ethernet fabrics, PCIe 7.0 interconnects, and CXL 3 memory architectures: the plumbing of large-scale inference clusters. Dhanasekaran called the opportunity "very early innings."

What makes the result a signal rather than merely a beat is the simultaneity. Test instruments are a leading indicator: hardware manufacturers must buy measurement gear before they can ramp production lines. When two structurally separate customer classes — government-funded militaries and technology-funded hyperscalers — converge on the same vendor with concurrent, non-deferrable demand, it suggests neither cycle is slowing or waiting for the other to exhaust itself.

CFO Neal Doherty acknowledged the company is "actively managing the supply chain more so than we were six months ago," though he stopped short of flagging a hard constraint. The company raised capital-expenditure guidance from $160 million to $200 million for the fiscal year, the bulk directed at ramping AI infrastructure product lines. A separate announcement of end-to-end manufacturing in Chennai is intended to spread supply-chain risk as tariff pressures — running at roughly $75 million annually — are worked through.

What to watch

Q3 guidance calls for revenue of $1.73–$1.75 billion, implying 29% growth at the midpoint. Book-to-bill has been above 1.0; if July-quarter orders sustain that ratio, the case for a durable, multi-year upcycle in test equipment strengthens considerably. Any divergence between defence and AI bookings — one accelerating while the other cools — will be the more telling data point. For now, the two are moving in lockstep, which is precisely what makes Keysight's numbers worth watching beyond the company itself.
