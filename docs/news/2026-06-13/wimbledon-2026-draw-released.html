Wimbledon 2026 draw released with several high-profile first-round matchups

Wimbledon's Centre Court, photographed in May 2019 ahead of a grass-court season — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Sinner leads the Wimbledon 2026 draw as heavy favourite after Alcaraz's withdrawal; the women's title looks a more open contest between Sabalenka, Rybakina, and Swiatek.

The 128-player singles draws for Wimbledon 2026 were released on Friday ahead of the tournament's opening Monday, confirming what the entry list had already made plain: this is a Championship reshaped by one notable absence.

Carlos Alcaraz, who pushed defending champion Jannik Sinner to a four-set final at SW19 last year before ultimately losing, withdrew in May with a right wrist injury that has kept him out since Barcelona. With the Spaniard gone, Sinner arrives as both top seed and heavy favourite — currently priced around -163 at major books — leaving the rest of the draw to contest what remains a large but somewhat theoretical prize.

The women's draw presents a more genuinely open contest. Aryna Sabalenka, seeded first, seeks a maiden grand slam title on grass; her results at the All England Club have improved steadily, and she arrives as the defending finalist. Iga Swiatek, the third seed, is the defending champion, having won the 2025 women's final 6-0, 6-0 in one of the more emphatic defences of a Wimbledon title in recent memory. Elena Rybakina, the 2022 winner, slots in at second. Behind them, the field is competitive enough that the draw's upper and lower halves will be scrutinised for paths to the semis rather than the final.

Draw day versus draw news

Draw ceremonies tend to generate media attention that outpaces their actual information content. The reveal shifts a handful of first-round prices; beyond that, the market is already priced around the seedings — and, above all, around Alcaraz's absence.

That absence was the real market-moving event, and it occurred weeks ago. Sinner's odds tightened sharply in May when the withdrawal was confirmed, while grass-court specialists who might have faced Alcaraz early found their paths through the first week meaningfully eased. That repricing is complete. What Friday's draw offers is confirmation of structure, not genuinely new information.

The record prize pool — £64.2 million in total, a 20 per cent increase on 2025, with singles champions each earning £3.6 million — and the introduction of video review technology on Centre Court, Court One, and four show courts add institutional colour. Neither carries an immediate market read.

For those watching the odds rather than the order of play, the next substantive data point is first-round results on the opening Monday — and, thereafter, whether any of the seeded names navigate the first week intact.
