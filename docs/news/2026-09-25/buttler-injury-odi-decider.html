Buttler hamstring injury derails England as Sri Lanka level ODI series ahead of Oval decider

Jos Buttler, whose hamstring injury during England's Headingley chase has left his fitness for Sunday's decider in doubt. — Simon Walker / No 10 Downing Street (Open Government Licence)
In one sentence
Jos Buttler's suspected torn hamstring wrecked England's chase as Sri Lanka won by 16 runs to level the ODI series 1-1 ahead of Sunday's Oval decider.

England had Sri Lanka's chase all but wrapped up at Headingley on Thursday night — right up until the moment their own chase fell apart instead. Chasing 322 to win the second one-day international and seal the series with a match to spare, England were cruising at 254 for 4 in the 38th over when Jos Buttler, on 67, reached for a drive off Dunith Wellalage's left-arm spin and felt something go in his hamstring. Sri Lanka went on to win by 16 runs. The three-match series is level at 1-1, and Sunday's decider at The Oval now arrives with England's talisman a doubt to play in it at all.

"You can look back and dwell on so many different areas but that is not what this team does — we go back to the drawing board and get ready for the Oval."

That was Harry Brook afterwards, choosing brisk pragmatism over post-mortem. It's the right instinct for a captain, if not necessarily a complete account of what went wrong.

A century, a stand, and a collapse

Sri Lanka's total of 321 for 6 owed almost everything to their captain. Kusal Mendis made 121 off, by the close, a genuinely front-foot innings — 14 fours and three sixes — and put on 137 with Kamindu Mendis, who chipped in 54 of his own. Pavan Rathnayake added a brisk 48 down the order. It was the kind of total that looks imposing on paper and slightly less so once Ben Duckett and Tom Banton got hold of the England reply: an opening stand of 143 took the hosts to 82 without loss inside ten overs, and by the 38th over, with Duckett gone for 67 and Banton for 73, England still needed only 68 off 75 balls with wickets in hand. It was, by any reasonable measure, a game England were winning.

Then Wellalage intervened twice over — first with the ball, dismantling the middle order figures of 3 for 49 that eventually accounted for Duckett, Banton and Brook in the same short spell, and then, almost incidentally, by being the bowler in whose over Buttler's hamstring gave way. Buttler limped to the boundary and did not return for ten overs, re-emerging only when England needed 27 off 14 with the game essentially gone. He could barely run and added just two more runs before being dismissed for 69, the final rites of a chase that had needed him fit far more than it needed him brave.

The cost of one fielding change

It is worth being precise about what the injury actually cost England, because it is easy to overstate a single moment's importance in a three-and-a-half-hour innings. The equation when Buttler went down — 68 needed off 75 balls, four wickets in hand — was comfortable rather than urgent. What followed in his absence was 52 for 5, a stretch in which England lost the ability to rotate strike through the specific batter who does that best, and lost their nerve along with him. Sri Lanka's fielders and bowlers deserve credit for pressing the advantage rather than assuming the game was already won; Wellalage's figures were built as much on control as on swing. But the honest reading is that England had a match-winning position and Buttler's hamstring turned it into a coin-flip that Sri Lanka then won.

A decider without its keeper

Buttler will be assessed over the next 48 hours, but the early signal from within the camp is not encouraging: reports on Thursday night described him as unlikely to be fit for Sunday, and England have already pencilled in Jordan Cox to keep wicket and bat in the middle order at The Oval if he doesn't recover in time. For a squad that swept Sri Lanka 3-0 in the preceding T20 series, largely on the back of Buttler's form with the bat, losing their senior batter and gloveman for a decider is a considerably bigger blow than one lost ODI.

It also reframes the narrative around Sri Lanka, who arrive at The Oval as more than convenient underdogs. Mendis's century was built on intent rather than fortune, and Wellalage — 22 years old and increasingly first-choice in Sri Lankan white-ball cricket — has now shown he can turn a game against a strong batting line-up on a good surface. A series that England were expected to win comfortably is instead going to the wire, and whichever way the toss and the fitness tests fall on Sunday, Sri Lanka have already made the stronger case that they belong in it.

The teams meet at The Oval on Sunday, September 27th, with Buttler's late fitness test — and Sri Lanka's evident momentum — the two threads that will decide how the series is remembered.
