Petrobras profit tops estimates on Iran war-driven oil rally

Petrobras' headquarters in Rio de Janeiro, the state-controlled oil giant whose earnings swung from a miss to a beat within a single quarter. — Edmund Nigel Gall / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Petrobras beat Q2 EBITDA forecasts as war-driven crude gains flowed through, a reversal from a Q1 miss when Brasília capped pump prices despite the same oil rally.

Petrobras posted second-quarter earnings that beat Wall Street's forecasts on Thursday, a swift reversal from a first quarter in which the state-controlled oil giant missed estimates despite crude prices that were, if anything, higher still. The difference between the two quarters was not operational — it was Tehran.

Adjusted EBITDA came in at 93.8 billion reais ($18.6 billion), above the 91.3 billion-real consensus compiled by Bloomberg. Net income nearly doubled year-on-year to 52.4 billion reais ($10.4 billion), and operating cash flow rose 46% to 61.8 billion reais. The board approved 17.4 billion reais in dividends and interest on equity.

A quarter apart, a different story

Three months earlier, Petrobras told a nearly opposite tale. In May, the company reported first-quarter adjusted EBITDA of 59.64 billion reais, short of the 64.5 billion-real consensus — a miss that landed even as the Iran conflict was already pushing global crude higher. The culprit then was policy, not price: Brasília, wary of feeding inflation in an election year, leaned on Petrobras to hold domestic pump prices roughly flat even as Brent surged, alongside fuel tax cuts and export levies that capped the pass-through to profit.

By the second quarter, that lid came off. Brent averaged well above year-earlier levels through April and May — trading above $115 a barrel at points as US and Iranian forces traded strikes near the Strait of Hormuz — before easing into a mid-June ceasefire and then flaring again in July as the truce frayed. Petrobras said the resulting jump in crude, gasoline and diesel prices during the quarter was the direct driver of the earnings beat. Brazil's own production also hit records — 2.7 million barrels a day domestically, up 15% from a year earlier, with total output of 3.34 million barrels of oil equivalent a day — but the swing between a miss and a beat, in back-to-back quarters, tracked the price of oil far more closely than it tracked the drill bit.

The geopolitical beta made visible

For traders, the lesson is less about Petrobras than about what it represents: a commodity producer whose earnings are, in essence, a levered bet on crude prices, with the lever's direction set in Washington and Tehran rather than in the boardroom. When Brasília blunted that pass-through in the first quarter, the beat evaporated even with oil elevated. When the government let prices move in the second, the beat reappeared — same war, same company, opposite outcome, one quarter apart. That is the geopolitical risk premium showing up on an income statement, not just a futures curve.

It is a pattern playing out across the sector. Refiners and producers globally have posted stronger results as the Hormuz standoff kept a war premium in crude even through periods of ceasefire diplomacy, underscoring how quickly a single flashpoint can move from headline to balance sheet.

The next test comes with third-quarter numbers, due around early November, by which point traders will have a clearer read on whether the July flare-up near Hormuz settles into a durable ceasefire or another price spike — and whether Brasília, past its most inflation-sensitive stretch, lets more of any rally reach Petrobras' bottom line.
