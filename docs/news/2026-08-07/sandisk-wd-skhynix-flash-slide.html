SanDisk, Western Digital shares slide as SK Hynix flash memory outlook disappoints

A NAND flash memory chip of the kind at the center of this week's storage-stock selloff. — Raimond Spekking / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
SanDisk and Western Digital beat earnings estimates but guided just shy of sky-high forecasts, and SK Hynix's own softer flash shipment outlook deepened the sell-off.

Memory-chip stocks had their roughest week of the year, and the trigger came from an unlikely place: better-than-expected earnings.

SanDisk and Western Digital both beat Wall Street's profit targets when they reported quarterly results on Wednesday. Then their shares got hammered anyway — Western Digital sank as much as 16%, SanDisk fell 11%, and Micron dropped 6% in sympathy. The reason: guidance for the current quarter fell just short of the sky-high numbers investors had priced in, and after a year of parabolic gains, "just short" was enough to trigger a rout.

"The selloff looks absurd," Yahoo Finance's Brian Sozzi wrote of the reaction, noting both companies had actually delivered solid results. SanDisk guided to first-quarter revenue of $10.3 billion to $10.8 billion — a midpoint of roughly $10.55 billion against a consensus near $10.6 billion — and EPS of about $45, below whisper numbers that had crept as high as $50. That was the whole story: a slight miss against expectations inflated by a stock that was up 469% to 574% this year. Western Digital, up over 200% year-to-date, got caught in the same downdraft.

SK Hynix set the tone first

The ground had already shifted. SK Hynix, the South Korean giant that dominates both high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators and a large share of NAND flash production, reported record quarterly revenue of 79.3 trillion won (about $55 billion) in late July — and still saw its stock drop more than 9%, because analysts had modeled in even more. HBM4 shipments came in below forecast, pushing some revenue into later quarters, and the company guided that combined DRAM and NAND flash shipments would decline 10% to 20% next quarter from this one.

That shipment guidance was a flash memory-specific warning sign sitting in the middle of an otherwise triumphant earnings report, and it planted the first seed of doubt about how much further the AI-driven memory rally could run. When SK Hynix's own US-listed shares fell again this week — down more than 6% at one point — traders were reacting as much to the SanDisk and Western Digital guidance as to any fresh news of their own, plus a bout of thin-liquidity volatility on South Korea's new premarket trading venue that has made the stock swing wildly on almost no volume.

A pricing problem, not (yet) a demand collapse

Underneath the guidance numbers is a more specific worry: how much of the memory boom is volume and how much is just price. SanDisk disclosed that roughly $2 billion of its sequential revenue growth came from pricing rather than shipments — a sign that NAND suppliers have been raising prices aggressively to capture AI-driven scarcity, not necessarily selling more chips. If pricing growth cools even slightly, as SK Hynix's shipment-decline guidance implies it might, margins that have looked spectacular this year become much harder to repeat.

SK Hynix's NAND arm, Solidigm, is meanwhile pushing ahead with a pre-IPO fundraising of up to $7 billion ahead of an expected Nasdaq listing at a valuation near $36 billion — a bet that investor appetite for flash memory exposure remains strong even as the near-term numbers wobble.

What to watch

Micron reports its own quarterly results later this month, and its shipment and pricing commentary will be the next real test of whether this week's slide is profit-taking after an extraordinary run or the first sign that the memory supercycle is cresting. Until then, the sector's whipsaw trading says more about stretched expectations than about underlying chip demand.
