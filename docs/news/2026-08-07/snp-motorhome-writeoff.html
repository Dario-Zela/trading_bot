SNP writes off Peter Murrell's £124,550 motorhome, still owes him £60,000

Peter Murrell, former SNP chief executive, pictured in 2014. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
The SNP's 2025 accounts quietly write off Peter Murrell's £124,550 seized motorhome — while still listing £60,000 owed to him.

The motorhome is gone from the books, if not from memory. In its latest annual accounts, filed with the Electoral Commission this month, the SNP has quietly written off the £124,550 it once carried on its balance sheet for a luxury Niesmann + Bischoff Smove 7.4e — the campervan bought by former chief executive Peter Murrell in late 2020 and paid for entirely with party funds.

The vehicle had already been depreciating for years by the time it vanished from the accounts altogether. Valued at £100,790 on 1 January 2024, it was written down to £41,284 by that December, and now, in the 2025 statements, to nothing. The accounts state simply that "the vehicle did not belong to the party" — a bloodless line for a saga that ran from a pandemic-era purchase pitched as a campaigning tool, to a short drive from a dealer in Rosyth, Fife, to the Dunfermline home of Murrell's mother, where it sat until police seized it as part of Operation Branchform.

Murrell, the estranged husband of former First Minister Nicola Sturgeon, pleaded guilty on 25 May to embezzling more than £400,000 from the party over roughly twelve years, and was sentenced on 23 June to five years and three months in prison. Operation Branchform cleared both Sturgeon and the SNP's former treasurer, Colin Beattie, of wrongdoing. A proceeds-of-crime hearing to determine what, if anything, can be clawed back was scheduled for the weeks after sentencing.

Still on the hook

What makes the accounts an odd read is the entry sitting a few lines away from the write-off: the SNP still owes Murrell £60,000, from a loan he made the party in 2021. There was never a formal loan agreement, the accounts note, no interest charged, and no repayment terms agreed — and the party says Murrell has not formally asked for the money back. Whether it will ever be repaid, or eventually written off too, is left open. The SNP has separately said it intends to pursue recovery of the embezzled funds in full and has no plans to pay Murrell anything.

The motorhome write-off itself was not announced with any fanfare; it surfaced as one line in a set of accounts otherwise built to tell a recovery story. The party reported a £509,903 surplus for 2025, a reversal from a roughly £400,000 deficit the year before, achieved largely by cutting staff from 26 to 19 and trimming the wage bill from £1.4 million to £978,538 after redundancies announced in December 2024. Treasurer Peter Grant credited "very difficult cost cutting decisions," and First Minister John Swinney described the party as being in a position of "formidable financial strength." Membership, meanwhile, has fallen to around 51,000, down from nearly 59,000 in 2024 and 64,000 the year before.

The party's own auditors were less upbeat, flagging that manual journal entries and transactions between related parties remained at what they termed "heightened risk of fraud" — a caveat that sits uneasily next to a set of accounts still working through the fallout of the last one.

The proceeds-of-crime process, which will determine how much of Murrell's embezzled money the SNP actually recovers, is the next point at which the numbers in these accounts could move again.
