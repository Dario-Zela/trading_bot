Farage faces record 34 candidates in Clacton by-election as Count Binface polls second

Count Binface, the satirical candidate currently polling second behind Farage in Clacton — Jpdfive / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Farage's resignation over a finances probe triggered a 13 August by-election with a record 34 candidates, and satirical bin-costumed candidate Count Binface is polling a distant second.

The by-election was never really about who might win it. Nigel Farage will win it — polling from Survation in early August put him at 73 per cent, with the seat's main challenger a man in a bin costume trailing on 20. What the 13 August vote in Clacton will be remembered for is its ballot paper: 34 names long, printed on a sheet roughly 90 centimetres across, the largest field ever to contest a UK parliamentary seat.

The previous record, 26 candidates in the 2008 Haltemprice and Howden by-election, stood for eighteen years. Clacton beat it by eight.

A resignation designed to be undone

Farage triggered the contest himself. He quit as MP for Clacton on 7 July, in the middle of a Parliamentary Standards Commissioner inquiry into whether he failed to declare a £5 million gift from the crypto financier Christopher Harborne, and a separate Sunday Times report alleging undeclared benefits — staff, security, housing — from George Cottrell, a Reform aide who served eight months in a US prison in 2017 after pleading guilty to wire fraud. The Sunday Guardian Live reported the National Crime Agency had also flagged the Harborne payment under anti-money-laundering rules.

Rather than face the inquiry, Farage resigned and stood again, framing it as a referendum on himself. "The voters of Clacton should be the judges," he said, casting the parliamentary standards process as a political weapon. It's a manoeuvre with precedent — MPs have long used by-elections to seek a fresh mandate mid-scandal — but rarely this openly telegraphed as a strategy to sidestep an ongoing probe rather than answer it.

Labour, the Tories and the Lib Dems all sat it out

What turned a routine by-election into a record-breaker was who didn't show up. Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats, the Greens and Restore Britain all declined to field candidates, treating the contest as Farage's personal errand rather than a genuine electoral test. That left the field to Reform UK, minor parties and a long tail of independents — and, crucially, cleared space for a novelty run to become the de facto opposition.

Enter Count Binface: an "intergalactic space warlord" played by the comedian Jon Harvey, a veteran protest candidate who has previously stood against Boris Johnson in Uxbridge and for London mayor. His 20-point manifesto — filling potholes with copies of the 2024 Labour manifesto, relocating Parliament to a new "Bin HQ" in Weeley, sending Clacton to host Eurovision 2028 — is a joke, but the backing behind it isn't entirely. He has drawn grassroots donations and a public endorsement from the green-energy entrepreneur Dale Vince, and describes himself, straight-facedly, as a "unity candidate" for voters with nowhere else to put a protest vote. Laurence Fox is also on the ballot for the Reclaim Party, one of dozens of smaller names competing for a distant third.

What the record actually measures

A 73-point lead is not a competitive race by any definition, and nobody seriously expects Farage to lose the seat he built Reform UK's national profile around. What the 34-candidate field measures instead is the absence of a credible mainstream alternative in a seat the big parties decided wasn't worth contesting, paired with a public appetite — visible in Binface's second-place polling — to register dissent through parody rather than through any of Westminster's actual opposition parties. It says more about the state of the opposition than about Farage's standing, which the finances questions have so far done little to dent.

The count itself, on the night of 13 August, will double as an unusually literal test of how long Britain's largest-ever ballot paper takes to process by hand.
