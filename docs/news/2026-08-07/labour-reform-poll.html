Labour edges ahead of Reform in poll as Burnham honeymoon continues

Prime Minister Andy Burnham and his wife Marie-France van Heel arrive at 10 Downing Street, 20 July 2026. — Simon Dawson / No 10 Downing Street (Open Government Licence v3.0)
In one sentence
A JL Partners poll gives Labour its first lead over Reform in over a year — 27% to 25% — but YouGov still has Reform ahead, 23% to 22%.

The last time Labour led Reform UK in a JL Partners poll, Rishi Sunak was still prime minister. That changed this week: a survey by the pollster puts Labour on 27%, Reform on 25% — a two-point lead that ends a run of more than a year in which Nigel Farage's party sat ahead, often by a wide margin, in the firm's tracker.

The shift lands three weeks into Andy Burnham's premiership. Burnham was acclaimed Labour leader on 17 July after Keir Starmer resigned under pressure from his own MPs following catastrophic local election results in May, and became prime minister on 20 July without facing a single rival for the job. JL Partners' figures are the clearest sign yet that voters have registered the change at the top: in a head-to-head prime ministerial matchup within the same poll, 53% back Burnham against 24% for Farage.

What's moved the needle

Burnham has used his first fortnight in office to move fast on two fronts that poll well with voters currently drifting towards Reform: cost of living and devolution. His government has confirmed it will scrap VAT on household electricity bills, and it has promised to hand England's metro mayors — the job Burnham himself held in Greater Manchester until last month — new tax-sharing powers. Both moves are aimed squarely at the economically anxious, provincial voters Reform has been courting.

There are limits to reading too much into the bounce. A separate poll found three in four voters don't think the VAT cut will meaningfully help with their bills, suggesting the policy's popularity has more to do with signalling than substance. And Reform has had troubles of its own — financial scandals involving figures around the party have dented its standing at the same time Burnham's has risen, muddying how much of Labour's gain is really about Labour.

The YouGov problem

The JL Partners numbers sit awkwardly next to YouGov's tracker, which as of its 2–3 August poll for The Times and Sky News still had Reform ahead: 23% to Labour's 22%, with the Conservatives on 19%, the Greens on 13% and the Liberal Democrats on 12%. That is a far tighter race than JL Partners describes, and it puts Labour in a three-way scrap for second place rather than clear of the pack. YouGov's favourability tracker does show Labour warming up — 34% now view the party positively, the highest reading since August 2024 — but warmer feelings haven't yet translated into YouGov's headline voting-intention figure overtaking Reform.

Part of the gap is methodological: pollsters weight likely turnout, past-vote recall and undecided-voter allocation differently, and with five parties now routinely polling in the teens and twenties, small modelling choices move the order of finish. Part of it may simply be timing — JL Partners' fieldwork is more recent and could be catching a Burnham bounce that hasn't fully shown up elsewhere yet.

What it means

Either way, the broader picture is consistent: Labour has closed a gap that looked insurmountable through the spring, when Starmer's Labour briefly fell to third place in some trackers. Whether that constitutes a genuine narrowing of the race or a single outlier depends on which pollster is asked, and the divergence itself is instructive — with an election not due until 2029, no single poll should be read as settling the question of Reform's ceiling or Labour's floor. The next fortnight of surveys, and whether other firms corroborate JL Partners' lead, will do more to answer that than this one number.
