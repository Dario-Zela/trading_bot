LG and Nvidia expand alliance on humanoid robots and AI factories

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, whose company signed a robotics, data-centre and mobility pact with LG Group on August 13. — Presidential Office (Taiwan) via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0
In one sentence
LG and Nvidia signed an MOU to build a bipedal humanoid robot by Q1 2027, an AI data centre, and self-driving car software.

LG Group and Nvidia have moved their year-long courtship into contract territory. On August 13, at Nvidia's Santa Clara headquarters, LG Group chairman Koo Kwang-mo and Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang signed a memorandum of understanding covering three areas: humanoid robots, AI-dedicated data centres, and self-driving car software. It builds on a broader framework the two firms sketched out in June at LG's Seoul headquarters — this time with dates and hardware attached.

The centrepiece is a bipedal humanoid robot LG intends to unveil in the first quarter of 2027. It will run on Nvidia's Jetson Thor computing module and Isaac GR00T foundation model, with safety software from Nvidia's newer Halos system. LG is drawing the rest of the robot from within its own conglomerate — actuators from LG Electronics, sensors from LG Innotek, batteries from LG Energy Solution — which is less a partnership flourish than a reminder that LG makes most of what goes into a robot already; it just hasn't had the AI brain until now.

A simpler, wheeled robot called CLOiD is further along. LG plans to run it on a washing-machine production line at its factory in Clarksville, Tennessee, this year, testing whether it can handle repetitive factory tasks before any wider rollout to plants, homes or shops.

"The most important opportunity that physical AI offers lies in giving every machine the ability to understand and reason about the real world," Huang said. Koo's framing was more pragmatic: "We will accelerate the adoption of AI by building industry-leading reference cases."

Chips, then cars

The other two pillars are less flashy but arguably more consequential for near-term spending. LG and Nvidia plan a reference AI data centre running on Nvidia's next Vera Rubin chip platform by the first half of 2027, followed by an 80-megawatt facility in Cheonan, south of Seoul, by the first half of 2028 — LG supplying cooling and power systems, Nvidia supplying the silicon. On the mobility side, LG will build self-driving and in-car AI software on Nvidia's Drive Hyperion platform, aiming to sell the combined package to other carmakers rather than just its own affiliates.

None of this is contractually binding yet — an MOU is a statement of intent, not a purchase order, and humanoid robotics has produced plenty of flashy demos that never reached a factory floor. What distinguishes this one is the specificity: named facilities, a named production line already scheduled for this year, and a chip platform that doesn't ship until next year. That is a different category of commitment than a stage demo, and it is precisely the kind of order book Nvidia needs to justify its bet that physical AI, not just chatbots, is the next leg of chip demand.

The first checkpoint is concrete: the Tennessee CLOiD trial, running through the rest of 2026, followed by the humanoid unveiling in the first quarter of 2027.
