FTSE earnings round-up: Persimmon lifts guidance, Harbour Energy raises buyback

An oil platform in the North Sea, where Harbour Energy raised production and free cash flow guidance. — Erik Christensen / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Persimmon, Harbour Energy and Michael Page all raised guidance or profit this week, but for three unrelated reasons — not a broad UK recovery.

Three FTSE-listed companies gave the market reasons for optimism this week, but the optimism doesn't travel well between them. Persimmon, the York-based housebuilder, lifted its 2026 delivery forecast. Harbour Energy, the North Sea's largest independent producer, raised both its free cash flow guidance and its production outlook, and approved a fresh buyback. And Michael Page's parent, PageGroup, reported a sharp jump in first-half operating profit. Read as a set, the three updates look less like evidence of a broadening UK recovery and more like three unrelated companies each solving their own, specific problem.

Housebuilder finds its floor

Persimmon's half-year results, published on 6 August, showed underlying pre-tax profit up 3% to £170.1 million for the six months to June, on completions that rose 13% to 5,189 homes and revenue up 15% to £1.73 billion. The company now expects to deliver around 12,500 homes for the full year — the top of its previous guidance range — and shares rose more than 4% on the update.

The caveats matter as much as the headline. Persimmon flagged £40–50 million of inflation-driven cost pressure over the next 18 months, tied in part to fallout from the Iran conflict, and said open-market sales — excluding bulk deals — had slipped below year-earlier levels in the five weeks to early August. Buyer enquiries softened in July. The guidance raise reflects a strong build pipeline already secured, not a resurgent buyer.

Harbour Energy cashes in on prices

Harbour Energy's improvement is more straightforwardly a function of the oil and gas price environment. The producer posted record first-half output of more than 500,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day and raised full-year production guidance to 490,000–500,000 boepd, from 480,000–500,000 previously. Full-year free cash flow guidance rose to $1.8 billion, up from $1.4 billion, on higher oil and European gas prices. The board approved a $250 million share buyback, part of a commitment to return at least $800 million to shareholders in 2026 — including at least $500 million beyond the ordinary dividend. Cost and capital-spending guidance were left unchanged, at roughly $14.5 a barrel of oil equivalent and $2.2–2.4 billion respectively, underlining that the upgrade is a pricing story rather than an operational one.

Staffing profit jumps, but off a low base

Michael Page's improvement is the most fragile of the three. Operating profit rose to £9.7 million from £2.1 million a year earlier — a large percentage gain, but from a depressed starting point — while group gross profit fell 2.4% on a constant-currency basis to £385.2 million. Growth in Asia-Pacific, the Americas and southern Europe offset continued weakness in France, northern Europe and the UK. The board cut the interim dividend from 5.36p to 1.46p, a reduction of roughly 73%, to retain cash for investment — a signal that management sees the recovery as partial rather than assured.

What the mix tells traders

The absence of a shared cause is the point. A housebuilder, an oil producer and a staffing group don't respond to the same macro lever, so three simultaneous upgrades say little about UK growth momentum on their own — particularly with 30-year gilt yields at their highest since 1998 and UK inflation data due to show price growth approaching 3%. For now, each of these is a stock-specific signal rather than a market one. The next test comes with Persimmon's autumn trading update and Harbour Energy's Q3 production figures, both of which will show whether these gains hold once softer buyer demand and price-dependent cash flow meet a colder macro backdrop.
