UK retail sales rebound 0.5% in August

A department store on Oxford Street, London — department stores led August's rebound in UK retail sales. — Smithr32 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
UK retail sales rose 0.5% in August, exactly reversing July's fall, leaving spending flat rather than genuinely recovering.

UK retail sales volumes rose 0.5% in August, the Office for National Statistics said on Friday — a figure that exactly reverses July's 0.5% decline and beat economists' forecasts, who had penciled in a further fall of 0.2%. Annual growth came in at 2.4%, ahead of the 1.9% expected and faster than July's 1.2%.

On the surface, that looks like a good number. Underneath it, the picture is flatter than the headline suggests: two months that cancel each other out is not the same thing as a trend, and the ONS's own three-month measure — sales volumes up 0.9% in the three months to August versus the three months to May — points to a retail sector inching forward rather than accelerating.

What moved

Supermarkets did the heavy lifting, continuing a strong run through July and August that the ONS attributed partly to promotions, hot weather and, in the case of alcohol sales, the World Cup. Department stores recovered 0.6% on the month, bouncing back after firms cited stock-availability problems that had dragged on July's figures. Non-store retailers — mostly online sellers — rose 1.7%, recovering some of the ground lost when June's earlier promotional activity had pulled forward demand and left July looking weak by comparison.

Fuel was the drag: sales fell 1.3% on the month as prices rose sharply enough that the ONS said drivers were changing habits, including partially filling tanks rather than topping up in full. Clothing retailers and art dealers had shown some strength back in June, but non-food stores overall added just 0.2% across the three-month window — hardly a sign of a consumer in expansive mode.

"Retail sales performed better than expected in August as improving consumer confidence and the last of the summer heatwaves encouraged an uptick in spending across almost all categories," said Oliver Vernon-Harcourt, head of retail at Deloitte.

Reading the noise out of the signal

The symmetry is the story here. A 0.5% fall followed by a 0.5% rise leaves the level of retail sales almost exactly where it was in June, once the compositional swings between categories — supermarkets up, department stores recovering a hole they dug themselves, fuel down — are netted off. That is consistent with a household sector that is spending cautiously and reactively: buying more when the weather or a promotion nudges it, buying less when fuel prices spike, but not building the kind of sustained momentum that would show up as several consecutive months moving the same direction.

For a data series that forecasters lean on as a proxy for consumer health, that matters. Retail sales feed into GDP estimates and inform how the Bank of England reads the state of household demand. A bounce that simply undoes the prior month's decline tells a very different story from a bounce that builds on it — and August's numbers are the former, not the latter. The MPC held its rate at 3.75% at its last meeting, with gilt yields still pushing toward 5.3%; a retail sector oscillating around a flat line does little to change that calculus in either direction.

What's next

The ONS publishes September's retail sales figures in mid-October, alongside the next GDP estimate. A third data point — showing whether spending breaks out of the narrow band it has occupied since June or falls back again — will do more to settle the debate over UK consumer momentum than either of the past two months managed on their own.
