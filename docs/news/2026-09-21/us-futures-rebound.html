US futures rise as Dow tries to snap three-week losing streak

The New York Stock Exchange trading floor, where futures pointed higher Monday on the back of a narrow, tech-led rally. — Carol M. Highsmith / Library of Congress, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Nasdaq futures led a Monday bounce on tech strength and the Nvidia-Hugging Face deal, but elevated 10-year yields near 5% kept the Dow's three-week losing streak in doubt.

Wall Street opened the week trying to shake off three straight losing weeks for the Dow Jones Industrial Average, with futures pointing higher Monday morning as traders looked past a bond market that refuses to cooperate.

S&P 500 futures climbed roughly 0.3-0.4%, while contracts on the Nasdaq 100 outpaced them, up about 0.4-0.6%, as technology names led the early bid. Dow futures also rose, but more modestly — consistent with a market where gains are concentrated rather than broad-based. The underlying Dow shed 1.7% last week, its worst weekly showing since March, while the S&P 500 slipped about 0.1%. The Nasdaq, propped up almost entirely by a handful of large-cap tech names, was the lone gainer among the majors, up 0.7% on the week.

The yield problem hasn't gone away

The reason the Dow keeps stumbling while the Nasdaq climbs is sitting in the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield has been hovering close to 5%, not far from levels last seen in 2007, after the Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike in three years last week under new chair Kevin Warsh. The Fed's own projections showed 16 of 18 officials penciling in at least one more hike before year-end, which is exactly the kind of signal that keeps long-dated yields elevated and squeezes the rate-sensitive, cyclical names that make up more of the Dow's roster — industrials, financials, consumer names — than the Nasdaq's.

Oil, still trading close to $100 a barrel, adds a second layer of pressure on the same group of stocks, feeding into the inflation concerns that gave the Fed cover to hike in the first place.

Tech carries the tape again

What's actually moving Monday's futures is a narrower story: gains concentrated in chipmakers and megacap platform stocks, with Nvidia among the standouts after its roughly $12.9bn agreement to acquire Hugging Face, one of the largest deals in the AI infrastructure buildout to date. The Nasdaq 100 extended a run that took it to fresh records last week and close to the 22,000 mark, even as the rest of the market struggled.

That divergence is the thing worth watching rather than the headline futures numbers themselves. A single sector carrying an index to green while breadth elsewhere stays weak is not the same as a market finding its footing — it's a market where a handful of stocks are doing most of the work, and where a stumble in that group would remove the only source of upward pressure. Traders have seen this pattern before this cycle: an AI-driven bounce papering over broader hesitancy tied to yields.

Also on the radar Monday: US and Chinese officials opened trade and AI-related talks in New York ahead of a planned summit between Presidents Trump and Xi later this week, a backdrop that has kept the dollar relatively steady but has not been enough on its own to broaden the rally beyond tech.

The test for the week is whether the Dow can string together enough sessions to actually close the three-week losing streak, or whether Monday's bounce fades the way earlier attempts have once yields reassert themselves. Friday's close will be the first checkpoint; any fresh inflation data or comments from Fed officials before then could move the 10-year yield and, with it, the fate of the more cyclical parts of the index.
