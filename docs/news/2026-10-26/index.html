ISRAEL · ELECTION · By C. Tanaka

Deeply divided Israel braces for pivotal election

Israelis vote on Tuesday with final polls showing neither Netanyahu's coalition nor the opposition reaching 61 seats, pointing to prolonged coalition talks or a repeat election.

Israelis are voting in a national election that must be held by 27 October, the deadline set by the four-year cycle since the last ballot on 1 November 2022. The vote asks whether voters back the current government's security and war policy, including its approach to Gaza, Iran and the West Bank, in an electorate that remains deeply divided over it.

The result will turn on coalition arithmetic. The Knesset has 120 seats, and a party needs 61 to form a government. Parties must clear a 3.25% electoral threshold to win any seats, so small lists that fall short send their votes to nobody and can shift the balance between blocs.

Whoever forms the next government will set Israel's policy toward Gaza, Iran and the West Bank, which shapes regional risk and how investors price Israeli assets. Watch the coalition talks that follow the count, since they will show which policies can survive a divided Knesset.

WORLD · CONSUMERS · By C. Tanaka

New private parking code aims to curb 'rip-off' charges

Private parking firms that issue charges on supermarket and retail car parks in England and Wales are now subject to a new private parking code, which is meant to curb what motorists call 'rip-off' fees. Operators in this sector have long collected charges on private land without council or police enforcement, and drivers have often found the appeal routes slow and uneven.

The current system leans on membership of accredited trade associations. Under the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012, a keeper can be held liable for an unpaid private charge only where the operator belongs to an accredited body, principally the British Parking Association or the International Parking Community. The code's central question is whether its rules cover every operator or only those already in those bodies, since the firms most likely to cause complaints may be the ones least likely to join.

Whether the code changes enforcement or simply adds paperwork will depend on how it treats charge levels and appeals. Watch for whether operators must follow one shared appeals process, and whether the independent appeals service gains real power to overturn charges.
