Isak starts to justify his Liverpool fee

In one sentence
Alexander Isak's recent form is easing pressure on Liverpool after a reported £125m British-record fee and an injury-hit first season, though one good run does not settle the case.

Alexander Isak was Liverpool's most expensive signing and, for much of his first season at Anfield, its most conspicuous question mark. A year on, the balance of the argument appears to be shifting. Reports of his recent form suggest the striker is starting to look like the player Liverpool thought they were buying.

The case for patience is easier to make than it was.

The price and the problem

Liverpool signed Isak from Newcastle United on the last day of the 2025 summer window, 1 September, for a reported £125m. That was a British record, and it beat the £115m Chelsea paid Brighton for Moisés Caicedo in 2023. Earlier reports had put the figure nearer £130m. Isak reportedly signed a six-year contract. The move followed a long stand-off in which Newcastle rejected an opening bid of around £110m and insisted he was not for sale.

A fee like that sets an expectation before a ball is kicked. Isak's first campaign did not meet it. He made only 13 starts for Liverpool in 2025-26 after a serious ankle injury, according to Africa Soccer. A record signing who is rarely on the pitch invites exactly the question of whether the club overpaid.

What has changed

The more recent evidence is that he is fit and scoring. The BBC Sport report that prompted this piece was not available to this writer in full, so the detail of his latest performances is not set out here. Liverpool's own website quotes him describing a good pre-season and a good start to 2026-27, with the aim of keeping that rhythm going. A striker who says he feels good is not news. A striker who strings together starts is.

It is worth being plain about what this does and does not show. Early-season goals ease pressure on a club; they do not settle a valuation. The fee was paid for years of output, not a strong autumn. At £125m spread across a six-year deal, the sum is about £21m a season in accounting terms. The test is whether he stays fit enough to deliver across all six.

What it says about big signings

The Isak story is a small version of a larger Premier League habit. Clubs pay heavily for proven forwards, then judge them within months. Injuries, a new system and a change of country are all treated as evidence about the fee rather than about the circumstances. Isak's first year was disrupted by the ankle problem as much as by anything on the pitch, and a fair reading of the fee has to allow for that.

For Liverpool the practical point is simpler. A fit, scoring Isak takes weight off a team that spent heavily and needs its centrepiece to justify it.

The next checks are the matches ahead in the league and in Europe. Sustained minutes and a steady goal return over the winter, not a single hot run, are what would turn this from an encouraging start into a settled verdict.
