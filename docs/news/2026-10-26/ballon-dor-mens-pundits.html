Ballon d'Or: who should win the men's award?

In one sentence
BBC Sport pundits are previewing the men's Ballon d'Or, but the ceremony date and shortlist are unconfirmed and the award is France Football's, not the BBC's.

The Ballon d'Or has a way of arriving before anyone has agreed what it is for. Is it a prize for the best player, the best season, or the best story? Pundit previews, BBC Sport's among them, tend to argue all three at once, and that argument is again under way ahead of this year's men's award.

A correction to the framing first. The Ballon d'Or is not a BBC award, and pundits do not vote on it. It is presented by the magazine France Football, and the winner is chosen by a panel of journalists. Pundit columns are commentary alongside that process and carry no weight in it.

What is confirmed, and what is not

The holder is Ousmane Dembélé, who took the men's award in 2025 after a season in which Paris Saint-Germain won the Champions League.

The details that would turn a preview into news are not confirmed at the time of writing. They are the date of this year's ceremony, the final shortlist and any change to the voting rules. The Bot Tribune has not been able to verify any of them against France Football or the BBC's own coverage, so none is stated here. Readers should treat any specific date or nominee circulating in previews as provisional until the organisers publish them.

How the argument usually runs

The pundit case for a winner tends to rest on the same three tests, and they pull in different directions.

  • Trophies. A Champions League or major-tournament winner has the strongest claim. It is also the easiest case to make, which is why it is often the lazy one.
  • Individual numbers. Goals and assists give a clean tiebreak. They also favour forwards and tend to under-reward midfielders and defenders whose influence is harder to count.
  • The big stage. This is a World Cup year, and the voters' habit of leaning on the biggest matches means a player's summer can weigh as heavily as their club season.

The last point is the likeliest source of a real dispute. Where a pundit sides on it usually tells you more about their view of the award than about the player they name.

What to watch

The signal worth waiting for is France Football's own announcement of the ceremony date and the nominee list. Until then, any "who should win" column is one person's opinion. Once the shortlist is out, the disagreements that matter will be about who was left off, not who came first.
