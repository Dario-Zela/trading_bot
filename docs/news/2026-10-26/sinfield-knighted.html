Prince William knights Sinfield, the Lionesses' former coach

In one sentence
Kevin Sinfield, the rugby league figure behind a £11m-plus MND fundraising drive, was dubbed a knight by Prince William at Windsor on 7 October.

Kevin Sinfield, the rugby league great who turned a friend's motor neurone disease diagnosis into one of the most successful charity campaigns in British sport, has been made a knight. He is a rugby man, not a football coach. He has no known link to the Lionesses or to Sarina Wiegman, whatever a headline might imply.

The Prince of Wales dubbed him a Knight Bachelor on Wednesday 7 October at Windsor Castle. He was one of 60 people honoured at that investiture. The award was announced in the King's Birthday Honours in June, for services to the MND community and to rugby league and rugby union.

A long wait

The honour came after a delay that had irritated many supporters. Sinfield was left off the New Year Honours list, and campaigners complained loudly. Under the rules, an honour cannot be upgraded within three years of the previous award, and Sinfield already held a CBE. William had presented that CBE in 2024 at Headingley, Leeds, to Sinfield and to his late friend Rob Burrow, the former Leeds Rhinos scrum-half whose MND diagnosis in 2019 prompted the fundraising.

Since then Sinfield has run, and run again, in Burrow's name. His marathon-a-day challenges, including the final "7 in 7" run, have raised more than £11 million. The Prince and Princess of Wales surprised him during that last challenge.

'Massively humbled'

After the ceremony Sinfield said he was "massively humbled by the whole experience" and "very proud to accept it on behalf of the MND community and their families". He said William had been "fantastically supportive" of the campaign. He also said Burrow would "laugh" at the sight of his friend as a knight, but would be "really proud".

The same summer honours list included six Lionesses, who received MBEs, which may explain how the two stories became tangled. Wiegman herself received an honorary damehood at the turn of the year. As a Dutch national she is not formally dubbed and cannot use the title.

What next

The campaign continues. Sinfield's fundraising is tied to MND research and care through the Rob Burrow Centre for MND, and he has given no sign of hanging up his running shoes. Whether the next challenge is announced as a challenge or as a quiet one is the only open question.
