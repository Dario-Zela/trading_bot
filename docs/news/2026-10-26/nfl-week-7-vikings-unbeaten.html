NFL Week 7: Vikings are the last unbeaten team as Chiefs lose

In one sentence
Seattle's 20-3 win over Kansas City leaves the 6-0 Vikings as the NFL's only unbeaten team, with a trip to Detroit next.

One team in the NFL is still perfect. The Minnesota Vikings are 6-0 after a 16-10 comeback win over the Indianapolis Colts at home, and they are the league's last unbeaten side because the Kansas City Chiefs lost 20-3 at Seattle on Sunday night.

The Chiefs had arrived at 5-1 and left with the lowest-scoring game of the Patrick Mahomes era that most readers will remember: one field goal, from an offence that has spent years making such totals look implausible. Seattle moved to 4-3. Kansas City's other defeat this season is not covered in the sources reviewed here, so this was not the Chiefs' first loss of the year, though some reports framed it as the end of their unbeaten run.

A win that doesn't look like a trend

Minnesota's case for being the league's best team is less obvious than its record. The offence has struggled to get going, and the Colts game was another that the Vikings had to come from behind to win. The reports agree on the pattern: they are not dominating opponents, but they keep finding ways to win.

That can be a virtue or a warning. Narrow wins tend to be the most volatile part of a record, and a 6-0 team that scores 16 points against a middling opponent is a different proposition from one that wins by three scores. The Vikings have not yet had to prove which they are.

The rest of the weekend

The week was unkind to other contenders. San Francisco fell to 6-1 after a 29-20 home loss to Atlanta, in which the Falcons forced four turnovers, including two Brock Purdy interceptions. Baltimore lost for the second straight week without Lamar Jackson, beaten 35-13 by a Cincinnati side in which Joe Burrow threw four touchdowns for 292 yards, and the Ravens have no return date for their quarterback. Cleveland, at 5-2 after a late 27-24 win in Tennessee, are back on top of the AFC North.

Denver, 5-2 after beating Arizona 30-16, will host the Chiefs next, which turns the Broncos' week into a useful test for both. Elsewhere, Pittsburgh beat New Orleans 16-10 in the NFL's first game in France, and the Chargers are the league's only winless team after Miami edged the Jets 28-25.

What comes next

The immediate question is how long Minnesota's run lasts. The Vikings travel to Detroit next week, facing a Lions side that beat Green Bay 20-17 on a last-second field goal from Jake Bates. A divisional road game against a team with that kind of late composure is the first real stress on a record that so far rests on close finishes. Kansas City, meanwhile, will want to show in Denver that one dismal night in Seattle was an outlier rather than a new pattern.
