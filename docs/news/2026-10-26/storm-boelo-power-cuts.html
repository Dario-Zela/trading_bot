Storm Boelo kills two and cuts power to about 90,000 properties

In one sentence
Falling trees killed two men as Storm Boelo crossed the UK, leaving about 90,000 properties without power and disrupting rail and road travel.

Two men were killed by falling trees as Storm Boelo crossed the UK over the weekend. By Sunday evening about 90,000 properties were still without electricity, and rail and road travel had been disrupted.

Police Scotland said a 60-year-old man was struck by a tree on a minor road at Mid Kelton, near Castle Douglas in Dumfries and Galloway, at about 10.30am on Sunday. He was pronounced dead at the scene. Separately, a man in his 20s died when a tree fell onto his car on Dollis Road in Finchley, north London. Neither man has been named in the reports seen so far.

A storm with a long warning

The Met Office named Boelo ahead of the weekend. It forecast a deep low bringing strong winds from Saturday night into Sunday across Northern Ireland, Scotland and northern England. A yellow wind warning covered mainland Scotland, the Western Isles, Orkney and the far north of England. Gusts of 70–80mph were expected on exposed coasts and high ground, and 50–60mph more widely. Met Éireann issued an orange wind warning for Donegal, Galway, Leitrim, Mayo and Sligo. Boelo was the second named storm of the 2026/27 season and a separate system from Storm Austen, which brought wind to southern England and Wales earlier in the week.

The deaths in Finchley suggest that damage was not confined to the areas under warning. A yellow warning is the lowest level that signals possible disruption, and it did not cover London.

Power and transport

Power figures varied through the day. One report put the number of properties without electricity at about 90,600 by evening, while another cited more than 160,000 at an earlier point. The difference is probably timing: outage counts rise during the gusts and fall as engineers reconnect supplies. No network operator has yet given a final total or a restoration timetable in the coverage reviewed here.

On the railways, Network Rail imposed speed restrictions across Scotland and closed several lines. It said most services should return to normal on Monday. The Police Service of Northern Ireland warned that fallen trees had blocked roads across the country. Heavy rain also caused localised flooding and travel disruption in Scotland, northern England, North Wales and Northern Ireland.

What the damage points to

Falling trees were behind both deaths. They are also a common cause of power cuts and line closures, because branches bring down overhead lines and block tracks and roads. That is a pattern in earlier UK storms. Storm Arwen in November 2021 also killed two people by falling trees and left more than 100,000 customers without power in some areas.

It is too early to say whether Boelo points to a specific weakness in grid or rail resilience. No official cause, operator review or policy response has been reported. The questions to watch are how quickly the remaining 90,000 properties are reconnected, whether regulators ask network companies to explain restoration times, and how Network Rail and local authorities report on vegetation management along lines and roads.
