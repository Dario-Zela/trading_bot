British couple were celebrating anniversary before Bahamas tragedy

In one sentence
A British mother, Leanne Gregory, 36, died off Bimini after going into the sea to rescue her sons on a trip marking her 10th wedding anniversary; police suspect no foul play.

A British mother died after going into the sea off Bimini in the Bahamas to rescue her two sons. The family was on an early holiday to mark a wedding anniversary. The BBC's report that a "couple" were celebrating before a "tragedy" is shorthand. The woman who died was one half of that couple. Her husband and both boys survived.

Leanne Gregory, 36, from Stapleford, Nottinghamshire, died on Tuesday 20 October. She had been snorkelling with her husband, Nathan, and their sons. A strong current reportedly swept the boys away, and she entered the water to reach them. Both boys were rescued. She was not.

What happened

The Royal Bahamas Police Force said officers at Alice Town police station were told at about 10am that a woman was unresponsive at the North Bimini Ferry Dock. The incident was reported near the island's southern point. She was taken to a local clinic, where a doctor tried to save her but she was pronounced dead.

Police said foul play was not suspected. An autopsy will be carried out to establish the cause of death. The sons' ages have not been given in the reports available.

Nathan Gregory wrote that a "freak wave swept the boat away from her" and that she did not have the energy to save herself. He thanked his wife for keeping their son above water "even as you were sinking". Of her, he said she "was our foundation, our support, she made us who we are."

The anniversary

The trip was an early celebration of the couple's 10th wedding anniversary, which falls in December. That detail is the human-interest hook in the coverage, and it comes from the family. It does not change what is known about the circumstances of the death.

Aftermath

The Foreign Office said it was supporting the family and was in contact with local authorities. The family has since returned to the UK. Nathan Gregory has set up a GoFundMe in memory of his wife to support the boys. A report put donations at nearly £9,000.

The account rests on police statements and the husband's own words as relayed by UK outlets. Only the autopsy can establish the formal cause of death. Neither the police nor the Foreign Office has suggested any wider safety issue at the site, and nothing in the reporting points to one.
