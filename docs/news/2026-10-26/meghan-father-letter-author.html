Disputed letter about Meghan's father was written by his first wife, BBC understands

In one sentence
The BBC understands, from unnamed sources, that Thomas Markle's first wife wrote a 2019 letter to police about him; he denies the wider allegations, and the Met is reviewing its handling.

The BBC reports that a letter making claims about Thomas Markle, the father of the Duchess of Sussex, was written by his first wife, Roslyn Loveless. The corporation attributes this to what it "understands", which is a formula for information from unnamed people. It has not published the document or any on-the-record confirmation of who wrote it. The BBC's own report does not set out what the letter said.

What is established

The Metropolitan Police have confirmed that they received a letter in 2019. They say they shared it with Prince Harry and Meghan at the time but did not pass its contents to other law enforcement agencies. The force is now reviewing how it handled the matter. Those points rest on a police statement. The authorship rests on the BBC's sources.

Loveless and Markle divorced in 1975. They had two children, Samantha Markle and Thomas Markle Jr. Markle married Doria Ragland, Meghan's mother, in 1979, and Meghan was born in 1981. Loveless has since died. The sources consulted give no consistent date, so none is given here.

The wider story

The letter sits inside a much heavier story. Reports say that child sexual abuse images allegedly belonging to Markle were found at his former home in the Philippines. Markle denies this. Thomas Markle Jr. told The Sun that he found such material and told police. He has since apologised to his half-sister, saying he had been "protecting the wrong person" for nine years. He also contacted the FBI, which says it is aware of the allegations. Philippine authorities are investigating through an anti-trafficking and child-exploitation task force.

Markle told the Mail on Sunday that he had paid for sex on past visits to the Philippines. He denied sleeping with underage girls and denied ever possessing abuse images. His position is that the accusations are "sick lies".

On Friday, Meghan said she had been told of "disturbing allegations" and was deeply distressed. She said her thoughts were with any children who may have been harmed. She added that she had largely cut off contact with her father and does not allow him contact with her children. The BBC understands that she ended contact last year, after telling him that she and the authorities knew he had abused young girls. That too is an anonymously sourced line, and Markle has not accepted it.

How such claims travel

The authorship claim is a small one, but it shows how information moves in a family dispute that is also a public story. A letter reaches police in 2019. It passes quietly to the people it concerns. Years later its author is named, by sources, after the author has died and so cannot be asked. Nobody can check the claim against the writer, and the BBC says nothing in its report about whether it has seen the letter itself.

The authorship does not say whether the contents are true. A letter from a former spouse is an allegation. It is not evidence, and Markle's denials stand alongside it. The things that will settle matters are the police review of how the 2019 letter was handled and the Philippine task force inquiry. The Met has given no timetable for its review.
