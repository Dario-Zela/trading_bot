Carrick's Man Utd position weakens as fans blame the owners

In one sentence
After a 3-0 home defeat to Bournemouth, Man Utd fans sang for Carrick and aimed their anger at the owners, leaving his position weak but the blame spread wider.

Manchester United lost 3-0 at home to Bournemouth on Saturday, and the loudest noise at Old Trafford was not aimed at Michael Carrick. The boos went to the players as they left the pitch. The anger from the stands went higher still, to the owners. That makes the manager's weakening position look like a symptom of a wider dispute rather than a story about one man's tactics.

A run that has run out

According to BBC Sport's account, the defeat extended United's winless run to eight games, the club's worst sequence since 2015-16. It was the second heavy loss in a week. BBC Sport described Carrick's end as approaching. The Guardian reported that the club's hierarchy held crisis talks over whether to dismiss him, and the Telegraph said his future was hanging by a thread. United have not issued a statement on his position. These details come from secondary copies of the reports, since the original pages could not be opened, so the exact run and the status of those talks should be read with that caveat.

The booing, BBC Sport said, carried on until the last player, Diogo Dalot, had left the field, and Carrick had by then gone down the tunnel. Supporters sang his name. A manager who is losing his job does not usually get that.

Who the fans blame

Gary Neville, speaking on Sky Sports, put the distinction plainly. Carrick, he said, is popular with the fans, but the owners, the sporting department and the players are not. In his reading, the chants for Carrick were a kind of farewell, because supporters have seen this sequence before and know how it ends.

The Telegraph reported that furious fans directed their anger at the co-owners, Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer family, as well as Jason Wilcox, the sporting director, and the squad. The grievances are well worn. The Glazers' ownership has left the club carrying debt of roughly £1.1bn, with the interest costs that come with it. Ratcliffe's minority stake, which came with control of football operations, has been marked by high-profile hirings and firings, ticket price rises and sweeping cost cuts.

That is not new. Fan group The 1958 organised a march to Old Trafford in February, before a home game with Fulham, against what it called the club's "dysfunctional, inept ownership". Carrick, then interim manager, said he respected the supporters and did not feel offended by the protest. That stance now looks like a manager who knew the target was elsewhere.

What the evidence supports

The case that this is an ownership story rests on three things: Neville's comments, the reported direction of the crowd's anger, and the history of organised protest against the owners. What is missing is a named decision or a dated fan action for the coming weeks. No new protest has been announced in the reporting seen so far, and no poll or supporter survey backs the view that fans blame the owners over the manager.

There is also an awkward detail for the hierarchy. Carrick's interim spell last season was strong enough that he became the favourite for the permanent job, and he was given it. Dismissing a popular appointee after one bad run would hand the supporters exactly the argument they are already making.

What comes next

The immediate question is whether the board acts after the crisis talks, and whether United's next fixture brings a result to ease the pressure. If Carrick goes, the crowd's verdict on who is responsible is unlikely to go with him.
