Antonelli keeps the F1 title fight alive

In one sentence
Antonelli's Austin win leaves the Mercedes driver a few points short of clinching the F1 title, with the crown now likely to be decided in Mexico or soon after.

The headline on this one needs turning round. Kimi Antonelli's win in the United States Grand Prix did not keep a title fight alive. It left him a few points short of ending one.

The Mercedes driver took pole in Austin and won the race. Ferrari's Charles Leclerc finished second, 13.1 seconds behind, with Max Verstappen third and Lewis Hamilton fourth. Antonelli's team-mate George Russell, his closest rival in the standings for most of the season, came home eighth, almost a minute adrift. Russell called his own pace "rubbish" and said he could not explain it.

Why he is not champion yet

Antonelli could have clinched the title in Texas, but only if three things happened together. He had to win. Russell had to finish ninth or worse. Hamilton had to finish fifth or worse. He won, but Russell and Hamilton each finished one place too high.

The margin is vast. One report puts the standings at 365 points for Antonelli, 242 for Russell and 241 for Hamilton. That is a lead of 123 points with five Grands Prix left. Those figures come from a single report, so check them against the official table. The direction is not in doubt. Antonelli has led the championship since the Japanese Grand Prix in March, when he became the youngest leader in the sport's history at 19. His gap was 66 points after the Italian Grand Prix in September, where he beat Russell with a comeback drive.

The fight that is still alive is the one for second place. Russell and Hamilton are separated by a single point.

A season, not a duel

Earlier in the year it did look like a contest between team-mates. Antonelli won the Chinese Grand Prix, then Japan, and led by nine points after three races. Russell's retirements in Canada and Belgium, the second after a first-lap collision with Hamilton, turned a skirmish into a procession. Antonelli won both of those races.

The Austin result adds an awkward footnote for Mercedes. Russell's struggles in a car that carried his team-mate to a comfortable win are the sort of puzzle the team will want solved before the season ends. One report described a "Mercedes mystery" deepening.

What comes next

The series moves to Mexico City next weekend. Antonelli has a first chance to take the crown there, though the exact scenario was not given in the sources checked. Because the championship needs only a small further points swing, a strong weekend would do it. If Russell and Hamilton both score heavily, it moves on to Brazil.

Either way, the "champion who isn't the champion yet" line fits better than the title-fight framing. He is a champion in waiting, with a few points left to collect.
