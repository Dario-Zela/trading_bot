Texas carries out first US execution since the botched Christa Pike injections

In one sentence
Tennessee, not Texas, botched Christa Pike's lethal injection on 2 October; a Texas execution was set for 7 October but its outcome is unconfirmed.

The premise behind this story does not hold up. Christa Pike is held in Tennessee, not Texas, and it was Tennessee's lethal-injection protocol that failed on 2 October. A Texas execution was scheduled for 7 October, but no report seen by this desk confirms that it took place. Texas has not been shown to have botched anything.

What happened in Tennessee

Pike, the only woman on Tennessee's death row, was due to be executed on 2 October. The procedure was meant to begin at 10am. A federal appeals court halted it about an hour beforehand, the state appealed, and the US Supreme Court's conservative majority took roughly eight hours to overrule the lower court and clear the way for an injection of pentobarbital.

According to accounts carried by US broadcasters, Pike sang with her spiritual adviser. After several minutes she said her arm was hurting or burning and asked whether that was supposed to happen. Her lawyers filed an emergency motion during the procedure, citing witnesses who said both syringes had been emptied and that Pike was "still alive and snoring", as NBC News reported.

The attempt ended with Pike alive and, according to her attorneys, in critical condition. The Death Penalty Information Center says she is the only person to have remained alive after execution drugs were administered. Her lawyers said they did not know her chances of recovery. The Tennessee Department of Correction said it followed every step of a lawful protocol approved by the state attorney general's office. It said the drug had consistently been effective and that the protocol allowed no additional procedures beyond those carried out.

Pike's current condition, and whether Tennessee will try again, were not clear in the reports reviewed.

The Texas date

The Texas Department of Criminal Justice listed Jamaal Howard for execution on 7 October. Howard, born in 1980, was sent to death row in 2001 for shooting a convenience-store clerk in Silsbee, Hardin County, in May 2000. The department's page had not, in the material available, been updated to show an outcome, and no news report or court order found says the execution was carried out or stayed.

If it went ahead, it would be the first Texas execution since Tennessee's failed attempt. Even then, the novelty would lie in the timing. Tennessee's difficulties would say nothing about Texas's procedures, and Texas has not changed its protocol in response. Texas has also used pentobarbital for years, though that is a general observation and was not checked against current sources.

What to watch

The questions are in Tennessee. The first is Pike's medical condition and whether the state can lawfully attempt a second execution; her lawyers can be expected to argue it cannot. The second is whether courts or the state order any review of how the drugs were administered. For Texas, the first step is the corrections department's confirmation of what happened on 7 October. Texas lists two more executions for November: John Rubio on 12 November and Reinaldo Dennes on 19 November. Any challenges to those dates that cite Tennessee will show whether the failed injection has spread beyond one state.
