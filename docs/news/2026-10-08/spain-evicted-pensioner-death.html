Spanish pensioner whose eviction sparked nationwide protests has died

In one sentence
Maricarmen Abascal, 87, whose September eviction in Madrid triggered nationwide protests, has died, deepening Spain's housing row before a November election and general strike.

María del Carmen Abascal, the 87-year-old Madrid pensioner whose forced removal from her flat set off protests across Spain, has died. The Madrid Tenants' Union announced her death on Wednesday. She had not been able to go home.

Known as Maricarmen, Abascal was evicted on 23 September after an international investment fund bought her building and demanded a rent increase of more than 200 per cent, which reports put at roughly €1,600 to €1,900 a month. Outlets differ on the exact figures. Tenant groups say such increases are used to clear long-standing occupants so flats can be converted into tourist lets. Images of her being taken from the property on a stretcher, among protesters and police, spread quickly. Police reportedly used tear gas during the operation.

From one flat to a national argument

The response was swift. About 10,000 people marched in Madrid within hours, and hundreds went on to camp in the Puerta del Sol, the square that became the centre of Spain's 2011 indignados movement. After news of her death, those in the square held a minute of silence.

The union said a "historic agreement" had been reached that would have let her return to her home, but that the solution "came too late". It called her death "an entirely avoidable situation following a harrowing ordeal." Ángel Víctor Torres, the minister for territorial policy, said Abascal had become a symbol of what was happening to many families, old and young, who struggle to find a home or keep the one they have.

The politics

The case has landed in a political system already under strain. Parliament rejected decrees last week that were meant to curb evictions and limit rent pressure. Press reports link that defeat and the protests to Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's decision to call an early general election for 29 November. Trade unions have also announced a nationwide general strike over housing for 11 November.

There is a precedent. Spain saw a wave of anti-eviction activism after the 2008 property crash, when activists formed human chains to block bailiffs, mostly in cases involving mortgage default. The present anger differs in kind. The pressure now comes from rising rents and investor purchases of occupied buildings, rather than from repossessed mortgages.

What to watch

The general strike on 11 November and the election on 29 November are the next tests. They will show whether the movement holds its size beyond the shock of her death. Whether any housing measure passes in the meantime is the other question. Details of the circumstances of her death and of the aborted agreement to let her return have so far come largely from the tenants' union, and further accounts may refine them.
