Guardiola to attend Man City's first home game since the guilty verdict

In one sentence
Guardiola, who left City last summer, is reported to be returning to the Etihad next week as the club appeals a guilty verdict on 114 of 115 charges, with sanctions still undecided.

Pep Guardiola is expected back at the Etihad Stadium next week, the first time he will have been there since leaving Manchester City. The visit comes as the club faces the consequences of an independent commission finding it guilty of breaching the Premier League's financial rules.

The plan is reported, not confirmed. BBC Sport says Guardiola is set to attend City's first home game since the verdict. Other outlets, citing the Express, put it at the Champions League tie against Paris Saint-Germain on 14 October, after the international break. Neither City nor Guardiola had confirmed it at the time of writing, and the BBC's report was the only primary source checked for this piece.

What the verdict says

In late September a Premier League commission found City guilty on all but one of the 115 charges against them. The charges cover alleged breaches between 2009 and 2018, including inaccurate accounts and sponsorship deals the commission is reported to have regarded as 'sham', plus a failure to cooperate with the investigation from December 2018. The league's own process is not finished.

The point that matters most is that no sanction has been set. The available penalties run from fines and points deductions to the stripping of titles and expulsion from the league. Reports have called the likely outcome 'drastic', but that is speculation until the commission rules. City lodged a full appeal on 1 October, arguing the findings contain 'clear material errors, of law, principle and fact'. A fresh three-person panel would hear it, and the sanctions stage and the appeal could take months. Unlike City's earlier case over a Uefa ban, this one cannot go to the Court of Arbitration for Sport.

Why a visit counts

Guardiola left the club at the end of last season after a decade in charge, so he comes back as a former manager and not as the man in the technical area. That makes the gesture more pointed, not less. After the verdict he wrote on social media that he is 'behind my club' and that 'we will get through this together'. Reports say he wants to 'be with his people', and that chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak and the board will be in the stands too.

The statement sits awkwardly with something Guardiola said earlier: that he would no longer be the hierarchy's 'friend' if they lied to him about their innocence. Sports Mole noted the tension. A man who staked his word on the club's innocence is now standing beside it after a panel rejected that case on almost every count.

What to watch

The first marker is whether the 14 October appearance happens at all, and how City choose to stage it. Rival clubs and supporters will read any welcome as defiance. The second, and far weightier, is the commission's sanction decision and the timetable for the appeal. Until a penalty is announced, the Etihad visit is a show of solidarity, and the club's standing in the league remains undecided.
