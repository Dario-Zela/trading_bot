Samsung's Q3 profit guidance beats estimates as AI demand outstrips memory supply

In one sentence
Samsung guided to a record Q3 operating profit of about 107.4 trillion won (roughly $80bn), a narrow beat that shows AI-driven memory demand still outrunning supply.

Samsung Electronics has guided to a third-quarter operating profit of about 107.4 trillion won, a record for the company and for any South Korean firm. At current exchange rates that is roughly $80bn, which is the figure the BBC headline carries. The $80bn is a quarterly operating profit converted from won, not an annual total or a net profit. The scale looks startling beside Samsung's history, but it fits the company's own recent run.

Sourcing note: the figures below come from secondary outlets and Korean press reports. They could not be checked against Samsung's own newsroom release, and they should be read as provisional until that is done.

What the number is

The reported guidance is consolidated sales of about 195 trillion won and operating profit of 107.4 trillion won. That is up roughly 783% on a year earlier and about 20% on the second quarter's 89.4 trillion won, which was itself a record. It is the fourth straight record quarter, following 20.1 trillion won in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 57.2 trillion won in the first quarter of 2026.

The currency conversion holds up. Pre-release coverage translated 106.1 trillion won to $79.1bn and 108.1 trillion won to about $80.2bn. Both imply roughly 1,340 won to the dollar, so 107.4 trillion won comes out at about $80bn. Samsung guides in won, and the dollar figure moves with the exchange rate. Analysts have already cut estimates once this quarter because of won strength.

A beat, but a narrow one

The headline says the guidance beat estimates. How far depends on whose estimate is used.

  • One pre-release consensus put operating profit at 106.1 trillion won, so 107.4 trillion won is a beat of about 1.2%.
  • Hana Securities forecast 108 trillion won and IBK Securities 101.7 trillion won.
  • NH Investment & Securities had trimmed its consensus from 113.1 trillion won to 109.5 trillion won. Against that figure the result is a slight miss.

The preliminary release is also only a headline estimate. Samsung's guidance gives sales and operating profit only. Divisional detail, including how much of the profit came from memory, arrives with the full results later in October.

Why the memory cycle is the story

The first-quarter detail shows how concentrated the profit is. The chip division earned 53.7 trillion won of that quarter's 57.2 trillion won, about 94% of the group total. The cause is a supply squeeze: AI infrastructure spending has pushed up prices for the memory used in AI servers faster than makers can add capacity. Samsung said in April that it expected investment in AI infrastructure to keep expanding and to keep memory prices rising.

That is the read-across for SK Hynix, Micron and the equipment makers. A guidance figure at the top of the range implies that pricing power held through the summer. A flat second-to-third-quarter margin would be a different message. Some commentary has already pointed to stalled chip margins, and some analysts argue that the peak in memory prices may have passed. The sequential profit growth of 20% is slower than the 56% jump in the second quarter.

What to watch

The full third-quarter results are due later this month, with the divisional breakdown and any commentary on contract pricing and capacity for 2027. Until then, the guidance supports the claim that AI demand is still outrunning memory supply, but it does not show that the cycle is still accelerating.
