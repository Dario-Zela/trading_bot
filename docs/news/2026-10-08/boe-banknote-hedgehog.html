Bank of England picks a hedgehog as one of four animals for its new banknotes

In one sentence
The Bank of England has named wildlife designs for its next banknote series, with a hedgehog among four animals, though the other three and the timing have not been confirmed.

The Bank of England is putting wildlife on its banknotes. A hedgehog is one of four animals chosen for the next series, according to the BBC's business desk, which reported the selection on Thursday.

The detail is thin so far. The other three animals, the denominations each will appear on and the date the notes might reach wallets have not been confirmed in the material available to this desk. They are left out here rather than guessed at.

A change of cast

The notes in circulation today are a gallery of the human great and good. Winston Churchill is on the £5, Jane Austen on the £10, J.M.W. Turner on the £20 and Alan Turing on the £50. All four are polymer, introduced between 2016 and 2021. When the King's portrait began appearing on the front of the notes in 2024, the reverse designs stayed as they were.

A series built around animals would break with that tradition. A British banknote has never been a place to find a hedgehog, an animal that tends to turn up in the national imagination in children's books and garden-lawn conservation appeals rather than in the vaults of Threadneedle Street. Its selection reads as a deliberate nod to the familiar and the domestic. Why it was chosen over other candidates has not been reported in the sources available.

Note design is rarely whimsy. Portraits and animals alike have to survive the Bank's security brief: fine detail, tactile features and imagery that is hard to counterfeit. A hedgehog's spines may suit that task rather well, though the Bank has not said so.

What it does and does not change

The stakes are modest. This is a story about what is printed on future notes, not about interest rates, gilt yields or the Bank's next policy move. Nothing about the choice of animals bears on inflation or the pound.

It also does not alter the notes people carry now. The Bank has previously stated that current polymer notes remain legal tender until it announces a withdrawal date, and that it gives ample notice before doing so. Nothing reported so far suggests any change to that.

The design process is a long one. Years usually separate an announcement of themes from notes in tills, and cash use has fallen sharply over the past decade, which gives each redesign a slightly elegiac quality. A note that fewer people handle is also one that more people photograph, and a hedgehog is likely to do well on that front.

What to watch

The obvious next piece of news is the Bank's own announcement, which should name the other three animals, say which denominations they will appear on and give some indication of timing. Until the Bank publishes those details, the picture is of a hedgehog and three unnamed companions, with the schedule still unknown.
