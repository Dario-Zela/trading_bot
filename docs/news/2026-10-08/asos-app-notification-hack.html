Asos confirms hackers sent 'unauthorised' notifications to app users

In one sentence
Asos says hackers sent an unauthorised push alert claiming a data theft; it is investigating, says names and contact details may have been accessed, and has not confirmed the hackers' claim.

Asos, the British online fashion retailer, is investigating a security incident after hackers used its mobile app to push an unauthorised alert to customers' phones. The message was visible to anyone with the app installed, which made the breach public almost immediately.

The notification went out on Tuesday 6 October. According to reports, it was headed "Asos hacked" and linked to a Telegram account. The text read: "Dear ASOS DPO and IT, we have full compromised the Snowflake instance. Engage with us, or we will leak it." That is a ransom-style demand, addressed to the company's data protection officer rather than to shoppers.

What Asos says

The company has confirmed the alert was unauthorised. It has told customers to ignore it and not to click the link, both by email and in an in-app notice, and it has apologised.

Asos says it moved quickly to restrict access to the notification platforms. It is working with specialist advisers and the authorities. The company says personal information such as names and contact details "may have been accessed". It says payment-card details and account passwords do not appear to have been affected, and that the website and app are working normally.

The wording is cautious. "May have been accessed" does not say that data was taken, and Asos has not said how many of its roughly 17 million customers worldwide could be involved.

What is not established

The Snowflake claim is the hackers' own. Snowflake is a cloud data platform that many large companies use to store and analyse customer records. Asos has not confirmed that its Snowflake environment was compromised. Snowflake has said it investigated and found no compromise of its own platform. That fits a pattern seen before, in which attackers get into a customer's account rather than the vendor's systems. Nothing published so far says that is what happened here.

The means of the break-in is also unknown. Sending a push notification needs access to the messaging tools behind the app, not necessarily to the customer database. The alert shows that access was gained. It does not show how far the access went. The attackers have an obvious interest in making the breach sound as bad as possible.

Why it matters

Markets reacted before the facts were clear. Asos shares fell by more than 10% on Tuesday morning. The National Cyber Security Centre has offered help.

If customer data was exposed, Asos would face data-protection obligations under UK law. Organisations must generally tell the Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable breach, and tell affected individuals when the risk to them is high. Neither Asos nor the regulator has said publicly that such a report has been made, so that part remains open.

The next things to watch are whether Asos says what data, if any, was taken and from which system, and whether the Telegram group publishes samples to back its claim. A statement from the ICO would show whether the regulator is treating this as a reportable breach.
