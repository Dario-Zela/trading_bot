Tereina integrates Circle's USDC and EURC into its payments infrastructure

In one sentence
Tereina has reportedly integrated Circle's USDC and EURC, a small sign of stablecoin uptake in European payments whose significance depends on volumes not yet disclosed.

A payments company has said it will move stablecoins through its plumbing. Tereina has integrated Circle's USDC and EURC, the dollar and euro stablecoins issued by Circle, into its payments infrastructure, according to a Benzinga headline. That is nearly all that can be established. Searches turned up no company announcement, no independent coverage and no description of the deal's terms.

What is known

USDC is pegged to the US dollar and EURC to the euro. Circle says each is redeemable one-for-one for the underlying currency. Integrating both means a payments firm can, in principle, settle in either currency over a blockchain rail rather than through correspondent banks. A euro token alongside the dollar one is the more notable half of the pairing. Dollar stablecoins dominate the market, and euro-denominated ones remain a small niche by comparison.

What the integration involves is unclear. It could be settlement, customer-facing wallets, treasury management or merchant payouts. The Benzinga item as summarised does not say, and the choice matters. A back-office settlement option is a feature. A product that customers actually use is a business line.

What is not known

The scale of the deal is unreported. So are its timing, Tereina's customer base, and any volume figures or launch markets. The company itself could not be independently identified in a search of Circle's announcements and general coverage, so its size and regulatory status are unconfirmed. Until those gaps are filled, the item reads as a partnership notice rather than evidence of adoption.

The wider setting

The move fits a pattern Circle has been building. In July 2026 it launched a Payouts API for partners using Circle Mint France, allowing USDC and EURC disbursements to third-party hosted wallets through a single integration, with Travel Rule compliance tools built in, per Crowdfund Insider. Intended uses include merchant settlements, supplier payments and cross-border business-to-business transfers. Circle has also been extending both coins to further blockchains, including Plasma.

So the plumbing for euro stablecoin payments in Europe is being laid by the issuer as well as by its customers. A single integrator adds little to that on its own. It is one more small sign that euro stablecoins are being wired into European payment flows, no more.

What would change the picture

The brief becomes a story only if Tereina's volumes prove material or the EURC rail is used at scale. Three things would show it: a disclosed transaction figure, named customers going live, and a rollout beyond a pilot. None has yet been reported. Tereina or Circle publishing any of them would be the next data point worth checking.
