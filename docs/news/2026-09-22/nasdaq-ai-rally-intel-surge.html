Nasdaq 100 rallies as AI trade regains footing, Intel surges

Intel's headquarters in Santa Clara, California. — Lijorijo / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0)
In one sentence
Falling yields and oil, not fresh company news, drove Monday's Nasdaq record — though Intel's 14% jump on SK Hynix foundry talk gave the move a genuine catalyst.

US tech stocks jumped Monday as falling oil prices and retreating Treasury yields revived risk appetite across the market, with Intel the standout performer among a broad rally in semiconductor names. The Nasdaq 100 climbed to its highest level in three months, and the Nasdaq Composite closed at a record 27,122.09, up 2.26% — its first record finish since June.

Intel shares soared 13.71% to $123.49, the best performer in the Russell 1000 index, as reports built that SK Hynix could lease space in Intel's planned Ohio fab or form a joint memory-chip venture there. Tigress Financial raised its price target on Intel to $145 from $118, citing the company's Terafab plans, firmer Xeon processor demand and progress on its 18A manufacturing process. Intel stock has roughly tripled over the course of 2026.

A chip rally with company-specific legs

Intel was not alone. Advanced Micro Devices rallied 9.1% to $610.51, pushing its market capitalisation past $1 trillion for the first time, while Qualcomm added 7.5% and Micron rose 2.4%. That breadth matters: a rally confined to Intel could be dismissed as a single-name squeeze, but AMD's and Qualcomm's gains suggest investors were rotating back into semiconductors as a group.

The SK Hynix talks are still just that — talks. SK Hynix has stressed that no specific arrangement has been finalised, and any deal covering Intel's long-delayed Ohio complex would take years to materialise into revenue. What moved the stock Monday was optimism that Intel's costly foundry build-out might finally attract outside customers, following a second quarter in which Intel's data-centre and AI revenue rose 59% year-on-year and foundry revenue climbed 31%.

The macro backdrop did the heavy lifting

Beneath the chip-specific story, the broader market move was a mechanical response to two numbers moving the same direction. US crude fell about 3.5% to roughly $96.76 a barrel, its fourth straight decline, while Brent dropped nearly 2.9% to $100.85. The 10-year Treasury yield slipped back below 5%. Cheaper oil eases inflation expectations; lower yields reduce the discount rate applied to companies — like most large tech names — whose earnings are weighted toward the future. Put together, both moves mechanically inflate valuations for exactly the stocks that led Monday's advance, independent of anything those companies actually did.

That distinction is the crux of the story. AMD's jump to a trillion-dollar valuation and Intel's analyst upgrade are real, idiosyncratic developments. But the scale of the broader Nasdaq move — a 2.26% single-day gain to a fresh record — owes more to the retreat in yields and crude than to any one company's news.

What to watch

The open question is durability. If yields and oil stabilise or reverse — crude has swung sharply within a narrow band all month — the macro tailwind evaporates and Intel's move will need to stand on its own, resting on whether the SK Hynix talks produce an actual signed agreement rather than continued speculation. The next scheduled catalyst is Intel's fab and foundry updates expected alongside third-quarter results, still weeks away; until then, the SK Hynix headlines remain the only thread connecting Monday's rally to something more than a rate-and-oil trade.
