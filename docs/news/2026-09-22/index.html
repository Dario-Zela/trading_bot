BOE · RATES · By M. Halloway

Bank of England holds rate at 3.75%, pauses gilt sales in QT overhaul

The BoE held rates at 3.75% on a 6-3 split vote and paused gilt sales to 2027, easing long-end yields but leaving the rate path unresolved.

The Bank of England held interest rates unchanged on Monday, but a split decision and a sharp pivot on bond sales signalled mounting uncertainty within the central bank over inflation's trajectory and the timing of future rate cuts.

In a 6-3 Monetary Policy Committee vote, the BoE kept Bank Rate at 3.75% while announcing a halt to gilt sales—its quantitative tightening programme—until April 2027. The move sent 10-year yields down toward 5.2%, reflecting market relief that additional tightening has been paused.

The vote split reflects genuine disagreement among policymakers. While the BoE maintains that rate cuts remain some way off, the decision to freeze bond sales removes future upward pressure on yields and signals it sees inflation risk receding. For traders, the suspended QT and rate hold leave the path forward opaque: no immediate cuts, but tightening off the table.
