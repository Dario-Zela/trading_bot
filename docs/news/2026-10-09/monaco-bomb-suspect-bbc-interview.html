Monaco bomb-attack suspect speaks to the BBC from a Ukrainian prison

In one sentence
A suspect held in Ukraine over the Monaco bombing and the murder of the woman who planted the device tells the BBC that military intelligence approved it; the claim is unverified.

A man held in a Ukrainian prison on suspicion of murder has given the BBC an interview about a bomb attack in Monaco. He says the operation was sanctioned by Ukrainian military intelligence. That is one defendant's account, offered while he faces a murder charge, and it has not been independently confirmed.

What he says

The man is Vitalii Zhykovych, a former Ukrainian security officer. According to the BBC's account, he is one of two suspects in the killing of Anastasiia Berezovska, the woman filmed planting the device in Monaco. He describes himself as a "freelancer" for Ukraine's SBU security service and calls Berezovska his "agent".

Zhykovych says a man named Reut, whom he calls "The Boss", proposed the Monaco operation against the businessman Vadym Yermolayev. He says Reut told him it had been authorised by Reut's superiors in Ukraine's military intelligence agency, HUR, and that he took it to be an official mission.

He also says Reut wanted Berezovska to carry the bomb. Zhykovych says he refused because she was not fully trained, so she was sent on reconnaissance instead. He denies sending her to kill and denies killing her.

A killing, and two versions

Berezovska's body was found a week after the bombing, in woods west of Kyiv, with gunshot wounds to the head. Reut first confessed to investigators and led them to the shallow grave. Days later, in court, he changed his account and blamed Zhykovych. Each now blames the other for the shooting.

The interview does not settle that dispute. It adds a third question, which is who, if anyone, in Ukrainian intelligence approved the operation.

Reaction and what can be checked

HUR has previously said it is "committed to a full investigation" and is giving "comprehensive support" to law enforcement. The SBU has described Zhykovych in a statement only as a "former law enforcement officer". Berezovska's husband told the BBC the allegation against her was "nonsense" and said he believes she was drawn in unwittingly. Yermolayev has said he believes serving HUR officers, "individuals close to the agency's current and former leadership", were linked to the attack.

The two accounts that point at HUR, Yermolayev's and Zhykovych's, come from a victim and a defendant with obvious stakes. Neither is documentary evidence. The detail to look for is whether prosecutors produce anything beyond testimony, such as messages, payments or orders, that ties the operation to the agency.

The next marker is the court process in Ukraine. The thing to watch is whether the two suspects keep trading blame, and whether HUR's promised cooperation produces any public finding.
