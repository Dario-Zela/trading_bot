CMS publishes 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings: 37% of contracts earn four stars or more

In one sentence
CMS's annual Medicare Advantage star ratings reportedly put about 37% of contracts at four stars or more, the threshold for bonus payments that feed insurer earnings.

The federal government's annual report card for private Medicare plans is out, and by one account roughly 37% of Medicare Advantage contracts have earned four stars or more. The figure comes from Benzinga's report of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) release. It could not be checked against CMS's own tables before publication, and the published count may differ depending on whether it counts contracts, plans or enrolled members.

What the stars do

Medicare Advantage (MA) is the privately run alternative to traditional Medicare. CMS grades each contract on a one-to-five scale, using measures that include member satisfaction surveys, medication adherence, preventive screening rates and call-centre performance. The ratings are published each autumn, in time for the annual enrolment period that opens on 15 October, when beneficiaries compare plans.

Four stars is the line that matters financially. Under the programme's quality bonus rules, contracts rated four stars or above are eligible for bonus payments from CMS. Those payments are large enough that a half-star movement in a big contract can alter an insurer's revenue outlook for the following year. A rating published now shapes the 2027 plan year's bonus eligibility.

Why a 37% share is a talking point

A headline share of 37% says the four-star line is neither a formality nor out of reach. That sits in the context of a tougher run. For the 2026 ratings, CMS's average score fell to 3.65 from 3.92 a year earlier, according to Becker's Payer Issues. A contract-level share is also not the same as an enrolment-weighted one: the large national insurers hold much of the enrolment, so the share of members in bonus-eligible plans can look quite different from the share of contracts.

The 2026 cycle shows how wide that gap can be. UnitedHealthcare reported 78% of its MA members in plans rated four stars or higher, and CVS Health's Aetna more than 81%. Humana reported only about 20%, down sharply from earlier years, and said it expected to return to "top quartile results for the 2027 star ratings", per Fierce Healthcare. Whether the 2027-cycle results bear that out for Humana is the main insurer-specific question; it was not possible to confirm company figures for this cycle.

What to watch

CMS has also been rewriting the methodology. Its 2027 rule drops the Health Equity Index and reinstates a reward factor, among other changes, as described by Healthcare Dive. Those changes make year-on-year comparisons less clean than a single percentage suggests.

The next data points are the insurers' own disclosures of the share of members in four-star-plus plans, which typically follow the CMS release within days, and third-quarter earnings later this month, where guidance on bonus revenue and margins will show how much the scorecard moves the financials. Enrolment figures after the 15 October opening will show whether the ratings also draw members.
