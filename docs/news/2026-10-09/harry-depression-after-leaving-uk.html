Prince Harry says he 'slipped into depression' after leaving the UK

In one sentence
Prince Harry says he 'slipped into depression' after leaving the UK, per BBC UK; the original interview could not be checked, and the piece sets it against his 2021 remarks.

Prince Harry has said he "slipped into depression" after leaving the UK, in remarks reported by BBC UK. The comments are a fresh account of how the Duke of Sussex's mental health fared after he moved abroad.

The details of the original interview were not available when this piece was prepared. That covers its date, format and wording, and the quotation marks above come from the BBC headline alone. This article therefore adds no further direct quotes. It sets out what is established about Harry's earlier statements on the subject, so readers can judge how far the new remarks extend them.

A familiar theme, a new chapter

Harry has spoken about his mental health more often than almost any other senior royal, past or present. In a 2021 interview with James Corden, he said the British press had been "destroying my mental health" and that he needed to get his family out of the UK. That was the explanation he gave for stepping back from royal duties and relocating to North America with his wife, Meghan.

That earlier account placed the damage before the departure and treated the move as a remedy. The headline on the latest remarks points the other way. It describes a low period that followed the move, once the immediate pressures had been left behind. If the interview bears that out, it complicates the neat story in which leaving was the cure. It would also fit a pattern common in accounts of upheaval, where the strain of a big change surfaces only once the change has happened.

What the remarks do and do not show

A public figure's disclosure of depression is a personal matter first. It is not evidence of anything wider about the royal family, the press or the UK, and the available material does not support drawing such conclusions. The interpretive question is narrower: whether Harry is revising his own timeline, or simply adding detail to one he has long described.

The wider context for his audience is plain enough. Mental health has become a standard subject for public figures, and Harry has been a prominent voice in that conversation for years. Whether this latest account adds anything new will depend on the specifics of what he said about the period, the support he received, and how he describes his situation now. Those specifics could not be confirmed here.

What to watch

The full BBC report is the next thing to check, for the exact wording and the setting of the interview. Any response from Buckingham Palace or from Harry's own representatives would also help establish how the remarks are being received. Until the original is in hand, the headline claim is best read as reported, not independently verified.
