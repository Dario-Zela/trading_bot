How can the home nations qualify for the Women's World Cup?

In one sentence
None of the four home nations has qualified for Brazil 2027; each plays a two-legged UEFA play-off, with England at Greece today and round two due from 26 November.

Four teams, one continent-wide process, and no shared ticket. England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are separate FIFA member associations, so each must reach the 2027 Women's World Cup in Brazil on its own record. None of them has qualified yet. All four are in UEFA's play-offs, and the first legs start this week.

The tournament expands to 32 teams, up from 24 in 2023. Europe's share is 11 places outright, plus one slot in the inter-confederation play-offs. Four of those places went to the League A group winners — Denmark, France, Germany and Spain — after the league phase that ran from March to June. Every other side, home nations included, went into a two-round play-off draw.

The route

Round one is two-legged, played between 7 and 13 October. Round two follows between 26 November and 5 December, also over two legs. The seven round-two winners with the best overall league rankings go straight to Brazil. The eighth drops into the inter-confederation play-offs in February 2027.

That last rule matters. A win in round two does not guarantee a ticket, because league finish decides who is left holding the short straw. Standing in the Nations League-style pyramid counts for something even after the group games are over.

The four ties

The pairings below come from the June draw, as reported by the Irish News. Each home nation's league finish affects who plays the second leg at home.

  • England finished second in League A Group A3 behind Spain, with five wins and a defeat from six games. They face Greece, with the first leg on 9 October and the return on 13 October. The round-two opponent would be the winner of Slovakia against Ukraine. England are the reigning European champions, having beaten Spain 3-1 in Basel in July 2025, which makes a play-off an awkward place to be.
  • Wales won League B Group B1, with four wins and two draws and a goal difference of +12, and so earn promotion to League A. They meet Albania, with the second leg at home. Romania or Norway await in round two.
  • Scotland play the Czech Republic, with the second leg at home. Lithuania or Sweden would be next.
  • Northern Ireland finished third in League B Group B2, with two wins from six. They face Portugal and play the second leg away. Croatia or Iceland would follow.

The map is uneven. England and Wales are favoured on paper in round one, while Northern Ireland's away second leg is a reminder of how far down the seeding they sit. Scotland's league finish was not confirmed in the sources checked.

What to watch

England's tie opens in Greece today. The first hard data on the shape of round two comes on 13 October, when the round-one second legs are played. The pairings for the next stage are then set for late November and early December, when the seven direct places are decided.
