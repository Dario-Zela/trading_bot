Cummins unworried by potential Khawaja revelations ahead of the Ashes

In one sentence
Pat Cummins says he is unbothered by Usman Khawaja's forthcoming autobiography and its account of Sandpapergate, though nobody outside Khawaja's circle has read it.

Pat Cummins says he is not troubled by the prospect of Usman Khawaja revisiting the 2018 Sandpapergate affair in print. Khawaja is writing an autobiography, due next month, and it will be the first book by any Australian who played in that Cape Town Test.

The framing needs correcting. The triage description tied Cummins's remarks to the Ashes. The reporting does not. The book is due a few days after the end of Australia's first Test tour of South Africa since 2018, and the ABC's account of Cummins's comments does not mention the Ashes. The unexplained threat is real. The Ashes link is not.

What Cummins said

According to the ABC, Khawaja rang his captain to say he was writing the book. Cummins said Khawaja "didn't go into too much detail" and that he told Khawaja he was free to tell his own story. Asked what it might contain, Cummins said: "You would have to ask him." His broader line was that "as a group we've moved on." Cricinfo reports that he added he has "answered every question a million times" in the eight and a half years since.

That is a practised answer. It is also a careful one. It does not say the book holds nothing awkward. It says the squad has made its peace with whatever it holds.

What is known about the book

The book is reported to be titled Sabr, with a release date of 10 November. Khawaja wrote it without a ghost-writer, and the publisher, Penguin Random House, has not circulated advance copies, according to the reports. Nobody outside Khawaja's circle has read it, so any claim about its contents is guesswork.

The background is well documented. David Warner received a 12-month ban for his part in the 2018 ball-tampering plan. Steve Smith was suspended for a year and Cameron Bancroft for nine months. A Cricket Australia investigation found that no other player knew of the sandpaper plan. Khawaja was in the side that day and is a childhood friend of Warner. A first-hand account from the dressing room, from a player who was never sanctioned, is something the story has not had before.

What to watch

Two dates matter. The first Test in Durban will show how the squad handles the questions before the book appears. Publication on 10 November will then show whether it reopens a settled account or simply adds colour to one. Until then it is a watch item. Cummins has said he is unbothered, and nothing yet tests that.
