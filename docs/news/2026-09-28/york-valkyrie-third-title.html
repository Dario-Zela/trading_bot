York Valkyrie beat Wigan to win third straight Women's Super League title

In one sentence
York Valkyrie beat Wigan 8-6 at Wigan's own ground to win a third Women's Super League title in four years — the most in the competition's history, but not consecutive.

York Valkyrie held out Wigan Warriors 8-6 in front of a raucous home crowd at the Brick Community Stadium on Sunday, surviving eighteen unbroken defensive sets in the second half to become the first club to win three Women's Super League Grand Finals.

The scoreline undersells how little separated the sides. York's captain, Sinead Peach, dummied and spun her way over for the game's opening try inside nine minutes; Wigan hit back through Mia Atherton to level it before half-time. Atherton's yellow card for a tip tackle proved the difference — Ellie Williamson slotted the resulting penalty to put York 8-6 up, and the visitors spent the rest of the match simply refusing to let Wigan back in. Amelie Lenham, named player of the match, made three try-saving tackles in a rearguard the York club described afterwards as one of the most courageous defensive performances in the competition's history.

A title, not a run

The scale of the achievement needs a caveat the club's own celebrations skated past: this is York's third Grand Final win in four years, not three in a row. Wigan won the title in 2025, beating St Helens 16-12 to complete a treble of Grand Final, League Leaders' Shield and Challenge Cup — and arrived at Sunday's final chasing a second. That defending champions with home advantage and the League Leaders' Shield in the cabinet could still lose by two points to a side that finished fourth in the regular season is the more interesting story than the raw title count. York's three championships (2023, 2024, 2026) are still the most any club has won since the Grand Final format began in 2017, but the gap in between shows a competition that punishes complacency rather than one dynasty running away with it.

That fits the season York actually had. The Valkyrie flirted with missing the play-offs altogether before winning three of their last four regular-season games to scrape into third, then needed a shutout win away at Leeds in the semi-final just to reach Wigan's ground. It is also the first season in charge for head coach Leon Pryce, the former Bradford, St Helens and Great Britain international, whose side went from also-rans to champions inside a single campaign.

What's next

Wigan will point to their bye through the League Leaders' Shield and a squad still good enough to reach three of the last four finals; York have now shown they can win it as both favourites and underdogs. The rematch question — whether Wigan can reclaim the shield they built a treble on, or York can finally string two titles together back-to-back for the first time — starts again when the 2027 Women's Super League season kicks off next spring.
