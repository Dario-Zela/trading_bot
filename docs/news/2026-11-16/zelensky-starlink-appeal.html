Zelensky urges US and Musk to lift Starlink restrictions

In one sentence
Zelensky is urging Washington and Musk to lift Starlink limits on Ukrainian forces, a dispute over who imposed the caps and what they cost in drone defence.

Ukraine's president has taken a long-running dependency to a public stage. Volodymyr Zelensky is urging Washington and Elon Musk to lift limits on how Ukrainian forces use Starlink, the satellite internet service that carries much of the army's battlefield communications. The appeal, reported by BBC World, has been the only source behind this account. The detail below comes from Zelensky's own earlier remarks, reported by Ukrainian and US outlets, and is not independently verified.

What the restrictions are

The constraint described in Zelensky's recent interviews is a cap on speed, not a blackout. Starlink and Starshield, the SpaceX services used by Ukrainian units, were slowed in October. Speaking to NBC's Meet the Press on 18 October, Zelensky said service had not been fully restored. Asked about the cap, he said it was "maximum 161 km/h" — apparently a reference to the speed limit on connected terminals, which matters for fast-moving platforms such as drones.

The practical cost, he told CNN's Christiane Amanpour, is in air defence. Ukraine has built high-speed interceptor drones to bring down jet-powered Shahed-type attack drones, and a throttled link blunts them. "Now it's still limited," he said, adding: "you cannot intercept anything." CNN reported that some units saw faster connections after a reported restoration, but unevenly, and that speeds remained "clearly not enough" for the task, according to a source.

Who decided, and who denies it

The origin of the limits is disputed. On NBC, Zelensky said "the message came from the government" and that SpaceX carried out the change, without naming an official or agency. On CNN days later he said he did not know whether the decision came from the company or from government institutions, though he noted SpaceX could technically impose it.

Washington has denied involvement. Donald Trump said on 16 October that the White House had nothing to do with it, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the administration "had nothing to do with" the restriction, describing Starlink as a commercial product run by SpaceX. The Pentagon denied ordering a slowdown. The Telegraph reported that Musk himself proposed slowing the services to Trump; that has not been confirmed. Trump later credited Musk with restoring service.

The argument predates October. In late July, Zelensky asked Trump to help win Musk's consent for wider Starlink use in strikes inside Russia, a step Musk is reported to have regarded as dangerous escalation. Zelensky has said Ukraine does not use Starlink to attack Russian military or energy targets.

Why it matters

The dispute exposes a structural point more than a personal one. Whether the order came from Washington or from Musk's company, the effect is the same: a Western-backed army's communications run on a commercial network whose terms of access can change without a vote, a treaty or a published rule. Kyiv has little to bargain with beyond public pressure and appeals to Trump.

The scope of what Zelensky is now asking for, and whether it covers the speed cap, the use inside Russia, or both, is not clear from the single report. Watch for a response from SpaceX or the White House, and for any confirmation from Ukrainian units that speeds have changed.
