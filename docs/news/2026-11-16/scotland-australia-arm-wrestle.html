Scotland's defence holds in an 'arm wrestle' against Australia

In one sentence
Scotland beat Australia 15-12 at Murrayfield, with Gregor Townsend calling it one of the team's best defensive performances in years, and moved up to fourth in the table.

Scotland beat Australia 15-12 at Murrayfield in a Nations Championship fixture that never loosened its grip, and their head coach, Gregor Townsend, said afterwards that the defence was the reason. The win came a week after a loss to New Zealand and lifted Scotland back above Wales into fourth place in the Northern Hemisphere table.

A match decided by tackles

The game was low on fluency and high on contact. Scotland led 12-5 at half-time after tries from Matt Fagerson and Jack Dempsey, then spent much of the second half absorbing Australian pressure rather than adding to the total. Finn Russell's late penalty stretched the margin from five points to eight, and Townsend defended the choice to kick for the posts rather than chase a third try.

"I think the right decision was to go for the three points to get more than a score ahead."

The call mattered. Australia's Max Jorgensen scored in the 80th minute, following Harry Potter's earlier try, and the final gap was three points rather than the one it would have been without Russell's kick. Australia's 12 points were their lowest return since a 25-7 loss to England in November 2025.

Townsend was unusually emphatic about the defensive work.

"I think that's one of our best defensive performances over the last few years," he said. "The shots going in in the first half and the effort that went into the second half was outstanding."

He added that Scotland "deserved the win given the effort that went in and the fact we led throughout the game." It was Scotland's fifth win in six meetings with Australia.

Absences and what comes next

Scotland managed it without their captain. Sione Tuipulotu was ruled out before kick-off with a knee injury, and lock Scott Cummings left in the first half with a thigh problem. Townsend said an ultrasound scan looked encouraging but that an MRI is likely, and he described Cummings as a doubt for next week.

That matters because the table is tight and the margin for error is small. Scotland missed a bonus point against Australia, which limits how far they can climb, and Townsend said a bonus-point win would be needed to go higher. Japan are the next opponents, and the fitness of Cummings and Tuipulotu will shape the team Townsend names for that game.
