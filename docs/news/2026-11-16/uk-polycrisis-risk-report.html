Risk assessment warns of UK 'polycrisis' and government complacency

In one sentence
A House of Lords committee says Britain is unready for overlapping 'polycrisis' emergencies, accuses ministers of complacency, and urges paid time off for emergency training.

Britain is poorly placed to cope with several emergencies arriving at once, and the government is not treating the problem with enough urgency. That is the verdict of the House of Lords National Resilience Committee, whose report Are We Ready? Building a Culture of Resilience was published on 16 November after a nine-month inquiry. The peers call the country's emergency planning "out of date, underfunded and untested" and describe the UK as "seriously underprepared for escalating threats and emergencies".

The polycrisis argument

The committee's central worry is not any single hazard. It is that crises now overlap or trigger one another, a pattern it labels a "polycrisis". Baroness Coussins, who chairs the cross-party committee, gave the example: "A cyber attack can quickly morph into a power cut, then a shutdown of transport, then empty food shelves in the shops."

Britain might cope with a bout of flooding or a fuel shortage taken alone. The peers' point is that current plans do not prepare central and local government for several happening together. They describe the UK as "acutely vulnerable" because it relies on a narrow set of suppliers for food, water, medicines and fertiliser.

The report offers some figures. About 95% of UK food is sold through just nine retailers. The country imports 80% of the active ingredient for penicillin-type antibiotics from China, and its last domestic antibiotic plant closed in 2025. Recovering from cyber attacks is put at £14.7bn a year.

Paid time to train

The proposal that may travel furthest beyond Westminster concerns people rather than infrastructure. The peers say workers should be able to take paid time off to train for national emergencies. There is currently no legal entitlement to paid leave for those who volunteer in roles such as coastguard rescue, firefighting or St John Ambulance.

The committee has not set an exact entitlement. It suggests "proportionate arrangements" designed with employers, which leaves the cost, and who bears it, unresolved. That is the obvious point of friction: small firms may be wary of a duty to release staff, and no figure has been put on the bill.

What the peers want from ministers

On governance, the committee asks for:

  • a cabinet minister with sole responsibility for resilience;
  • a coherent UK resilience strategy within six months;
  • an annual statement on resilience from the prime minister;
  • rebuilt domestic manufacturing of vital medicines, and more varied supply chains for medicine, food and water;
  • an information booklet for every household and an app modelled on Finland's;
  • scrapping plans to switch off digital terrestrial television.

The report also takes aim at the way government talks to the public. Trust in central government is low, the committee says, but people would respond better to candour. Ministers, Baroness Coussins said, "should stop being squeamish about telling it straight to the public".

Complacency, and a familiar pattern

The committee does give some credit. It says the government's Be Ready campaign, which urges households to hold three days' worth of essentials, shows "the right ambition". But it argues that leadership, funding and urgency are still missing.

The warning also echoes an earlier one. A Lords committee on risk assessment concluded in 2021 that the pandemic had exposed a risk-management system that was "deficient and too inflexible", and urged annual parliamentary scrutiny of the National Security Risk Assessment. Ministers accepted some of those recommendations. The new report suggests peers think the follow-through has been thin.

No official government response had been reported at the time of writing. The first test is whether ministers accept the six-month deadline for a resilience strategy, and whether the paid-leave idea is taken up or quietly shelved.
