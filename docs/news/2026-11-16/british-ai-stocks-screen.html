Three British AI-exposed stocks screened

In one sentence
A Simply Wall St-style screen of three UK 'AI-exposed' stocks could not be confirmed: no companies were named, and £4.7bn appears to be one Haleon regional segment, not a total.

A familiar genre returned to the investor-news feeds this week: a screen of three UK-listed companies said to be exposed to artificial intelligence, with revenue running as high as £4.7bn. The format is a Simply Wall St staple, in which a filter is applied to a stock universe and the survivors are written up with a few ratios. The sums can be checked more easily than the premise.

What the screen does not say

The item as it reached the desk names no companies, gives no per-company revenue and carries no date. The one source, Simply Wall St, is a stock-screening and investor-education publisher. It does not publish a fixed definition of "AI-exposed" that carries from one article to the next.

That looseness shows in the archive. Simply Wall St's recent UK AI lists, syndicated on Webull, include "3 British AI Stocks With Up To 14% Revenue Growth", a 17% variant, a healthcare edition and a cybersecurity-spending edition. The "AI" label spans software vendors, a cloud-hosting firm, drugmakers and a consumer-health group.

The £4.7bn figure

A search found no Simply Wall St article that puts £4.7bn on a single company's total revenue. The number does appear in the healthcare editions, as Haleon's revenue from Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America. The same pieces put its North American revenue at £3.8bn and Asia-Pacific at £2.6bn. The £4.7bn is therefore a regional segment, not a group total. The headline's "up to £4.7bn" may be a misreading of that line, though the candidate's source article could not be matched, so that is an inference.

The spread within these lists is wide. Bytes Technology, a software reseller that features in some of them, reports revenue of about £220m. Putting it beside a consumer-health group with a market value above £30bn shows that the screens select on a tag, not on size or business model.

What would make it news

A screen alone does not move a share price, and none of the claims here is confirmed against company filings. A piece on these stocks would need a hook: a results release, a contract win or a re-rating. None has been identified. The more consequential AI-and-markets stories this edition sit elsewhere, notably the report that Washington will name Nvidia among AI "national champions".

The next data points are the companies' own half-year and full-year trading updates. A reader wanting to use any such list should check the named firms' revenue lines against those filings, and check what, specifically, each firm earns from AI.
