Teenager Mirra Andreeva wins WTA Finals title

In one sentence
Mirra Andreeva, 19, beat Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 at Indian Wells to win the WTA Finals on debut without dropping a set, rising to a career-high world No. 2.

Mirra Andreeva has won the WTA Finals, beating Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 in Sunday's final at Indian Wells to take the season-ending championship on her first appearance. She is 19. She did not drop a set all week.

The result also corrects the tidy assumption that the tour's showpiece wrapped up early this month. It finished on 15 November, and the champion was worth the wait.

The final

It was closer than the scoreline suggests. Andreeva served for the first set at 5-4, then hit two double faults and a long backhand to let Svitolina back in. She broke in the next game and held on to take the set after 55 minutes.

The second turned on a 10-minute seventh game, the longest of the tournament, which went to six deuces before Svitolina held for 4-3. It was the last thing she won comfortably. Andreeva held easily, broke for the sixth time in the match and closed it out when Svitolina put an overhead into the net on match point. The whole thing took one hour and 48 minutes.

Afterwards Andreeva credited her team, led by coach Conchita Martinez. "I'm super thankful to my team for helping me work hard every day and pushing me to my limits," she said.

Svitolina, 32, the 2018 champion, is back at this level after pregnancy. "Hopefully it inspires some ladies and athletes around the world to believe that it's possible," she said. She did not shake hands at the net, consistent with her long-standing stance on Russian and Belarusian opponents.

The week

Andreeva's route was clean. She beat Svitolina 6-4, 6-0 in the round-robin stage, then Aryna Sabalenka, and later got past Jessica Pegula in the semi-final to set up the rematch. Five matches, five wins, no sets lost: reports describe her as the first player to win the title on debut while unbeaten throughout.

The reward was the maximum $3.37m in prize money, the Billie Jean King Trophy and, from the sponsor, a custom Mercedes-Benz AMG SL 55 in the WTA's purple. She does not yet have a driving licence.

She is the youngest champion at the event since Maria Sharapova in 2004. The title is her fourth of 2026 and her seventh overall, and it lifts her to a career-high world No. 2 in Monday's rankings, within 300 points of Elena Rybakina at the top.

A displaced event

The tournament was staged in California only because it was moved from Riyadh, which was changed because of the US-Israeli war on Iran. It goes to Charlotte, North Carolina, next year. Andreeva will be among those arriving with a target on her back.

In doubles, Katerina Siniakova and Taylor Townsend beat Sara Errani and Jasmine Paolini in the final.

The next test is the new season's opening Grand Slam in Melbourne in January, where Andreeva will be one of the players to beat. A rankings gap that small at the top will not stay closed for long.
