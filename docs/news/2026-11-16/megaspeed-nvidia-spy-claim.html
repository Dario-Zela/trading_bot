Megaspeed says suspected China spy tied to Nvidia's ex-boss left in 2025

In one sentence
Megaspeed says Alice Huang, whom US officials suspect of being a Chinese asset, left in August 2025, as Nvidia's biggest Southeast Asian buyer faces smuggling probes in Washington and Singapore.

A Singapore-based chip company at the centre of a US-China smuggling inquiry has said that the woman US officials suspect of being a Chinese intelligence asset left its ranks more than a year ago. The statement, issued by Megaspeed on Sunday, answers some of the allegations against it. It leaves the central one, that Nvidia's most advanced AI chips reached China through a third-country intermediary, unanswered.

Much of the reporting here comes from the BBC, CBS News and others, and several details rest on a single outlet. They are attributed below.

What Megaspeed said

Megaspeed told reporters that Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, left the company in August 2025 and has no current role there. It did not address the suspicion that she is an asset of China's Ministry of State Security, a claim Beijing denies. "Megaspeed does not comment on Ms. Huang's personal and private matters," the company said, according to CBS News.

The company went further on its own conduct. It said it "conducts legitimate and lawful business", that recent assertions were "not supported by verifiable evidence", and that its controls are designed to comply with the law, including US export controls. It said it has hired outside legal counsel for an internal investigation.

Her departure date is not settled in the coverage. One account says she also stepped down as a director near the end of 2023, so the timeline of her formal roles is unclear. The Washington-based security analysis group C4ADS says she remained "practically in control" of Megaspeed after handing over the company. That claim directly contradicts the idea that her exit ended her influence.

Alice Huang has no family relationship to Jensen Huang, the Nvidia founder who resigned as chief executive last week. CBS News reports that he had a personal relationship with her. It also reports that President Trump said the FBI was investigating their romantic and business ties. Nvidia's own position on those claims was not included in the reports reviewed.

The allegation: a distributor as a conduit

US investigators say Alice Huang used industry contacts and shell companies to reach about 240,000 chips that are barred from export to China, according to CBS News. Megaspeed has also been suspected of giving Chinese companies backdoor access to Nvidia hardware through data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand, the BBC reports.

The scale is what makes the case notable. According to The New York Times, as cited by CBS, Megaspeed bought nearly $5bn of AI chips and hardware from Nvidia over three years, making it one of the company's largest foreign customers. It began as an offshoot of a China-based gaming firm. That origin is one reason regulators have been wary of it.

The case shows how export controls can fail in practice. Washington restricts sales of advanced chips to China, but it does not regulate every buyer in every third country. A distributor in a permissive jurisdiction can buy legally, host the machines in regional data centres, and sell access remotely. Singapore does not prohibit moving advanced AI chips abroad, though such transfers can breach US rules. It does not enforce other countries' unilateral export controls, but says it expects companies to respect them.

Investigations on several fronts

Singapore has looked at Megaspeed before. One account says police investigated Alice Huang in February 2025. CBS reports that authorities seized her passport in June 2025 while seeking US government support, and a source told it that Singapore received no meaningful help from Washington at the time. The two accounts differ on dates, and neither could be checked against official records.

The political fallout is now largely American. Representative John Moolenaar says Jensen Huang has agreed to testify before several House committees, with closed-door dates still being finalised. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has said Nvidia should hire its next chief executive from outside the company. According to the BBC, he has also blamed the Commerce Department, led by Howard Lutnick, for failing to police high-end chip exports. He said several agencies had favoured putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months. Lutnick is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month.

Beijing has rejected the framing. It denies that Alice Huang is a spy and has warned Washington against using the affair as a "pretext for imposing a technological blockade" on AI technology.

The BBC understands that Jensen Huang's chances of returning as chief executive are very low while Nvidia's internal investigations continue. Some tech leaders, including Elon Musk, reportedly want him reinstated.

What to watch

Three things will show how far the allegations hold. The first is whether Commerce adds Megaspeed to the entity list, which would cut it off from US-origin technology. The second is what Megaspeed's outside counsel finds, and whether the company publishes it. The third is Lutnick's Senate testimony next month, along with the dates of Jensen Huang's House appearances. Until then, the claims about Alice Huang and Megaspeed's chips remain allegations. The company denies wrongdoing, and no charges have been reported.

The case also bears on Nvidia's wider standing in Washington, where the company is reportedly in line to be named among the administration's AI "national champions".
