FDA clears wave of new drugs including multiple myeloma and narcolepsy treatments

The FDA's White Oak campus in Silver Spring, Maryland, where both approvals were finalized in August. — Michael J. Ermarth / U.S. Food and Drug Administration (public domain)
In one sentence
FDA cleared Bristol Myers Squibb's Zenbexus for multiple myeloma and Takeda's Orzeyful for narcolepsy type 1 in August — here's whose revenue each one actually offsets.

The Food and Drug Administration cleared two notable therapies in August — one for a blood cancer that recurs even after treatment, one for a sleep disorder that has never had a drug addressing all of its symptoms at once. Both are worth a closer look, because the trading logic behind FDA approvals only works once you know whose balance sheet it lands on.

The myeloma approval: a patent-cliff rescue for Bristol Myers Squibb

On August 13, the FDA granted accelerated approval to Zenbexus (iberdomide), made by Bristol Myers Squibb, for use alongside Johnson & Johnson's Darzalex and dexamethasone in adults with multiple myeloma who have relapsed after at least one prior line of therapy. It is the first approved drug in a class called CELMoDs — cereblon E3 ligase modulators — and the first time the FDA has accepted minimal residual disease (MRD) negativity, rather than the usual overall-survival or progression data, as the basis for accelerated approval in myeloma.

The underlying trial, EXCALIBER-RRMM, found 41% of patients on the Zenbexus combination reached MRD-negative complete response, versus 21% on a standard daratumumab-bortezomib-dexamethasone regimen (p<0.0001).

The timing matters for BMS specifically. Its older myeloma franchise is eroding fast: Revlimid sales fell 49% year-on-year to $425 million in the second quarter as generics took share, and Pomalyst sales dropped 71% to $204 million after its own patent cliff. William Blair analysts project Zenbexus could clear $1 billion in annual sales by 2031 — a plausible but not yet proven offset. BMS shares (NYSE: BMY), trading in the mid-$60s, rose on the news and held those gains through a subsequent earnings update in which the company raised 2026 revenue guidance to roughly $49.5 billion.

The narcolepsy approval: a small but symbolic Takeda bet

A week earlier, on August 5, the FDA approved Orzeyful (oveporexton) from Takeda Pharmaceutical, the first drug to treat the full symptom range of narcolepsy type 1 — excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hallucinations and disrupted night sleep — by directly restoring orexin signaling rather than masking individual symptoms. Two placebo-controlled trials covering 273 adults showed significant improvement on wakefulness and cataplexy measures over 12 weeks; the most common side effects were insomnia and increased urination.

Takeda has pegged peak sales at $2 billion–$3 billion, though some analysts model a risk-adjusted figure closer to $1.8 billion, and the company says it does not expect a material revenue impact in the fiscal year ending March 2027. That caution is notable: Takeda (NYSE: TAK; TSE: 4502) is leaning on Orzeyful as one of three prospective blockbusters meant to fill the gap left by biosimilar erosion of Entyvio, its top seller. Shares rose roughly 3% on approval day and are up nearly 16% year-to-date, though the move looks more like sentiment confirmation than a repricing of the stock's underlying earnings power.

What to watch

Neither approval is a binary trading event on its own — both drugs were already priced into analyst models before the FDA acted, and both companies face the same test now: how fast oncologists and sleep specialists actually prescribe them. The clearer signal is what each approval offsets. BMS's Zenbexus ramp needs to outrun the Revlimid and Pomalyst declines quarter by quarter; Takeda's Orzeyful needs to show up in guidance before the Entyvio patent cliff bites harder. Third-quarter earnings from both companies, due in late October and early November, will carry the first real prescribing data.
