Royal Mail misses delivery targets again despite turnaround plan

A Royal Mail delivery van in Mundesley, Norfolk. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Royal Mail again missed Ofcom's delivery targets in Q1 despite management calling its £500m turnaround plan's trajectory 'encouraging.'

Royal Mail's five-year turnaround plan is producing better numbers than a year ago. It is not producing compliant ones.

Figures released this week show that in the three months to the end of June, Royal Mail delivered 85% of first-class post the next working day and 91.4% of second-class post within three working days. Both fall short of the delivery thresholds Ofcom set for the quarter — 90% and 95% respectively — thresholds the regulator had already lowered from 93% and 98.5% when it overhauled the universal service obligation on April 1.

Jamie Stephenson, Royal Mail's chief operating officer, called the results "encouraging" and said first-class performance was running "well ahead of where we expected to be at this stage of our improvement plan," with second-class "tracking in line with the plan." Measured against a year earlier, the improvement is real: first-class was at 76% and second-class at 89.3% in the same quarter of 2025. Royal Mail also cleared the newer backstop Ofcom introduced alongside the lower targets — a requirement that 99% of mail arrive no more than two days late — hitting 98.1% for first-class within three days and 98.4% for second-class within five.

A pattern, not a blip

What undercuts the encouraging framing is the record. Royal Mail missed its statutory targets for the 2024-25 year so badly — 77% first-class against a 93% goal, 92.5% second-class against 98.5% — that Ofcom fined it £21 million in October. It then missed again for 2025-26, delivering just 75.7% of first-class post on time against that year's target, prompting Ofcom to open a fresh investigation in June into the second consecutive year of failure. Against that backdrop, a quarter of renewed misses under a newly softened target regime reads less like a company nearing its goal and more like one for which underperformance has become the baseline.

The turnaround itself carries real money behind it. Since Czech billionaire Daniel Křetínský's EP Group completed its takeover of parent company International Distribution Services in 2025, Royal Mail has committed £500 million over five years to hit Ofcom's standards by May 2027, including a new delivery model it expects to have rolled out across all 1,200 delivery offices by Christmas — a rollout itself delayed earlier this year while the company negotiated terms with postal unions.

Citizens Advice has used the latest miss to press Ofcom for a cap on stamp prices while performance remains below target, arguing customers are paying more for a service that still falls short of what regulation requires. For a company trying to convince investors and a regulator that this time the plan will stick, the credibility test is not whether the trend line moves up — it has — but whether it crosses the line Ofcom actually drew. The next quarterly figures, due in November, will show whether the autumn peak-season strain widens the gap again or whether the office rollout starts closing it.
