Bitcoin surges toward $75,000 on crypto-friendly White House signals

In one sentence
Bitcoin jumped 22% in a week to a three-month high on a Treasury bond buyback and Trump's CLARITY Act push, but much of the move was short covering, and the bill still needs seven Senate Democrats it doesn't have.

Bitcoin climbed above $75,000 early Friday, its highest level since May, capping a week in which the token gained roughly 22% and briefly touched $77,692. The move was fast even by crypto standards — more than $10,000 added in two days — and it arrived courtesy of two events that had nothing to do with each other converging within 24 hours.

The first was a Treasury Department move: Secretary Scott Bessent announced an expansion of the long-end bond buyback program, roughly doubling the size of purchases covering 10-to-20-year and 20-to-30-year debt, effective September 9. Thirty-year Treasury yields had been sitting near their highest since 2007, pressured by Iran-related risk premia and a widening federal deficit. When the buyback eased that pressure and yields pulled back, some of the money that had been demanding a safe return went looking for a riskier one instead.

The second was political. On August 19, President Trump hosted crypto executives — including the heads of Coinbase, Kraken, Robinhood, Ripple and Chainlink — at the White House and urged the Senate to pass "a fair version" of the CLARITY Act, the stalled market-structure bill that would split oversight of digital assets between the SEC and the CFTC. "Keep us ahead of China, keep us ahead of everyone else," Trump said of the legislation.

A rally with a mechanical assist

What happened next had less to do with new money entering crypto than with money already betting against it being forced out. Traders positioned for a pullback found themselves squeezed as prices rose, and roughly $2.7 billion in bearish crypto positions were liquidated across the market — with over $1 billion of that in bitcoin alone within a single hour, by some estimates. Liquidations beget more liquidations: as short positions get closed out, the buying pressure that results pushes prices higher still, forcing the next tranche of shorts to cover. Ether rose in tandem, up more than 25% on the week to $2,363, while Solana, XRP, BNB and Dogecoin all posted double-digit gains.

That mechanical dynamic is the crux of the skepticism analysts have voiced since. A rally built substantially on short covering is not the same as one built on sustained new demand, and several trading desks have described the move as a liquidity event rather than a durable shift in appetite for the asset — some have gone as far as predicting a "final flush" toward the $44,000–$48,000 range once the squeeze exhausts itself, before any genuine bull run resumes. Others read the Treasury and White House signals as evidence that policy is now doing real work under the price, not just providing a headline. The market itself is split, which is a fair description of where the evidence leaves things too.

The legislation the rally is pricing

The CLARITY Act, for its part, is still not law. The Senate Banking Committee advanced it 15-9 in a May markup, and updated text released in late July added a government-ethics title barring federal officials and their spouses from issuing or profiting from a digital asset tied to their public role — a provision written to answer Democratic objections, several of which called earlier drafts a "nonstarter." The bill needs 60 votes to clear the Senate, meaning at least seven Democrats must cross over, and a procedural vote is now scheduled for September 15 after the chamber left for its August recess without acting. Investors had all but written the bill off for the year before Trump's intervention revived it as a live catalyst.

That gap — between a rally already priced for passage and a bill that still needs seven votes it does not yet have — is the story's real tension. Bitcoin remains about 40% below its October 2025 peak near $126,000, a reminder that this week's move, however sharp, has retraced only part of a much larger decline.

What comes next

The September 15 procedural vote is the next hard date that will test whether the rally reflects durable policy support or got ahead of itself. A failed cloture vote, or another round of Democratic objections to the ethics language, would strip out one of the two pillars under the current price with little warning. The Treasury buyback program takes effect September 9, six days earlier, and will offer the first real read on whether easier bond-market conditions are enough on their own to keep risk appetite elevated once the short-covering flow runs dry.
