European telecoms M&A heats up as Deutsche Telekom and a Bouygues-Iliad-Orange group chase deals

Deutsche Telekom's headquarters in Bonn, Germany. — Kalligraf / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Deutsche Telekom bought Polish fibre assets for €1bn as Bouygues, Iliad and Orange press a €20.35bn consortium bid for SFR through French antitrust review.

Two deals, announced days apart, tell a single story about European telecoms this month: standing still is no longer an option, and increasingly, going it alone isn't either.

Deutsche Telekom said on August 18 it would buy Polish fibre network operator Fiberhost and retail broadband provider Inea from Macquarie Asset Management for roughly €1 billion. The deal adds Fiberhost's open-access network, which passes more than 1.4 million homes, and Inea's base of over 300,000 retail customers, to T-Mobile Polska — until now a mobile-first business. "By bringing together strong mobile and fibre capabilities, we are creating an even better foundation for simple, reliable services for our customers," said Dominique Leroy, Deutsche Telekom's CEO for Europe. Closing is expected by the end of 2026, subject to Polish competition clearance.

It is a straightforward bolt-on. What is happening in France is not.

From rejected bid to signed memorandum

Bouygues Telecom, Free-Iliad and Orange first approached Altice France's SFR jointly in October 2025, offering €17 billion for the bulk of its telecoms assets — an unusual three-way structure that would have carved up France's second-largest mobile operator among its three rivals rather than leaving it intact. Patrick Drahi's Altice rejected the approach within hours.

The consortium did not walk away. By April 2026 it had raised its offer roughly 20%, entering exclusive talks with Altice France. On June 6, the three signed a memorandum of understanding valuing SFR at €20.35 billion ($23.44 billion) including debt — Bouygues taking the largest share of the assets, with Iliad and Orange splitting most of the rest. The plan would reduce France's mobile network operator count from four to three for the first time since Iliad's Free entered the market in 2012.

Regulators are now the obstacle. On July 17, the European Commission referred the antitrust review to France's Autorité de la concurrence, judging the three linked transactions to bear too directly on domestic fixed and mobile markets to handle from Brussels. Orange chief executive Christel Heydemann has already flagged "behavioural remedies" as a possible route to clearance — a signal that the companies expect resistance, not a rubber stamp. Definitive legal documents are due in the second half of 2026; completion is not pencilled in until the second half of 2027.

The common thread

What links a €1 billion Polish fibre purchase to a €20 billion French carve-up is the economics underneath both: fibre and 5G networks are capital-intensive to build and expensive to maintain twice over in the same market. Deutsche Telekom bought scale outright. Bouygues, Iliad and Orange concluded that only by acting together could they absorb SFR without any one of them overpaying or overleveraging alone. Watch the Autorité de la concurrence's review through the rest of the year — its verdict on whether three national operators is workable will shape how far this consolidation logic spreads to Italy, Spain and beyond.
