Fed's Jackson Hole message strains as jobs crack and inflation stays sticky

July payrolls fell 23,000 and prior months were revised down 103,000, even as inflation holds near 3.4% — leaving new Fed Chair Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech unable to point a clear way forward.

The Federal Reserve faces an impossible choice as it heads into Jackson Hole. Weak labor data argue for lowering interest rates, but elevated inflation blocks that path—leaving policymakers caught between competing demands.

July nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 against expectations of 83,000, a shortfall of 106,000 jobs. Prior months' figures were revised down sharply. Core inflation remains stuck near 3.4%, well above the Fed's 2% target.

When weak jobs and sticky inflation strike together, the policy signal turns ambiguous. Weak labor alone would suggest rate cuts; hot inflation alone would argue for holding steady. This combination leaves investors uncertain about the Fed's next move and increasingly skeptical of its forward guidance.
