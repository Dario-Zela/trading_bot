Hull shock Manchester United with dream Premier League return

The MKM Stadium (formerly KC Stadium), Hull City's home ground, pictured on a matchday — Chmoul / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
Newly promoted Hull City beat Manchester United 2-0 at the MKM Stadium in the Premier League's opening weekend.

Manchester United arrived at the MKM Stadium as the visiting aristocrats and left as the punchline. Hull City, back in the Premier League for the first time since 2017 and given little hope of troubling one of the division's grandees, beat them 2-0 on Saturday to open the new season — a result that had United's own captain reaching for grim comparisons to the campaign just gone.

Semi Ajayi put Hull ahead in the 17th minute, poking home from close range after United's defence failed to deal with a corner. Nobel Mendy, a record signing arriving from Spanish football with no previous experience of England's top flight, doubled the lead in the 38th with a header that owed as much to United's set-piece frailty as to his own timing. From there, Sergej Jakirović's side did what newly promoted teams have always needed to do to survive: defended with organisation, denied space, and made a talented opponent look hurried.

A familiar United problem

For Michael Carrick, four months into his first full season as permanent manager after being promoted from interim charge in January, it was a chastening opener. United had never lost a season-opening fixture to a newly promoted club before Saturday — a run stretching back decades — and Carrick's assessment afterwards was measured but unsparing: "Some of it was OK, there's bits to improve on. It was never going to be perfect, but going behind and giving ourselves a lot to do makes it more difficult."

Captain Bruno Fernandes was blunter, pointing to a pattern rather than a one-off. "Same mistakes as we did last season," he said, "every away game we give too much of the ball to them." It is the kind of line that will follow United into next weekend's fixture more than any of Carrick's diplomacy will.

Hull's night, and a debut worth noting

For Hull, the win completed a story that started with an Oli McBurnie goal in May's play-off final against Middlesbrough and continued through a summer of modest recruitment against clubs with vastly larger transfer budgets. Ajayi, the scorer, played down any temptation to enjoy the noise around the result: "We're not focused on outside noise, we back ourselves to do well and hopefully we will have a really positive season." It is the correct answer for a newly promoted captain, and also, on this evidence, not an unreasonable one.

The other subplot belonged to United. Marcus Rashford made his first competitive appearance for the club in 618 days when he was introduced in the second half — a low-key return, overshadowed entirely by the scoreline he was sent on to try to fix.

Hull's next test comes at home again in a fortnight; United, meanwhile, will want answers before their away-day habits described by Fernandes turn from a one-match gripe into a pattern the rest of the league starts to bank on.
