England thrash Pakistan inside three days to take first Test

Joe Root, back for a second spell as England's full-time Test captain, has already led the side in a national-record 65 Tests. — Ben Sutherland / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
England beat Pakistan by an innings and 103 runs in three days at Headingley, giving Joe Root's second stint as Test captain an emphatic start.

Three days. That is all it took England to dismantle Pakistan at Headingley, winning by an innings and 103 runs and handing Joe Root the kind of opening statement that no incoming captain gets to script for themselves. Pakistan were bowled out for 171 on the first day, England piled up 409 in reply, and by mid-afternoon on day three Pakistan had subsided again, this time for 135. The margin put England 1-0 up in the five-Test series with the second match starting Thursday at Lord's.

It was Root's first Test since being reappointed as England's permanent Test captain, a second spell in the job he held for a national-record 65 Tests between 2017 and 2022 before Ben Stokes took over. The reappointment itself had been read in some quarters as an admission of a problem rather than a solution to one — England have now won just three of their last eleven Tests — and Root arrived at Headingley under new red-ball coach Stephen Fleming, still settling in after Brendon McCullum's departure, with interim help from Marcus Trescothick in the meantime. "We spoke every day," Root said of Fleming, who has yet to take up the post in person. "He's kept tabs on things. It's been great to have him on the end of the phone."

A rout built on bowling, not just Root's bat

Root's own contribution — 66 in England's first innings — was solid rather than spectacular, and that in itself tells the story of this match: the batting was a team effort, with Harry Brook making 91, Jordan Cox 73 and Dan Lawrence 51 as England built a total Pakistan never looked capable of chasing down. But the result was decided by England's seam attack. Josh Tongue and Ollie Robinson took eight wickets apiece across the match — Tongue following up 5 for 46 with 3 for 43, Robinson mixing a five-wicket haul with three more in the second innings — while Jofra Archer chipped in with three as Pakistan folded for the second time in 37.3 overs.

"I don't think they really understand how good they are, how well they complement each other and what they are capable of doing in these conditions," Root said of his pace quartet. "Clearly we will have different challenges and surfaces to manage, but it's a brilliant start to things."

Abdullah Shafique's 61 and a defiant 41 from wicketkeeper Mohammad Rizwan were the only real resistance Pakistan mustered across both innings; nobody else passed 25. On a Headingley pitch offering seam movement throughout, England's four-pronged attack simply had too much in reserve.

What a first-Test thrashing does and doesn't prove

One dominant win against a Pakistan side that collapsed twice inside 90 overs combined is not, by itself, proof that English Test cricket has turned a corner — the team's underlying record over the past eleven matches remains poor, and the opposition here were beatable rather than fearsome. But it is exactly the start Root and the England management wanted: a statement of intent that vindicates, for now, a decision to recycle a captain rather than promote a fresh one, and it buys Fleming time to settle into the head-coach role without an immediate crisis on his hands. England are believed to want Root in the job through next year's home Ashes, a stretch of thirteen Tests that also takes in a difficult tour of South Africa and a series in Bangladesh — assignments considerably less forgiving than a shorthanded Pakistan attack at Headingley.

The series resumes at Lord's on Thursday, where a second England win would secure their first series victory since beating New Zealand away in December 2024 — and would go some way to answering whether Headingley was the start of a genuine revival or simply one good week against modest opposition.
