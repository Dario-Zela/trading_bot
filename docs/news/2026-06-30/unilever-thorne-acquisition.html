Unilever in advanced talks to buy US supplements brand Thorne for up to $4 billion

In one sentence
Unilever is competing for Thorne Health at up to $4B — roughly six times revenue — as it accelerates its pivot from mass-market staples to premium wellness.

Unilever is in advanced discussions to acquire Thorne Health, the US premium supplements brand, in a deal that could value the company at up to $4 billion, according to people familiar with the matter. The transaction, if completed, would be the UK consumer goods giant's most substantial bet yet on high-margin health and nutrition — and would land just weeks after it closed an earlier supplements acquisition.

Thorne was founded in 1984 and is headquartered in Summerville, South Carolina. It manufactures dietary supplements across a range of formats — powdered ready-to-mix beverages, softgels, capsules — with flagship lines in magnesium and omega-3. The company went private in October 2023 when LVMH-backed investment firm L Catterton acquired it for $680 million. Since then its revenue has more than doubled, from $229 million in 2022 to more than $500 million in 2025, at a compound annual growth rate exceeding 30%. Thorne is on track to reach $650 million in annual revenue this year — making the implied $4 billion valuation roughly six times current-year sales.

Unilever is not bidding alone. Consumer healthcare group Haleon — carved out of GSK in 2022 — is also among interested parties, and Procter & Gamble has been cited by sources as a potential counter-bidder. The process remains competitive and no agreement has been signed.

Unilever's longer pivot

The Thorne discussions reflect a strategy Fernando Fernandez has been executing with notable speed since becoming Unilever's chief executive in March 2025. His stated target is for beauty, wellbeing and personal care to account for roughly 66% of group sales — up from approximately 52% today. The playbook: exit legacy food and personal care categories, and acquire premium, digitally-native health brands with demonstrable customer loyalty and recurring revenue.

Unilever completed its acquisition of Grüns — a greens supplement gummy brand that scaled to $300 million in revenue in under four years — on 1 June for a reported $1.2 billion. Thorne, at up to $4 billion, would be more than three times that price and would add meaningful scientific credibility: the brand's clinical-grade positioning differentiates it from mass-market rivals and is part of what has driven its subscription-heavy customer base. Thorne would join an existing wellbeing stable that includes Liquid I.V., Olly, Nutrafol, SmartyPants and Onnit — a collection Unilever has been assembling since 2019, and which Fernandez is now treating as the core of the group's growth story rather than a side portfolio.

The deal's unconfirmed status limits immediate market reaction — a signed agreement carries different weight than a competitive process still in motion. What to watch: whether Haleon or Procter & Gamble moves first with a binding offer, which would force Unilever's hand, and whether Unilever signals final terms before its Q2 2026 trading update, expected in late July.
