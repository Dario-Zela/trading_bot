BP and Iberdrola win approval for Spain's largest green hydrogen site at Castellón refinery

A large-scale PEM electrolyser unit — the technology at the core of the Castellón green hydrogen plant — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
BP and Iberdrola secured €211m in EU funding approval for their 25MW Castellón refinery hydrogen plant — set to be Spain's largest — even as the sector cancels projects globally.

Spain's Ministry for Ecological Transition has approved the reallocation of €211 million in European funding toward BP and Iberdrola's joint green hydrogen venture at BP's Castellón refinery — effectively clearing the way for what will become Spain's largest operational green hydrogen facility. The move comes as the broader hydrogen sector is deep in a reckoning over cost, demand, and viability.

The project is being developed through Castellón Green Hydrogen S.L., an equally-owned joint venture the two companies formed to decarbonise one of BP's core refining assets on Spain's Mediterranean coast. A 25-megawatt electrolyser array — built using PEM technology supplied by Plug Power — has now completed construction and moved into commissioning and testing. The plant is expected to produce 2,800 tonnes of green hydrogen per year, powered by 200 GWh of renewable electricity annually under a power purchase agreement with Iberdrola. First output is anticipated before the end of 2026.

Once fully operational, the facility will replace a portion of the grey hydrogen currently consumed in the Castellón refinery's industrial processes — avoiding an estimated 23,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions per year. The €211 million flows from the EU's IPCEI Hy2USE programme, administered through Spain's Institute for Energy Diversification and Saving (IDAE), and supplements an earlier €15 million awarded through Spain's NextGenerationEU recovery plan.

Against the grain

The approval lands at an uncomfortable moment for the green hydrogen industry globally. Close to 60 major clean hydrogen projects were cancelled in 2025, collectively representing more than 4.9 million tonnes of annual production capacity. Governments worldwide cut hydrogen funding allocations by roughly 20% that year. The sector's central problem — bankable offtake agreements remain scarce, costs have proved stickier than projected, and regulatory compliance under EU additionality rules is administratively demanding — has not resolved.

BP itself cancelled two large hydrogen bets in late 2025: the 1.5 GW Duqm green hydrogen project in Oman and the 1.2 GW H2Teesside blue hydrogen plant in the UK. That context makes the Castellón commitment notable, not for its size — 25 MW is a modest footprint against gigawatt-scale ambitions shelved elsewhere — but for its specificity. This is not a prospectus project; it is a plant in commissioning, backed by committed capital, at an existing industrial site with a clear near-term offtake: BP's own refinery.

Iberdrola, which already operates Europe's largest green hydrogen plant for industrial use in Puertollano, brings the renewable power and electrolyser operating experience. BP brings the industrial demand and the site. The structure sidesteps the offtake problem that has derailed larger, merchant-model projects: the hydrogen doesn't need to find a customer, because the customer is one of the owners.

What to watch

The project's real test will be whether the commissioning phase delivers on throughput targets — 2,800 tonnes annually from a 25 MW array is an achievable number, but electrolyser utilisation rates at scale remain a persistent variable. Iberdrola has earmarked the expanded Castellón site as a potential anchor for a broader Valencia hydrogen economy serving ceramics and chemicals sectors; whether that second phase ever reaches a final investment decision will be the signal worth watching once initial operations are confirmed.
