Wimbledon 2026 opens at the All England Club

Centre Court at the All England Club, Wimbledon — the world's most famous tennis venue, hosting its 139th Championships. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Wimbledon 2026 opened with a historically bad Day 1 for British players — 10 of 11 lost — as top seeds Sinner, Sabalenka and Djokovic came through.

The 139th Championships are under way at the All England Club — and for home fans, the opening day delivered something between disappointment and mild catastrophe.

Wimbledon got under way on Monday with the customary pageantry of Centre Court's first balls, a record prize fund of £64.2 million (up a fifth on last year), and the debut of video-review technology on the show courts. What unfolded in the draws for British players, however, was rather less ceremonial. Ten of the eleven home competitors who took to court on Day One lost — the worst collective performance by British players in Wimbledon's singles draws in the twenty-first century, according to figures compiled by Tennishead.

The most stinging result came on No. 2 Court, where 26th seed Cameron Norrie went down in five sets to American qualifier Michael Zheng, 6–7, 6–2, 6–7, 6–3, 7–6. The 22-year-old Zheng, ranked outside the top 200, struck 21 aces and outlasted Norrie in a match tie-break to complete one of the first-round upsets of the fortnight. Norrie had reached the semi-finals here in 2022 and the quarter-finals in 2025; this was emphatically not the homecoming anyone in SW19 was scripting.

The losses on court were compounded by withdrawals off it. Jack Draper, the men's British No. 1 and one of the more credible domestic hopes for a deep run, pulled out Monday morning with a recurrence of an arm problem that has troubled him on and off for months. Emma Raducanu, seeded to face women's top seed Aryna Sabalenka in the last eight, followed on Tuesday, also citing injury. British Tennis enters the second day with a thin bench.

The top of the draw holds firm

Among the seeded field, there was less drama. Defending men's champion Jannik Sinner came through a tricky five-setter against Miomir Kecmanović — a reminder that Wimbledon grass rarely allows the top seed an easy ride from the off. Sabalenka and Novak Djokovic, the latter seeded seventh, both progressed in straight sets. Félix Auger-Aliassime, seeded third in the men's draw, was clinical, and Coco Gauff on the women's side barely broke a sweat.

The cull of seeds was still notable: Casper Ruud, Andrey Rublev, and several women's seeds all exited before the week was out, ensuring that the bottom half of several draws is already more open than the bookmakers priced it.

The fortnight runs to 12 July, with the Ladies' Final on the Saturday and the Gentlemen's on the Sunday. The most pressing question for a UK audience is whether anyone in British colours will still be standing by the middle Saturday, when the second week traditionally separates contenders from the rest. Given Tuesday's starting position, that is a question that may answer itself rather quickly.
