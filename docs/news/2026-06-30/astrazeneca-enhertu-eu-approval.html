AstraZeneca's Enhertu wins EU approval across all HER2-positive solid tumours, opening multiple new indications

AstraZeneca's Discovery Centre headquarters at the Cambridge Biomedical Campus, the base for its global oncology R&D operations — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
The European Commission approved Enhertu as the EU's first tumour-agnostic HER2-directed therapy, giving AstraZeneca and Daiichi Sankyo a sixth EU indication and opening the drug to patients across all HER2+ solid tumour types.

The European Commission on 29 June granted approval for Enhertu — AstraZeneca and Daiichi Sankyo's antibody-drug conjugate — as a treatment for adults with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive solid tumours, regardless of where in the body the cancer originated. The decision, the first tumour-agnostic clearance for a HER2-directed antibody-drug conjugate in the EU, adds a sixth European indication for the drug and opens the label to patients whose cancers have so far had no targeted option.

Broad by design

The approval covers any solid malignancy showing HER2 overexpression at an immunohistochemistry score of 3+ — the standard marker for strong HER2 positivity — in patients who have received prior therapy and have exhausted other satisfactory options. Prior EU approvals for Enhertu were tied to specific cancer types: breast, gastric, biliary tract. The new label cuts across tissue of origin entirely, meaning a patient with a HER2-overexpressing colorectal tumour or a rare carcinoma now has access to the same regimen as a breast cancer patient.

The clinical evidence rests on three Phase II trials. DESTINY-PanTumor02 — the pivotal study — enrolled 111 HER2-positive patients across seven solid tumour types and produced a confirmed objective response rate of 52.3%, with a median duration of response of 21.1 months. Supporting data from DESTINY-Lung01 (non-small-cell lung cancer, 52.9% ORR) and DESTINY-CRC02 (colorectal cancer, 46.9% ORR) strengthened the case for breadth over tissue specificity.

"The approval of trastuzumab deruxtecan as a tumour agnostic therapy opens a new treatment option for patients with HER2-positive cancers regardless of where the tumour originated," said Dr Benedikt Westphalen of the University of Munich, one of the principal investigators.

Commercial gravity

The strategic weight of this approval is considerable. Enhertu has become one of the fastest-growing oncology assets globally: combined sales reached $3.75 billion in 2024, and rose to $1.42 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone — a 31% year-on-year increase. A tumour-agnostic EU label materially widens the addressable patient pool in a region where HER2-positive cancers span breast, gastric, lung, colorectal, and other solid tumour types in aggregate.

Dave Fredrickson, AstraZeneca's executive vice president for oncology, described the approval as reflecting "precision medicine reshaping cancer care by helping inform treatment decisions based on the molecular and biological characteristics of a patient's disease." That framing points at the commercial logic: a tissue-agnostic label aligns prescribing to a biomarker rather than an anatomy, which means oncologists can reach for the drug whenever a diagnostic shows HER2 IHC 3+, whether the patient is in a breast unit or a gastrointestinal ward.

The approval also triggers a $25 million milestone payment from AstraZeneca to Daiichi Sankyo under the 2019 collaboration agreement. Under that structure, Daiichi Sankyo retains sales recognition in most EU markets; AstraZeneca's economic interest runs through its share of profit and co-commercialisation rights in markets including the United States and the United Kingdom.

Enhertu's main competition in the HER2-directed ADC space remains Roche's Kadcyla (ado-trastuzumab emtansine), which held the larger EU market share through 2025. Kadcyla's label, however, remains confined to breast cancer — no comparable tumour-agnostic designation exists for it in the region. That distinction is now the central competitive claim AstraZeneca and Daiichi Sankyo can press commercially, in particular against smaller rivals whose programmes remain single-indication.

What comes next

The EU approval follows an FDA tumour-agnostic clearance for the same indication granted in April 2024. AstraZeneca now characterises Enhertu as approved in more than 40 countries and regions for HER2-positive metastatic solid tumours, with six separate EU indications on the label. "Enhertu is now approved for six indications in the EU," said Ken Keller, Daiichi Sankyo's chief executive. "This demonstrates our commitment to advancing innovative medicines in areas of high unmet need."

Additional EU submissions are pending for combination regimens in breast cancer — including earlier lines of therapy where peak-sales potential is largest. Interim data from DESTINY-Breast06 and related studies, expected at major oncology congresses in the second half of 2026, will be the next decisive test: whether Enhertu sustains its response rates when deployed before disease has progressed to the metastatic setting. That is where long-term revenue projections are anchored, and where AstraZeneca's oncology case to investors will need to keep delivering.
