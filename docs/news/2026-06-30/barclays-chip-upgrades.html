Barclays turns constructive on chips: Samsung, SK Hynix and STMicro all lifted on HBM and AI demand

Samsung Electronics headquarters in Suwon, South Korea — the conglomerate's semiconductor division is central to Barclays' constructive memory thesis. — Oskar Alexanderson / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Barclays analyst Simon Coles lifted targets on Samsung, SK Hynix, and STMicroelectronics, arguing AI-driven HBM demand creates a structural floor that decouples memory chips from the broader semiconductor cycle.

Barclays lifted price targets on Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix on Sunday, maintaining overweight ratings on both Korean chipmakers while crediting accelerating high-bandwidth memory demand from AI data centres as the force most likely to insulate the sector from a broader semiconductor slowdown.

Analyst Simon Coles raised Samsung's target — rolling the valuation forward to 2027 estimates — citing reports that the company has already shipped $1 billion of its latest HBM4 product, a figure that marks a meaningful step toward narrowing the technology gap with rival SK Hynix. Barclays models Samsung's HBM revenue tripling in 2026, with 2027 estimates revised upward by roughly 17%. The SK Hynix revision carries an even more substantial implied move: the new target of €2,900 (up from €2,300) points to roughly 90% upside from current levels, underpinned by anticipated pricing tailwinds that Coles projects will trigger "another round of material upgrades" in 2027, with revenues exceeding 20% growth. Supply, the firm argues, will not catch up with AI-driven demand until 2028 at the earliest — the point at which blended average selling prices may begin to moderate.

External validation arrived last week from Micron, whose quarterly results confirmed memory prices are still rising, a signal Barclays reads as grounds to view any near-term sector weakness as a buying opportunity rather than a warning.

The structural argument at the core of both calls is that high-bandwidth memory has effectively decoupled from the commodity chip cycle. Standard DRAM and NAND flash remain susceptible to inventory correction swings; HBM, purpose-built for AI accelerator packages and sold under longer-horizon supply agreements, has a demand floor that looks structurally different. AI hyperscalers are not pulling back capex — they are competing for a constrained supply of stacked memory.

The European angle

On the same day, Barclays issued a sharper revision for STMicroelectronics, the Franco-Italian chipmaker whose mix spans automotive, industrial, and increasingly AI-oriented silicon. The house lifted STM from underweight to equal weight and nearly doubled its price target from $34 to $65 — a scale of revision that implicitly acknowledged the prior bear case had run its course.

Coles' original underweight rested on gross margin concerns. Those, the note argues, are "no longer valid as the revenue outlook continues to improve across a number of areas." Barclays now models AI revenues for STM reaching approximately €1 billion in 2026 and climbing to €2.3 billion in 2027, while satellite revenues are projected to grow from a €600–900 million range to €1.3 billion by 2027. Data-centre revenue forecasts were doubled, from roughly $500 million to around $1 billion for 2026. The house is running approximately 6% ahead of Street consensus on 2027 revenue estimates. STM shares jumped nearly 4% on the announcement.

The one disclosed risk: a modest probability that Q3 guidance prints below seasonal norms, given Apple's product cycle weighting in STM's order book. Coles expects optical acceleration in Q4 to compensate.

The three upgrades in aggregate make a single structural claim: that AI infrastructure spending is creating a durable, isolated demand pool capable of lifting chipmakers across geographies and subsegments simultaneously — decoupling select chip segments from the cycle that still governs the rest of the sector. The next test arrives with STM's Q3 guidance and Micron's following quarterly update, which will either confirm or begin to complicate the memory pricing trajectory on which the entire thesis depends.
