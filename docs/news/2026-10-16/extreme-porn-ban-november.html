Ban on porn depicting choking or incest takes effect in early November

In one sentence
Possessing or publishing porn showing strangulation, incest or adults role-playing as under-16s becomes a crime in England and Wales on 6 November; platform enforcement is still unclear.

Pornography that shows strangulation, or sex between relatives, becomes a criminal matter in England and Wales on 6 November. From that date, possessing or publishing it will be an offence. Whether the regulator, Ofcom, or the police is the main enforcer for platforms is less clear than the headline suggests.

What the law covers

The government says the new offences cover three categories of material: pornography depicting strangulation or suffocation; pornography depicting sex between people who are related, or pretending to be; and pornography showing adults role-playing as children under 16. The powers come from amendments to the Crime and Policing Bill, which became law in April.

The measures trace back to a 2025 review led by Baroness Bertin. It found that depictions of choking were widespread on mainstream porn sites and had helped normalise the act among young people. Justice minister Alex Davies-Jones said: "It's not as harmless as you may think, and after 6 November it is criminal." Bertin called it "very good news these offences will come into force, and not a moment too soon."

The government has stressed that the law targets particular content, not private tastes. Davies-Jones said it is not about criminalising harmless sexual preferences. The coverage reviewed here does not set out maximum penalties.

Who enforces it

The offences are in criminal law, so the default enforcers are the police and prosecutors. Ofcom's role comes from a separate route: the Online Safety Act. Reports say the government will also designate depiction of strangulation in pornography as a priority offence under that Act. Priority status requires platforms to take proactive steps to stop such content appearing, and Ofcom can fine firms that fail. I could not confirm that designation from the legislation itself, and it appears to cover strangulation only, not the incest or child role-play categories.

So there are likely two tracks. Individuals who possess or publish the material face prosecution. Platforms face regulatory duties, and only where the Online Safety Act is engaged. Campaigners are pushing for more. The End Violence Against Women Coalition wants the Act updated so that "online platforms are required to remove this content", and wants the ban "effectively enforced".

What to watch

Three things are unconfirmed. These are the precise offence definitions, the penalties, and how the law applies in Scotland and Northern Ireland, since the announcements refer to England and Wales. The first test for platforms comes on 6 November. Watch for Ofcom guidance on how adult sites must treat the newly illegal material, and for the first prosecutions after that date.
