Streeting presses Washington to keep backing Ukraine after the Starlink row

In one sentence
Defence Secretary Wes Streeting says Pete Hegseth assured him Ukraine's Starlink access is restored, but the cause of the cutback remains unexplained.

The UK's Defence Secretary, Wes Streeting, used his first Washington visit in the job to press the Trump administration to keep backing Ukraine. The trip was overshadowed by a row over Starlink, the satellite service Ukrainian forces rely on to fly drones and coordinate on the front line. After meeting his US counterpart, Pete Hegseth, at the Pentagon on Thursday, Streeting said the service had been restored. He added that London still does not know why it was interrupted.

What happened to Starlink

The disruption began overnight on Wednesday. According to an adviser to President Volodymyr Zelensky, service was limited without warning for terminals moving faster than 100mph, the kind mounted on drones. Serhii Beskrestnov, Ukraine's defence-technology adviser, said the country had lost "one of the few chances" to defend against Shahed attack drones. He said frontline logistics and long-range missions were also affected.

Who made the decision is unresolved. Elon Musk said on X that the US military, and ultimately the president, decide any limits on Starshield, the military version of Starlink. The White House neither confirmed nor denied that, and the Pentagon did not respond to Al Jazeera's request for comment. Reuters, as relayed by other outlets, reported the instruction had come from the White House, without naming who gave it.

Kyiv called the restriction politically motivated. Zelensky, who had been in London the day before, said it was a serious error that would cost many lives. Senator Jeanne Shaheen said the limits must stop immediately. Poland's foreign minister, Radoslaw Sikorski, whose government pays for Ukrainian Starlink subscriptions, criticised Musk for possibly mixing politics with his products.

Streeting's message

At the Pentagon, Streeting said: "I am reassured, and was reassured directly by Secretary Hegseth, that the service has been restored." He said he did not understand the Pentagon to have restricted Ukraine's offensive or defensive capabilities in order to prevent strikes on Russian energy sites. He was also scheduled to speak at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, where he urged Washington to stay the course on Ukraine.

That denial matters because of what else has been reported. Reuters sources said the US had threatened to cut the intelligence behind early missile-strike warnings, to press Kyiv to halt attacks on Russian oil refineries. Trump said on Tuesday that the US had not stopped sharing intelligence. Zelensky said Ukraine did not receive its usual early warnings from Western allies for a second day, as Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv for a second straight night, killing at least nine people and damaging power and heating infrastructure.

The wider backdrop

Beskrestnov said the slowdown "cannot be a coincidence" and linked it to Trump's decision, taken the previous week, to lift sanctions on Russian diesel imports. The link is his reading, not an established finding, and no one in Washington has confirmed it. It does explain why the episode has fed existing doubts in European capitals about American commitment to Kyiv.

It also revives an old unease. Musk said in 2025 that Starlink would never switch off its terminals for Ukraine, and he had earlier acknowledged that the front line could collapse if he did. The episode shows how much battlefield communications depend on one company's network, and on whoever controls its military tier.

What to watch

The open question is the cause: whether this was a policy decision, a technical change or a bargaining move over Ukraine's refinery strikes. Until Washington or SpaceX explains it, Hegseth's assurance rests on his word alone. The next test is whether service holds and whether Kyiv's missile warnings return to normal.
