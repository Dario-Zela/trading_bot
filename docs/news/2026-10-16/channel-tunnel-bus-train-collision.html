Bus hits train at French end of Channel Tunnel, injuring 19

In one sentence
A minibus carrying lorry drivers hit a train at Eurotunnel's Coquelles loading platform, injuring 19 with no deaths reported; effects on services and the cause remain unclear.

A minibus carrying lorry drivers collided with a train at Eurotunnel's French terminal on Thursday evening, injuring 19 people. The crash happened at the loading platform at Coquelles, near Calais. It is not clear yet whether it will disrupt crossings.

The Pas-de-Calais prefecture said the drivers were preparing to board a Le Shuttle service when the vehicle hit the train. Early reports described a "bus", but later accounts specify a minibus ferrying HGV drivers to the platform. The prefecture's first count was 19 people involved, the driver among them. No one was reported killed and no one's life was said to be in danger.

What is known

Accounts of the injuries differ. One report says two people, including the driver, were taken to hospital with serious but non-life-threatening injuries, while the other 17 had minor ones. Another says an earlier figure of five seriously hurt was revised down to two. Such counts commonly shift in the first hours after an accident, and the prefecture's own figures are the ones to rely on.

The train is also described inconsistently. Some reports call it a stationary freight shuttle; others quote the sub-prefect as saying the minibus struck a departing train on the platform. That matters, because it bears on who was at fault and on what the investigation will examine. The cause has not been established. The sub-prefect reportedly said it was the first time an incident of this kind had occurred on an Eurotunnel loading platform.

Why it matters

For now this is a single-incident story. The Coquelles terminal is the French gateway for Le Shuttle, the vehicle-carrying service through the tunnel, and a stoppage there can quickly back up lorry traffic towards Calais and Dover. Eurotunnel service disruption was described as possible in initial reporting, but nothing confirmed has emerged. ITV reported that the incident does not involve or affect Eurostar services between London and the Continent, which run on separate trains and platforms.

The story would grow in two cases. One is a prolonged suspension of freight shuttles, which would affect hauliers already working to tight schedules. The other is a formal safety inquiry into how a road vehicle came to be in contact with a train on a loading platform, a place designed to keep the two apart.

What to watch

The next markers are the prefecture's updated casualty figures, the opening of any official investigation, and Eurotunnel's own statement on whether Le Shuttle timetables at Coquelles are running normally. Until then, the main question is whether the platform returns to service quickly.
