Ex-DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson sentenced to 15 years for child sex abuse

In one sentence
Former DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson was jailed for 15 years at Newry Crown Court for rape and child sex abuse; he will serve about seven and a half and is appealing.

Jeffrey Donaldson, who led the Democratic Unionist Party until 2024, was sentenced at Newry Crown Court on 15 October to 15 years in prison for the rape and sexual abuse of two women when they were children. A jury had convicted him in June of 18 offences committed between 1985 and 2008. He denied all of them and is appealing the conviction.

The case is a Northern Ireland criminal-justice and political-accountability story. It has no direct bearing on markets or technology.

The sentence

Judge Paul Ramsey imposed 11 years for the rape and the other offences against the first complainant. He added four years, to run consecutively, for the offences against the second. Donaldson, who is 63, qualifies for automatic 50% remission. He will serve about seven and a half years in custody and the remainder on licence.

The convictions comprised one count of rape, 13 counts of indecent assault and four counts of gross indecency. He will be on the sex offenders' register for life and is barred from working with children and vulnerable adults. A pre-sentence report assessed him as a "medium" risk of reoffending. He has been held in Maghaberry Prison since the trial ended in June.

The judge said Donaldson had "doubled down in his denials" after the verdict. He called the case "disturbing and disquieting" and described Donaldson's fall as a "catastrophic descent into disgrace". He said he had "no doubt whatsoever" that the abuse left a permanent mark on both women. Local reports also recorded his remark that there had been no "regret, apology or shame".

Both women were in court. One described a "living nightmare" and said the case's public nature had affected her social life and career. The other, addressing Donaldson and his wife, said: "You took away my ability to trust people."

His wife's case

Eleanor Donaldson was found to have aided and abetted her husband's crimes after a trial of the facts, a procedure used when a defendant is unfit to stand trial, here on mental health grounds. She cannot be imprisoned. The court could make a treatment order or grant an absolute discharge. She is appealing the outcome, and the judge will decide her case at a later date.

The political fall

Donaldson was MP for Lagan Valley for four decades and was knighted for political services in 2016. He became DUP leader in 2021 and resigned in 2024. He was stripped of his knighthood in July.

He led the party through the dispute over post-Brexit trade rules and the return of power-sharing at Stormont. That is why the verdict carries weight in unionist politics as well as in the courts. The offences, however, are historical and concern private conduct, not his public role.

What comes next

Two legal steps remain. The first is Donaldson's appeal against conviction. The second is the judge's decision on the disposal of Eleanor Donaldson's case, which is also under appeal. Neither has a date in the reports reviewed.
