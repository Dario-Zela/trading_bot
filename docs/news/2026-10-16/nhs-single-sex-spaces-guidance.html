NHS England says trans people should use hospital spaces by biological sex

In one sentence
NHS England's new guidance says single-sex hospital wards and staff facilities should follow biological sex, with case-by-case clinical exceptions, applying the 2025 Supreme Court ruling.

NHS England has published guidance telling hospitals in England to provide single-sex accommodation, staff changing rooms and toilets on the basis of biological sex. Trans patients and staff are covered. The guidance was reported on 15 October 2026. It turns the Supreme Court's April 2025 reading of the Equality Act 2010 into operational rules for wards and changing areas. Clinicians and trans patients now sit between those rules and a legal question that is still being argued.

What the guidance says

The central line, as quoted by PinkNews, is that "single-sex services, such as hospital accommodation, should be provided on the basis of biological sex." Trans patients are to be placed on wards or bays matching their sex registered at birth, or offered a single room. Reports describe the single room as a way to reduce the risk of discrimination.

The rule is not absolute. Decisions can be made case by case. Where placing a patient by biological sex is not "clinically appropriate", trusts are told to make other arrangements and keep every patient's dignity and safety in mind. Coverage citing the guidance says clinical need takes priority in some settings, including emergency and urgent care. I could not retrieve the NHS England document itself. These details come from press accounts of it, and its exact wording and exceptions should be checked against the original.

For staff, changing rooms, toilets and similar facilities are to be used by biological sex. Trusts are encouraged to provide enough alternatives, such as single-occupancy toilets, and to audit whether they do.

The legal backdrop

In For Women Scotland v Scottish Ministers, decided on 16 April 2025, the Supreme Court held that "woman" and "sex" in the Equality Act 2010 refer to biological sex. Earlier NHS England guidance had allowed patients and staff to use facilities according to gender identity. It was withdrawn after the ruling.

Pressure on existing arrangements is visible in NHS England's own figures, as reported. Breaches of mixed-sex accommodation rules, where patients are placed on wards of the opposite sex, have risen every year since 2021. There were 5,520 in August 2026, against 3,853 in June 2025. Those figures cover all patients, not trans patients specifically, so they say little about how often the new rule will bite.

Two employment tribunal cases frame the staff side. In January 2026 a tribunal found that the trust at Darlington Memorial Hospital indirectly discriminated against nurses who objected to a trans colleague using the female changing rooms. The nurses say they are seeking £400,000 from the NHS for legal costs. In Scotland, Sandie Peggie's tribunal in December 2025 found she had been harassed over NHS Fife's interpretation of single-sex rules but dismissed her other claims. An appeal is reported to be heading for the courts in 2027.

Reaction

Sex Matters, whose chief executive is Maya Forstater, welcomed the guidance but raised concerns about how it will be applied. Forstater said NHS patient data is a mess, with potentially thousands of trans people recorded as the opposite sex. Bethany Hutchison, president of the Darlington Nurses Union, also welcomed it. She said its success depends on whether trusts follow it.

TransActual and the Trans+ Solidarity Alliance said that putting clinical judgement first is welcome. They warned that staff will be unclear on how to care for trans patients safely, and that placing people by birth sex could push them away from care. They also said the guidance would be hard to apply in understaffed hospitals.

What to watch

The triage note anticipated legal and political challenge. None of the coverage I could retrieve reports a filed challenge to this guidance, so that remains a forecast rather than a fact. The practical tests are likely to be the case-by-case judgments trusts make on real wards, and the tribunal appeals already in train, including the Peggie appeal expected in 2027. Those will show how far "clinically appropriate" bends the biological-sex default.
