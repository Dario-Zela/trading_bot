GILTS · BUDGET · By M. Halloway

30-year gilt breaks 6% for the first time since 1998 as borrowing overshoots ahead of Healey's Budget

The 30-year gilt crossed 6% on 1 October for the first time since 1998, and estimates put Healey's £23.6bn spring headroom at roughly £8.5bn–£14bn before the 28 October Budget.

A new chancellor faces an early pre-Budget test: public borrowing has come in above expectations, narrowing the margin the Treasury had planned to work within for spending and tax decisions. The Irish News reports the jump as unexpected, and it lands before the Budget that will set the government's fiscal path.

The overshoot matters because it shifts the starting point for the Budget arithmetic. A larger borrowing figure tends to be read by markets as a signal about future debt issuance, and the cost of that debt, particularly at the long end of the gilt market, feeds back into how much room the government has left. The full article looks at how the figures affect that room and what the Treasury and the Office for Budget Responsibility are likely to say about it.

Watch for the next OBR forecast and for any further movement in long-dated gilt yields, which would show whether markets are pricing the borrowing news as a lasting fiscal problem or a one-off.
