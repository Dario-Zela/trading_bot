Three children and a teacher killed in Oregon primary school shooting

In one sentence
A 16-year-old killed three children and a teacher at Silver Rail Elementary in Bend, Oregon, before a bystander shot him, reviving a gun debate centred on the stalled Measure 114.

A 16-year-old opened fire at a primary school in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday, killing three children and a teacher, police said. A bystander shot the attacker dead. The shooting, in a state where a voter-approved gun-licensing law has been tied up in the courts for nearly four years, will test whether this kind of attack changes policy at state level or only sharpens rhetoric in the run-up to the midterms.

What happened

Police in Bend, a city of about 100,000 more than 100 miles south-east of Portland, received a call at 12:59pm on 15 October reporting a shot fired into a car outside Silver Rail Elementary School. Officers arrived within minutes, and more 911 calls then came from inside the building. The school serves pupils from kindergarten to fifth grade and had 459 enrolled last year, according to the Oregon Department of Education.

The dead are three children, aged 5, 8 and 11, and a teacher. Two others, a 7-year-old child and a 54-year-old teacher, were taken to St Charles Bend with critical injuries, according to NBC News. Names had not been released.

Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said the suspect, a 16-year-old local resident, was found dead inside the school. A member of the public had pursued him into the building and shot him, Krantz said. Authorities credited that person with preventing further deaths. According to a law enforcement official briefed on the case, the gunman had forced his way in by firing at locked doors, and used a pump-action shotgun bought by his mother. Police have not said why he did it. These details come from early reporting and could change.

The Bend-La Pine school district placed the school in lockdown while officers cleared it room by room. "This is the kind of thing we never want to see happen in Bend. It's devastating," said Sheila Miller, the police department's communications manager.

Oregon's unfinished gun law

Oregon is an awkward setting for the argument. In 2022 voters narrowly passed Ballot Measure 114, which requires a permit, with a background check and training, to buy a firearm, and bans magazines holding more than ten rounds. It has never taken effect. State and federal court challenges have kept it on hold, and the Oregon Supreme Court heard argument on its constitutionality in November 2025. Reporting from earlier this year did not show a ruling.

The legislature has meanwhile moved the dates. HB 4145, which passed in March, would delay the permit requirement to 1 January 2028 and the magazine limit to 1 January 2027, and raise the permit fee from $65 to $150. I could not confirm that the governor signed it.

The difficulty for advocates on either side is that the facts of Bend do not map neatly onto the law. Measure 114 governs purchases by the buyer. Reports so far say the shotgun was bought by the suspect's mother, so a permit system would have applied to her, not to him. A magazine cap would matter little for a pump-action shotgun. Whether the weapon was stored securely is the kind of question that could shift the debate towards safe-storage rules, but nothing in the early reporting settles it.

The political reading

School shootings have a record of producing intense national argument and patchy legislative results. Change tends to come, where it comes at all, in individual states and often years later, which is why Oregon's stalled licensing scheme is the more telling indicator than anything said in Washington. Voters in a midterm year will hear the case made both ways: that Bend shows the cost of delay, and that a bystander with a gun stopped it. Both framings will be used.

What to watch

The first marker is the police account of the suspect's motive and how he obtained the gun, which will determine which policy argument gains purchase. The second is the Oregon Supreme Court's pending ruling on Measure 114. Any statement from Oregon's governor on the 2028 timetable would show whether the shooting moves state policy or only the campaign.
