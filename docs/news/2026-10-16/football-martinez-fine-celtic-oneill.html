Martinez fined by Man Utd, and Celtic's O'Neill is defiant after another big defeat

In one sentence
Manchester United have fined Lisandro Martínez after he criticised their recruitment, reviving the Big Six debate, while Celtic's O'Neill defends his side against 'flat-track bullies' jibes.

Manchester United have fined Lisandro Martínez after he publicly criticised the club's recruitment. The fine is a routine piece of dressing-room housekeeping. It has revived an older argument about whether English football still has a 'Big Six' at all.

The defender is reported to have spoken out after United's Champions League defeat by Atlético Madrid on Tuesday. He told Amazon Prime: "We have to bring in more players to compete." He added, of technical director Jason Wilcox: "I expect more from our technical director, I have to be honest." The Athletic reported that a fine was inevitable. BBC Sport reports that Martínez has since apologised. The size of the fine has not been published, and the club has not confirmed the amount.

A fine, and a wider grievance

The sanction itself is unremarkable. Clubs fine players for public criticism all the time, and the money usually goes to charity. What gives this episode some weight is what he said. Martínez was not complaining about a referee or a team-mate. He questioned whether the squad is good enough for the competitions United are in. Jamie Carragher and Wayne Rooney both criticised the timing. Many supporters backed him.

The Guardian reports that some at the club suspect he wants to leave on a free transfer next summer. That makes the contract question the one to watch. United hold an option to extend his deal by 12 months, and doubts now surround whether they will use it.

Does the Big Six still exist?

The 'Big Six' is Arsenal, Chelsea, Liverpool, Manchester City, Manchester United and Tottenham. The complaint of a United player that the squad is short is an awkward fit with that label. It is also the kind of remark that fuels the argument that the label no longer describes the league.

That argument is not new. A year ago Pep Guardiola said Bournemouth's early-season form, which had them second in the table in late October 2025, showed the concept was outdated. Aston Villa's football director has said there is no longer a top six in English football. Unai Emery, Villa's manager, has taken a different line and still lists Newcastle among the bigger contenders. Bournemouth have since built a reputation for taking points off the established sides.

The debate gains force only if results turn into a lasting change in the table. No current Bournemouth league position or points total was available to this writer, so the claim that the hierarchy has shifted stays unproven. A fine at Old Trafford is a weak basis for declaring the Big Six dead. A United side short of players and Bournemouth above them in the table would be a stronger one.

In Glasgow, a different row

At Celtic, Martin O'Neill is defending his team. Critics have reportedly called the side 'flat-track bullies', but this writer could not find the original source or speaker of that phrase, so it is not attributed here. Nor could the opponent, score and date of the latest defeat be confirmed.

What is documented is September's run. Celtic lost 3-0 to Rangers at Ibrox in the League Cup, then 1-0 at Parkhead in the league, a Ryan Naderi goal after 33 minutes ending a 13-game winning run. O'Neill, 74, said afterwards that the team must "rethink" and called it a "reset", not a disaster. Celtic still led Rangers by two points at the time.

What next

The next clues are on the pitch and in the contract. United's next league fixtures will show whether Martínez keeps his place after the apology. The decision on his 12-month option will show whether the club still wants him. For Celtic, the next test against a top-tier opponent will show whether O'Neill's defiance is justified.
