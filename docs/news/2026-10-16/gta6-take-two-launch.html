GTA 6 is set for 19 November, and Take-Two leans on one launch that has slipped twice

In one sentence
GTA 6 is due 19 November after two delays, and Take-Two's $8.0bn–$8.2bn net bookings guidance for the fiscal year depends on that date holding.

Rockstar Games' Grand Theft Auto VI is due on 19 November, five weeks from today. Take-Two Interactive, its publisher, has staked most of its fiscal year on that date holding. The game was first promised for fall 2025, then moved to 26 May 2026, then moved again to 19 November. The second delay was announced in early November 2025, alongside Take-Two's quarterly results.

What is confirmed

At launch the game is confirmed for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. No PC version has been announced.

Take-Two has twice reiterated the date this year, most recently with its first-quarter results on 7 August. Management said the game has not been delayed, and chief executive Strauss Zelnick described pre-orders, which opened on 25 June, as "unprecedented and astonishing." The company gave no unit or dollar figure, and analysts noted the lack of hard numbers.

The arithmetic

The financial case rests on one guidance range. For the fiscal year to March 2027, Take-Two forecasts net bookings of $8.0bn to $8.2bn. That is about 20% growth at the midpoint on fiscal 2026's $6.72bn, and the company attributes the increase primarily to the November launch.

The quarters on either side of the launch look thin by comparison. First-quarter net bookings were $1.39bn, down about 3% from $1.42bn a year earlier, though slightly above the top of the company's own $1.32bn to $1.37bn range. The GAAP net loss widened to $34.1m, or $0.18 a share, including a $43.4m impairment tied to cancelling an unannounced third-party project.

The implication is that the second half of the year has to carry the full-year guidance. A launch on 19 November leaves about four and a half months of the fiscal year in which to book it. A further slip beyond 31 March would push the main revenue event into fiscal 2028 and leave a large hole in the current year's numbers.

The guidance may also be conservative. Coverage of the May results noted it sat below a consensus figure of about $9.13bn, which suggests the market is pricing in a bigger launch than the company has been willing to put in writing.

Why the wider sector watches

The game matters beyond one publisher. A release this heavily anticipated is expected to lift console hardware and accessory sales, and to pull spending away from, or at least test, other titles clustered around the holiday window. Rival publishers have spent two years scheduling around Rockstar's date. A third delay would reset those expectations as well as Take-Two's.

The sceptical view is that guidance reiterated in August says little about whether the game is finished. The company has been precise about the date and vague about pre-order volumes, and it has so far offered no figures that could be checked.

What to watch

The launch on 19 November is the test. Take-Two's fiscal second-quarter results, covering July to September, should arrive in early November and are the next point at which the company can confirm or alter the date and its $8.0bn to $8.2bn range. The company's investor relations page will carry the exact date; it was not confirmed in the sources reviewed. Any change in wording about the date, or any cut to the range, would be the signal the market has been waiting for.
