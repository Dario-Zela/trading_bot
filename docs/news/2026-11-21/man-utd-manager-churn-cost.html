Manchester United's managerial churn has cost nearly £100m

In one sentence
Estimates put Manchester United's compensation for sacked managers since Ferguson at £85m-£110m, and Michael Carrick's exit this month adds an undisclosed sum.

Manchester United have now dismissed seven managers since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in May 2013, and the compensation bill for the churn is put at close to £100m. The figure is an estimate, not an audited total. It is also not final: Michael Carrick was sacked on 12 November, and the cost of his exit has not been disclosed.

The context matters, because the triage version of this story is out of date. Erik ten Hag was dismissed in October 2024 and Ruben Amorim arrived the following month. But Amorim was himself sacked in January 2026. Carrick, a former United midfielder, took over on an interim basis and was later given a two-year deal. He left after a disastrous start to the season that had United 18th in the Premier League with eight points from 10 games. They visit Liverpool on Sunday without a permanent successor. Mauricio Pochettino, currently managing the United States, is reported to have agreed terms in principle, though there has been no official announcement.

Where the number comes from

The £100m headline traces to reporting in January 2026, after Amorim's exit. GB News put the total at nearly £100m, with Amorim's severance at around £10m. Other tallies differ, mostly because they count different things:

  • Footballtransfers.com calculated €101.8m (£85.2m) to sack managers since 2013.
  • Goal's earlier tally, before Amorim's dismissal, was £69.8m paid to sacked managers and their coaching teams.
  • The Telegraph, as cited by Football Whispers, took the widest view. It counted coaching staff and senior football executives as well, and put the bill above £108m if the Pochettino appointment goes through.

The Associated Press, in a piece republished by theScore, gave the individual payouts it could attribute:

  • José Mourinho: £19.6m for him and his staff, 11 months after signing a long-term deal in 2018.
  • Amorim: £8.2m to pay off him and his staff, on top of the £11m United paid to release him from Sporting Lisbon in 2024.
  • Ten Hag: £10.4m for him and his staff, three months after a contract extension.
  • Louis van Gaal: £8.4m, two days after he won the FA Cup in 2016.
  • David Moyes: £5.2m, fired 11 months into a six-year contract.

The AP did not give an exact total, saying only that United had spent "well in excess of $100 million" on failed coaches and executives, and it did not set out its methodology. Only one number, the £4.1m cost of sporting director Dan Ashworth's exit, is attributed to the club's accounts. The rest are reported figures. The honest reading is that the cost is somewhere between £85m and £110m, depending on whether staff, executives and the cost of buying managers out of other clubs are counted.

From results to governance

The payments themselves are the smaller part of the argument. The pattern is more telling: Mourinho, Ten Hag and Carrick were each dismissed within months of a new or extended contract. Each such decision puts a payout on the balance sheet and restarts a rebuild.

AP puts the cost under minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, whose Ineos group took over football operations in 2024, at £33.7m. That covers Ten Hag, both Amorim payments and Ashworth, and it excludes Mourinho. Ratcliffe's team arrived promising to end the improvisation. Within two years they have dismissed three head coaches.

What to watch

The next data points are the appointment itself and the Carrick settlement. Both would add to the total, and a Pochettino deal would raise the question of whether the structure around the head coach has changed or only the name on the door. United's next published accounts should show how much of the severance is booked as an exceptional cost, which would give the first firm figure in a story that has so far relied on estimates.
