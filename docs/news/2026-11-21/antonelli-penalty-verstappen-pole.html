Antonelli fastest in qualifying, but a penalty leaves Verstappen on pole

In one sentence
Kimi Antonelli set the fastest qualifying time in Las Vegas, but a five-place engine penalty drops him to sixth and hands Max Verstappen pole.

Kimi Antonelli was the quickest man in Las Vegas qualifying on Saturday. He will not start from pole. A five-place grid penalty for an engine change drops the Mercedes driver to sixth, and Max Verstappen inherits the front spot.

The fastest lap and the pole position went to different people, which is rare enough to be worth spelling out. Antonelli's 1m 33.893s in Q3 was the best of the session. Verstappen was second in the Red Bull, 0.258s adrift at 1m 34.151s. The grid, though, is set after penalties, and the engine change took Antonelli out of the picture.

A tow down the Strip

Antonelli's lap owed a good deal to the Strip's long straights. Reports from the session say he made use of the slipstream, gaining roughly 16kph on his team-mate George Russell through turn 14. On a circuit where the tow is worth that much, timing a lap behind another car is half the job, and the 20-year-old managed it.

The penalty, a standard sanction for taking a new power unit component beyond the permitted allocation, is a matter of arithmetic rather than misbehaviour. Antonelli had the pace to be on pole, but the engine count had already been spent.

The grid

Verstappen's pole is his third of the season. The provisional order behind him:

  1. Max Verstappen (Red Bull)
  2. George Russell (Mercedes)
  3. Lando Norris (McLaren)
  4. Isack Hadjar (Red Bull)
  5. Arvid Lindblad (Racing Bulls)
  6. Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes)
  7. Oscar Piastri (McLaren)
  8. Liam Lawson (Racing Bulls)
  9. Pierre Gasly (Alpine)
  10. Franco Colapinto (Alpine)

Lindblad is under investigation over a pit-exit incident with Alexander Albon, so the order could still move. Oliver Bearman carries a 35-place penalty and will start at the back.

Ferrari had a poor afternoon. Both cars went out in Q2. Charles Leclerc ran wide at turn seven and lost time, and Lewis Hamilton said: "I just don't have any grip for some reason."

What it means

For Mercedes the day is mixed. Russell starts second, so the team has a car on the front row, but its quickest driver has to climb from sixth. Antonelli's pace suggests he can make up ground. Las Vegas has a long run to the first braking zone and plenty of slipstream, both of which help a driver starting behind. Verstappen, by contrast, has the clean air of pole and a Mercedes alongside him.

The result has no direct bearing on the championship standings in this report, which come from qualifying alone. The race, and whether Antonelli's straight-line speed can turn a sixth-place start into something better, is the next test.
