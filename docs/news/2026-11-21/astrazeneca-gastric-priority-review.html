AstraZeneca gets FDA priority review for gastric cancer filing

In one sentence
The FDA has accepted AstraZeneca's application for sonesitatug vedotin in advanced gastric cancer with priority review; a decision is expected in Q2 2027.

The US Food and Drug Administration has accepted AstraZeneca's application for a new gastric cancer drug and given it priority review. The drug is sonesitatug vedotin, known as Sone-Ve. It is an antibody-drug conjugate aimed at a protein called claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2). The company announced the acceptance on 19 November. It expects the FDA to act in the second quarter of 2027.

The filing is for second-line or later treatment of locally advanced or metastatic gastric, gastro-oesophageal junction (GEJ) or oesophageal adenocarcinoma. Tumours must be CLDN18.2-positive, defined as at least 25% of tumour cells staining for the protein. The drug remains investigational in the US. Priority review shortens the FDA's target timeline. It is not approval.

What the trial shows

The application rests on CLARITY-Gastric01 (NCT06346392), a global, open-label, randomised phase 3 trial of 594 patients. It compared Sone-Ve at 2.2 mg/kg with investigator's choice of treatment. In the second line, that was ramucirumab plus paclitaxel, or paclitaxel or docetaxel alone. From the third line, it was irinotecan, trifluridine/tipiracil or, in China, apatinib. The dual primary endpoints were progression-free survival (PFS) in all patients and overall survival (OS) in third-line-and-later patients, as reported by OncLive.

The results are mixed, and the mix matters for this brief.

Endpoint Sone-Ve Control Hazard ratio (95% CI)
PFS, all patients 4.2 months 3.8 months 0.77 (0.62–0.96)
OS, all patients (secondary) 10.6 months 6.4 months 0.58 (0.47–0.73)
OS, third line or later (primary) 10.0 months 5.0 months 0.47 (0.36–0.62)

The survival result is large. Median OS in the third-line-and-later group doubled, from 5.0 to 10.0 months. The PFS gain is small: 0.4 months at the median. The reported p-value of 0.053 sits just above the conventional 0.05 threshold. A hazard ratio of 0.77 with a confidence interval that excludes 1 does not sit easily beside a p-value above 0.05. That is a discrepancy the available reporting does not explain. Readers should wait for the full presentation or publication before leaning on the PFS figure. The OS data are the stronger part of the package, but the trial was open-label, which leaves room for bias in how later treatment and assessment were handled.

AstraZeneca's Susan Galbraith called Sone-Ve "the first antibody-drug conjugate targeting CLDN18.2 to improve OS for patients". She said priority review "accelerates the ability to bring forward a new targeted approach and alternative to traditional chemotherapy".

Safety

The adverse-event profile was broadly similar between arms, with some differences worth noting:

  • Serious adverse events: 34.9% with Sone-Ve versus 31.3% with control.
  • Discontinuations for adverse events: 5.0% versus 4.4%.
  • Adverse events leading to death: 4.2% versus 2.4%. Treatment-related deaths were 1.1% versus 0.8%.

The gap in fatal adverse events is small in absolute terms, but it is the kind of figure a reviewing panel will examine.

What to watch

The FDA's decision is expected in Q2 2027. Before then, the full CLARITY-Gastric01 data should be presented at a medical meeting or published, which would settle the PFS question. AstraZeneca's own press release was not accessible when this was written, and the figures above come from trade-press reporting of it. They should be checked against the primary source.

Priority review is granted to drugs that may offer significant improvements over existing options. On the OS numbers reported, the application has a plausible case. The Q2 2027 action date is the next hard catalyst.
