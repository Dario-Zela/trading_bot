Wall Street idles near records in thin mid-August trade

The New York Stock Exchange trading floor, where the S&P 500 has notched 27 record closes so far this year. — Eduard Hueber / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Cooler CPI and PPI prints have removed the threat of a near-term Fed hike, leaving stocks parked near records in thin August trade ahead of Jackson Hole.

Wall Street spent last week doing what it rarely manages in August: standing still at the top. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,798.99 on August 13 — its 27th record close of the year — after touching an intraday high of 7,816.70. The Nasdaq Composite added 0.81% to 26,803.03 the same session. By Friday the indices had eased back slightly, but the S&P 500 still notched its third straight weekly gain, and both benchmarks remain within a percent or so of their peaks heading into the new week.

The trigger was a one-two combination of inflation data that came in softer than economists had modelled. July's Consumer Price Index rose just 0.1% on the month, leaving the annual rate at 3.4%. Two days later, producer prices were flat against forecasts for a 0.2% rise; the core measure rose 0.2% versus an expected 0.3%, and the year-on-year final-demand gauge cooled to 4.7% from 5.5% in June. Neither report was dramatic on its own, but together they did something specific: they took the near-term odds of the Federal Reserve resuming rate increases at its September meeting off the table, with pricing for the next hike pushed out toward 2027.

A calendar built for consolidation

What happens next is, by design, not much. This week's docket is thin by comparison — Wednesday's FOMC minutes, a wave of retail earnings led by Home Depot, and Wednesday's July housing starts and industrial production figures are the notable fixtures, alongside Reddit's debut in the S&P 500 index itself. Futures were mixed into Monday's open, with Dow contracts down about 0.11% and S&P and Nasdaq futures up 0.1% and 0.35% respectively — the kind of narrow, low-conviction drift that defines a market waiting rather than one still climbing.

August is structurally the thinnest trading month of the year, and thin liquidity cuts both ways: it can exaggerate a rally, but it also means there is little fresh capital pushing indices meaningfully higher once the initial catalyst — here, the inflation prints — has been digested. Traders are effectively marking time until Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote on August 28, an appearance he has signalled will address the Fed's broader policy framework rather than offer a narrow rate signal. Oil drifted higher alongside renewed Israeli strikes in Lebanon and the prospect of fresh US sanctions on Iran, a reminder that the market's current calm rests on an assumption that geopolitical risk stays background noise rather than becoming the next catalyst.

For now, the inflation data has done its job: it removed a reason for stocks to fall. It has not yet supplied a reason for them to go meaningfully higher. That leaves the next two weeks — light on data, heavy on earnings and one closely watched speech — as the window in which markets either find a new catalyst or simply keep idling near the top.
