Barcelona agree £65m deal to sign Manchester City's Rodri

Rodri playing for Spain in 2026, the year he won the World Cup Golden Ball before completing his move from Manchester City to Barcelona. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Barcelona have completed a £65m deal for Rodri, ending his seven-year, 12-trophy spell at Manchester City just months after Pep Guardiola's own exit.

Rodri will train at Barcelona next season, not Manchester. Six weeks after Pep Guardiola left the Etihad Stadium for the last time, the Ballon d'Or holder and World Cup Golden Ball winner is following his old manager's advice out of English football entirely — to the club Guardiola once built into a dynasty of his own.

Barcelona have agreed a £65m (roughly €76.5m) deal to sign Rodri from Manchester City, ending a saga that ran the length of the summer and, for a while, looked destined for Madrid rather than Catalonia. The 30-year-old will sign a four-year contract worth around €30m a season gross and is expected to be presented at Camp Nou this week, ahead of Barcelona's Joan Gamper Trophy fixture against Al Ahly.

Seven years, twelve trophies, one phone call

Rodri arrived at City from Atlético Madrid in 2019 for what was then a club-record £62.8m. He leaves having made 196 appearances and scored 23 goals for the club, but the number that matters more is the honours list: four Premier League titles, two FA Cups, four League Cups, the 2022–23 Champions League, and the 2024 Ballon d'Or — awarded after a season in which he anchored City's midfield so completely that Guardiola once called him, without much exaggeration, undroppable.

This past year complicated that reputation without diminishing it. Rodri ruptured the anterior cruciate ligament in his right knee against Arsenal in September 2024 and did not play again competitively until May 2025. City visibly missed him: they fell 20 points off the Premier League pace and were knocked out of the Champions League by Real Madrid during his absence. He returned in time to help Spain win the 2026 World Cup, taking the Golden Ball as the tournament's outstanding player — proof, if City needed it, of what they were about to lose.

Barcelona's move only came together late. Their first bid, at £38.4m, was waved away by City as unserious; a second offer was also rejected. It took club president Joan Laporta stepping into the negotiations personally, and head coach Hansi Flick placing a direct call to Rodri to lay out his role in the team, to tip the decision Barcelona's way over Real Madrid — who had appeared, for much of the summer, to be the likelier destination. Rodri is said to have leaned on Guardiola's judgment in making the call: Barcelona's possession-based system, his old boss reportedly told him, suited his game better than anything on offer at the Bernabéu.

A City in transition, minus its anchor

What makes the exit sting more than a straightforward departure is timing. Guardiola left City in May, ending eighteen years in English football and handing the reins to Enzo Maresca — himself a former City assistant during the 2022–23 treble season, returning after stints at Leicester and Chelsea. City finished as Premier League runners-up in 2025–26, their first season without a title since 2019–20, and Maresca inherits a squad that is losing its defensive midfield fulcrum in the same summer it is losing its manager's decade-long system.

City have moved to soften the blow. Names linked to the vacancy include Chelsea's Enzo Fernández, at a reported £120m valuation, Real Sociedad's Martin Zubimendi, Atalanta's Ederson, Torino's Samuele Ricci, and the 18-year-old Lille prospect Ayyoub Bouaddi, whom City are said to view as a longer-term successor rather than an immediate like-for-like replacement. None arrives with Rodri's trophy cabinet, and a raw teenager in particular represents a bet on development over continuity — exactly the kind of gap a club accustomed to buying certainty is not used to leaving open.

What the fee says about the balance of power

For Barcelona, completing a deal of this size matters as much as the player himself. The club spent recent seasons registering signings under La Liga's financial fair play strictures, occasionally unable to field new arrivals until squad costs were trimmed elsewhere. Landing a Ballon d'Or winner and World Cup Golden Ball recipient for a fee in the region of City's own valuation — after two rejected bids — is a statement that Barcelona's spending power has caught back up with its ambition, even as Real Madrid also chased the same player and lost.

City, for their part, are not short of resources; letting Rodri go was as much about his contract situation — he had entered the final year of his deal and City risked losing him for nothing next summer — as about need. But a club whose success under Guardiola was built on assembling a deeper, more expensively insured squad than anyone else is now testing whether that model holds without either its manager or its defensive midfield lynchpin in the building at the same time.

Rodri is due to link up with Flick's squad this week, with his Barcelona debut likely to come in the Joan Gamper Trophy or shortly after in La Liga's opening rounds. City, meanwhile, face the more immediate test: their Premier League opener arrives with Maresca still assembling a midfield that, for the first time in seven years, does not have Rodri's name pencilled into it.
