BP, Marathon, 7-Eleven and Walmart sued over AI-driven petrol price coordination

California drivers sue BP, Marathon, 7-Eleven, and Walmart, alleging Kalibrate's shared AI pricing tool coordinated pump prices at 1,700+ stations — inflating gasoline by up to 22¢/gallon.

Four major energy and retail companies face a California lawsuit alleging they used artificial intelligence pricing algorithms to coordinate and inflate gasoline pump prices. The suit targets BP, Marathon, 7-Eleven, and Walmart over pricing behavior that, if directed by humans, would constitute price-fixing.

The case tests a central legal gap: whether companies can evade antitrust liability by delegating pricing decisions to autonomous algorithms. As algorithmic pricing spreads across energy and retail markets, courts have not yet established how accountability works when algorithms behave in ways that restrain competition.

A ruling in the plaintiffs' favor would reshape antitrust law and signal that companies bear liability for what their pricing algorithms do — carrying implications for traders and retailers using automated pricing strategies across markets.
