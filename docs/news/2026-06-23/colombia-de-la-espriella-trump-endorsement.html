Trump endorses Colombia's new conservative president, signalling closer bilateral ties

Abelardo de la Espriella, Colombia's president-elect, pictured in 2026. The criminal defence lawyer won Sunday's runoff by fewer than 250,000 votes. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Trump claimed credit for Colombia's razor-thin right-wing presidential upset, pledging a close bilateral relationship and a White House visit for president-elect 'El Tigre'.

A criminal defence lawyer who has never held public office will become Colombia's next president — and Donald Trump wants credit for it.

Abelardo de la Espriella, the 47-year-old Bogotá-born attorney known as 'El Tigre', won Sunday's presidential runoff by fewer than 250,000 votes out of 26.3 million cast, a margin of less than one percentage point that makes it the narrowest presidential victory in Colombia's recorded history. His left-wing opponent, Senator Iván Cepeda — backed by outgoing President Gustavo Petro — has alleged irregularities without producing evidence; both the OAS and EU observer missions dismissed the complaints.

Within hours of the preliminary result, Trump was on Truth Social. "He Won, BIG!" the post began — the emphasis perhaps compensating for the sliver of a margin. In a second post in Spanish, Trump offered a fuller picture: "It was my Great Honor to endorse him, and I look forward to working together to build a powerful relationship between Colombia and the United States of America." He extended a formal White House invitation. Secretary of State Marco Rubio followed with a call pledging cooperation on "regional security, illegal immigration, and economic ties."

The endorsement itself had come earlier this month, when Trump wrote on Truth Social that de la Espriella would be "tremendously successful" in growing the economy, promoting trade, stopping illegal immigration, and cracking down on crime and drugs — a checklist that tracks the administration's regional priorities more than Colombia's domestic debate. De la Espriella responded by thanking Trump with conspicuous warmth: "With my head held high and a heart full of patriotic gratitude, I receive your words."

What the relationship is actually built on

The substantive overlap between Washington and Bogotá under de la Espriella centres on security architecture and commodity policy. The president-elect has pledged to join the Trump administration's proposed "Shield of the Americas" regional security alliance, align with its counter-narcotics doctrine — placing aerial coca eradication at the centre of his platform — and restore diplomatic ties with Israel, severed under Petro.

For markets, the more immediate signal is in hydrocarbons. De la Espriella has promised to reverse the Petro-era moratorium on new oil and gas exploration contracts, open the door to fracking, and cut state spending by as much as 40%. The US is already Colombia's largest trading partner, absorbing 36% of its exports; crude petroleum was Colombia's top export at $1.25bn in March 2026 alone. A genuine upstream re-engagement — which analysts at Colombia Risk Analysis describe as contingent on de la Espriella assembling a workable congressional coalition — could meaningfully accelerate US investment in Colombian extractive acreage.

JP Morgan's Diego Pereira flags one tension: de la Espriella has signalled interest in renegotiating the US-Colombia free trade agreement's provisions on dairy, which he views as damaging to domestic producers. Pereira calls it "a modest, sector-specific risk rather than a threat to the FTA architecture itself" — but it is a reminder that even aligned governments negotiate.

The political arithmetic remains fragile. De la Espriella enters office without a congressional majority and, as Sergio Guzmán of Colombia Risk Analysis put it, with a mandate so thin he "will have to tone down his incendiary rhetoric" to govern. Petro's left, energised and aggrieved, is positioning as a combative opposition.

The official manual vote count will conclude in the coming days; inauguration follows on 7 August. What happens in the weeks between — whether the margin holds, whether a coalition takes shape — will determine whether the relationship Trump is claiming is more than a photograph.
