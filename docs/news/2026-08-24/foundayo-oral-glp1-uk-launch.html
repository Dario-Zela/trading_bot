New once-daily diabetes and weight-loss pill Foundayo launches in UK pharmacies

Eli Lilly's corporate headquarters in Indianapolis, Indiana — the company behind Foundayo (orforglipron) and the injectable Mounjaro and Zepbound. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Eli Lilly's oral pill Foundayo reached UK pharmacies today, but Novo Nordisk's rival oral Wegovy beat it to MHRA approval by two months and is outselling it in the US.

A once-daily pill that treats both type 2 diabetes and obesity went on sale in UK pharmacies today, the latest attempt by a drugmaker to prise the booming GLP-1 market away from the syringe. Orforglipron — sold under the brand name Foundayo — was cleared by the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency on 10 August, and Eli Lilly says it is now reaching patients through private prescription and pharmacist-led supply services across the country.[^1]

The headline claim attached to the launch — that this is Europe's first oral GLP-1 pill — needs a footnote. It isn't quite right, and the distinction matters for anyone trying to work out who is actually winning this race.

What the MHRA actually approved

Foundayo is licensed for two things: weight management in adults with a BMI of 30 or above (or 27–30 with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure), and glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes, both alongside diet and exercise.[^2] Dosing starts at 0.8mg and climbs through five further steps to a maximum 36mg, with at least a month between increases — a slower, more forgiving escalation than some injectable regimens. Lilly's selling point is flexibility: the tablet can be taken at any time of day, with or without food or water, which is a genuine difference from Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide, which requires an empty stomach and a 30-minute wait before eating.[^3]

Julian Beach, the MHRA's executive director of healthcare quality and access, said the agency was "pleased to be the first regulator in Europe to authorise this tablet for weight management and type 2 diabetes" — a carefully worded claim about this molecule, not about oral GLP-1s as a class.[^2]

That qualifier is doing real work. Novo Nordisk's Wegovy pill — oral semaglutide, the same active ingredient as the injectable version — was approved by the MHRA on 11 June, two months before Foundayo, and had already reached UK pharmacies in stages through July and early August.[^4] So Britain's actual first oral GLP-1 was Danish, not American, and it beat Lilly to market by roughly six weeks. What Lilly has is the first authorisation of a structurally different drug: orforglipron is a small-molecule, non-peptide GLP-1 agonist, manufactured more like a conventional tablet and, Lilly argues, more cheaply at scale than peptide-based pills. Novo got there first; Lilly got there differently.

The efficacy trade-off

The clinical picture cuts in different directions depending on the indication, and it is worth being precise here rather than declaring a winner.

For weight loss, Novo's pill currently has the edge: in trial data, the high-dose Wegovy tablet produced average weight loss of 13.6–16.6% of body weight over roughly 64 weeks, against 12.4% for Foundayo's top 36mg dose over 72 weeks — a longer course for a smaller result.[^5] But in diabetes, the order reverses. In Lilly's own head-to-head ACHIEVE-3 trial, published in The Lancet, orforglipron's 36mg dose cut HbA1c by 2.2 percentage points and produced 9.2% weight loss in people with type 2 diabetes, against 1.4 points and 5.3% for oral semaglutide's top dose.[^6] Discontinuation due to side effects — mostly nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, the class-wide GLP-1 complaint — ran somewhat higher on orforglipron in that trial (8.7–9.7%) than on oral semaglutide (4.5–4.9%).[^6]

Neither drug is available on the NHS yet. Both require a separate assessment by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence before they can be prescribed for weight management on public funds; Lilly says Foundayo can already be used on the NHS for diabetes, but is seeking a NICE extension to cover obesity.[^1] Guidance on that question is expected in November.[^1] Until then, access runs through private prescription, at a cost — pharmacies are quoting Foundayo at roughly £129 to £179 a month, undercutting Wegovy's pill (£99–£269) and both companies' injectables, which run from around £140 to £375 depending on dose.[^7]

A market that Lilly is not yet winning

The UK launch lands against an awkward backdrop for Lilly. Foundayo has been on sale in the United States since earlier this year, and its second-quarter sales there came in at $98m — slightly below analyst forecasts, and dwarfed by the roughly $500m Novo's oral Wegovy took in over the same three months.[^8] Citi analysts called the number "uninspiring."[^8] Lilly's response has been that this is a slow-build launch: its prescriber base nearly quadrupled from around 8,000 to 36,000 physicians during the quarter, a ramp its executives compare to the early trajectory of the injectable Zepbound before it became a blockbuster.[^8]

It is a curious inversion. Lilly dominates the injectable GLP-1 market — Mounjaro and Zepbound have consistently out-sold Novo's Ozempic and Wegovy shots on a head-to-head efficacy basis — yet in oral formulations, the company chasing convenience is currently the one playing catch-up. Some analysts still expect that to flip: Goldman Sachs has projected oral GLP-1s will make up around $22bn, or roughly a quarter, of the global weight-loss drug market by 2030, with Lilly's orforglipron eventually capturing the larger share of that pool.[^9] Whether that forecast holds will depend less on regulatory firsts than on something more mundane — pricing, prescriber comfort, and whether patients tolerate the tablet's side-effect profile well enough to stay on it.

What's next

The UK's community pharmacies have already flagged one immediate concern: counterfeit and unlicensed GLP-1 products circulating online, a problem that predates Foundayo and is likely to intensify as demand for oral options grows.[^1] The nearer-term catalyst is regulatory rather than clinical — NICE's November verdict on whether Foundayo can be prescribed on the NHS for weight management will do more to determine its real UK market size than anything in this week's pharmacy launch. Lilly's next quarterly results, in the autumn, will show whether the US prescriber ramp is translating into the sales growth the company is promising.
