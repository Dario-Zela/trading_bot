Tesla recalls nearly 3 million China vehicles over door-handle risk

A Tesla Model 3, one of four models covered by China's nearly 3 million-vehicle door-handle recall. — iMoD Official / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0)
In one sentence
Tesla leads a record 4.3 million-vehicle China recall over trapdoor-style handles that can fail after a crash, ahead of a 2027 ban that forces a redesign industry-wide.

Tesla is recalling nearly 3 million vehicles in China because the door handles can trap people inside after a crash — the centrepiece of a recall covering 4.3 million cars across eleven manufacturers, the largest safety action in the history of the country's auto industry. The regulator's fix, for now, is a warning sticker and a software patch. The real fix, which the industry has until 2027 to build, is putting a proper handle back on the car.

In one sentence: Tesla leads a record 4.3 million-vehicle China recall over trapdoor-style handles that can fail after a crash, ahead of a 2027 ban that forces the whole industry to redesign.

A handle that disappears when it's needed most

The design at issue is the flush, electronically retracting door handle Tesla popularised on the Model S and made standard across its lineup — panels sit level with the bodywork and pop out on approach or at the press of a button, a look every EV maker from Xiaomi to Zeekr has since copied. China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) found the problem is what happens when the electronics that pop the handle out stop working. In a severe collision, the low-voltage system that powers the mechanism can fail, and the manual backup release — tucked into the door trim in a colour that blends with the cabin — becomes nearly impossible to find under stress, in the dark, or with smoke in the cabin.

The scale of Tesla's exposure is precise: SAMR's filing covers 2,975,910 vehicles — Model 3, Model Y, Model S and Model X, imported and locally built, spanning model years 2018 through 2026. Xiaomi's SU7 accounts for another 390,435 cars, Leapmotor 371,200, Xpeng nearly 265,000 and Zeekr close to 93,000, with Chery, Dongfeng, Arcfox and FAW also named. Bloomberg has tied at least 15 US deaths to Tesla door handles failing after crashes, including a 2023 Tacoma, Washington case in which a bystander could not free a trapped driver, Wendy Dennis, from a burning Model 3. In China, the immediate trigger was a March 2025 crash in which a Xiaomi SU7 hit a barrier and caught fire, killing three university students after witnesses said they could not get the doors open. Tesla's remedy, rolling out from 25 September, pairs free warning labels showing occupants where the manual release sits with an over-the-air update that automatically drops all four windows the instant a crash is detected — a stopgap, not a redesign.

Beijing sets a hard deadline the rest of the world hasn't

What makes this more than a routine recall is the standard sitting behind it. SAMR and China's Standardization Administration finalised GB 48001-2026 in January, and it does not merely encourage a fix — it specifies one. Every door but the tailgate must retain a mechanical release, front and back, that works with no battery and no airbag deployment, able to withstand 500 newtons of force without breaking, with a grip cavity generously sized for an adult hand groping in the dark. New models seeking type approval in China must comply from 1 January 2027; existing models on the road have until 2029 to be retrofitted. No other market has gone this far. The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, after declining in July to open a formal defect investigation into Tesla's mechanical release, said instead it would explore new industry-wide egress rules — a slower, more open-ended process than Beijing's fixed date. Hong Kong has separately moved to bar new EVs sold there from relying on electronic handles alone.

The cost of designing for China first

China is Tesla's second-largest market and the one most exposed to Beijing's regulatory reflexes — this is also the country's fourth vehicle recall since 2021, on top of a 1.6 million-car steering and software action in 2024. None of that has been fatal to sales, but the pattern is now familiar enough to read as a cost of doing business there rather than a one-off. The bigger signal is aimed past Tesla, at every manufacturer that treated a flush handle as a premium, aerodynamic flourish rather than a safety-critical part. Beijing's 2029 retrofit deadline effectively puts a shelf life on millions of cars' current form as designed, and any manufacturer still shipping flush-only handles into China after January 2027 will be locked out of new type approval altogether. That is a harder constraint than a fine or a recall notice — it is a design brief, delivered by regulation, for the world's largest EV market.

Watch two dates from here: 25 September, when Tesla's software patch and labelling begin reaching Chinese owners, and 1 January 2027, when GB 48001 takes effect for new models. NHTSA's rulemaking process, still undefined in scope, will show whether the US follows China's lead or leaves the fix to manufacturers' discretion.
