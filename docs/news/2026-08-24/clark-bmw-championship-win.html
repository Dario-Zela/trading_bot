Wyndham Clark holds off Rory McIlroy fightback to win BMW Championship

Wyndham Clark, who closed with three straight birdies to win the BMW Championship at Bellerive. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Wyndham Clark blew a five-shot lead then birdied his last three holes to beat McIlroy and Cantlay at Bellerive for his third win of 2026.

Wyndham Clark had built a five-shot cushion by the turn at Bellerive Country Club. By the time he stood on the 16th tee, it was gone.

Clark went two-over for his first seven holes on Sunday, missing putts he'd been making all week, while Rory McIlroy reeled off birdies at 8, 9 and 11 to draw level. For a stretch on the back nine, the BMW Championship — the second leg of the PGA Tour's FedExCup playoffs — looked like it might slip away from the 32-year-old entirely.

It didn't. Clark answered with a 232-yard 5-iron to 15 feet at the par-3 16th and made the putt, then birdied the par-5 17th as McIlroy found bunkers and made bogey. A third straight birdie at 18 sealed it: Clark closed with an even-par 70 to finish at 17-under 263, three clear of Patrick Cantlay, with McIlroy third at 13-under.

"I wasn't really nervous. I was kind of like ... out of it. I was making dumb bogeys," Clark said afterward.

A messy final round, a clean finish

Cantlay made his own run, five birdies in his first ten holes pulling him into contention, before a double bogey at the 11th — his ball found the water — took the wind out of it. He rallied again, birdieing his final three holes to secure outright second. McIlroy's chance effectively ended at 17, where a wayward drive led to bogey just as Clark birdied the same hole from the group ahead.

It was the kind of final round that made for restless watching rather than a coronation: a five-shot lead reduced to nothing by the turn, then rebuilt in three holes. Clark's own read on it was blunt rather than triumphant.

"Three wins in one season is my best year yet — coming off my worst year ever," he said, a nod to a 2025 campaign that never got going after his breakthrough 2023 U.S. Open title.

Third win, and a case for player of the year

The victory is Clark's third of the 2026 PGA Tour season, a rare clustering that puts him into the player-of-the-year conversation alongside Scottie Scheffler, given that one of the three came at a major and this one at a FedExCup playoff event carrying a $20 million purse. Clark took home $3.6 million of it.

For McIlroy, third place extends a familiar pattern this season: contending deep into Sundays without adding to his win tally. "I always felt like I needed to just get my nose ahead to really try to put the pressure on Wyndham, and I couldn't,” he said.

Further down the leaderboard, the result carried its own consequences. Cantlay's runner-up finish vaulted him from 43rd into the top 30 of the FedExCup standings, as did a strong week from Gary Woodland. Rickie Fowler, Bud Cauley and Kurt Kitayama were among those who fell out of the top 30, with Ryan Fox claiming the final automatic spot.

Attention now turns to East Lake in Atlanta, where the top 30 in the FedExCup standings tee off next week in the Tour Championship — the last event of the playoffs and the one that decides the season's biggest prize.
