Barclays names Kinder Morgan among preferred North American energy infrastructure picks

Kinder Morgan's headquarters in Houston, Texas — the company operates roughly 79,000 miles of pipelines and 139 terminals across North America. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Barclays reaffirmed a $34 Buy target on Kinder Morgan (KMI) — an 8% upside call anchored in a $10.1B project backlog and data-center pipeline deals, not a new upgrade.

Barclays reaffirmed a Buy rating on Kinder Morgan (NYSE: KMI) on June 12, and named the Houston-based pipeline giant among its preferred North American energy infrastructure picks — though the reasoning behind that endorsement requires some digging to reconstruct, since the most visible version of the call has circulated primarily through aggregator sites rather than the original research note.

Kinder Morgan is one of the largest energy infrastructure companies in North America and the largest independent transporter of petroleum products in the United States, transporting approximately 2.4 million barrels per day.

The bank attached a $34 price target, implying roughly 8% upside from the stock's mid-June trading range around $31.50–$32. The broader analyst consensus sits slightly higher: 23 analysts polled by S&P Global carry an average 12-month target of $35.33, with the range spanning from $31 at the low to $43 at the high.

What the numbers actually say

Kinder Morgan's investment case rests less on commodity exposure — the company's pipelines and terminals earn mostly fee-based, volume-linked revenues — and more on the durability of its backlog and a structural tailwind from power demand. At the end of Q1 2026, the project backlog stood at $10.1 billion, up materially from a year ago. During the quarter, the company placed approximately $230 million of projects into service and added $375 million in new projects, including three data center supply contracts.

For fiscal 2026, management targets adjusted EBITDA of $8.6 billion — roughly 4% above 2025 guidance — and adjusted EPS of $1.36, a 5% year-on-year increase. Net income is projected at $3.1 billion, broadly flat year-on-year. The stock's dividend yield sits at 3.80%, which, combined with the fee-based cash flow profile, positions KMI as a relatively defensive income play within the energy complex.

Among the more concrete near-term projects feeding the backlog is the Creekside Lateral, designed to serve power generation, industrial, and data center demand under binding long-term contracts, targeted for commissioning in Q4 2026. Further out, the approximately $1.8 billion Trident Intrastate Pipeline — a 216-mile, 2 Bcf/day project running from Katy, Texas, to the industrial corridor near Port Arthur — is pencilled in for first phase delivery in early 2027.

The listicle problem

The Barclays call has reached most readers filtered through Insider Monkey's "12 Best American Oil Stocks to Buy Now" aggregation, which ranks a range of names without disclosing KMI's specific position on the list or reproducing the bank's original reasoning. That framing — analyst listicle without a rating change, without a new price target, without an articulated catalyst — limits the immediate actionability of the news. The June 12 reaffirmation of the Buy and $34 target was not a new initiation or an upgrade; it was a maintenance call.

For a trading desk, the signal worth monitoring is not the list itself but the underlying drivers: natural gas pipeline throughput trends, the pace of data center contract additions to the backlog, and whether Q2 2026 results — expected around late July — show the Creekside and other projects tracking on schedule. Goldman Sachs, for reference, carries a $35 target on KMI, one dollar above Barclays, and the stock has traded within a 52-week range of $25.60 to $34.81.
