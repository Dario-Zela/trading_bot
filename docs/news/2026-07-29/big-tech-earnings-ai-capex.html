Microsoft and Meta open Big Tech earnings week as Nvidia's OpenAI backstop rattles chips

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, whose fiscal 2027 capital expenditure guidance is the figure investors are watching most closely tonight — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Microsoft and Meta report today, Nvidia's reported $250bn OpenAI financing backstop hit chip stocks, and Salesforce's VA deal is fueling a software rotation — all testing whether AI capex is paying off.

Two of the world's five most valuable companies report earnings after the bell today, and for once the headline numbers may matter less than a single line item: capital expenditure guidance. Microsoft and Meta Platforms both post second-quarter results on July 29, kicking off a week of Big Tech earnings that will test whether the trillion-dollar bet on AI infrastructure is starting to pay for itself — or is still, eighteen months in, an act of faith.

The stakes were sharpened over the weekend by an unrelated but pointed reminder of how much money is now moving through the AI supply chain on promises rather than contracts. Nvidia shares fell roughly 5% and AMD dropped as much as 8% on Monday after reports that Nvidia is in talks to backstop up to $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's data-centre buildout in Ohio — a project that could eventually cost more than $500 billion. It is the kind of number that makes Microsoft's and Meta's own capex disclosures, due within hours of each other, unusually consequential.

The Wednesday test: Azure, Copilot and Meta's ad machine

Wall Street enters Microsoft's fiscal fourth-quarter report expecting earnings per share of roughly $4.24, up 16% year-on-year, on revenue near $87.6 billion, up about 15%. The number that will actually move the stock is Azure's growth rate — analysts are watching for whether the cloud unit holds in the high-30s percent range or shows the first signs of deceleration — alongside any detail on how Copilot usage is converting into paid seats rather than free trials.

But the figure investors say they care about most sits below the income statement: fiscal 2027 capex guidance. Analysts expect Microsoft to signal growth of 20–30% in infrastructure spending, which would put the annual figure near $220 billion. Given that Microsoft's capex has already climbed from roughly $56 billion in fiscal 2024 to well over $80 billion since, a guide at the top of that range would confirm the buildout is accelerating, not plateauing — welcome news for chip suppliers, less so for anyone hoping AI spending is close to a ceiling.

Meta reports the same day, with consensus estimates around $7.18 in EPS and revenue near $60.2 billion, up about 27%. Reels engagement and AI-driven ad targeting have kept the core advertising business humming even as the company's Superintelligence Labs and Reality Labs divisions burn cash at a pace that has periodically alarmed investors. Meta's own capex has ballooned past $70 billion annually, and chief executive Mark Zuckerberg has told investors repeatedly this year that he would rather overbuild compute capacity than risk being caught short — a framing that treats spending discipline as the greater risk, not the lesser one.

Nvidia's OpenAI backstop and the circularity question

The Nvidia news landed at an awkward moment for that argument. According to reports first surfaced by Benzinga and TradingKey and amplified by investors including Michael Burry, Nvidia is negotiating a guarantee structure that would help OpenAI secure debt financing for a 10-gigawatt data-centre campus, despite OpenAI lacking an investment-grade credit rating of its own. The amount and precise mechanics remain unconfirmed by any primary source — Nvidia has not issued an official statement — but the market reacted anyway, wiping out tens of billions in chipmaker market value in a single session.

The worry is a familiar one dressed in a bigger number: so-called circular financing, in which a chip supplier effectively funds its own customer's ability to buy its chips, inflating both sides of the ledger without new external capital entering the system. Nvidia has made similar commitments before, and just two days earlier had announced a separate $5 billion investment in Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence — a startup explicitly built around AI-safety research. The juxtaposition, safety capital and buildout capital moving through the same balance sheet in the same week, is why the stock reaction, however thinly sourced, found an audience. AMD's steeper fall — down as much as 8% against Nvidia's 5% — reflects how much of AMD's 124% year-to-date rally has rested on the MI450 accelerator's role in exactly this kind of hyperscaler buildout; any wobble in the financing story for one AI campus threatens the demand narrative for all of them.

Software's rotation, and Thursday's second act

Not every AI-adjacent stock has been dragged down by capex anxiety. Salesforce shares rose roughly 4.5% — and as much as 16.6% since Thursday's close, adding an estimated $21 billion in market value — after the Department of Veterans Affairs awarded the company a three-year, $1.6 billion Agentforce contract to deploy AI agents across VA healthcare and benefits systems. Salesforce's own disclosures put combined Agentforce and Data 360 annualised recurring revenue near $3.4 billion, up 205% year-on-year, and chief executive Marc Benioff has called agentic AI the company's largest growth opportunity in years.

The VA deal has become a proof point in a broader argument playing out this month: that markets have priced flawless execution into semiconductor names while leaving software platforms — which monetise AI through subscriptions rather than capital-intensive infrastructure — comparatively cheap. ServiceNow and Workday posted similar bounces in late July on the same thesis. Whether that rotation holds past this week depends heavily on what Microsoft says about Copilot conversion tonight; software's re-rating requires evidence that AI features are actually being paid for, not merely shipped.

The pattern continues Thursday, when Amazon and Apple report. Amazon's free cash flow has already told an uncomfortable story this year, collapsing from roughly $26 billion to just $1.2 billion on a trailing basis as the company committed close to $200 billion in 2026 capex — a figure some analysts expect CEO Andy Jassy to raise further — toward AI data centres, custom silicon and its Leo satellite constellation. AWS grew 28% in the most recent quarter, its fastest pace in fifteen quarters, which is the number Amazon needs investors to keep believing justifies the cash burn. Apple's earnings carry a different kind of scrutiny: its AI-driven iPhone upgrade cycle, still a largely unproven growth story, gets its first real earnings test.

What ties it together

The throughline across four days of earnings and one unconfirmed financing report is the same question asked from different angles: is AI capital spending an investment with a visible return, or an arms race that only ends when someone blinks? Microsoft and Meta's numbers tonight will show whether the two largest, most diversified AI spenders can point to revenue — Azure growth, Copilot seats, ad targeting gains — that scales with the capex. Nvidia's OpenAI exposure shows what happens to sentiment when the financing behind the buildout looks more like vendor-supported leverage than organic demand. And Salesforce's rally is the market's tentative answer to a narrower version of the same question: that AI monetisation, when it shows up as an actual signed contract rather than a spending commitment, still moves stocks in the direction fundamentals would predict.

The next data points land fast. Amazon and Apple report Thursday, July 30, with Amazon's capex guidance and Apple's AI upgrade-cycle commentary likely to either extend this week's rotation or reverse it. Whatever Nvidia says — officially or not — about the OpenAI financing structure will also keep circulating; a primary-source confirmation or denial, whenever it comes, is the next real catalyst for chip stocks rather than the secondary reporting that moved them this week.
