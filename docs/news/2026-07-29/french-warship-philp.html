French warship accused of firing warning shots at Tory MP in the Channel

The French Navy patrol vessel Flamant, identified as the ship involved in the Channel incident, pictured docked in Dublin. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Chris Philp says a French warship fired 17 rounds near his boat as "intimidation"; France calls it routine training, and neither claim is independently confirmed.

Chris Philp was mid-sentence with a BBC camera rolling when the shots rang out. The shadow home secretary was aboard a small boat in the Channel on Monday, filming a piece on migrant crossings, when a French navy patrol vessel came within about 300 metres and, he says, fired 17 live rounds.

"French warship approached close and fired 17 live rounds behind us," Philp wrote on X shortly afterwards. "There was no notice or training exercise in place. Felt like warning shots or attempted intimidation."

The vessel was the Flamant, a 1990s-built offshore patrol ship based out of Cherbourg and more usually deployed on fishery patrols and search-and-rescue than on migrant interdiction. It was around two miles off Boulogne when, according to Philp's account, a naval officer fired 17 handgun rounds at the point the ship passed closest to his boat — and nothing before or after.

Paris says training, London wants context

French defence staff moved quickly to offer an explanation, telling their British counterparts the firing was a routine training exercise unrelated to Philp's presence. "French navy units train regularly," a statement said. "Individual combatant training is a normal activity for a vessel on operations," adding that "the statutory signals required by international standards for this type of firing exercise were displayed, and all safety protocols were followed."

Philp was unmoved. "The French are now claiming that the shots were a training exercise," he posted. "This is not credible. There were no warnings in place about live firing training exercises. They fired the live rounds at the closest point of approach to us — and didn't fire anything that we saw before or after. It looked and sounded exactly like deliberate intimidation."

He has produced no independent witnesses or corroborating footage beyond his own account and that of the BBC crew present, and no injuries or damage were reported. A UK government spokesperson took a more measured line, telling reporters only that officials were "in contact with the French authorities to understand the full context of this incident."

Not everyone reads it Philp's way. Kevin Rowlands, a retired Royal Navy captain, noted that footage of the officer showed the weapon pointed in what he called a safe direction — the kind of detail that cuts against a deliberate-intimidation reading, whatever the lack of warning.

A charged backdrop

None of this is happening in a vacuum. Small-boat crossings have been the most combustible issue in UK-France relations for years, and Philp — who has previously described being pelted with bottles and having a knife pulled on him during a visit to a French migrant camp near Calais — has built a political identity around confronting the issue in person rather than from a select committee bench. That history lends his account credibility with sympathetic audiences and invites scepticism from others, given the incentive for an opposition frontbencher to cast any Channel friction as evidence of French indifference or worse.

The episode also lands just as Parliament breaks for summer recess, a period when a single dramatic claim can dominate a slow news cycle far more than its underlying facts warrant. There is, as yet, no independent verification of intent on either side — only Philp's account, the French explanation, and a widening gap between them.

What happens next depends on whether the Foreign Office presses Paris for more than a boilerplate readout, and whether any of the video or radio logs from the encounter surface publicly. Until then, this is a dispute of dueling narratives rather than confirmed fact — one that says more about the temperature of Channel politics than about what actually happened 300 metres off Boulogne.
