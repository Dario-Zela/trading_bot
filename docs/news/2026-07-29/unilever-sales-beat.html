Unilever shares jump 8% on fastest sales growth in a decade

In one sentence
Unilever's Q2 underlying sales grew 5.8% — beating the 4.3% consensus and its fastest pace in a decade — driven by volume, not price, sending shares up 8%.

Unilever shares had their best single session in two years on Tuesday, climbing as much as 8% after the consumer goods giant reported the fastest quarterly sales growth it has managed in more than a decade — and, crucially, showed the growth coming from people buying more, not just paying more.

Underlying sales rose 5.8% in the second quarter, comfortably ahead of the 4.3% analyst consensus compiled by the company. Volumes — the number of tubs of Dove, bottles of Persil and jars of Hellmann's actually leaving shelves — climbed 5.5%, more than double what the market had pencilled in. Chief executive Fernando Fernandez called it "the best volume quarter at Unilever in over a decade," and pointed to the acceleration from the first quarter's 3.8% underlying growth as evidence the business had turned a corner rather than had a lucky three months.

Why volume, not price, is the number that matters

For two years, staples companies have leaned on price increases to offset input cost inflation, a strategy that flattered revenue lines while quietly testing how much shoppers would tolerate before trading down to private label. That dynamic has been central to the bear case on the sector: raise prices enough and eventually volumes crack.

Unilever's numbers argue the opposite happened. Group underlying operating margin ticked up just 10 basis points to 20.3%, and underlying earnings per share rose a modest 2.4% to €1.61 — hardly a margin story. The growth is a demand story. Power Brands, the roughly 30 names Unilever has prioritised for investment such as Dove, Knorr and Liquid I.V., grew underlying sales 6.0% with 5.4% of that from volume. Emerging markets did even more of the work, up 8.3% in the quarter, led by India at 10%.

The guidance shift

Unilever raised its full-year outlook to underlying sales growth within its existing 4%-6% multi-year range, having previously guided to the bottom of that band. It's a narrower revision than the headline share move might suggest — the company hasn't torn up its framework, just moved within it — but for a stock priced for caution, even a modest upgrade delivered by a beat this size was enough to send shares to roughly 4,925p, their highest level since March, and their strongest one-day gain in about two years.

Morgan Stanley analysts described the print as a clean "beat and raise," the kind of result that forces short-term skeptics to cover rather than simply hold. Not every line was spotless: Unilever flagged intensifying competition in condiments and sauces, a reminder that category-level pressure hasn't disappeared even as the group aggregate improved.

What it says about consumer staples positioning

Investors have spent much of 2026 rotating out of defensives on the theory that stretched household budgets would eventually show up as weaker volumes across staples names. Unilever's print — the fastest sales growth in ten years, delivered mostly through units sold rather than price — cuts against that thesis, at least for a company with the brand strength and emerging-market exposure to hold volume while still growing. Whether it marks a genuine inflection in consumer demand or simply reflects Unilever's own execution, including its emerging-market weighting, will become clearer as peers report over the coming weeks.

The next test comes with rival staples results still due this earnings season, which will show whether the volume recovery is a Unilever story or a category-wide one.
