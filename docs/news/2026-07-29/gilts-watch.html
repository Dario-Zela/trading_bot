Gilt yields hold near two-month high before BoE report

The Bank of England on Threadneedle Street, which publishes its rate decision, minutes and Monetary Policy Report on Thursday. — David Iliff / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
UK 10-year gilt yields eased to 5.07% after a two-month high, with BoE minutes and its Monetary Policy Report due Thursday as QT continues toward £492bn.

Ten-year gilt yields eased to 5.07% on Tuesday, a modest retreat from the 5.06% peak struck the previous Thursday — the highest level since May and the sharpest reminder yet that Britain's borrowing costs remain stuck near two-month highs heading into Thursday's Bank of England announcement. The 30-year gilt, more sensitive to fiscal than monetary signals, told a similar story, climbing to 5.78% — its own highest print since May 19.

The proximate cause is oil. Brent crude briefly touched $100 a barrel amid Middle East tensions before retreating, and the swing has been enough to keep inflation expectations elevated and gilt traders reluctant to bid yields much lower. Markets are pricing in close to two further quarter-point Bank Rate increases by year-end, a notable shift for a central bank that spent much of 2025 focused on the path down from restrictive territory.

What Thursday's report needs to answer

The Bank's Monetary Policy Committee announces its rate decision at noon on Thursday, alongside the minutes of the meeting and a full quarterly Monetary Policy Report carrying new growth and inflation forecasts. Governor Andrew Bailey holds a press conference roughly half an hour later. Bank Rate has sat at 3.75% since June, when the committee held for a second consecutive meeting on a 7–2 vote — one more hawkish dissent than April's 8–1 split, and a signal that patience among some members is thinning.

The inflation picture the MPC must reconcile is genuinely mixed. Headline CPI slowed to 2.6% in June, a 15-month low and softer than the Bank's own forecast. But services inflation, the metric policymakers watch most closely for underlying price pressure, rose to 3.7% from 3.2% in April — above the 3.6% consensus estimate. Bailey is expected to stress that the committee is watching wage and price-setting behaviour that isn't simply a pass-through from energy costs, even as the ceasefire-driven pullback in oil offers some near-term relief.

Quantitative tightening continues in the background regardless of Thursday's rate call. The Bank's balance sheet, run down from an £895bn peak, stood at £492bn as of July 22, on track for a slower reduction pace than in prior years — the QT envelope was trimmed from £100bn to £70bn annually, with a landing zone of £65bn-£75bn. Fewer gilt sales flowing back to the market takes some technical pressure off yields, but has not been enough to offset the inflation-driven repricing of the rate path.

Any surprise Thursday — whether in the vote split, the forecast revisions, or Bailey's tone on services inflation — is likely to move gilt yields sharply in either direction. The next scheduled data point after that is August's CPI release, which will show whether June's cooling in headline inflation was a turning point or a one-off against a still-firm services backdrop.
