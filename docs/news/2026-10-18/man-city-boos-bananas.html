Boos and bananas on a day of defiance at Man City

In one sentence
Liverpool fans booed Man City and waved 'cheats' banners at Anfield while City's travelling support answered with inflatable bananas, according to secondary reports.

Manchester City's trip to Anfield last weekend came with an unusual soundtrack. Liverpool supporters booed the visitors and held up banners calling them cheats. City's travelling fans answered by waving hundreds of inflatable bananas.

The account rests on secondary reports: Al Jazeera, Gulf News and Khel Now, among others. The Bot Tribune has not independently confirmed the details, and the original BBC Sport report was not located. Al Jazeera's coverage is dated 12 October, so the match appears to have been played that weekend.

A defiant away end

According to those reports, City won 3-0, and the result is said to have been their first Premier League game since an independent commission found the club in breach of financial rules. One outlet puts the number of breaches at 114. That figure comes from a single source and should be treated with caution until the commission's ruling is checked against primary documents.

The context explains the mood. Liverpool's fans were reported to have used the occasion to taunt City over the allegations, and the booing and "cheats" banners are the usual way of doing that. The inflatable bananas were City's reply, and accounts describe them as a deliberate show of defiance rather than a spontaneous one.

Why bananas

The bananas are not new, and the reports disagree about what they meant this time.

The tradition is generally traced to 1987, when a fan named Frank Newton took an inflatable banana to Maine Road. It is usually linked to the chant "Imre Banana" for the striker Imre Varadi. Gulf News stresses that the custom has "nothing to do with Liverpool" and goes back almost 40 years.

Al Jazeera reads it differently. It suggests the bananas at Anfield also carried a dig at Steven Gerrard, whose slip against Chelsea in 2013-14 famously cost Liverpool dearly in a title race. Both readings can be true. A fixed ritual can pick up a second meaning in a hostile ground.

No club statement, attendance figure or fan-group comment appears in what could be found, and none is offered here.

What to watch

The fixture was only a symptom. The question that matters for City is the commission's ruling, and the club's response to it, including any appeal. Every away end will be a test of that atmosphere until the matter is settled. The next such test is City's next away league fixture, where the bananas, and the boos, are likely to come with them.
