Tesla unveils what it calls a flying car

In one sentence
Tesla reportedly showed a 'flying car', but no specs, price or independent account were available; the demo appears to involve SpaceX-style thrusters on a Roadster.

Tesla has shown what it describes as a flying car, according to a BBC Business headline. The details are thin. As of Sunday, no specifications, price, range, delivery date or certification status had been published in anything retrieved for this report, and Tesla's own account of the event was not available. What follows is what can be established, and where the gaps are.

What is known

The unveiling appears to be connected to the redesigned Roadster, the sports car Tesla first showed in 2017 with deliveries promised for 2020. Reuters reported on 13 September that Elon Musk said the company would unveil the long-delayed car on 1 October. Musk later wrote "New Tesla Roadster Unveil 10.01". An Italian outlet, Startupitalia, put the event on 15 October and noted two earlier postponements this year. The two dates conflict, and no report of the event itself was found.

The "flying" part has a specific, less dramatic description. The Information reported that the event could include a limited-edition Roadster fitted with cold-gas thrusters developed with SpaceX, shown at SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas. A Taiwanese report on the same plan said the demonstration would have the car briefly leave the ground. A syndicated CNN report described Musk showing a car climbing a ramp, hanging in the air and landing with air jets. That account rests on Musk's own description of what the car will do.

Cold-gas thrusters push out compressed gas, much as a fire extinguisher does, to create thrust. They are used on spacecraft for small manoeuvres. A car that hops or hovers on them for a few seconds is a demonstration of thruster hardware. It is not an aircraft in the sense regulators use the word, and nothing retrieved suggests otherwise.

What is not known

The claim as reported has no figures attached. There is no stated flight time, altitude, payload, power source for sustained flight, or production plan. Aviation regulators in the US and Europe certify aircraft through lengthy processes, and nothing indicates Tesla has started one. Musk has also hinted at a flying car for years without producing a finished one. On the Joe Rogan podcast in late 2025, he said a prototype would be shown before the end of the year, and he declined to say whether it would have wings or vertical take-off.

The summary line that investors are "weighing how credible the product claims are" could not be tested. No share-price move tied to the announcement was found.

What to watch

Three things would settle the matter. The first is Tesla's own account of the event, including video of the vehicle leaving the ground and any numbers on duration and height. The second is whether the thruster package is an option on a road car or a separate aircraft programme. The third is any filing or statement from a regulator, such as the US Federal Aviation Administration, on how such a vehicle would be classified. Until those appear, the reasonable reading is a staged demonstration of a car with rocket-style thrusters, not a flying car in the sense of something people can buy and use.
