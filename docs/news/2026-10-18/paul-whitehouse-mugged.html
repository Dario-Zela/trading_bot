Comedian Paul Whitehouse mugged at London cash machine

In one sentence
Comedian Paul Whitehouse was reportedly robbed at a London cash machine; the BBC's basic account is all that is confirmed, with date, losses and arrests unknown.

The comedian Paul Whitehouse was robbed at a cash machine in London, according to a BBC report. Few details were available when this piece was written, and the account below is limited to what could be confirmed.

What is known

The BBC reported that Whitehouse, 67, was the victim of a robbery at an ATM in the capital. The basic account is a street crime, a well-known face and a cashpoint. This article cannot confirm when or where it happened, what was taken, whether he was hurt, or whether anyone has been arrested. Independent searches for further reporting found nothing beyond the BBC's headline. Those details should be treated as unknown until police or Whitehouse's representatives say more.

Whitehouse is one of British comedy's most recognisable performers. He co-created and starred in The Fast Show, the BBC sketch series that gave the language catchphrases such as "Suits you, sir" and "Brilliant!". He also worked for years with Harry Enfield on Harry Enfield & Chums and Harry & Paul. He was born in the Rhondda Valley in South Wales and moved to London at the age of four.

A familiar kind of crime

Whatever the specifics, the setting is a familiar one. Cash machines are a recurring site of opportunistic theft, because a person standing at one is distracted, often stationary and about to be holding cash or a card. Police forces regularly advise people to shield their PIN, to be wary of anyone standing close, and to stop a transaction if they feel uneasy. None of that is specific to this case. It is general advice, and nothing in the available reporting says how this robbery was carried out.

The fame of the victim explains the attention more than the crime does. A street robbery is common, but when the person robbed is someone the public has laughed along with for three decades it travels further. It also changes little beyond the individual. There is no wider policy thread in what has been published so far.

What to watch

The next development is likely to come from the Metropolitan Police, if it is the investigating force. It could release a description of a suspect, appeal for witnesses or CCTV footage, or announce an arrest. Whitehouse or his representatives may also comment. This article will need updating if any of that appears.
