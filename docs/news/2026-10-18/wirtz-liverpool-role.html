Wirtz delivers for Liverpool but remains a puzzle for the manager

In one sentence
Wirtz scored for Liverpool, but where he plays best in the side is still unsettled.

Florian Wirtz is scoring for Liverpool, and the club is still working out where he should play. Those two facts do not sit comfortably together, and the second is not new.

The German playmaker joined Liverpool as one of the most expensive signings in the club's history. The expectation was that a player who ran Bayer Leverkusen's attack would do the same at Anfield. His record since suggests it has been less simple than that. Goals, when they have come, have tended to settle the argument for a week rather than end it.

BBC Sport's latest report on his goal frames it the same way: a decisive contribution, and a manager who has still not settled on the role that gets the most from him. The report as supplied to this desk did not include a scoreline, opponent or quote from the manager, and none is given here.

A familiar debate

The question is one of geometry. Wirtz is at his best with the ball between the lines, and a side's shape decides how much of that space he gets. Liverpool have tried him in several places.

The Premier League's own analysis of his arrival described him as the central No 10 in a 4-2-3-1, flanked by Cody Gakpo and Mohamed Salah. A later piece on his form noted that he had also been used this season as a false nine, as a left winger and as a deeper midfielder, while his head coach searched for a way to unlock him. The same piece described a central role as his favoured position.

Not everyone agrees. Jamie Carragher has argued that Wirtz does his most damaging work from the inside-left pocket, not as a traditional No 10, according to Anfield Index.

That range of opinion is the point. A player who can be framed as a No 10, a false nine, a winger or a pocket runner has plenty of uses. Without one fixed home, he can also look like a puzzle.

What a goal does and does not settle

Scoring is the clearest evidence a forward can offer, but it is weak evidence about role. A goal from a central position supports the No 10 case. The same goal from the left supports the opposite one. Without the details of where he played and how the goal came, the report gives selectors and supporters no fresh data on the underlying question.

That is why this reads as a recurring selection debate, not a turning point. It does not change Liverpool's league position or the wider title picture. It matters to supporters who follow the line-up closely, and to a squad whose attack is built around fitting several expensive forwards into one system.

What to watch

The next team sheets are the real test. If Wirtz starts the next league match in the same position as his goal, the club may be settling. If he moves again, the debate carries on.
