Trump waives several Russia sanctions as row with Zelensky deepens

In one sentence
Washington has eased Russian diesel sanctions while Trump calls for Zelensky's replacement, leaving allies in London and Brussels pulling the other way.

Washington has eased sanctions on Russian diesel at the same moment it is in open dispute with Kyiv. The two moves look linked. President Donald Trump has publicly called for Ukraine to get a new leader, after Volodymyr Zelensky criticised the easing.

The detail of the sanctions relief is still thin. The reporting available describes an easing of restrictions on Russian diesel, and does not say which measures were waived, under what legal authority, or from what date. That matters, because it decides how far the change reaches. Those points should be treated as unconfirmed until the Treasury publishes the instruments.

What was done

On Friday, Trump said he had spoken with Vladimir Putin and that Russia would release large volumes of diesel onto world markets. The next day, according to AFP, he said Ukraine should have a new leader. His stated grievance was that Zelensky had defied instructions not to strike Russian refineries. Those strikes, Trump said, risked worsening fuel shortages linked to the US-Israeli war on Iran. He also criticised Ukraine for holding no election since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.

Zelensky called the diesel decision a "weak decision". Replying to the call for his replacement, he said it was "up to our people" to choose their leader. He added that Ukraine could hold elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire. He also said the dispute should not damage unity among Ukraine's allies.

A pattern, not a first

This is not the first such waiver. In April, Zelensky condemned a month-long US waiver on Russian oil. Democratic senators called it "shameful" and a reversal of earlier statements by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Whether the October easing extends that earlier relief or is a separate action is not clear from the reporting I could find.

The sequence suggests sanctions are being used as a flexible instrument rather than applied on a fixed schedule. Fuel prices are the most visible pressure. Trump faces midterm elections next month, and he explicitly tied the Russian diesel release to the fuel shortages. On that reading, the waiver is as much domestic energy policy as Russia policy. The inference is mine and rests on Trump's own framing. No official has said the two are formally linked.

Allies push back

The reaction has been sharp outside Washington. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said easing pressure on Russia would only sustain its war effort. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham expressed "complete solidarity" with Zelensky, and Downing Street said Britain would strengthen its own Russia sanctions. That points to a possible divergence, with London and Brussels tightening as Washington loosens. Six US senators have urged Trump to reverse the diesel plan, and some Republicans have joined the criticism.

Diplomacy has continued alongside the row. US, Ukrainian and European officials met in Miami, and envoy Steve Witkoff called the talks "constructive". The Kremlin said Putin received Trump's proposals "with understanding". Trump has also said Russia and Ukraine agreed an energy ceasefire, a claim this report could not independently confirm.

The fighting goes on. Russian strikes killed at least 21 people across Ukraine in the latest reporting, including 20 in a glide-bomb attack on Zaporizhzhia, according to Ukraine's rescue service. Russia occupies roughly a fifth of Ukrainian territory.

What to watch

Three things will settle how large this is. The first is the Treasury's licence text, which will show the scope and expiry of the relief. The second is whether the 60-day ceasefire Zelensky floated is taken up. The third is whether Congress moves to constrain the waiver. For markets, the immediate channel is diesel and wider energy prices, which are already under strain from the Gulf. The November midterms set the political clock.
