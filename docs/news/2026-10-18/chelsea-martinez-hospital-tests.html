Chelsea's Martinez taken to hospital for precautionary tests

In one sentence
Chelsea goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez was taken to hospital with a concussion after the win at Everton, and his place in the next three fixtures is in doubt.

Chelsea goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez was taken to hospital for "precautionary tests" after suffering a concussion in the 2-0 win at Everton on Saturday, leaving his availability for the next fortnight in doubt.

Manager Xabi Alonso said Martinez left the stadium conscious. Mike Penders replaced him. Chelsea had not published test results or a fuller medical update at the time of writing.

What is known

The account rests on Alonso's post-match comments, as relayed by BBC Sport and several outlets that carried the same report. Alonso described the concussion and said the hospital visit was a precaution. Some reports use Martinez's nickname, "Dibu", but they consistently identify the player as Emiliano Martinez.

The reports do not give the exact moment of the injury beyond describing a collision during the game, and no scan results have been disclosed. "Precautionary" is the standard club word for this kind of check, and it says little about severity.

Why the fixtures matter

A hospital visit becomes a selection question because of what comes next. Chelsea's next three matches reportedly include Tottenham, Manchester United and a Carabao Cup tie at Liverpool. Premier League concussion protocols typically require a graduated return-to-play process of at least 14 days if a concussion is confirmed. Martinez could therefore miss all three games.

If he does, Penders is the obvious replacement, with the Tottenham and United games as his first tests. A goalkeeper stepping in for a derby is a bigger ask than the same change in a cup tie against lesser opposition, though the League Cup trip to Liverpool is no easy draw either.

What to watch

The next marker is a club statement on the test results, most likely at Alonso's pre-match press conference before the Tottenham game. If it confirms a concussion, the graduated return-to-play process starts and the three-match absence becomes the working assumption. If the tests come back clear, Martinez could still be held out for caution.
