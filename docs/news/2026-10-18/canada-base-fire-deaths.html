Deaths confirmed in major fire at Canadian military base

In one sentence
A fire and explosion at CFB Wainwright in Alberta killed an unspecified number of people; sources say at least seven, but the toll and cause are not officially confirmed.

A fire and explosion at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in central Alberta on Saturday killed people and injured others. The Defence Minister confirmed that much. The number of dead has not been officially stated, and the cause has not been confirmed.

The account below rests on early reporting from Global News, which cites unnamed military sources, and on public statements by officials. It will change as the investigation proceeds.

What is known

Defence Minister David McGuinty said people had died and been injured in "an incident on the base". He called it "a very, very active and delicate situation" and said: "I cannot recall an incident like this in recent memory." He gave no casualty figures.

Global News, citing multiple Canadian Armed Forces sources, reported at least seven dead and dozens injured. That figure has not been confirmed by the government.

The blast came at about 4 p.m. local time, and a fire followed. Wind gusts of up to 100 km/h were fanning it, and much of Alberta was under wind warnings. The Municipal District of Wainwright opened an emergency coordination centre. Its 7 p.m. update said the fire south of the town had crossed the base fence earlier in the day and was being actively managed. NASA satellite data showed a cluster of active fires just southeast of the town. Global News reported no evacuations of residents.

Acute Care Alberta said paramedics were on scene providing care and transport. STARS air ambulance crews were dispatched from Edmonton and Saskatoon, and firefighters from multiple jurisdictions responded.

The base

CFB Wainwright lies about 200 km southeast of Edmonton. It is one of the country's busiest military training bases and hosts the Canadian Manoeuvre Training Centre and the 3rd Canadian Division Training Centre, which provides basic infantry training. It is a large training area rather than a barracks alone, so the people on site on a Saturday afternoon could have included trainees, instructors and support staff. Officials have not said who the victims were.

The unconfirmed cause

Multiple military sources told Global News that a live-fire training exercise struck a gas line or gas well on the base, setting off the explosion. Neither the Department of National Defence nor the RCMP, which is involved, has confirmed this. Until investigators say so, it should be treated as a lead rather than a finding.

If it holds, the questions would concern how live-fire ranges are sited near energy infrastructure, and what safeguards exist. Those are matters a defence inquiry would be expected to examine.

Reaction

Prime Minister Mark Carney said his thoughts were with "all the brave women and men affected" and that he was in close contact with Premier Danielle Smith. Smith described a "significant and tragic fire" that affected many military personnel. Opposition leader Pierre Poilievre urged people to pray for Wainwright and keep roads clear for responders.

The fire also disrupted voting. The Wainwright advance polling station closed early on the last day of advance voting before Monday's Alberta referendum, and voters in that district were redirected to Lloydminster and Vermilion. Elections Alberta said ballot materials were moved securely.

What to watch

The next markers are an official casualty count and the names of the dead from the Defence Department, and a statement on cause from the RCMP or the military. Containment of the fire will depend on whether the wind eases. Whether Ottawa orders a formal board of inquiry will show how seriously it treats the safety questions.
