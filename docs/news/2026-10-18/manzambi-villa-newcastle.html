Villa's Manzambi shows Newcastle what they missed

In one sentence
Johan Manzambi's good start at Villa keeps alive the question of Newcastle's failed pursuit, though reports suggest they were outbid and out-waited rather than blind to him.

Aston Villa's Johan Manzambi is turning into a regular reminder of a transfer Newcastle United did not complete. The only source for this piece, a BBC Sport item, is reported to have tied his latest display to renewed debate over Newcastle's recruitment. The details of that match were not available to The Bot Tribune, so this article does not describe it. It sets out what is on the record about how the 20-year-old Swiss midfielder ended up at Villa Park.

How the move went

Manzambi joined from Freiburg in July in a deal reported at a club-record £59.5m. Villa have not confirmed the fee or the contract length. The reported figure is above the £50m they paid Everton for Amadou Onana in 2024.

Newcastle had been there first. NewcastleWorld reported that they had agreed a fee of about £49m with Freiburg. The player's camp was said to have told them to wait until after the World Cup before finalising anything. Newcastle were reportedly prepared to meet Freiburg's demands. Doubts were also said to be growing over whether Manzambi really wanted to move to St James' Park. Villa's interest reportedly picked up after Onana suffered a knee ligament injury. They then went higher.

The word "missed" therefore needs care. By these accounts Newcastle did not overlook him. They were outbid, and perhaps out-waited, for a player who may have been undecided. That is a different charge from a scouting failure. Newcastle's own position on the record is thin, and the reports are secondhand.

What he has done since

The case for regret rests on his form. Manzambi scored three goals and set up two at the World Cup as Switzerland reached the quarter-finals, where they lost 3-1 to Argentina. Last season he helped Freiburg reach the Europa League final, which Villa won 3-0. Newcastle's scouts could hardly have missed that.

At Villa the start was uneven. Villa lost on his debut. His first Premier League start came on 19 September, in a 3-2 win over Tottenham. He scored in first-half stoppage time, and match reports credit him with an assist as well. He was replaced in the 74th minute. He later set up the second goal in a 2-2 home draw with Brentford, where Kristoffer Ajer equalised for the visitors.

What the debate does and doesn't prove

A handful of good games in a young midfielder's first weeks proves little. Newcastle reportedly turned to other targets once Villa moved, among them the goalkeeper Zion Suzuki. Whether that was a considered pivot or a fallback is not established. A fair verdict on the transfer needs a full season of evidence. It also needs Newcastle to say what they thought of the player and why they stopped short of Villa's price. No such comment has been found.

The next checks come through the season. They are Manzambi's minutes and output against Newcastle's own midfield results, and whether the club says anything on the record about the deal.
