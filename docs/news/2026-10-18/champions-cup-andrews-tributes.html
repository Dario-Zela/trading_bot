Tributes to Andrews before Champions Cup matches

In one sentence
Champions Cup matches opened with a moment's silence for Hyron Andrews, the Lions lock who died aged 31 at his team hotel in France before their match with La Rochelle.

Rugby's Champions Cup opened its pool stage this weekend under a cloud. Every match in the opening round began with a tribute to Hyron Andrews, the Johannesburg Lions lock who died on 16 October, aged 31, at his team's hotel in France.

The Lions were due to play La Rochelle, with kick-off at 17:30 BST. The match went ahead as scheduled, and the Lions dedicated it to Andrews. A moment of silence was observed before all of the round's fixtures.

A quiet career, a well-liked player

Andrews never won a senior cap. His international record stops at the Junior Springboks in 2015. He was the kind of second-row who is valued by the people who share a dressing room with him and noticed less by everyone else.

He came to England in February 2024, signing for Sale from the Sharks. He arrived initially as injury cover for Jonny Hill and went on to make 45 appearances. This summer he left Sale to return to South Africa and join the Lions.

Sale's director of rugby, Alex Sanderson, said of him: "He was just a brilliant guy and a brilliant teammate." Lions chief executive Rudolf Straeuli called him "a cherished member of our Lions family". SA Rugby president Mark Alexander described the news as "terrible and distressing".

Andrews is survived by his wife, Maighlin, and their sons, Hendrix and Emerson.

What is known about his death

SA Rugby has classified the death as an accident caused by a fall. Accounts differ on how much more is established. One report says no definitive cause has been publicly confirmed, and French prosecutors are reported to be investigating. Until their findings are public, little beyond the federation's statement can be said with confidence.

An old echo

The timing carries a grim echo. Munster head coach Anthony Foley died in a Paris hotel on the eve of a Champions Cup match about a decade ago, and European rugby stopped for him. The sport's response then set a template for how a tournament honours one of its own on the day: silence, a dedication, and a match played because the players choose to play it.

Andrews was not a household name, and the tributes did not need him to be one. They were paid by a competition that has, twice in a decade, had to mark a death in a hotel on a matchday.

What next

The Lions' next fixture will show how the squad has taken the loss. The club has said further details of an appropriate tribute will follow. The outcome of the French investigation is the other open question.
