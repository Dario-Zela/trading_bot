European diplomats attacked by Israeli settlers in West Bank

In one sentence
European diplomats from France, Belgium and Poland say settlers attacked them near Taybeh; Israel is investigating, and Belgium wants further EU sanctions.

European diplomats say they were attacked by Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank on Saturday. The incident extends a pattern of settler violence, which has mostly fallen on Palestinians, to officials from European states. Israel's military called the attack "severe". The Prime Minister's Office urged caution before conclusions are drawn.

What happened

The incident took place near Taybeh, a Palestinian village in the occupied West Bank, on 17 October. Israeli broadcaster Kan said the diplomats came from France, Belgium and Poland. Belgian Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot said Belgium's consul general was among those targeted, along with French and Polish diplomats and Catholic representatives. No outlet gave a total number of diplomats.

Israeli Army Radio reported that seven masked settlers arrived on all-terrain vehicles. It said they surrounded the diplomatic convoy and demanded identity documents, and that the window of a Belgian consulate vehicle was smashed. Belgium said its team was safe. Poland said its representatives were unharmed.

A French local official was also hurt. Bernard Baudoux, mayor of Aulnoye-Aymeries, who was travelling with the delegation, said he was kicked, beaten with iron bars and thrown to the ground. He reported no serious injuries and said the attackers arrived "completely enraged".

Reaction

Prévot linked the attack to Israel's settlement policy. "Harvesting olives, reporting the news or carrying out diplomatic duties should never put anyone at risk of attack," he said, and he pledged to push for further EU sanctions. Poland's foreign ministry condemned the incident "in the strongest possible terms" and said settlers may have been responsible. It added that there was no indication the diplomats had been singled out.

Israeli police opened an investigation and said they were working to identify suspects. The military said the visit had not been coordinated with it and that no soldiers were at the scene. The Prime Minister's Office questioned the evidence and suggested the reports might be part of a campaign to smear Israel during the olive harvest. It also noted that Baudoux had flown a Palestinian flag over his town hall, which French authorities ordered removed.

Why it matters

The olive harvest, which runs through the autumn, is a recurring flashpoint in the West Bank. Palestinian farmers often need to reach groves near settlements, and clashes are common. The attack on the diplomats came a day after settlers seriously injured Heidi Levine, a photographer working for The Washington Post, near Abu Falah. US Ambassador Mike Huckabee called for a full investigation.

An attack on accredited foreign officials is harder for governments to treat as routine than violence against Palestinians. The diplomats' visit was not coordinated with the Israeli military, so the circumstances will probably be argued over. But Poland's account suggests the diplomats were not deliberately targeted, which may dampen any push for a firm response.

The evidence of an EU-level reaction so far is thin. Prévot's call for sanctions is a national position, and no EU official was quoted in the coverage reviewed. Any sanctions on violent settlers would need agreement among member states, a process that has historically been slow.

What to watch next: whether the Israeli police investigation produces arrests, and whether Brussels or the capitals concerned summon Israeli ambassadors or put sanctions on a Foreign Affairs Council agenda.
