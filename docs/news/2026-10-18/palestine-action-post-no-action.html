Police take no further action over Palestine Action post by adviser's wife

In one sentence
Police have closed their assessment of Sarah Helm's Palestine Action post with no action, but the row over her husband Jonathan Powell's position as national security adviser continues.

Police have closed their assessment of a social media post by Sarah Helm, the wife of the prime minister's national security adviser, Jonathan Powell, and will take no further action, according to reports on Sunday. The decision removes any criminal question. The political one is still open: whether a close family member's public stance on a proscribed group makes the adviser's position awkward.

The post

Helm, an author and journalist, wrote on X on 15 October. She was replying to a thread about the novelist Sally Rooney, who had said she would stop her books being sold in Britain so she could direct her royalties to Palestine Action. Helm wrote: "God help us authors who support Palestine and Palestine Action."

Palestine Action was banned under terrorism law in 2025, and expressing support for it can be an offence. The group Defend Our Juries says it knows of 36 people who were arrested, and in some cases had their homes raided, over similar social media posts. The Metropolitan Police said the post was "in the process of being assessed to determine whether any further police investigation may be required". The police conclusion that no action is needed comes from the triage summary of BBC and Guardian coverage, and I could not independently confirm it.

The wording of the post is ambiguous. It can be read as a declaration of support or as a sarcastic remark about the climate facing authors. That ambiguity may explain why the assessment was short.

Why the row continues

Downing Street moved quickly to put distance between the government and Helm. A spokeswoman said she is "an individual who does not represent the government in any capacity." That answers the question of who speaks for ministers. It does not answer the one critics are asking, which concerns Powell.

Powell advises on security and counter-terrorism policy, and has served in that role under both Keir Starmer and Andy Burnham. Danny Kruger of Reform UK wrote to the prime minister about the post. The Campaign Against Antisemitism urged Powell to denounce it. On the other side, Green leader Zack Polanski said Helm "should be commended for her bravery" and renewed his call for the ban to be lifted.

The objections have not been about anything Powell has said or done. They are about perception: an official who helps shape national security policy, living with someone who has publicly engaged with a group the state treats as terrorist. I found no reporting that Powell has commented, and no evidence that he has breached any rule or had any part in the post.

What to watch

Police inaction makes it harder to argue that the matter is a legal problem. It also lets critics ask why other posters were arrested while this one was not, a charge that will land with those already uneasy about how the ban is enforced. The ban itself is in legal dispute, with a High Court ruling against it and a government appeal.

The next test is whether Powell or Burnham addresses the matter directly, and whether Kruger's letter gets a formal reply. Unless new evidence emerges that the adviser's position is compromised, this is likely to fade as a Westminster row rather than become a resignation story.
