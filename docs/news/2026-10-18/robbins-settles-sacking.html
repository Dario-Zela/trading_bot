Ex-top official Robbins settles with government over his sacking

In one sentence
Sir Olly Robbins, sacked as Foreign Office permanent secretary over Mandelson's vetting, has settled with the government, ending his judicial review on undisclosed terms.

A former head of the Foreign Office has settled with the government over his sacking, bringing an end to a legal fight that had kept an awkward episode in Whitehall's recent history alive. Sir Olly Robbins, until this year the permanent secretary at the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), reached the agreement with ministers, the FDA union announced on Friday. No figure and no other terms have been disclosed.

Who Robbins was, and why he went

As permanent secretary, Robbins was the most senior civil servant in the FCDO and head of the diplomatic service. He was removed over the vetting of Peter Mandelson's appointment as ambassador to Washington. Reports said he had lost the confidence of the then prime minister, Sir Keir Starmer, and the then foreign secretary, Yvette Cooper.

The background is a chain of events that began with Mandelson himself. He was sacked as ambassador in September 2025 after disclosures from the US-released Epstein files. Months later, reporting in April 2026 said Mandelson had failed a background check, but that Foreign Office officials had overruled the finding and cleared him. Starmer said he had not known that Mandelson received high-level clearance against the advice of UK Security Vetting. Robbins was dismissed.

The legal challenge

In July, Robbins brought proceedings seeking a judicial review. His case rested on three points: that the reasons given for his dismissal were irrational, that the prime minister lacked statutory authority to dismiss the head of the diplomatic service, and that no fair procedure had been followed. The FDA, which represented him, argued the decision rested on a "grievous misunderstanding" of how vetting works.

The settlement means those arguments will not be tested in court. The union said the government settled out of court to "rectify the circumstances surrounding his departure", a phrase that suggests something more than a purely financial arrangement, though the terms are unpublished. A government spokesperson said only that a settlement had been reached and that it would not comment further on HR matters. One outlet has described the sum as "six-figure", but that figure is unconfirmed by the government and should be treated with caution.

Robbins said he was "looking forward to moving on to the next chapter of my life."

Reaction and what it leaves open

Dave Penman, the FDA's general secretary, said the settlement may end the judicial review but that the affair "has damaged the bond of trust between civil servants and ministers". He accused the government of making Robbins a scapegoat to mask the former prime minister's misjudgement, and called for clarity on ministers' involvement in vetting.

Robbins's treatment drew criticism beyond the union. Former cabinet secretaries including Lord O'Donnell, Lord Butler and Lord Sedwill criticised the sacking, and some called for his reinstatement. That did not happen: Lindy Cameron was appointed in September as the first woman to hold the FCDO permanent secretary post.

The settlement also sits alongside another costly departure. In September it emerged that Lord Wormald, the former head of the civil service, whom Starmer had forced out, received an £860,000 payout. Taken together, the two cases show how expensive it can be when a government removes its most senior officials and the removal is later contested.

The central question the settlement leaves unanswered is where responsibility for vetting decisions lies between ministers and officials. With no court ruling and no published terms, that dispute is unresolved. What to watch is whether the government discloses the settlement's cost and conditions, for example through parliamentary questions or in departmental accounts, and whether it responds to the call for clearer rules on ministerial involvement in vetting.
