STANDING WATCH · UK FISCAL · By M. Halloway

Gilts near post-2007 highs squeeze Healey's headroom as banks brace for Budget levies

UK government borrowing costs have risen toward their highest levels since 2007, and that is now eating into the margin the Chancellor has against her fiscal rules. The 10-year gilt yield is reported near 5.4%. Bloomberg Economics estimates the move could cut about £12bn from a fiscal headroom of £23.6bn, before any policy change.

Headroom is the buffer between forecast borrowing and the Chancellor's own targets. A loss of that size, driven by market rates rather than decisions taken in the Budget, would leave less room for spending or tax choices unless yields ease. The arithmetic is simple, but the consequence is that the Budget's margin for error now depends on bond-market pricing as much as on the Treasury's plans.

Watch the gilt market into the Budget and any revised official forecasts. Banks are also preparing for Budget levies, so the effect will show up in sector pricing as well as in the public accounts.
