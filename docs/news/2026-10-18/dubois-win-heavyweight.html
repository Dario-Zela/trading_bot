Dubois' dominant win turns heavyweight attention to unification fights

In one sentence
Daniel Dubois stopped Fabio Wardley in round two at the O2 to keep the WBO heavyweight title, but talk of unification fights is still speculation with nothing announced.

Daniel Dubois kept hold of the WBO heavyweight title at London's O2 Arena on Saturday, stopping Fabio Wardley in the second round of their rematch. The win was quick and one-sided. It also revives the question the division keeps asking: when do the champions fight each other?

The fight

Dubois floored Wardley with an overhand right 17 seconds into round one. Wardley survived the opening round. In the second, referee Steve Gray stepped in with 39 seconds left, at the same moment Wardley's trainer, Ben Davison, threw in the towel, according to the Jamaica Gleaner.

Dubois is now 24-3 with 23 knockouts. Wardley drops to 20-2-1.

The fight was a rematch of their meeting in Manchester in May. Dubois won that one by 11th-round stoppage and became a two-time heavyweight champion. Five months on, the second fight lasted barely a round and a bit.

"It was my time and it's my era," Dubois said afterwards. "I had bad intentions from the beginning."

Wardley was gracious. "It's never fun to lose, but it is what it is," he said. "The game is the game."

One caution on the record. Live coverage varied on how long the fight lasted, with one outlet's headline saying under four minutes. The round and the stoppage are consistent across the reports checked here. The precise clock time is not.

The unification question

The claim that this win "reshapes" heavyweight boxing is ahead of the evidence. A defence against a man he had already beaten adds little to the sport's competitive picture. What it does is leave Dubois with nobody left in his immediate circle to beat.

That is where the unification talk comes from. Reports from the night say Dubois and Filip Hrgovic faced off in the ring after the fight. Hrgovic and Agit Kabayel are described in those reports as world champions who, like Dubois, are promoted by Frank Warren. If that is accurate, a unification fight in 2027 would not need two promoters to agree. It would need only Warren to decide which of his champions meets which.

No date, venue or purse has been announced for any such fight. A ring-side face-off is the cheapest form of matchmaking in boxing, and many have led nowhere.

What to watch

The next signal is whether Warren names an opponent for Dubois's next outing. A mandatory challenge from the WBO could also narrow his options. A Hrgovic or Kabayel fight would be the first real sign that the titles are being collected rather than merely defended. Until one of those is confirmed, the dominant win remains a strong performance and not much else.
