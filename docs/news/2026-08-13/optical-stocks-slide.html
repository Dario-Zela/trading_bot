Optical stocks slide as transceiver rally reverses

High-speed optical transceiver modules of the kind at the center of the sector's AI-driven rally and reversal. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Coherent and Lumentum reversed August's AI-optics rally, then split sharply on earnings — Lumentum jumped, Coherent gave its gains right back.

Optical-networking stocks gave back a chunk of their August gains this week, with Coherent Corp sliding more than 14% and Lumentum Holdings dropping over 8% in a session that had no fresh bad news attached to it — just investors deciding a very fast rally had run ahead of itself.

The rally in question began August 4, when reports surfaced that the Trump administration and the FCC were drafting rules to ban imports of new Chinese-made optical transceiver modules — the high-speed 800G and 1.6T parts that shuttle data between AI accelerators inside data centers — citing national-security risk. The prospect of US cloud providers shifting that procurement to domestic suppliers sent the sector flying: Applied Optoelectronics jumped 17% that day, Coherent gained around 10%, Lumentum rose 9%. Coming into the following week, Coherent was up roughly 105% year-to-date and Lumentum 142%.

That kind of move leaves little room for error. By August 11, with both companies days from reporting quarterly results, the trade unwound: Coherent's trailing price-to-earnings ratio had reached roughly 160 times — against a five-year median closer to 35 — and Lumentum's sat near 146 times. Traders took profits rather than hold richly priced positions into an unknown. It was, notably, not the first such air-pocket this cycle: both stocks had already sold off sharply on July 28 — Coherent down 11%, Lumentum down 9% — when investors briefly soured on the broader AI capital-spending story.

Earnings landed, and it still wasn't enough

The irony is that the numbers, once they arrived, were strong. Lumentum's fiscal fourth-quarter report on August 11 showed revenue up 109% year-over-year to $1.01 billion, adjusted earnings of $3.23 a share against a Street estimate near $2.97, non-GAAP gross margin crossing 50%, and record 800G transceiver shipments alongside early 1.6T production. Guidance for the current quarter — $1.225 billion to $1.275 billion in revenue — came in well above what analysts had penciled in. The stock jumped roughly 14-15% the next day, dragging Coherent, Corning and Applied Optoelectronics higher with it.

Coherent's own report, out August 12, beat on every headline metric too: $2.05 billion in quarterly revenue, up 34%, and non-GAAP earnings of $1.74 a share versus a $1.62 estimate. Full fiscal-year revenue reached $7.12 billion. Yet the stock's reaction told a different story than Lumentum's — shares that had rallied nearly 9% into the print gave almost all of it back in after-hours trading, a classic sell-the-news move once a beat that was already priced in met a stock up over 100% for the year.

The split outcome is the clearest evidence yet that this rally needs more than confirmation of existing demand to keep climbing — it needs a catalyst investors haven't already spent. Optical connectivity remains structurally necessary as AI clusters outgrow copper's reach, a point neither company's numbers dispute. What the past ten days have shown is that the market has stopped taking that thesis on faith and started pricing each data point individually. The next tests come from Applied Optoelectronics' own results and any further word from Washington on the transceiver import rules — the sort of concrete catalyst that, this time, actually moved the stocks for a reason.
