Canada and US remain apart on tariff deal

The Ambassador Bridge links Detroit, Michigan and Windsor, Ontario — the busiest commercial crossing for auto-sector trade between the two countries. — Ken Lund / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Ottawa floated a new auto-tariff proposal ahead of the Aug. 19 deadline, but both sides say a deal isn't close.

Canadian and American negotiators are running out of road. With a Wednesday deadline bearing down, Ottawa is weighing a new offer on auto tariffs — but the fact that it's still bargaining over the shape of a deal, rather than signing one, tells its own story.

In one sentence: Ottawa floated a fresh auto-tariff proposal while both sides say a deal isn't close, leaving the August 19 deadline to loom over autos, the loonie and cross-border supply chains.

According to The Globe and Mail, Canadian officials are considering accepting a reduced U.S. tariff of 10 to 15 percent on Canadian-built vehicles — down from the 25 percent currently in place — in exchange for dropping Ottawa's retaliatory levies on American-made cars altogether. The mechanics matter here: roughly half the value of a Canadian-built car is already U.S.-content, and that content would stay exempt from the tariff calculation, so the new rate would apply only to the non-U.S. share. Canada has also pushed for a broader carve-out covering all North American content, not just American parts, which would blunt the tariff further.

Not yet where it needs to be

Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc and chief negotiator Janice Charette met U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer in Washington this week and exchanged written proposals — LeBlanc's third sit-down with Greer in as many weeks. The American counter-offer, tabled Tuesday, would trim some sectoral tariffs but by less than Ottawa wants, according to CBC News. LeBlanc's own summary was blunt: "We're not yet where we need to be to get a deal that's in the best interests of the two economies."

Industry is skeptical too. Flavio Volpe, who represents Canadian auto parts makers, told the Globe the emerging auto framework "does not work for car makers or parts makers," even as he professed optimism that some agreement gets done.

The deadline and the stakes

The date that matters is August 19. President Trump has threatened a 50 percent tariff on an additional US$20 billion of Canadian exports if no deal materializes, layered on top of tariffs he already imposed in July on Canadian vehicles, dairy and alcohol. Prime Minister Mark Carney has called those July measures "a direct violation" of the USMCA and vowed Canada is "ready to intensify" talks. Canada's negotiators have separately warned Washington that imposing fresh 50 percent tariffs on the deadline could stall the talks altogether rather than force a resolution.

The auto-tariff proposal, then, reads less like a breakthrough than a hedge — Ottawa signaling flexibility on its own retaliatory levies while still failing to secure the relief it's after on the core 25 percent rate. Both governments have committed to near-daily meetings through the deadline, and negotiators are reportedly hoping to hand Trump a workable framework before Monday. USD/CAD has traded in a tight band near 1.407 through early August, with traders holding off on directional bets until the deadline resolves one way or the other.

What to watch next: whether Washington and Ottawa can present Trump a deal outline before August 19, and whether the 50 percent tariff threat on additional Canadian exports takes effect if they can't.
