China factory activity slows further, missing forecasts

Container stacks at the Port of Shanghai's Yangshan deep-water harbour, a bellwether for Chinese export volumes. — Alex Needham / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
In one sentence
China's RatingDog (formerly Caixin) manufacturing PMI fell to 50.9 in July from 51.7, missing forecasts as the official PMI slipped into outright contraction.

China's factories grew more slowly than expected in July, the clearest sign yet that the resilience Beijing has been advertising all year is starting to fray. The RatingDog China manufacturing PMI — the closely watched private-sector survey long known as the Caixin PMI before a Shenzhen credit-research firm bought the naming rights from S&P Global last August — fell to 50.9 from 51.7 in June, undershooting the 51.5 consensus and marking a four-month low.

The reading still sits above the 50-point line separating expansion from contraction, and the underlying detail was not uniformly weak: new orders extended a 14-month growth streak, the longest since 2018, helped by foreign sales rising for the first time in three months, while employment grew for a second straight month at its fastest pace since August 2023. Input-price inflation eased to its softest since January, suggesting factories are not being squeezed on costs even as output cools.

A sharper drop in the official gauge

The softness looked worse in China's official statistics. The National Bureau of Statistics' manufacturing PMI, which skews toward larger state-linked firms, slid to 49.2 in July from 50.3 in June — its first contraction since February and a miss against expectations for a hold near 50. New orders in that survey fell to 48.5 from 51.2, and new export orders slipped to 49.6 from 50.1. The bureau pointed to a run of typhoons that halted construction and factory work across several provinces, on top of domestic demand that had already been fading as earlier front-loaded orders worked through the pipeline.

The weakness spread well beyond factories. China's construction PMI slumped to a record low of 47.0, the services gauge fell to its weakest since the initial Covid-19 lockdowns, and the composite output index dropped to 49.3 — the lowest since the pandemic ended in 2022. Not every corner of industry slowed: high-tech manufacturing held at 53.3 and equipment manufacturing at 51.4, both still expanding, pointing to a two-speed economy where advanced-manufacturing investment is holding up even as broader construction and consumer-facing activity stalls.

Exporters also flagged softer demand from Europe, where buyers have turned cautious amid recession fears tied to the fallout from the Middle East conflict — a reminder that China's slowdown is not purely domestic in origin.

The divergence between the two surveys — a still-expanding private gauge against an outright contraction in the official one — leaves room for debate about how much of the July stumble is noise from storms and calendar effects versus a genuine loss of momentum. But both point the same direction, and both missed forecasts, which is enough to sharpen bets that Beijing will lean harder on stimulus in the months ahead. For markets exposed to Chinese demand — industrial metals, shipping, emerging-market equities — a factory sector growing at its slowest pace in four months is one more data point arguing for caution rather than confidence in the recovery narrative. The next read arrives with August's PMIs in the first days of September, alongside any policy response Beijing chooses to signal before then.
