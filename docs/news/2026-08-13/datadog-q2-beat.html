Datadog jumps on strong Q2 results and raised analyst targets

Datadog's Q2 revenue rose 36% year-over-year, but an initial post-earnings selloff gave way to a Wall Street rebound days later. — Datadog, Inc. / Wikipedia
In one sentence
Datadog shares rebounded 11% as analysts called last week's post-earnings selloff overdone, citing five straight quarters of accelerating growth.

Datadog shares jumped 11.3% on Monday, closing at $260.78, in a rebound that says as much about Wall Street's second thoughts as it does about the cloud-monitoring company's underlying business. The move recovered roughly half the ground lost four days earlier, when the stock suffered its steepest one-day drop in more than six years despite beating Wall Street's numbers on every headline metric.

The swing captures a real tension in how investors are pricing enterprise software right now: a genuine earnings beat can still trigger a sell first, ask questions later reaction if the guidance underneath it looks even slightly soft.

A beat that didn't feel like one

Datadog's second-quarter revenue rose 36% year-over-year to $1.12 billion, ahead of the company's own guided range of $1.07–$1.08 billion, with adjusted earnings of 65 cents a share against a roughly 58-cent estimate. Non-GAAP operating margin held at 23%, free cash flow came in at $279 million, and the number of customers paying $100,000 or more annually climbed 23% to about 4,720. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to $4.45–$4.47 billion, up from $4.30–$4.34 billion, and lifted its adjusted-earnings outlook to $2.50–$2.54 a share.

None of that stopped the stock falling roughly 17–19% on August 6th and 7th. The culprit was third-quarter guidance implying growth decelerating to around 29%, driven largely by normalized usage from Datadog's largest customer — widely understood to be OpenAI, which renewed its contract but at a lighter run rate than the market had priced in. At a stock trading near 90 times adjusted earnings going into the print, a whiff of deceleration tied to a single account was enough to spook holders.

Analysts talk the selloff back

By Monday, sentiment had turned. Citi, BMO, Cantor Fitzgerald, Morgan Stanley, Baird, Canaccord and Rosenblatt all raised price targets, pushing the range to roughly $280–$327 while keeping Buy-equivalent ratings. The argument, broadly, was that the initial reaction overweighted one customer's usage pattern against a broader trend: revenue growth has now accelerated for five consecutive quarters, Datadog counts more than 750 AI-native customers and all ten of the largest AI companies as clients, and non-AI revenue growth alone climbed into the high-20s percentage range — evidence, in the analysts' reading, that observability demand is broadening rather than narrowing.

That broadening is the part that matters beyond one stock. Datadog's platform sits downstream of enterprise cloud and AI infrastructure spending generally, so its numbers are often read as a proxy for how freely companies are still opening their wallets for compute and the tools to monitor it. One earnings cycle, even a volatile one, isn't proof that cloud budgets are recession-proof — but a fifth straight quarter of accelerating growth, layered under a scare that didn't hold, is the kind of pattern that keeps that thesis alive. The next test comes with third-quarter results, when investors will see whether the largest-customer normalization was a one-off adjustment or the start of a trend.
