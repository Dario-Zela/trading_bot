SK Hynix, Samsung rally as Singapore's GIC adds memory stakes

In one sentence
SK Hynix and Samsung shares rallied on a report that Temasek plans direct stakes in both — a bet on AI memory demand the market had already largely priced in.

Shares in SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics jumped on August 12th after a South Korean financial daily reported that Temasek, Singapore's state investment company, has decided to buy direct stakes in both memory-chip makers — a move that, if confirmed, would mark the fund's first-ever direct equity position in the Korean stock market.

According to the Asia Business Daily, Temasek has been in touch with South Korean government officials to discuss timing, and intends to execute the trades through its own in-house investment team rather than hiring outside managers, as it has typically done to access Korean equities in the past. Temasek has not confirmed the report. Samsung closed up 6.68% at 255,500 won (about $192), and SK Hynix rose 5.54% to 1,504,000 won; both stocks traded more than 8% higher intraday, and the gains helped lift the wider Kospi index.

Betting on a bottleneck everyone can already see

The rationale is straightforward: Temasek has told investors it wants artificial-intelligence exposure to rise from roughly 6% of its portfolio today to as much as 15% by 2031, spread across five buckets — energy and data centres, semiconductors, cloud infrastructure, foundation models, and AI software. It already holds Nvidia, TSMC, ASML, Anthropic and OpenAI. Memory chips, according to people familiar with the fund's thinking, are seen as the most undervalued link in that chain relative to the rest of the AI buildout.

There's a reasonable case for that view. SK Hynix supplies roughly half of Nvidia's high-bandwidth memory orders and holds an outright majority of the broader HBM market; Samsung, the newer entrant, has been closing the gap since becoming a qualified HBM4 supplier to Nvidia this year and is expected to take somewhere in the mid-20% range of that allocation. Global HBM revenue is on track to grow from roughly $38 billion in 2025 to $58 billion in 2026, and both companies have said supply will stay tight into next year.

What's harder to argue is that this is news. Both stocks have already had a supercycle year — Hynix hit a single-day limit-up of 30% during a record Kospi surge in July, and SK Group's own chairman bought shares directly the same week. A Temasek allocation, even a large one, mostly confirms a thesis the market had already priced. Temasek's own portfolio filings show its net asset value hit a record S$518 billion ($400 billion) in the last fiscal year, so its move here says less about hidden value in memory chips than about a large allocator formally catching up to where the stock prices already sit.

The open questions are size and timing, neither of which Temasek has disclosed. Confirmation from the fund, or silence that lets the report fade, will matter more to the shares from here than the report itself did. Samsung and SK Hynix both report third-quarter earnings in October, when HBM4 shipment volumes should give the clearest read yet on whether the pricing power behind this rally is durable.
