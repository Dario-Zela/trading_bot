Ofcom investigates Meta over Instagram Instants feature

In one sentence
Ofcom has opened a formal Online Safety Act investigation into whether Meta properly assessed the risks of Instagram Instants, a disappearing-photo feature launched in May.

Ofcom, the UK communications regulator, has opened a formal investigation into whether Meta properly assessed the risks of Instagram Instants before launching it. The feature, which went live on 13 May 2026, lets users share photos and videos captured inside the app. The content is ephemeral: it disappears after being viewed, and it cannot be screenshotted.

The probe is not about what Instants does. It is about paperwork that the law requires to be done first.

What Ofcom is examining

Under the Online Safety Act 2023, platforms must carry out a "suitable and sufficient" risk assessment before making significant changes to a service. Ofcom wants to know whether Meta did that for Instants. Reporting on the case says the investigation will look at two kinds of risk: illegal content, and harm to children.

If Ofcom finds a breach, it can fine a company up to 10% of its qualifying global revenue. An investigation is not a finding, and Ofcom has not said Meta broke the law.

Meta's defence

Meta says it did the work. According to reports, the company ran a risk analysis and briefed Ofcom on Instants several times before launch. It also points to built-in protections: the feature blocks screenshots, reshares and forwarding.

That gives the case its shape. Whether those safeguards are good is one question. Whether the assessment behind them was thorough enough, and was finished at the right time, is the one Ofcom is asking.

Reading the signal

Some early coverage has framed this as regulators stepping in before harm occurs. The timeline does not quite support that: Instants has been live for nearly five months. A more accurate reading is that Ofcom is now checking the homework on individual product launches, after the fact, rather than only looking at platforms' general safety systems. I could not confirm from the sources I found that this is the first investigation of a single new feature under the Act, so that claim is left out here.

This fits a pattern. In March 2026 Ofcom sent legally binding information requests to Instagram, Facebook, Roblox, Snapchat, TikTok and YouTube about child protection, and in May it published a report on Instagram's response. Meta is also appealing Ofcom's decision to place Instagram and WhatsApp in the stricter "Category 1" tier, which carries extra transparency and user-control duties.

For Meta, the practical cost is process. If every feature launch can be audited for the quality and timing of its risk assessment, product teams need documented sign-off before shipping, and compliance spending rises. Whether that dents user growth is speculation. Nothing in the reporting suggests Instants will be pulled or restricted.

What to watch

Ofcom's investigations typically run for months before a provisional decision. The first thing to look for is whether Ofcom publishes a provisional notice of contravention, which would set out what it thinks Meta missed. The second is the outcome of Meta's Category 1 appeal, which will show how far the regulator's reach extends.
