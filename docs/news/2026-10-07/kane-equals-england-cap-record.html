Kane scores twice and equals England cap record in win

In one sentence
Harry Kane's two goals in a 3-0 win over Czechia matched Peter Shilton's England appearance record of 125 caps, but the team still leans heavily on its 33-year-old captain.

Harry Kane did what he usually does at Wembley, and on Tuesday night it carried a bit more weight than usual. His two goals in a 3-0 Nations League win over Czechia came in his 125th England appearance. That drew him level with Peter Shilton's men's record, which had stood for 36 years.

The night belonged to the captain, and the result was never in doubt. The rest of the performance gives a more mixed picture of how England get their goals.

The record

Kane's first goal came in the first half, a right-footed finish after a move involving Elliot Anderson and Morgan Rogers. His second, in the 57th minute, was a downward header from a corner. England were already ahead through a Vladimir Coufal own goal, which Kane's involvement helped create. Accounts of the order differ, so the sequence of the first two goals is not certain.

The two goals took Kane to 91 for England, a record for the men's team. He now trails only a few names on the all-time international list, and the Iranian Ali Daei is the next target.

He can take the appearance record outright when England meet Croatia at Wembley on 12 November.

A flat night

The occasion itself was muted. One match report described "a flat, midweek feel from the first whistle". Wembley never really lifted, even as the milestone approached.

The scoreline flatters the question hanging over the team. England scored three, but two of the three goals were Kane's, and the other was an opponent's mistake. I could not verify the match ratings from BBC Sport, so what follows is inference from the goals themselves. The pattern is a familiar one. When England need a goal, the most reliable route is still Kane, whether he finishes a move or attacks a corner.

That is a strength, but it also makes the side dependent on one player. Kane is 33. The squad around him has talent in Rogers, Anderson and others, yet on this evidence none of them has yet taken over the job of scoring regularly. Succession planning is a structural problem, and a comfortable win against a modest opponent does not settle it.

What next

The win leaves England on nine points in their Nations League group. One more point in November would confirm a place in the knockout stage. The Croatia match on 12 November has two things riding on it: Kane's chance to stand alone as England's most-capped men's player, and a test of whether the supporting cast can create chances without him deciding the game.
