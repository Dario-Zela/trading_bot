Jaguar unveils polarising Type 01 electric GT aimed at young Americans

In one sentence
Jaguar's polarising, 5.2-metre Type 01 EV targets a $130,000 US average price, but there is no sales evidence yet that its divisive design will drive demand.

Jaguar has shown the production version of the Type 01, the first car of its reinvented, electric-only range. The four-door GT was unveiled in New York on 6 October, an early sign of where the brand expects its next customers to be. It is a deliberate break with the old Jaguar, and the reception so far has been divided.

The car

The Type 01 sits on a new platform, the Jaguar Electric Architecture, with an 850-volt electrical system and a 120kWh battery. Three motors, one at the front and two at the rear, are reported to deliver more than 1,000PS and over 1,300Nm of torque. Jaguar says it is designed, developed and built in the UK.

The car is long: reported dimensions are about 5,200mm overall on a 3,200mm wheelbase. The mismatch with British parking bays is the detail that has drawn the most comment, but Jaguar has said openly that the size suits American roads, garages and parking spaces better than European ones. The size looks intended for the US market rather than overlooked for the UK one.

The commercial case

The numbers behind the launch matter more than the styling. Jaguar's US average transaction price is currently around $70,000. Reported company comments put the target at $130,000, roughly a 85% jump. UK pricing was previously expected to start from about £120,000, and buyers are encouraged to add paint-to-sample and other bespoke options.

The retail network has been cut to match. The US dealer count has fallen from roughly 200 to 80 over three years. Jaguar's US leadership says the survivors have all seen the car and are "all in". Many also sell Range Rovers, so they already serve the wealthy customers Jaguar wants. The US is described as the brand's top priority for volume and profit, and the company is courting what it calls independent, "rebellious" buyers. The car is EV-only, with no hybrid or plug-in variants planned.

What is missing

The polarised reaction is real: the design was divisive from the Type 00 concept onward, and company leadership says early negative reaction to the rebrand has since turned positive. But that is an assertion from the company, and none of the reporting reviewed here offers order books, deposits or registration data to support it. Divisive design can build a following. It can also erode the heritage value that a $130,000 price depends on. A concept car's online reception says little about whether a buyer will pay that price.

The structural risks are plain enough. Jaguar is raising prices sharply, shrinking its distribution and using a single-fuel, single-body-style product to enter a crowded luxury EV segment where established rivals have far larger networks and customer bases.

What to watch

The first hard evidence will be order and deposit figures and the delivery start date, along with US pricing for the launch edition. Until then, the bet remains unproven: the car is distinctive, but there is no data yet showing that distinctiveness will sell.
