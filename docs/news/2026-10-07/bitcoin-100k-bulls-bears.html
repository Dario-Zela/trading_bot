Bitcoin: analyst calls for $100,000 as bulls and sceptics clash

In one sentence
Analysts are again calling for $100,000 Bitcoin on whale flows and chart levels, while Burry and Schiff repeat long-held warnings; nothing in the coverage suggests a new catalyst.

Bitcoin is being talked toward $100,000 again. The loudest sceptics, Michael Burry and Peter Schiff, are saying the rally is a trap. The call is not new, and neither is the dissent. What makes it worth a note is how the bullish case is being made: on positioning data and chart structure, not on a fresh catalyst.

The bull case

The most quoted call this week comes from John Gillen of the Milk Road crypto podcast. He told Benzinga he is "long as hell" and described a "lockout rally" toward $100,000. His evidence is flow data. Bitcoin whales, he said, have added about $1.22bn since 1 October.

The technical commentary points the same way. Ali Martinez, another analyst, identifies a support zone at $83,300–$84,600, with $86,700 as the breakout level. Benzinga has also reported that Bitcoin recorded its highest weekly close in 35 weeks, after touching a seven-month high in September. A separate analyst quoted by Benzinga, who had called the earlier bottom, expected a pullback from that September peak. Bullish forecasters are not unanimous even among themselves.

The target also has institutional company. Anthony Scaramucci has predicted a move through $100,000, and Benzinga's August coverage noted that Standard Chartered's 2026 year-end target remained in place. None of this is a forecast with a stated timeframe, and the specific price path to $100,000 was not published in the pieces reviewed.

The sceptics

Burry and Schiff are consistent critics. Burry, the investor made famous by The Big Short, has called Bitcoin the "tulip bulb of our time" and described the six-figure era as speculative mania. Schiff, a long-standing gold advocate, calls it a "fake asset" and has previously argued for a fall to $50,000–$60,000, pointing to the average cost basis of MicroStrategy's holdings.

Both have been wrong before in one sense: Schiff said in 2019 that Bitcoin would never reach $100,000, and it did, in December 2024. That is a reason to weigh their warnings as a standing view rather than a signal. Nothing in the reporting identifies a new trigger for their latest remarks, and a sceptic repeating a thesis is not evidence that the thesis has changed.

What the pattern does and does not show

The case for reading this as crowding is that the bulls are citing flows and round-number targets, which tend to cluster when positioning is already long. The case against is that every element is familiar. Analysts have been naming $100,000 since the last time Bitcoin crossed it, the sceptics are the usual ones, and a seven-month high is a modest backdrop for a consensus trade. Sentiment that is simply recycled carries little information.

The macro setting is the swing factor. Crypto traded lower ahead of the latest Federal Open Market Committee minutes, with one analyst arguing that $100,000 could "come back into focus" depending on the Fed's tone. Rate expectations have been moving; markets now price only about a one-in-five chance of an October hike, a shift that generally helps non-yielding assets.

What to watch

Three things would separate a real move from commentary. The first is whether Bitcoin holds the $83,000–$85,000 support zone Martinez describes and clears $86,700. The second is whether the whale accumulation continues past the first weeks of October. The third is the Fed's next inflation and policy signals, which will decide whether the sceptics' warnings or the bulls' flow data prove the better guide.
