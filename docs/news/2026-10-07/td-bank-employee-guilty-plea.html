Former TD Bank employee pleads guilty over $4.8m laundering to Colombia

In one sentence
Ex-TD Bank employee Gerardo Aquino pleaded guilty to taking bribes and laundering over $4.8m to Colombia, the latest insider case to feed scrutiny of TD's anti-money-laundering controls.

A former TD Bank employee in Florida has pleaded guilty to taking bribes and laundering more than $4.8 million to Colombia. The case is small beside the bank's own record, but it sits on a long-running weakness: the people inside a bank can defeat the controls the bank builds.

Gerardo Aquino, 40, of Hollywood, Florida, pleaded guilty on Tuesday to conspiracy to launder monetary instruments and to receipt of a bribe by a bank employee, according to the Justice Department. Prosecutors say the scheme ran from April 2022 to November 2023. Aquino opened 86 fraudulent customer accounts, all registered to a single commercial address in Miami. He issued hundreds of debit cards to co-conspirators and unblocked cards that TD's own fraud systems had restricted. Those 86 accounts alone moved more than $3 million to Colombia.

The mechanism was ordinary. Cash went into US accounts, and debit cards then drew it out of ATMs in Colombia. The Justice Department did not break down the withdrawals in this case, but a similar TD case, that of Florida employee Leonardo Ayala, involved more than 10,000 ATM withdrawals.

Sentencing and exposure

Aquino faces up to 20 years for the money laundering conspiracy and up to 30 years for bank bribery. Sentencing is set for 23 March 2027. As in earlier cases, the personal gain reported was small against the sums moved. Ayala was paid a little over $6,000 for helping move more than $5.5 million.

A pattern, not an outlier

Aquino is at least the third former TD employee to plead guilty in Colombian ATM laundering cases. Ayala, a Florida employee, admitted to facilitating $5.5 million in 2023. Oscar Marcel Nunez-Flores, a New Jersey employee, admitted to helping move more than $26 million between March 2021 and October 2023. The three cases run over overlapping periods and involve the same method, which suggests the problem was not one person's lapse.

That matters because of what came before. In October 2024 TD pleaded guilty to US anti-money-laundering failures and agreed to penalties of about $3.1 billion. Regulators also capped the growth of its US assets and imposed a monitorship. These individual pleas show the conduct behind that settlement, in which staff were bribed and monitoring did not stop the flows. The prosecutors' account is also consistent with an insider who held enough system access to override a fraud block.

What it means for the bank

The plea itself adds no new penalty for TD. It is a prosecution of an individual, and the bank is not charged in this case. The market relevance is indirect and runs through three things.

  • Remediation cost. Insider-abuse cases keep the monitorship and the cost of rebuilding controls in view.
  • The asset cap. It constrains US growth, and evidence of lingering control gaps makes it harder to argue for relief.
  • Credibility with supervisors. Each case that dates from the period before the 2024 settlement reads as historical. Any that emerge from later periods would be more damaging.

The dates here fall before the settlement, with the conduct ending in November 2023. That limits the read-across, but it does not remove it.

What to watch next: further charges or pleas tied to the same Colombian networks, any progress reports on the monitorship, and Aquino's sentencing in March.
