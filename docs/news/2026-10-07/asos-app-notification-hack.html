Asos confirms hackers sent 'unauthorised' notification to app users

In one sentence
Asos confirmed hackers used a third-party notification platform to push an extortion-style 'ASOS HACKED' message to app users, saying cards and passwords appear unaffected.

Asos, the online fashion retailer, has confirmed that hackers sent an unauthorised message to customers through its app. The message looked like a ransom note, and it carried a link to the attackers' Telegram channel. The company says it believes payment-card details and account passwords were not affected.

Dozens of people told the BBC they received a pop-up on Tuesday morning reading "ASOS HACKED". Some said it left them scared to open the app. The text was addressed to the company rather than to shoppers:

Dear ASOS DPO and IT, we have fully compromised the Snowflake instance. Engage with us, or we will leak it.

The "DPO" is the company's data protection officer. Snowflake is a cloud data platform widely used by retailers to store and analyse customer data. The message is the hackers' own claim. Asos has not confirmed that a Snowflake environment was breached, and the extent of any data theft is unverified.

What Asos has said

The company says it is investigating "unauthorised activity involving third-party platforms" that it uses to communicate with customers. It says basic personal details may have been accessed, and it added: "We do not believe that payment-card information or account passwords were impacted."

Asos also says it took "immediate action to restrict access to the notification platforms" on Tuesday. It is working with specialists inside and outside the company, and with the relevant authorities. The BBC understands that the National Cyber Security Centre has offered assistance.

The detail that matters is where the attackers got in. A push notification is sent by a messaging service that sits outside the shop itself, and the retailer hands it a list of devices to reach. The route into this incident appears to have been one of those outside services, not Asos's checkout or account systems. That fits the company's wording about "third-party platforms", though the investigation will have to establish it.

Not the first time this year

This is separate from an earlier incident. Asos's US arm disclosed in a regulatory filing that an unauthorised party had used login details stolen elsewhere to access customer accounts. That filing put the number of people affected at roughly 138,828. That attack worked by reusing credentials leaked from other services, and Asos forced password resets in response. Nothing published so far links the two events, so they should be treated as unrelated until the company says otherwise.

What customers can do

Standard advice applies. Treat any message that mentions the incident with caution, particularly one asking for details or pointing to an unfamiliar link. Do not tap the Telegram link. Use a unique password for the Asos account, and change it if it is reused elsewhere. Watch for phishing emails or texts that use customers' names and addresses, which are the sort of basic details Asos says may have been exposed. Check bank statements as a matter of routine.

Why the market impact looks limited

Retail security incidents are common, and the market tends to react only when stolen data is large, sensitive or tied to payments. Asos has said it does not believe card data or passwords were involved, and it disclosed the incident the same day. Prompt disclosure of that kind usually limits reputational damage. Any price reaction in Asos shares will be the test. None is cited in the reporting reviewed, so none is assumed here.

The open questions are specific. Asos has to establish how many customers' details were reached and whether the hackers' claim about the data platform holds up. Whether the attackers publish anything on their Telegram channel will show if the threat is real. A data-protection notification to the Information Commissioner's Office would follow if personal data is confirmed to have been exposed.
