Tempus AI shares extend rally after Morgan Stanley lifts revenue outlook

In one sentence
Tempus AI shares are up ~30% on the week after Morgan Stanley flagged $330–400m of extra annual revenue from clearer diagnostic reimbursement — a sell-side reframing, not a new filing.

Tempus AI's stock has climbed roughly 30% over the past week, closing Thursday at $79.65 after a 13.8% single-session jump, following upbeat commentary from Morgan Stanley on the healthcare-AI company's reimbursement outlook. The move is large by any measure — but it says more about how thin the market's information set is on this stock than about any transformation at the company itself.

The catalyst was a Morgan Stanley Global Healthcare Conference appearance on September 15, where Tempus executives, including founder and chief executive Eric Lefkofsky, laid out the case for two of the firm's diagnostic products getting materially better reimbursement terms than the market had priced in. Lefkofsky said clearer pricing on the company's xT genomic sequencing test could add $80 million to $100 million in annual revenue, while newly clarified approval and pricing on its xF liquid-biopsy test could contribute another $250 million to $300 million. Combined, Morgan Stanley put the potential uplift at $330 million to $400 million a year — a meaningful add-on for a company that guided to roughly $1.6 billion in full-year revenue.

What actually changed

It's worth being precise about what did and didn't happen here. Tempus did not announce a new product, a new partnership, or a beat against consensus estimates that day — the rally followed prepared remarks and analyst framing, not a press release or an SEC filing with new financial detail. What changed is that Morgan Stanley — and by extension the sell-side consensus that leans on it — now has more clarity on reimbursement mechanics that were previously uncertain, and revised its model accordingly. Consensus forward EPS estimates have moved from roughly -$0.11 to a fraction of a cent positive over the past 90 days, with eight upward analyst revisions in the past month, according to data compiled by 24/7 Wall St.

The underlying business has also been running hot on its own terms. Tempus's most recent quarter showed revenue of $382.5 million, up 21.6% year-on-year, with oncology test volumes accelerating 31%. Lefkofsky told investors the company's data-licensing arm — which sells de-identified clinical and genomic data to drugmakers — booked roughly $200 million in the second quarter from clients including AstraZeneca, GSK and Merck, and said management now expects to exceed its own multiyear 25% growth target. Full-year guidance was lifted to $1.595–1.605 billion in revenue with adjusted EBITDA near $65 million.

The gap between the stock move and the reporting

Even after the rally, the stock trades below its 52-week high of $104.32, and the average analyst price target of $68.18 now sits well under Thursday's close — meaning the rally has outrun the models built to justify it, at least until those targets are revised upward, which several desks are already doing. At roughly 9 times sales, Tempus is priced as a growth story, not a diagnostics company with a conventional earnings multiple.

That combination — a single conference appearance, a sell-side reframing, and a 30% weekly move — is a reminder of how much of the current AI-adjacent healthcare trade rests on narrow, analyst-mediated information flows rather than independently verified data. Morgan Stanley's numbers come from company guidance relayed at an investor conference; they have not yet shown up in a 10-Q. Nothing here is disqualifying, but it is a distinction between an analyst upgrade and a demonstrated change in the addressable market for healthcare AI diagnostics, which remains as opaque as it was a week ago.

What comes next

The next real test is Tempus's third-quarter print, whenever it lands, which will show whether the xT and xF reimbursement gains Lefkofsky described are showing up in booked revenue rather than guidance language. Until then, the stock's next moves are likely to track further sell-side price-target revisions rather than new operating data — a pattern common to single-stock rallies built on a conference appearance rather than a filing.
