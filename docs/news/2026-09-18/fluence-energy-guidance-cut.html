Fluence Energy stock plunges after guidance cut raises production concerns

In one sentence
Fluence Energy shares fell about 22% after a second 2026 guidance cut tied to a stalled Houston plant ramp-up, raising doubts about battery-storage manufacturing capacity.

Shares in Fluence Energy fell roughly 22% on Thursday after the grid-scale battery storage maker slashed its full-year revenue and profit outlook for the second time in 2026, pointing to a manufacturing ramp-up that has gone badly wrong at its Houston plant. The stock closed near $7, deep into 52-week-low territory and a fraction of the $33.51 it touched a year ago.

"Our team underestimated the complexity of the ramp-up of the Houston facility, specifically the major issues that have emerged."

That admission, from chief executive Julian Nebreda, understates the scale of the miss. The Houston site — which makes thermal-management enclosures central to Fluence's battery systems — was supposed to be turning out eleven units a day by late summer as part of the company's push toward US-made content. Instead it managed less than one a day in August, undone by an underperforming automated welding line and a shortage of skilled labor. Output has since recovered to about three units daily, still a fraction of plan.

The numbers behind the drop

Fluence now expects fiscal 2026 revenue of about $2.4 billion, down from roughly $3 billion guided just last month — a $600 million write-down in a single quarter. Adjusted EBITDA guidance swung from a loss of about $10 million to a loss of about $200 million. Management attributed roughly 80% of the shortfall to US production problems: $450 million from the Houston delays themselves, another $65 million in late-delivery penalties owed to customers, and $85 million in added logistics costs from working around the bottleneck.

The order backlog, by contrast, remains large — $6.4 billion by the company's own count — which is precisely what has unnerved analysts rather than reassured them. Barclays cut Fluence to Underweight from Equal Weight and dropped its price target to $10 from $16, citing a widening gap between paper backlog and the company's ability to actually deliver and bill for it, alongside rising capital needs and thinning liquidity. Baird went further, cutting its target by 70% to $3 and downgrading to Underperform. Fluence has appointed a new chief operating officer, Bernard Dasant, to take charge of manufacturing and supply-chain execution, and has said it does not expect to need additional capital in fiscal 2027.

A bellwether problem, not just a Fluence problem

Fluence is not a niche supplier. As one of the largest makers of grid-scale battery systems, backed by Siemens and AES, it has functioned as something close to a proxy for the health of the storage build-out that utilities are counting on to firm up solar and wind generation and to backstop the surge in power demand from data centers. A single company's welding line does not, on its own, prove an industry-wide ceiling. But the specifics here are the kind that recur across capital-intensive manufacturing ramps: automation that does not scale as modeled, and not enough trained labor to make up the difference by hand. Those are structural constraints, not one-off accidents, and they are exactly the ones that show up whenever a sector tries to reshore fast to capture domestic-content incentives.

CFO Ahmed Pasha added a wrinkle worth watching: some data-center developers may now choose to forgo US tax credits altogether in favor of faster delivery from non-domestic supply chains — a sign that speed-to-power is starting to outweigh the incentive math for at least some buyers, which would cut against the very reshoring push Fluence's Houston plant was built to serve.

The next checkpoint is Fluence's fiscal fourth-quarter earnings call, when investors will look for evidence that the Houston line's output run-rate is climbing toward its original eleven-a-day target rather than plateauing near three. Until then, the backlog stays a promise rather than a number on the income statement.
