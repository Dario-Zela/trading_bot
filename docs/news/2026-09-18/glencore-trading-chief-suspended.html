Glencore suspends iron-ore trading chief amid $2bn Radiant World fraud dispute

Glencore's headquarters in Baar, Switzerland — Paradise Chronicle / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Glencore suspended iron-ore chief Peter Hill after messages showed him telling trading partner Radiant World to 'say nothing on email' amid a $2bn fraud lawsuit.

Glencore has suspended Peter Hill, the head of its iron ore trading desk, pending a review of the Swiss commodities giant's dealings with Radiant World, a trading partner it now publicly accuses of fraud. The suspension, first reported by Bloomberg, comes with an uncomfortable detail attached: messages showing Hill instructing a Radiant World counterpart to keep sensitive discussions off email.

"Say nothing on email," Hill wrote in a WhatsApp message to a Radiant World contact in early April 2025, according to Bloomberg, which reviewed the correspondence. Other messages dating back to 2023 show Hill describing Glencore as a key backer of the Radiant World group and suggesting he held effective decision-making authority over aspects of its trading — the kind of informal influence that sits awkwardly next to a company under active regulatory supervision.

From partnership to $2bn lawsuit

Glencore was, until recently, one of Radiant World's most important financial backers. That relationship has now collapsed into a Singapore lawsuit in which Radiant World and an affiliated trader, Sapphire Minmetals, are seeking $2 billion in damages, alleging fraud, breach of contract and conspiracy. Radiant World's claim centres on a 2021 episode in which it says Glencore demanded $1.1 billion in mark-to-market payments, then extracted more than $800 million from the relationship between 2021 and 2026 on the promise of a long-term partnership before cutting ties this year.

Radiant World has gone further, alleging that Glencore deliberately kept the arrangement out of its audited records because of concerns about the US Department of Justice's independent compliance monitors — installed for three years after Glencore's 2022 guilty pleas to foreign bribery and commodity price manipulation, which cost it roughly $700 million in fines, forfeiture and disgorgement in the US alone. Radiant World says the arrangement was made with the knowledge of Glencore's most senior leadership, naming chief executive Gary Nagle, chief financial officer Steve Kalmin and former chief executive Ivan Glasenberg.

Glencore has pushed back hard in the other direction. It says it uncovered evidence that Radiant World and Sapphire Minmetals sent lenders falsified invoices, fabricated contracts and emails purporting to be from Glencore staff. Separately, a Jefferies Financial Group fund has accused the same two companies of falsifying iron ore invoices, prompting a London court to impose a $499 million worldwide freezing order against them.

Why the emails matter

Hill, who joined Glencore in 2009 after a run of marketing roles at BHP Billiton and built out its iron ore trading franchise, is not a defendant in either lawsuit. But instructions to keep a paper trail off email land badly for a company already operating under a DOJ-appointed monitor tasked with certifying that its controls actually work. Whatever the merits of the fraud claims on either side, evidence that a senior trader sought to suppress written records is the kind of finding compliance monitors and prosecutors weigh independently of who wins the underlying commercial dispute.

Glencore has not disclosed the scope or expected timeline of its internal review, nor commented in detail on Hill's status beyond confirming he has been stood down from his duties.

The Singapore case and the London freezing order both continue, and Glencore's compliance monitorship — a condition of its 2022 settlement — runs for three years from that date, putting continued DOJ scrutiny squarely astride the current dispute. The next marker will be whichever court moves first: a Singapore hearing date on the $2 billion claim, or the outcome of Glencore's own internal review into what its iron ore desk was doing off the record.
