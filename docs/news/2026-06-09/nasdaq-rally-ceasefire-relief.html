Nasdaq 100 surges more than 2% as chip stocks rebound and Iran-Israel ceasefire lifts sentiment

The Nasdaq MarketSite screen in Times Square, New York — the index recovered 2.1% Monday after its worst session since April 2025. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Nasdaq 100 surged 2.1% Monday as Intel jumped 12% on an Alphabet foundry order and an Iran-Israel ceasefire lifted sentiment, but rate-hike odds above 50% and Wednesday's CPI will test whether it holds.

U.S. equity markets shook off last week's rout on Monday, with the Nasdaq 100 climbing 2.1% to 29,564 as a combination of company-specific chip news and a tentative ceasefire between Israel and Iran restored enough risk appetite for a broad, if cautious, recovery. The benchmark technology index had shed 4.8% on Friday — its worst single session since the tariff-driven selloff of April 2025 — so some of Monday's gain was mechanical. When a crowd overshoots, it tends to snap back.

The S&P 500 added 0.8% to 7,440, the Dow gained 0.2%, and the Russell 2000 rose 1.3%. The Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) jumped 3.1%. The VIX, the options market's fear proxy, fell 12%.

Chips find a floor — with help from Google and Nvidia

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index posted its worst single session since March 2020 on Friday, erasing more than $1 trillion in market value. Two catalysts compounded each other: a May payrolls report that came in at 172,000 jobs added — roughly double the 85,000 consensus — effectively closed the door on any Fed rate cut before year-end, and Broadcom's disappointing forward guidance added an earnings-specific weight to the sector.

Monday's reversal was broad and sharp. Micron Technology rose 10.7%, KLA Corp gained 9.9%, Applied Materials added 9.2%, and Lam Research climbed 8.4%. The semiconductor ETF (SMH) was up 4.6% on the session.

The standout mover was Intel, up 12.4%, after The Information reported that Alphabet has placed an order for more than three million tensor processing units to be manufactured by Intel's foundry in 2028 — a substantial vote of confidence in a company that spent several years ceding ground to TSMC. Nvidia, the report added, has been evaluating whether Intel's process technology can be used to manufacture a chip combining four graphics processors into a single unit, though no order has been placed. The prospect of Intel establishing itself as a credible second-source foundry for the two largest AI infrastructure spenders drove the stock's move more than any geopolitical read.

Marvell Technology was the S&P 500's top gainer at +13.4%, fuelled in part by its own catalyst: the chipmaker is set to join the S&P 500, triggering a wave of passive-index buying that markets began front-running on Monday.

The ceasefire premium

Geopolitical relief was real, if clearly provisional. President Trump posted early Monday that "both sides, Israel and Iran, are looking to do an immediate ceasefire." Iran's armed forces subsequently confirmed they had ended military operations against Israel, while warning of "harsher attacks" if strikes on Lebanon resume. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated Israel was "holding fire for now" while reserving "the full right to self-defence" — language that is legally precise and strategically ambiguous in equal measure.

Oil moved in a direction that complicates the straightforward narrative. WTI crude rose 1.0% to $91.41, Brent gained 1.6% to $94.62. Markets appear to be pricing a risk-off unwind in equities while simultaneously hedging that any ceasefire is reversible in energy futures — not an irrational pair of trades given the recent track record. Airline stocks were under separate pressure regardless: jet fuel costs were already running 78% above year-ago levels in April.

The ceasefire's effect on risk assets has historical precedent for brevity. An earlier round of Iran-Israel de-escalation in April produced a 1.2% Nasdaq gain that faded within days as the rate-hike calculus reasserted itself. Monday's bounce is larger, and carries a company-specific catalyst — the Intel foundry order is not purely sentiment-driven — but the macro overhang is structurally unchanged.

What breaks the bounce, or confirms it

Two data releases will do most of the work this week. Consumer Price Index figures arrive Wednesday, followed by Producer Price Index on Thursday. Markets are currently pricing roughly 52% odds of a Fed rate hike by October and nearly 70% by December — a sharp reset from the rate-cut consensus that prevailed as recently as April. A CPI print below expectations would give the Nasdaq's recovery some fundamental backing. A hot number reopens the argument that Friday's rout was recalibration, not overshoot.

The 10-year Treasury yield ended Monday at 4.56%, down two basis points from Friday's close but still well above the level that has historically weighed on long-duration technology equities. The 30-year remains above 5%.

The Intel foundry story is arguably the most durable signal from Monday's session — a named customer, a named order, a named delivery year. The ceasefire-driven bid, as ever, comes with an expiry date that nobody can read with confidence. Wednesday's inflation print will tell more about which Monday rally — chips or geopolitics — had the longer shelf life.
