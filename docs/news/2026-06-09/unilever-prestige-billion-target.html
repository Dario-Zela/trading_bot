Unilever Prestige chief targets billion-pound revenues as premium beauty bet takes shape

In one sentence
Unilever Prestige CEO MC Gasco-Buisson maps a decade-long route to £1bn per flagship brand, but offers vision over verifiable targets.

MC Gasco-Buisson leads a corner of Unilever that is easy to overlook in quarterly filings but increasingly central to the conglomerate's long-term pitch. As chief executive of the group's Prestige division — home to Dermalogica, Paula's Choice, Hourglass and K18 — she has used a wide-ranging interview with WWD to set out the most detailed public account yet of where the portfolio is heading.

The division was assembled from 2014 under then-chief executive Paul Polman and now sits at roughly €1.4 billion in combined revenues. Gasco-Buisson's stated ambition is to build four of those core labels toward £1 billion in revenues each. She is candid about the horizon: "Could take 10 years to get to a billion." Dermalogica is the frontrunner, with revenues estimated in the £500 million range; the others are several years behind that milestone.

House of companies

The operating model is deliberately decentralised. Each brand runs as an independent business with its own chief executive and, where possible, retained founder involvement — what she calls a "house of companies." The portfolio layer provides innovation resources, talent pipelines and Unilever's supply-chain heft; the brands keep their identities intact. "Too many people come into a brand and just change it — that hardly ever works," Gasco-Buisson said. "The job is to find what is beautiful and unique and special, and modernise it."

She has also made editorial choices. REN Clean Skincare and Kate Somerville were divested from the portfolio — a deliberate signal that the division intends to concentrate firepower rather than accumulate labels.

The globalization argument is straightforward: the portfolio is heavily American in origin but under-distributed in Europe and the U.K. "We are focused on making sure that not only are they present in Europe and the U.K., but that we're building equity and desire at scale." On acquisitions, Gasco-Buisson describes herself as actively searching for "people who want to scale insurgency" — founder-led American brands with genuine consumer equity and appetite for international distribution. No candidates were named.

The parent's backing is visible at the top. Unilever chief executive Fernando Fernandez has set a medium-term aim for beauty and personal care to represent two-thirds of group revenues, and the prestige division carries the highest-margin strand of that thesis.

What the interview does not supply — and what would be needed to separate this from routine category positioning — is specificity: no revenue targets by brand, no timeline for the European push, no named acquisition targets. The operational picture is genuinely improving: Hourglass and K18 delivered double-digit growth in 2025, and both Dermalogica and Paula's Choice returned to growth in the second half of the year after earlier declines. The next concrete test will be Unilever's half-year results, expected in late July, where the Beauty & Wellbeing segment's 2026 trajectory will be measured. An acquisition announcement, when it materialises, is likely to move the needle rather more than any vision statement.
