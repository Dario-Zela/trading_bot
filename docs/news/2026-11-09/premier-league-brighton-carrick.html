Premier League: Brighton's run stirs title talk as Carrick's United future stays in limbo

In one sentence
Brighton are second on 22 points, five behind Arsenal, with the league's best goal difference, while Manchester United sit 18th and Carrick's future is unconfirmed.

Brighton are second in the Premier League. After ten matches they have 22 points, five behind leaders Arsenal on 27 and one clear of Manchester City on 21, according to a Sports Mole table. So the title talk rests on something real. Whether it amounts to a challenge is a separate question.

What the table shows

The case for Brighton is mostly arithmetic. Their goal difference of +19 is the best in the division. Arsenal's is +10 and City's +8. A side that wins by margins like that is not riding a few lucky one-goal results.

The case against is also arithmetic. Five points after ten games is a real gap, and Arsenal have shown no sign of stalling. The league's most recent form guide was not available to this writer, so how Brighton got here, match by match, is not set out in this article. An October snapshot of the table had them third on 16 points from eight games, with a run of four wins in five. That suggests the climb is recent and quick rather than a slow burn.

One caution on the sources. Third-party tables sometimes disagree with each other, and the figures here come from a single site. They should be checked against the league's official standings before anyone writes Brighton into the title story. The next test is close. Brighton host Newcastle on 28 November, the day before Arsenal meet Manchester City. Both fixtures can narrow or widen the gap.

The chasing pack

Behind the top three the picture is less settled. Liverpool are fourth on 18 points. Chelsea are fifth on 16, level with Everton and ahead of Bournemouth on 15, which is a modest return for a club of Chelsea's spending. The summary supplied for this piece says Chelsea under Alonso are still searching for an identity. That could not be independently confirmed, including Alonso's position at the club, and sits here as reported rather than established.

The contrast is the point. The sides below Brighton are bunched within a few points of each other. Brighton have a cushion of goal difference that none of them can match.

Carrick in limbo

At the bottom end, Manchester United are 18th on eight points from ten, with a goal difference of -5, just ahead of Crystal Palace on seven. That is the backdrop to the continuing uncertainty over Michael Carrick.

Carrick took over as interim manager in January after Ruben Amorim's departure. In the spring he guided United to Champions League qualification with three league games to spare. In May he said his long-term status was "out of my hands" and that he expected a decision "pretty soon", with reports of a possible two-year deal. This article could not confirm what was decided, or whether his role has changed since. The newest reports found were from May, and no November match report or club statement could be located. The "turbulent match" in the triage brief is unverified.

The table does not need a new development to make the point. A side in the bottom three, with the manager's position still described as unresolved, is the kind of situation that tends to force a decision.

What to watch

Two fixtures on 28 and 29 November will say more than a week of debate. If Brighton beat Newcastle and Arsenal drop points against City, the gap shrinks to two or three. If not, five points with 28 games left is still catchable, but Brighton's goal difference will matter less than the points they bank.
