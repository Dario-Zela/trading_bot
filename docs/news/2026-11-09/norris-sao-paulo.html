Norris sparkles in Sao Paulo despite firework mishap

In one sentence
Lando Norris won in Sao Paulo from pole and was then hit by podium sparks, unhurt; the titles are already decided, leaving only second place in the drivers' standings.

Lando Norris won the Sao Paulo Grand Prix from pole position on Sunday, then spent the podium ceremony being hit on the head by a firework. He was unhurt. The win did not move the drivers' championship, which is already settled.

The race at Interlagos was run in changeable, stormy conditions. Norris started from pole but lost the lead early, and Kimi Antonelli led for a stretch. Antonelli then spun while under pressure from the McLaren, and Norris took the lead for good. He finished 14.5 seconds clear of Max Verstappen, with Red Bull's Isack Hadjar third. George Russell was fourth and Antonelli recovered to fifth. Norris called it one of the hardest races of his year for the concentration it demanded, according to the BBC's account. Reports differ on where and why Antonelli spun, so this piece does not say.

The podium

Norris's usual celebration is to shake and spray a champagne bottle. This time the bottle broke on the podium floor. He then stepped back into a sprinkler-style firework, and sparks hit the right side of his head. He was seen holding his face afterwards. Verstappen and Hadjar then soaked him with their own bottles.

McLaren team principal Andrea Stella had not yet spoken to Norris when he faced the media. He said: "I understand he is all right." Norris showed no ill effects in his post-race interviews. The reports available give no sign of an official inquiry or any change to podium procedures. The incident reads as an embarrassing accident, not a safety story.

What the win is worth

The brief's framing of a "title-relevant" weekend does not hold up. Antonelli clinched the drivers' championship in Mexico City, which ended Norris's reign as champion and made Antonelli the youngest world champion. Mercedes took the constructors' title in Brazil, its ninth, thanks to Russell and Antonelli finishing in the top five. It is the team's first since 2021 and it takes the title from McLaren. Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff said he does not expect a return to the dominance of the Lewis Hamilton years. He sees four teams (Mercedes, Ferrari, McLaren and Red Bull) as capable of winning races.

What remains for Norris is second place in the drivers' standings. He trails Verstappen by 33 points with a maximum of three rounds left. The standings figures come from PlanetF1 alone.

That is the live contest in the closing races: whether Norris can cut a 33-point deficit to Verstappen before the season ends. A win in Brazil was the best way to start.
