Virgin Media O2's Netomnia move prompts investors to reassess UK broadband

In one sentence
The £2bn nexfibre deal for Netomnia, backed by Virgin Media O2's owners, faces a provisional CMA objection, with a final decision due by 15 December 2026.

The Virgin Media O2 and Netomnia story is no longer the open question the triage note suggested. The deal is real, it has been in front of the competition regulator since the spring, and it now looks unlikely to complete. The Simply Wall St note that prompted this piece appears to be a late echo of a transaction announced in February.

The parties' own account of the terms is secondhand here, taken from trade press summaries. It has not been checked against a primary filing.

What was agreed

In February 2026, nexfibre agreed to buy Netomnia's parent for about £2bn, according to ISPreview and TechRadar. Nexfibre is the wholesale fibre vehicle backed by Virgin Media O2's owners, Liberty Global and Telefónica, together with the infrastructure investor InfraVia. The structure matters. Netomnia's network assets would go into nexfibre. Virgin Media O2 itself would take over the YouFibre and brsk retail customers, for a reported £150m. The seller is Substantial Group.

The combined footprint would have reached up to around 20 million premises, according to the same reports. That would make it the largest challenger to BT's Openreach. It also explains why the regulator took an interest.

The regulatory snag

The Competition and Markets Authority fast-tracked the deal to an in-depth Phase 2 review in July, at the parties' request (ISPreview). Its provisional findings, dated 2 October, are that the deal could lessen competition. The CMA's provisional view is that CityFibre would most likely have bought Netomnia's owner had the nexfibre deal not happened.

That is the counterfactual that matters. The CMA is not comparing the deal with a world in which Netomnia stays independent. It is comparing it with one in which a rival full-fibre builder absorbs it. In that world, a stronger third network would still stand against Openreach and the Virgin Media O2 network.

On 20 October, nexfibre and its shareholders said they would not submit remedies to the CMA. Trade press read that as making a block increasingly likely, and ISPreview has described the deal as effectively dead. I found no reference to a court challenge or appeal. A separate Spanish-language report mentioned a challenge to the regulator's position, and I could not confirm what form it takes.

What investors are weighing

The investor question is less about a Virgin Media O2 gain than about the shape of the sector. A blocked deal leaves Netomnia available to CityFibre, or to another buyer, and keeps the number of independent full-fibre networks higher for now. It also leaves smaller altnets with a clearer read on what the CMA will tolerate. The regulator has now shown it will look past the transaction in front of it to the one it considers more likely.

No price reaction is reported here. The Simply Wall St note is a generic investor summary, and it does not establish any move in listed UK telecom assets.

What to watch

The CMA's final decision is due by 15 December 2026. Until then, the provisional findings can be revised. The signal to watch is whether the parties offer any remedy before that date, and whether CityFibre moves on Netomnia if the nexfibre deal falls away.
