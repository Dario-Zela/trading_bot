Salesforce slides on downgrade, PepsiCo beats but shares slip anyway

Salesforce Tower in downtown San Francisco, the day after KeyBanc and Bernstein both downgraded the company's stock. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Salesforce, PepsiCo and AstraZeneca all sold off Thursday on unrelated news, but each move shows high-multiple names getting no benefit of the doubt.

Three blue-chip names took separate hits on Thursday, and the pattern is more instructive than any single move. Salesforce dropped on an analyst downgrade, PepsiCo fell despite beating revenue estimates, and AstraZeneca lost billions in market value on a failed drug trial. None of the three stories share a cause. What they share is a market that is no longer giving high-expectation names the benefit of the doubt.

Salesforce: the AI story stops selling itself

KeyBanc Capital Markets analyst Jackson Ader cut Salesforce (CRM) to Sector Weight from Overweight on Thursday, and the language was pointed rather than routine. In channel checks, Ader wrote, customers keep telling him their data isn't organized enough to work with AI in any meaningful way, and that Agentforce — the company's flagship AI agent platform — "just isn't there" yet. He said it is "difficult to find evidence" of upside even at current levels, with growth, current remaining performance obligations, and bookings all looking hard to support. Bernstein published a similar downgrade the same day, citing the same Agentforce shortfall.

Shares closed down 2.4% on the news, extending a rough stretch: Salesforce is now off roughly 40% for the year, and Ader notes it trades nearly 80% below its post-2020 peak on an enterprise-value-to-free-cash-flow basis. That a stock already this cheap can still draw a downgrade — rather than bargain-hunting — says something about where sentiment sits. Investors aren't asking whether Salesforce is a good business; they're asking whether the AI narrative that inflated software multiples across the sector still holds. Bulls have not entirely capitulated — most Wall Street ratings on the stock remain positive — but the marginal dollar is voting the other way.

PepsiCo: a beat that wasn't enough

PepsiCo (PEP) delivered a revenue beat and reaffirmed full-year guidance, and the stock fell anyway — down roughly 3.2% to about $138 in early trading. The mechanics were narrower than the headline suggests. Revenue came in at $24.18 billion against forecasts of $23.97 billion, but adjusted earnings of $2.20 a share landed a cent below the Street's $2.21 estimate, and management flagged that full-year profit could land toward the low end of its guided range.

The geography of the quarter did the real damage to sentiment. North American beverage volumes fell 4% and the convenient-foods unit was flat, evidence that price increases of recent years have caught up with domestic demand. International divisions — Asia Pacific, the international beverages franchise, and Europe/Middle East/Africa — all posted volume gains and were the only real source of momentum. A modest EPS miss and cautious guidance language proved enough to erase a revenue beat, which is itself a signal: investors are pricing these results against a high bar, not a low one, and punishing any shortfall from it disproportionately.

AstraZeneca: the outlier that fits the pattern

AstraZeneca (AZN) supplied the sharpest single-day move of the three. Its heart drug Wainua, developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals, missed the primary endpoint of the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform trial — the largest study ever run in transthyretin-mediated amyloid cardiomyopathy, enrolling 1,432 patients across 130 sites. Shares fell 9.6%, wiping out roughly £19 billion in market value. Analysts pointed to a trial-design complication — 57% of enrolled patients were already on a separate stabilizer drug, and another 24% added one during the study — that made isolating Wainua's benefit difficult even before the data came in. (Full coverage of the trial result and its knock-on effect on rival cardiac-drug makers is available separately.)

The common thread

Lay the three side by side and the connecting tissue isn't sector — software, snacks, and pharma have little in common operationally. It's valuation posture. Each of these companies had, in its own way, been trading on a story: Salesforce on AI monetization, PepsiCo on pricing power holding through a demand slowdown, AstraZeneca on a pipeline bet paying off in a hard therapeutic category. When each story hit a data point that complicated it, the market didn't wait for confirmation — it repriced immediately and by mid-to-high single digits or more. Whether that reflects broadening caution toward richly valued names generally, or three unrelated coincidences on the same Thursday, is the question the next round of earnings and trial readouts will start to answer.

What's next

Salesforce reports fiscal second-quarter results in early September, the first real test of whether Agentforce bookings can rebut the KeyBanc and Bernstein downgrades. PepsiCo's North American volume trend will be the line to watch in Q3 commentary, given the segment's 4% beverage decline this quarter. For AstraZeneca, attention shifts to whether Wainua's subgroup data — the nominally positive signal among patients not on background stabilizer therapy — can be salvaged in a future trial design, or whether rivals in the ATTR-CM space move to capture the opening.
