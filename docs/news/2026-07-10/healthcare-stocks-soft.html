Healthcare stocks drift lower in a quiet Thursday session

In one sentence
Healthcare stocks slipped 0.5% as an Ionis-AstraZeneca heart-drug trial miss overshadowed portfolio moves at Siemens Healthineers and ResMed.

Healthcare stocks eased through a subdued Thursday session, with the NYSE Healthcare Index shedding half a percent in late-afternoon trading. Taken alone, that is unremarkable — well within the normal daily static of a sector index. What gave the session its shape was a single clinical-trial readout that rippled through both drugmakers, while corporate news out of Germany and Australia offered a reminder that healthcare's story is bigger than any one afternoon's tape.

The catalyst hiding in plain sight

The headline index move undersells what actually happened to individual names. Ionis Pharmaceuticals fell as much as 23% during the session — its steepest single-day drop in more than five years — after its Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform trial failed to hit its primary endpoint. The drug, eplontersen, was being tested in partnership with AstraZeneca for transthyretin-mediated amyloid cardiomyopathy, a stiffening of the heart muscle caused by misfolded protein deposits. Through Week 140, the drug — marketed with AstraZeneca as Wainua — did not produce a statistically significant reduction in the combined risk of cardiovascular death and recurrent cardiac events compared with placebo.

The reaction was swift: Ionis shed roughly $3.2 billion in market value, and Jefferies cut its price target on the stock. AstraZeneca shares also came under pressure, though less severely, as investors reassessed the commercial trajectory of a drug that had been positioned as a genuine rival to Pfizer's and BridgeBio's cardiomyopathy franchises. With Wainua now the biggest single stock story in the sector this week, it is doing most of the work behind the index's modest afternoon slide — a reminder that a flat-looking headline number can conceal a much sharper move underneath.

Siemens Healthineers weighs a diagnostics exit

Away from the trial data, Siemens Healthineers confirmed it is in preliminary talks with private equity firms — reportedly including Blackstone, CVC Capital Partners and KKR — over a potential sale of its diagnostics division valued at roughly €6 billion ($7 billion) or more. The company cautioned that talks are ongoing and may not lead to a deal, but the mere disclosure fits a broader pattern among diversified med-tech names of shedding lower-margin diagnostics businesses to concentrate capital on imaging and AI-driven platforms. Siemens Healthineers separately won FDA clearance this week for its Magnetom Flow MRI platform, a helium-efficient 1.5-tesla scanner — a smaller but tangible sign that the imaging side of the business, the one it may be choosing to keep, is still adding product. The company reports third-quarter results on July 31.

ResMed's quieter repositioning

ResMed offered the sector's calmer counter-note. The sleep-apnea and connected-care device maker has been trimming its portfolio around a 2030 strategy centered on sleep, breathing and home healthcare, including the divestiture of its MatrixCare software unit — a business that carried about $220 million in revenue and $55 million in adjusted operating profit. Goldman Sachs added ResMed to its APAC Conviction List on July 1, and Wall Street's median price target on the stock sits near $274, with analysts split mostly between buy and hold. ResMed reports fiscal fourth-quarter results on August 6.

The through-line

What ties the day together is not the index's modest dip but the divergence underneath it: a cardiology bet that just got materially riskier for AstraZeneca and its partner, set against two other major names — Siemens Healthineers and ResMed — actively reshaping their portfolios rather than reacting to a single data readout. The next real test for the sector isn't Thursday's flat tape; it's whether AstraZeneca's cardiovascular pipeline can absorb the Wainua setback, and whether Siemens Healthineers' diagnostics talks turn into a signed deal before its July 31 earnings call.
