Putin praises Musk as a 'real genius' after satellite talks

In one sentence
Putin called Musk a 'real genius' and said they had discussed satellites, but no details have emerged on whether Starlink, which Ukraine relies on, was involved.

Vladimir Putin has publicly praised Elon Musk as a "real genius" and said he has spoken with the SpaceX and Tesla chief about satellites, according to BBC Technology. The remark is flattering. It also touches the most sensitive piece of commercial technology in the war in Ukraine.

The reported facts are thin. Putin did not, in the account available, say what was discussed. It is also unclear whether the exchange was recent, whether it led to anything, or whether "satellites" meant Starlink, launch services or something else. The Kremlin's public account is the only one so far, and Putin has reasons to present a conversation with Musk as warmer or more substantive than it was.

Why satellites matter here

Starlink has been a working part of Ukraine's war effort since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022. Ukrainian forces and civilians have used the service for communications, and it has been used in military operations. A satellite link that Moscow cannot easily switch off is a real asset, and Russia has no equivalent it can point to.

That is why any hint of Russian interest in Starlink draws attention. Access would erode Ukraine's advantage in battlefield communications. It would also raise sanctions and export-control questions for SpaceX, a US company, if terminals or service reached Russian state or military users.

A familiar pattern

This is not the first time Putin has praised Musk. In September 2023, at the Eastern Economic Forum in Vladivostok, he called Musk an "outstanding person" and a talented businessman. That came days after a controversy over Starlink coverage near Crimea. In April 2025 he compared Musk to Sergei Korolev, the Soviet rocket designer, and spoke of Musk's Mars ambitions.

The pattern says something about how Moscow treats Musk. The praise is consistent, personal and aimed at his space work. Whether it has ever produced anything beyond warm words is a separate matter, and the evidence is mixed. Reporting in 2023 said Musk told Pentagon officials he had spoken with Putin. Musk has said he did not discuss Ukraine with him. Separately, there have been reports that Russian forces obtained Starlink terminals through intermediaries. Those reports do not show that SpaceX supplied them, and SpaceX has said it does not do business with Russia.

What to watch

Three things would turn this from a diplomatic signal into a policy story. The first is a Kremlin or SpaceX statement on what was actually discussed. The second is any sign that the US government, which has a major stake in Starlink through defence contracts, treats the remarks as a compliance matter. The third is Ukrainian officials, who have been sensitive to Musk's influence over a system they rely on, and who may comment publicly.

Until one of those arrives, the story is a flattering remark with an unexplained conversation behind it. That is still worth recording, given what depends on Starlink.
