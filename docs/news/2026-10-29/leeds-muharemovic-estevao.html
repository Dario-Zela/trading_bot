Leeds close in on Muharemovic as Chelsea's Estevao becomes the forgotten man

In one sentence
Leeds are reported close to signing Muharemovic and Chelsea's Estevao is called the 'forgotten man', but there is no confirmed deal, fee or club comment on either.

Two Premier League squad stories are doing the rounds on the BBC Sport football pages: Leeds United are reported to be close to signing a player named Muharemovic, and Chelsea's Estevao is being described as the club's "forgotten man". Neither story involves a confirmed deal, a fee or a club statement. This is rumour territory, and it is labelled that way here.

Leeds and Muharemovic

The BBC Sport headline says Leeds are "close" to a deal for Muharemovic. That is all the available reporting establishes. The first name, the selling club, the fee and the contract length are not in the material seen for this piece, and none of them can responsibly be filled in from memory.

"Close" is a flexible word in transfer coverage. It can mean a medical is booked, or it can mean two clubs have exchanged a polite email. Until Leeds announce something, or a fee and agreed terms are reported, the accurate position is that interest is advanced and a signing is possible, not done.

It is also worth noting what has not appeared: no quotes from Leeds, from the player or from his representatives. A signing that is truly imminent usually produces at least a flurry of "here we go" confirmations from the usual reliable sources. Their absence from this account does not mean the deal is off, but it does mean the story rests on a single headline.

Estevao, out of view

The Chelsea item is different in kind. It is less a transfer story than a status story. Estevao, the club's young winger, is described as the "forgotten man", with questions over what his role at Stamford Bridge actually is.

That phrase is the BBC's framing, and it carries its own caveat. "Forgotten" is a judgement about playing time and prominence, not a statement from Chelsea about his future. No manager's comment, minutes tally or club line has been put forward here to back it up, so the reasons behind it remain open. Selection, form, fitness and squad depth can all push a young attacker down the pecking order, and a headline cannot tell them apart.

What can fairly be said is that a young winger being left out of the picture is a story squads of Chelsea's size produce every season. It becomes a larger one only if it leads somewhere: a loan, a change of role, or an open statement from the manager about where he fits.

What would change the picture

For Leeds, the trigger is simple: an official announcement, or credible reporting of an agreed fee and personal terms. Either would turn a modest squad story into a signing worth a proper write-up, including what the player offers and where he would slot in.

For Chelsea, the useful development would be a substantive report on Estevao himself. That means the manager explaining his selection choices, or word of a loan or other move. Until then, the label "forgotten man" describes a mood more than a fact.

One caution applies to both stories. Supporters of either club will be tempted to read a great deal into two headlines. The evidence so far supports very little: one deal that may be near, and one player whose standing is being questioned.
