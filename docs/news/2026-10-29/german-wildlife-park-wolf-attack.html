Girl seriously hurt in wolf attack at German wildlife park

In one sentence
An eight-year-old girl was seriously hurt by a wolf in an enclosure at Brandenburg's Wildpark Johannismühle during a children's keeper day; the park is closed pending review.

An eight-year-old girl was seriously injured by a wolf on Wednesday at a private wildlife park south of Berlin. She was flown to hospital by rescue helicopter. The park is shut while the district reviews how it operates.

The attack happened on 28 October at the Wildpark Johannismühle, near Baruth/Mark in Brandenburg, about 45 km from the capital. The girl was one of three taking part in a booked children's animal-keeper day, held before the park opened to the public. With a female keeper, they were in the polar wolf enclosure while the animals were being given water, according to the Teltow-Fläming district. At least one wolf attacked the girl, and police later confirmed it was a wolf.

The keeper reportedly stepped in quickly and stopped the animals reaching the other two girls. Those present, other than the injured child, were reported physically unharmed. Emergency counsellors looked after four people, three of them children.

What the review is asking

The central question is how three children came to be inside a predator enclosure at all. Police are investigating that, and whether negligence played a part. The district says it will examine the park's safety measures and operating procedures. The park stays closed until the responsible authority approves reopening, and the veterinary office will carry out an animal-welfare inspection.

The paper trail so far is clean. The county's last on-site inspection, on 23 July 2025, found no safety-relevant defects in fences or enclosures. The park says its security fences are checked every morning before opening, and its operating permit requires daily checks of predator and bison enclosures for weak points. That points away from a broken fence as the cause. The focus is on the arrangement itself: a guided programme that put children in with the animals.

No expert assessment of the wolves' health or behaviour exists yet. The girl's condition has not been detailed publicly, and the park has said only that it regrets the incident, is cooperating fully and is supporting the family. It has declined further comment while proceedings continue.

A local story, for now

The park keeps grey and polar wolves, along with deer, roe deer, a brown bear and a lynx. Wolf bites on people are rare in Germany. A boy of eight was bitten on the chest at the Filmtierpark Eschede in Lower Saxony in 2023, after a wolf sniffed his hand and then snapped. That episode, like this one, involved a captive animal and a close encounter arranged for visitors.

On current evidence this remains a single-site incident. It would become a wider story only if the review finds that supervised, hands-on sessions with predators are run on thin rules elsewhere, or that the permit conditions leave gaps. Whether any of that applies is not established.

What to watch: the police finding on how the children entered the enclosure, the district's decision on reopening, and whether the veterinary inspection leads to changes in how parks offer keeper-style experiences near wolves.
