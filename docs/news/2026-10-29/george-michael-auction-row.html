Family row over George Michael items, including the Faith guitar, at auction

In one sentence
A family dispute over George Michael memorabilia has surfaced as Sworders sells the Faith video guitar today, but the parties and legal grounds are not yet public.

A row inside George Michael's family has surfaced on the day a collection of his memorabilia, headed by the guitar from the video for "Faith", goes under the hammer. BBC News reports a dispute over the sale. At the time of writing, the public record does not say who is objecting, on what grounds, or whether the auction will go ahead as scheduled.

The sale

The items are being offered by Sworders, the Essex auction house, in a sale titled Faith & Fame: A Personal George Michael Collection, scheduled for 29 October 2026. The centrepiece is a 1940s Gretsch Synchromatic 160, the guitar the singer carried in the 1987 video. The house has put its estimate at £60,000 to £120,000.

The lots did not come from the singer's estate. According to coverage of the sale, the archive was assembled by Siobhan Bailey, Michael's personal assistant for some two decades, who died in 2014. It is her widower who is selling. The guitar's story is part of the appeal: Guitar World's account of the instrument says it spent years in a garage with other memorabilia after the Faith tour.

What is and isn't known

The BBC's report is the only one found that describes a family dispute, and its details could not be independently confirmed. Nothing in the sale material reviewed names a claimant or a legal challenge.

Sworders' own line on the guitar has been that its provenance is secure. Its chairman, Guy Schooling, was quoted in pre-sale coverage as saying the family agreed this was the original video guitar, so its authenticity "won't ever be contested". That concerns authenticity, not ownership or the right to sell. A disagreement about who is entitled to dispose of the objects, or about how they left Michael's possession, would be a separate matter, and the available material does not address it.

What to watch

The practical questions are narrow. Auction houses facing a claim to title typically withdraw a lot or hold the proceeds until the matter is resolved; a sale that proceeds with the guitar intact would suggest Sworders regards the challenge as weak or aimed elsewhere. Any statement from the auctioneer, the Bailey family or Michael's relatives on the day would settle which it is. The hammer price will also say something: the guitar's estimate is £60,000 to £120,000, and a figure well below that would suggest buyers were wary of a contested lot.

For now, the sale is listed for today, and the dispute is a headline without a case file behind it.
