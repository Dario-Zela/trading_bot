Sandpapergate probe to include 2017-18 Ashes; Bournemouth fans book final hotels

In one sentence
A reported widening of the sandpaper review to the 2017-18 Ashes is unconfirmed, while Bournemouth fans are booking Europa League final hotels before a final-stage tie exists.

A review of the 2018 ball-tampering scandal is reported to reach back further than the episode that gave it its name. The claim, attributed to BBC Sport, is that the inquiry would take in the 2017-18 Ashes. The geography makes that odd. The tampering happened in South Africa, and that series was played in Australia. The scope has not been confirmed independently, and this piece treats it as unconfirmed.

What is established

The core facts are settled. On 24 March 2018, during the third Test against South Africa at Newlands in Cape Town, Cameron Bancroft was caught using sandpaper to alter the condition of the ball. Cricket Australia banned Steve Smith and David Warner for a year each. Bancroft received nine months.

The 2017-18 Ashes sits outside that episode. It was played in Australia between November 2017 and January 2018, and Australia won it 4-0. It ended some two months before the Newlands Test, in a different country and against different opponents. Nothing in the public record from the original sanctions tied the Ashes to the tampering.

Why the scope matters

If the review really covers that series, it would be the first to reach a series the original scandal did not involve. That would turn a review of one incident into a wider look at how the team operated, and so at how Cricket Australia governed it. Such a step would need a stated reason: new evidence, a changed brief, or a decision that the culture inquiry was never finished.

None of those has been supplied. The report rests on a single source, and no terms of reference have been published. Several loose ends remain:

  • Whether the review has actually been extended, or the reporting loosely describes a look at the team's earlier culture.
  • Whether any new material prompted the change.
  • Why the framing refers to a "current" Ashes. The most recent series in Australia concluded in January, and no Ashes is under way now, so any ramifications would be for a later series rather than one in progress.

Until Cricket Australia or the reviewers set out the remit, the sensible reading is that the widening is reported rather than confirmed.

Meanwhile, in Bournemouth

A lighter item, from the same BBC Sport source. Bournemouth supporters are said to be booking hotels for the Europa League final, with the Cherries' European run leaving fans dreaming of the final. The club has no final-stage tie yet, so this is a story about hope rather than a fixture. Hotel bookings made this far ahead are a bet on a draw that has not happened, and refundable rates are doing a lot of work.

There is nothing wrong with the optimism. Early booking is a time-honoured supporter ritual, and it carries a cost only if the rooms are non-refundable. It is also not news in the sense that a probe's scope is news, and it should be read as colour.

What to watch

The thing to watch is a published remit. If Cricket Australia confirms that the review extends to the 2017-18 Ashes, the governance question becomes live. If it does not, the report will have been an overreach. For Bournemouth, the next real signal is the tie that puts them in the final stages, which has not yet arrived.
