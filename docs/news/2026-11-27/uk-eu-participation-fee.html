UK to pay a 'participation' fee to deliver EU deals

In one sentence
The UK will pay to take part in EU programmes agreed in the reset, with Erasmus+ costing about £570m in 2027 and later years' fees still to be negotiated.

The Brexit "reset" is turning from a diplomatic mood into a budget line. Since the London summit of 19 May 2025, ministers have talked about rejoining parts of the European Union's programme architecture. Being let back in has a cost, which falls on the Treasury and recurs each year.

The clearest example is Erasmus+, the student and youth exchange scheme the UK left at the end of 2020. A fee for taking part in programmes agreed in the reset is now in view. The detail of the latest fee, including its size and start date, could not be checked against an official text for this article, so the figures below come from the Erasmus+ agreement already on the record.

The price of the flagship

The government says the UK and the EU signed the legal text in Brussels to bring the UK back into Erasmus+ in 2027. The UK contribution for 2027 is put at about £570m, roughly €650m, and covers the 2027/28 academic year. Ministers say they won a 30% discount on the default contribution rate. The Times reported that London had pushed for 50%.

The government's case rests on reach. More than 100,000 people are expected to benefit in the first year. Skills Minister Jacqui Smith said Erasmus+ "offers transformative opportunities to enhance young people's life chances". EU Relations Minister Nick Thomas-Symonds called it "what a positive UK-EU relationship looks like in practice".

The scheme is also reciprocal. Britain's replacement, the Turing scheme, sent UK students abroad but did not bring EU students here. Erasmus+ does both. The British Council, which oversaw more than 8,000 projects and about €1.1bn in awards to UK organisations between 2014 and 2020, is due to become the UK national agency. That is subject to confirmation by the European Commission.

A fee that is not fixed

The bill beyond 2027/28 is open. Government material says participation in the next EU budget period "will need to be agreed in the future" on a "fair and balanced contribution". The Telegraph has reported that if Brussels raises Erasmus+ funding by more than 50% in 2028, the UK's annual fee could reach £1.25bn. That is a press estimate and not an official figure, but it shows how far the cost could move.

The pattern is familiar. When the UK joined Horizon Europe and Copernicus under a bespoke deal, the cost was set through payments and fee mechanisms agreed after the headline announcement. Older Commission papers cite a 4% fee on operational contributions. The December 2024 UK-EU committee minutes record a 2% fee in 2024, within payments of about €2.48bn. The sources do not reconcile those two figures. Outside Horizon Europe and Copernicus, the UK is otherwise treated as a non-associated third country, and access to programmes such as Creative Europe has to be negotiated one by one.

Value for money

The judgement on value for money will depend on who is asked. For universities, researchers and students, access to funding and exchange places the UK lost in 2020 is the benefit. For the Treasury, each programme adds a recurring commitment, set by EU budget decisions that British ministers do not control. Opposition Conservatives have said the reset "looks less like pragmatism and more like pro-EU ideology".

The discount gives ministers a talking point, but it is a discount on a rate the EU sets. Erasmus+ will test how the UK negotiates later fees, because the 2027 terms cover a single year.

The next decisions come with the EU's negotiations on its post-2027 budget, where programme sizes and contribution rates will be set. The Commission's confirmation of the British Council as the UK's national agency is also due. Those steps will show whether the 2027 fee holds, or becomes a precedent for a much larger annual payment.
