Siemens Energy jumps 3% on Venezuela power deals; Qantas set to favour Boeing

In one sentence
Siemens Energy rose about 3% after signing PDVSA and grid-repair deals in Venezuela, but no values were disclosed; Qantas reportedly leans to Boeing in an unconfirmed report.

Siemens Energy shares rose about 3% on Thursday, a day after the German group signed two agreements in Caracas. The deals put a Western equipment maker back inside Venezuela's power sector, but no value was disclosed, so the market is reacting to a signature rather than a sum.

What was signed

Chief executive Christian Bruch signed both agreements on Wednesday at a ceremony led by Acting President Delcy Rodríguez, according to Investing.com. One, with the state oil company PDVSA, covers power supply for oil operations. The other, with the electric energy minister, covers maintenance and repair of the national grid.

No financial terms were given, and the report offers no delivery timetable beyond Rodríguez's statement that work would begin immediately. Intraday quotes also diverged. Investing.com's own ticker showed a gain above 4% when the page was captured, and some other outlets reported a rise closer to 5%. The 3% figure is the one in the headline.

Why the sanctions backdrop matters

Rodríguez credited eased sanctions. She said restrictions had blocked access to parts and equipment for more than a decade, that much of Venezuela's thermoelectric fleet runs on Siemens equipment, and that the effort began two years ago. Siemens' history there is long. It has supplied turbines for PDVSA-linked plants, and in 2022 Bloomberg reported it had won US licences to work with Venezuelan state entities. Those licences were narrow.

The deals also follow a 10 November reform of Venezuela's electricity law, which lets private firms generate power under concessions of up to 25 years. That opens a route for outside capital, beyond a repair contract with the state.

Real reopening or small order?

The evidence so far supports a cautious reading. The case for a real reopening is the legal change, the sanctions easing and the scale of installed Siemens kit that needs servicing. The case for caution is that framework agreements and maintenance work are typically smaller and slower than new-build turbine orders. Payment is the other unknown. A separate report said that other suppliers approached about grid repairs, including GE Vernova and Mitsubishi Power, were unclear how they would be paid. A 3% move on a group of this size reflects some pricing of option value, not a booked order book.

Qantas, briefly

Elsewhere in industrials, Bloomberg reports that Qantas is leaning toward Boeing for most of a planned 20-jet order, with Airbus and Embraer also competing for the business. That is a single report and Qantas has confirmed nothing. Earlier reporting in June described the airline weighing about 20 wide-bodies, Boeing 787s or Airbus A350s, so the mix and the competitors in the latest account should be treated as unconfirmed. A split favouring Boeing would be a modest win for the US maker rather than a decisive one.

What to watch

The test for Siemens Energy is whether contract values or a work schedule emerge, most likely at its next quarterly results or in a company statement. Further US licensing decisions on Venezuela, and whether payment mechanisms are disclosed, will show whether the market was pricing a durable reopening or a single signing ceremony. For Qantas, the marker is a formal order announcement.
