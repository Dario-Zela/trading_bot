Flydubai co-pilot attacked captain with an axe, UAE official says

In one sentence
The UAE says a Flydubai co-pilot attacked the captain with a crash axe and tried to seize the plane; the flight landed safely, and investigators are examining his history and motive.

A co-pilot on a Flydubai passenger jet attacked the captain with the cockpit's crash axe and tried to take control of the aircraft, the United Arab Emirates said on Saturday. The UAE's attorney-general called it an attempted terrorist act. The plane landed safely, and the suspect was overpowered by crew and passengers.

The account that has emerged from officials and media reports leaves a question for regulators. A man whom one authority had reportedly flagged as a security risk was working in the flight deck of another country's airline.

What happened

Flight FZ1073 left Dubai for Tel Aviv on Wednesday morning carrying 174 people, most of them Israelis, according to NBC News. Al Jazeera reports that it climbed to 34,000 feet. About two hours into the flight the co-pilot struck.

"The co-pilot began executing his plan during the flight, attacking the captain inside the flight deck using a crash axe and attempting to take control of the aircraft," the UAE attorney-general, Hamad Saif Al Shamsi, said. Al Jazeera reports the aircraft dropped more than 17,000 feet in about two minutes.

The captain, Smit Machchhar, an Indian national, was badly hurt. He opened the cockpit door, which let crew and passengers in to subdue the attacker. "I knew I couldn't let all of them die," he later said, according to NBC. He was treated in hospital in Abu Dhabi, with his arm in a cast and his head bandaged.

Reserve pilots then landed the aircraft at Tabuk, in Saudi Arabia, at about 9:45am. Reports differ slightly on the figures. CNBC and others cited 172 people on board, while NBC and Al Jazeera said 174.

The suspect

Official channels have not formally named him. Multiple outlets identify him as Hamam al-Hammami, an Omani citizen born in the UAE to a Syrian mother.

According to Al Jazeera, he worked for Oman Air for seven years until February 2025, then for Royal Air Maroc from June 2025 to January 2026, before joining Flydubai. NBC and ABC report that Oman had earlier barred him from flying over concerns that he had adopted radical views. Al Jazeera adds that he was removed from flight training over similar concerns before being reassigned.

These details come from anonymous officials and press reports rather than a published charge sheet. They should be treated as provisional until the UAE releases its own findings.

The security question

Cockpit security has been built since 2001 around keeping outsiders out. Reinforced doors and locking procedures assume the threat comes from the cabin. An attack from inside the flight deck is a different problem. The two pilots are trusted by design, and the crash axe is standard emergency equipment.

The case also shows how thin the information-sharing can be between employers and states. A pilot who is reported to have been restricted in one country moved on to carriers in two others. Whether Oman's concerns were ever passed on, and whether Flydubai's vetting could have found them, are among the questions investigators will face.

Abu Dhabi is leading the inquiry, with Israeli involvement, NBC reports. Investigators are trying to establish a motive and whether the co-pilot acted alone or with others. No motive has been officially announced.

What to watch

The first marker is whether the UAE publishes a formal charge and a motive. The second is whether the aviation authorities of the UAE and Oman, and the regulators overseeing Royal Air Maroc, release their account of what was known about him and when. Any move by regulators on psychological and background screening, or on access to emergency equipment in the cockpit, would follow from those answers.
