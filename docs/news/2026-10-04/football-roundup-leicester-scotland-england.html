Leicester win their first league game of the season as Scotland and England impress

In one sentence
Pocognoli won his first Scotland game at the third attempt, beating North Macedonia 2-0, while Leicester Tigers ended a seven-match winless run with a 24-19 win at Newcastle.

Scotland's new manager has his first win. It took three matches, and it was not the opening-night statement the early reports implied. Sebastien Pocognoli's side beat North Macedonia 2-0 in the Nations League on Saturday, after two draws to start the group. Elsewhere in the weekend's sport, Leicester Tigers won their first league game of the season, in rugby, not football.

Pocognoli gets there at the third attempt

Kieron Bowie scored his first international goal after seven minutes. John McGinn added a penalty in the 69th. Andy Robertson, winning his 100th cap, became only the second men's player to reach that mark for Scotland. Scott McKenna's late red card left Scotland with ten men at the end, though not with any real damage.

Pocognoli was careful with the praise: "I was really pleased with his performance, and from many of the lads." On the schedule he was more pointed. "We stay stable because we have a game in three days."

The caution is warranted. Scotland remain in Nations League Group B1, and one win following two draws establishes a direction at most. It says little about whether the changes of manager and approach have taken hold. A first victory against North Macedonia is a reasonable return, but it does not show the side can beat better opposition or hold a line against sustained pressure.

Leicester, but the other kind

Some of the weekend's agency summaries blurred two Leicester stories. The team that ended a winless run on Saturday was Leicester Tigers, who beat Newcastle Red Bulls 24-19 at Kingston Park in Premiership Rugby. It was their first win of the season after seven matches without one.

Samiuela Moli, Adam Radwan and Will Hurd scored Leicester's tries. Billy Searle kicked three conversions and a penalty. Radwan, a former Newcastle winger, scored on his return to his old ground. Newcastle replied with tries from Obi Ene, George McGuigan and Cameron Jordan and pressed late, but could not close the five-point gap.

Leicester head coach Geoff Parling called it "gritty and dogged", adding: "Defensively, some good resolve and they're going to break some teams this year." Newcastle's director of rugby, Dan McFarlane, said: "We were not accurate enough in key areas — but that will grow."

The win was narrow. After seven games without one, though, a narrow win counts for more than the margin suggests.

What comes next

Scotland's test comes quickly. They host Slovenia at Hampden Park on Tuesday 6 October (7.45pm), with a second win the first sign of a pattern. Leicester's next league fixture will show whether Saturday was the start of a recovery or a one-off.
