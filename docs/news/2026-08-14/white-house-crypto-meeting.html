White House to host crypto and prediction market executives ahead of CFTC meeting

The West Wing of the White House, where crypto and prediction-market executives are expected to meet officials on August 19. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Crypto and prediction-market executives meet at the White House August 19, a day before the CFTC's first Innovation Advisory Committee session sets a regulatory agenda.

The White House is set to host senior executives from the crypto and prediction-market industries on August 19, according to people familiar with the plans cited by Politico — a day before the Commodity Futures Trading Commission convenes the first meeting of its new Innovation Advisory Committee. Neither gathering has produced a policy document yet, but together they mark the clearest signal so far that Washington is about to start writing rules for a corner of finance that has, until now, largely made them up as it went along.

Details remain thin. The final guest list hasn't been confirmed, it isn't clear whether President Trump will attend in person, and White House officials have not commented publicly. What is confirmed: Polymarket chief executive Shayne Coplan has said he'll be there, after thanking Trump for the invitation on social media. Executives from traditional finance may also be in the room, according to the reporting, widening the meeting beyond crypto-native firms.

The CFTC's opening move

The following day, CFTC Chairman Michael Selig will chair a three-hour virtual session of the Innovation Advisory Committee, a 35-member panel that includes the chief executives of Polymarket, Kalshi, Coinbase, Robinhood, FanDuel and DraftKings. The agenda, running from 1 p.m. to 4 p.m. ET, covers three baskets: crypto market structure — state licensing regimes, the absence of a federal framework, cybersecurity and overlapping jurisdictions; prediction markets — federal-versus-state authority, recent litigation, exchange requirements and manipulation surveillance; and artificial intelligence in trading and compliance. Written public comment stays open through August 27.

The prediction-markets item is not academic. On August 11, Selig invoked emergency powers to order Kalshi to keep operating in New York after state attorney general Letitia James sued the exchange, arguing it needed a state gaming license and was dodging the taxes licensed sportsbooks pay. The CFTC has now filed suit or intervened against nine states — Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, Kentucky, Minnesota, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island and Wisconsin — over who gets to regulate event contracts. Selig's position is that Congress gave the agency exclusive authority over derivatives and that a state-by-state patchwork isn't compatible with markets that trade nationally.

What isn't settled

None of this tells traders what the actual rules will look like. The industry's preferred vehicle, the CLARITY Act, remains stuck in the Senate behind a bipartisan ethics package and disputes over stablecoin yield provisions; lawmakers aren't expected to take it up again until September. On Polymarket itself, contracts on whether Trump signs a crypto market-structure bill this year were trading around a 21% probability this week, up from 17% a day earlier — a reminder that the meeting's real value to markets is informational, not decisional.

The White House sit-down is a listening exercise ahead of a regulatory process that is still being designed in public. The CFTC's August 20 committee meeting, and whatever emerges from the September CLARITY Act debate, will do more to shape how prediction markets and crypto exchanges operate than a single roundtable — but the roundtable is where the industry gets its first real read on what Washington intends.
