Wave Life Sciences floated as $1bn RNA-medicine takeover target

Wave's PRISM platform builds on RNA structural chemistry to design both RNA-interference and RNA-editing therapies. — Thomas Shafee / Wikimedia Commons / CC BY 4.0
In one sentence
Wave Life Sciences, valued at roughly $1bn after a 69% share slide, is being flagged as a takeover target for its PRISM RNA-editing platform.

Wave Life Sciences has spent much of 2026 being punished by the market. The Cambridge, Massachusetts biotech is down roughly 69% from its 52-week high of $21.73, trading near $5.24 a share — a slide that has left its market capitalisation sitting almost exactly at $1 billion, according to 24/7 Wall St. That gap between share price and pipeline value is precisely what has commentators floating Wave as a takeover target for larger pharmaceutical companies racing to build RNA-editing capability.

The attraction is PRISM, Wave's oligonucleotide platform, which combines RNA interference chemistry (branded SpiNA) with RNA editing tools (AIMers) capable of correcting faulty genetic sequences rather than simply silencing them. Two programmes built on it are approaching decisive readouts. WVE-007, a GalNAc-conjugated siRNA targeting the INHBE gene, is Wave's entry into obesity: trial data has shown a 14.3% reduction in visceral fat and 2.4% muscle preservation, positioning it as a potential maintenance or combination therapy for patients cycling off GLP-1 drugs. WVE-006, an RNA-editing candidate for alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, has shown reductions of up to 71% in the toxic misfolded protein that causes the disease, and Wave is due to meet the FDA about an accelerated-approval pathway before the end of summer.

A partner that walked, then a field that's watching

The deal history complicates any tidy narrative. GSK licensed WVE-006 globally in a 2022 collaboration worth $170 million upfront and up to $3.3 billion in potential milestones across eight programmes — but in February handed full rights to the AATD drug back to Wave. Wave's leadership framed the reversal as a division of labour, with GSK's respiratory unit focused on large-population diseases rather than a rare disorder better suited to a smaller, faster-moving developer. Analysts have also pointed to competitive pressure from Beam Therapeutics, which has pushed its own accelerated pathway for a rival AATD programme, and to a strategic reset under GSK's newer leadership.

That leaves 24/7 Wall St. naming a wider set of plausible buyers: Eli Lilly, which could pair WVE-007 with Zepbound as a muscle-sparing maintenance option; Novo Nordisk, down 9.4% this year and short of a differentiated obesity asset; and Amgen, whose $225 billion balance sheet and existing oligonucleotide experience with MariTide could support combining the two mechanisms. Wall Street's own numbers back the optimism — a mean analyst price target of $19.06, more than triple the current share price, with a consensus Buy rating.

Wave says it has $490.6 million in cash, enough to fund operations into the third quarter of 2028, which gives it room to negotiate rather than sell under duress. The next hard data points are the FDA's accelerated-approval feedback on WVE-006, expected around the end of summer, and Phase 2 readouts from the INLIGHT obesity trial and the RestorAATion-2 AATD study, both due in the second half of 2026. Either could reset the valuation gap that has made Wave a name in takeover chatter — before any acquirer has to decide whether to act.
