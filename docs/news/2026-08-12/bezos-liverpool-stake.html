Bezos-led consortium closes in on $2bn stake in Liverpool FC

Anfield's Main Stand — Liverpool FC's home ground, at the centre of a deal that would value the club at $6bn. — Yurificacion / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
A Bezos- and Saverin-backed consortium led by Amit Bhatia is close to buying roughly a third of Liverpool FC, valuing the club at $6bn.

Fenway Sports Group is preparing to sell roughly a third of Liverpool Football Club to a consortium fronted by the financier Amit Bhatia, in a deal that would value the Premier League champions at around $6bn — one of the largest transactions in the history of professional sport.

The buying group includes Amazon founder Jeff Bezos and Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin, according to reporting from Sky Sports and Al Jazeera. Neither FSG nor the consortium has confirmed a final agreement, but both outlets cite sources describing a deal that could be announced within days. The stake under discussion is put at close to 30%, worth in the region of $2bn to the buyers.

"An investment consortium led, managed, and represented by Amit Bhatia has expressed interest in making a strategic minority investment in Liverpool Football Club." — FSG spokesperson, July 2026

Who's buying, and why FSG is selling a slice, not the club

Bhatia, 46, is a British-Indian investor who spent 17 years as a shareholder and, latterly, chairman of Queens Park Rangers before stepping down this month to lead the Liverpool approach. He is married to Vanisha Mittal Bhatia, daughter of steel billionaire Lakshmi Mittal, and the deal is understood to carry backing from the Mittal family alongside Bezos and Saverin's personal capital.

Crucially, this is not a takeover. FSG — which paid £300m for Liverpool in 2010 and has since added Champions League and Premier League titles to the trophy cabinet — would retain majority control and the boardroom, with John Henry remaining in charge. The structure mirrors FSG's prior minority sales to RedBird Capital, Arctos Sports Partners and, in 2023, Dynasty Equity, which put in £164m. What's new is the scale: at $6bn, the implied valuation is roughly 20 times what FSG paid sixteen years ago, and dwarfs the previous minority tranches.

The bigger pattern

For a Bezos or a Saverin, a nine-figure ticket into a football club sits alongside — not instead of — a public-markets portfolio, but it is part of a recognisable pattern: private capital rotating into trophy sports assets that throw off predictable, growing media and sponsorship revenue and are largely insulated from the swings of rate policy or equity multiples. English top-flight clubs in particular have drawn a wave of American money in the past five years, from the Glazers and Todd Boehly's Chelsea to Ineos at Manchester United, as broadcast rights and global fan monetisation have made the asset class look more like infrastructure than entertainment. A $6bn tag for Liverpool would place it among football's most valuable clubs, behind only Real Madrid and Manchester United on most industry estimates, and would set a new marker for what a passive, board-seat-free stake in a Premier League winner is worth.

What happens next is procedural but consequential: FSG is expected to confirm terms within the coming days, at which point the Premier League's owners' and directors' test will need to clear Bhatia's group before any funds change hands. Liverpool's American owners have given no indication of wanting to sell control, so the more interesting number for markets watchers may not be the $6bn valuation itself, but whether it holds up as a benchmark the next time a big six club changes hands.
