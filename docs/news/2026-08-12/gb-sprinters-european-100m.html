GB's Glave and Azu claim European 100m one-two in Birmingham

Jeremiah Azu racing for Great Britain at the 2022 European Championships in Munich; he took silver behind Romell Glave in Birmingham's 100m final. — Sandro Halank / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Romell Glave beat injured champion Marcell Jacobs to win European 100m gold in Birmingham, with Jeremiah Azu taking silver for a British one-two.

Britain had come to Birmingham hoping for one medal in the men's 100 metres. It left with two of the top three, a national title reclaimed, and a story about redemption that Romell Glave had been waiting two years to tell.

Glave won the European 100m final in 10.09 seconds on Tuesday night, with fellow Briton Jeremiah Azu taking silver in 10.16. Germany's Owen Ansah completed the podium in 10.19. It was Britain's third British one-two in the event's history, following Darren Campbell's win over Dwain Chambers in 1998 and Zharnel Hughes's over Reece Prescod in 2018 — and it came at the expense of the defending champion, Italy's Marcell Jacobs, who pulled up injured halfway down the track and limped home in 12.26 seconds.

From bronze to gold

Two years ago in Rome, Glave stood on the same podium a step lower, watching Jacobs take European gold. "It's the redemption from the European Championships where I finished behind Jacobs," the 26-year-old said afterwards, framing Tuesday's win as unfinished business rather than a surprise. "Now is my time to dominate."

The Jamaica-born sprinter's route to a European title has not been a straight line. Once billed as the world's fastest 17-year-old after clocking 10.21 seconds in 2017, Glave spent much of his early twenties battling injuries before breaking the ten-second barrier for the first time in June this year. His personal best now stands at 9.97 seconds, set in London earlier this season.

Azu's silver was its own vindication. The Cardiff-raised sprinter, born in Rotterdam to a Ghanaian family, arrived in Birmingham as reigning world and European indoor 60m champion but without a global outdoor 100m medal to his name. That gap closed on Tuesday, adding to the Olympic bronze he won as part of Britain's 4x100m relay squad in Paris two years ago.

A short race, a long story

The drama, inevitably, attached itself to Jacobs. The Italian had run the fastest time of the semi-finals at 10.08 seconds, arriving in the final as the man to beat before pulling up with what appeared to be a muscular injury in the race's opening third. His limp finish left the field to sort itself out among the rest, and Britain's strength in depth did the rest — Glave and Azu were two of three Britons in the field.

For a domestic athletics correspondent, a British one-two on home soil is as good as this beat gets: a strong squad, a settling of old scores, and a data point — 10.09 and 10.16 — that suggests genuine strength heading into next year's global season rather than a one-off. There is no broader read-through here; sprinting results of this kind move television audiences and column inches, not markets.

Attention now turns to the World Athletics Championships next year, where Glave and Azu will hope to convert continental form into a first global outdoor medal apiece — assuming Jacobs, too, returns fit enough to reclaim his spot at the sharp end of the field.
