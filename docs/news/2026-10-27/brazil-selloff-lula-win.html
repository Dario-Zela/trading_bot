Brazil assets sell off after Lula's fourth-term win

In one sentence
Lula's runoff win over Flávio Bolsonaro, 51.79% to 48.21%, triggered a 13.88% Ibovespa fall and a real slump; the 21% Brazil ETF figure remains unconfirmed.

Brazilian assets were sold hard on Monday after Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term as president, reversing a first-round deficit that had led investors to position for a victory by his challenger, Flávio Bolsonaro. The Ibovespa closed down 13.88%, the real lost as much as 12% intraday, and trading in São Paulo was briefly halted by a circuit breaker.

The result

Lula took 51.79% of valid votes to Bolsonaro's 48.21% in Sunday's runoff, according to the electoral court (TSE). That is a margin of roughly 4.4 million votes. Three weeks earlier Bolsonaro had led the first round, 47.03% to 45.16%, and markets had rallied on it: the Ibovespa rose more than 8% that day, the real gained over 4%, and the iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ) jumped by double digits in the US session.

Monday's move was largely the unwinding of that trade. Bloomberg reported that Brazilian assets had been heavily positioned for a Bolsonaro win. Bolsonaro has not conceded and is expected to ask the electoral courts to annul the runoff, which adds a contested-result premium to the repricing.

The numbers

The cash index fell as low as 169,516 points, 17.34% below the previous close, before B3 halted trading. It recovered somewhat to close at 176,608, down 13.88%, which outlets describe as its worst day since March 2020. The dollar reached about R$5.60 intraday, roughly 12% above the prior close, and settled near R$5.40, up about 8%. JPMorgan moved its view on the real and on government bonds to neutral.

The headline figure in the triage notes, a Brazil ETF down about 21%, could not be confirmed. Reports reviewed for this article put the intraday index low at about 17% and the close at 13.9%; sources differ on the exact figures. A 21% fall in EWZ would exceed both. It may reflect a multi-session move that includes US-hours trading and the dollar's fall against the real, or it may be an error. Until the period and the base are sourced, it is best treated as unverified.

Contagion, or a local reset

Whether the selloff spreads to emerging markets more broadly is not yet visible in the data gathered here. The evidence so far points to a local, positioning-driven event. The rally after the first round was reversed within three weeks, and the surprise was in the outcome, not in anything new about Lula's policy. In 2022, EWZ fell about 5.7% in early trading after his first win, then recovered within days.

The case for wider effects rests on funds that hold Brazil as a large benchmark weight and may need to cut risk elsewhere, plus any knock-on to BRL carry trades. Neither has shown up in confirmed figures.

What to watch

Three things will settle the question: whether Bolsonaro's legal challenge gains traction at the TSE; whether the real and the Ibovespa stabilise in the next few sessions or the circuit-breaker pattern repeats; and the first signals on fiscal policy from Lula's economic team, which will determine how much of Monday's move is lasting repricing and how much is forced selling.
