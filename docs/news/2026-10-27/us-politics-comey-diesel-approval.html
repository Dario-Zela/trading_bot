US politics: Comey faces fresh charges, Democrats eye diesel deal, Trump approval at record low

In one sentence
Unverified reports say Comey faces new federal charges, Democrats are preparing a diesel-deal probe and Trump's approval has hit a record low; none is yet confirmed by primary documents.

A former FBI director has reportedly been charged by federal prosecutors again, according to a BBC World report. If the account holds, it would extend the legal conflict between the Trump administration and its critics. That is the first of three US political threads that surfaced on Tuesday, each resting on thin sourcing so far.

Comey, again

The BBC report says James Comey, who led the FBI before leaving the post, has been charged by federal prosecutors. Some accounts describe this as the third time he has faced federal charges. The Tribune has not seen a charging document, and the report as relayed gives no counts, no court, no filing date and no statement from either side. The "third time" tally has not been checked against court records.

Those gaps matter. A prosecution of a prominent administration critic would raise questions about how such cases are brought and who decides to bring them. Whether this is a fresh indictment, a re-filing of earlier allegations or something else cannot be told from what is available. Until a charging document or a second outlet confirms the basics, the report should be read as unconfirmed.

A diesel deal under scrutiny

Separately, Axios, as relayed by Benzinga, reports that Democrats are laying groundwork to investigate a diesel arrangement involving the Trump administration. The relayed summary names no counterparties, contract values, agencies or dates. It is not clear which Democrats are involved, or whether they hold any committee gavels that would give them subpoena power. Without the original Axios text, the nature of the deal and the substance of the complaint remain unknown. Declared intentions to investigate, from a party out of power, are also a weaker signal than formal requests or hearings.

A weak poll reading

Benzinga also headlines a record-low approval rating for President Trump, alongside a finding that 75% of respondents rate the economy "not so good" or "poor". The candidate material gives no approval figure, pollster, sample size or field dates. Those details decide whether a "record low" is a statistical shift or a movement within the margin of error, and whether the economic figure comes from the same survey or a different one. Both numbers are attributed to a single aggregator and have not been checked against the original poll.

What ties it together

Taken together, the three items sketch a familiar picture: a president under pressure over the economy, opponents preparing oversight, and a legal fight with a high-profile critic. But the threads rest on a headline apiece. What would settle them is concrete: a court docket for Comey, the Axios report and any letters or hearing notices from Democrats on the diesel arrangement, and the poll's own methodology. Until those appear, the claims stand as reported rather than established.
