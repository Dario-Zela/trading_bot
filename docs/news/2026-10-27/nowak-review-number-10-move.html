Home Secretary orders review into Henry Nowak killing; Downing Street move discussed

In one sentence
The Home Secretary has ordered a review linked to the killing of Henry Nowak, and whether it becomes a wider public-safety story depends on whether its terms reach policing, probation or sentencing.

The Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, has ordered a review connected to the killing of the student Henry Nowak. The exact terms of that review could not be confirmed from sources available at the time of writing. Whether it stays a moderate Whitehall story or becomes a national public-safety one depends on what it examines.

What is established

Nowak was stabbed in Southampton on 3 December 2025. Vickrum Digwa was convicted of his murder in May 2026 and sentenced to life imprisonment the following month, with a minimum term of 21 years. The Attorney General's office has been considering whether that sentence should be referred for review.

The case has already carried considerable political weight. Mahmood told the Commons in early June that the killing was "an act of pure evil". She also said politicians should not "seek personal profit" from it. She condemned violent, racially charged protests that followed the case as "completely unacceptable". She said an officer with no connection to the case had been wrongly identified online, received death threats and been forced to relocate with his family.

An existing inquiry also bears on the new review. The Independent Office for Police Conduct is investigating the response of Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary, examining body-worn video and trial material. Mahmood said at the time that it would report within three months.

What the review has to cover

The new review matters only for what it is asked to look at. Three kinds of terms would point to different stories:

  • Policing. If it covers how forces respond to threats and reports of violence, it overlaps with the IOPC work and reads as a continuation of it.
  • Probation, release and offender management. Terms in this area would suggest that someone in the system was thought to have missed a warning, which is the usual trigger for a wider public-safety argument.
  • Sentencing. A look at tariffs would be narrower, and it would sit alongside the Attorney General's consideration of Digwa's term.

A review confined to the handling of the case would leave the story where it is. One that asks whether the system's design allowed the outcome would put pressure on the Home Office as a department. The terms of reference, the named reviewer and the reporting deadline are the three details that will show which it is.

The Downing Street reports

Separate reports, attributed to BBC Politics, say the government discussed moving out of 10 Downing Street because of the condition of the building and the wider Whitehall estate. That is a report of discussion, not a decision. No costs, dates or named officials have been published, and searches found no confirming source. Commentary this year, such as a LabourList opinion piece, has argued that the building needs a full restoration and that a temporary move would be sensible. That is advocacy, not evidence of an official plan. Starmer has since stood down as prime minister, so any such talk would now belong to his successor's government. The two stories share a Whitehall setting but little else.

What to watch

The first marker is the publication of the review's terms of reference and its reporting date. The second is the IOPC's findings on the Hampshire force, which on Mahmood's timetable were due around the end of the summer and may still be pending. If the review is explicitly tied to those findings, it is likely to be treated as a systemic inquiry.
