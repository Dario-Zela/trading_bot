Dallas Fed factory gauge rises to 13.4; FHFA's Pulte promises housing moves

In one sentence
A Texas factory gauge reportedly rose to 13.4 from 9.8, but one regional survey is thin evidence for a national recovery, and FHFA's promised housing action has no stated content.

A regional factory survey from Texas has ticked up, and a federal housing regulator has promised news. Neither amounts to much yet.

The Dallas Fed's Texas manufacturing index rose to 13.4 in October from 9.8 in September, according to Benzinga, which is the sole source for the figure. The Dallas Fed's own October release could not be located at the time of writing, so the number has not been checked against the primary data. Benzinga describes the reading as better than expected, but it does not give a consensus forecast. The size of the beat cannot be measured.

The Dallas Fed publishes several measures from the same monthly survey, including production, new orders, shipments and a headline general business activity index. Which of them the 13.4 refers to is not stated in the material available. A positive reading in these surveys means more respondents reported improvement than deterioration. It is a diffusion index, not a growth rate.

A regional read, not a national one

The survey covers Texas manufacturers only, and roughly 100 firms usually respond. In September, the most recent release found, 63 of 113 surveyed firms replied. That is a thin sample. Month-to-month swings of several points are routine and can reverse the following month.

The Dallas Fed's own September report pointed the same way as this month's headline. It put the production index at 29.5, up 13 points, with shipments and capacity utilisation also higher. A second consecutive firm month would be more persuasive than one. Still, one state's survey does not establish a national recovery.

The national picture rests on other series. The ISM manufacturing index and the Federal Reserve's industrial production data cover the whole country, and the other regional Fed surveys (New York, Philadelphia, Richmond, Kansas City) offer cross-checks. Until those align with Texas, the Dallas number is a small positive and nothing more. It does not alter the policy outlook on its own.

Pulte promises housing moves

Separately, Benzinga reports that Bill Pulte, director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, signalled further action on housing-market competition "this week". No policy, rule or counterparty was named in the source material, and a search turned up no corroborating announcement.

Pulte's record gives some hint of the territory. In 2025 the FHFA cleared Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to accept VantageScore 4.0 credit scores as an alternative to the longstanding Classic FICO model, a change aimed at loosening a single vendor's hold on mortgage underwriting. Whether the coming move relates to credit scoring, title or appraisal costs, or something else is not known. Speculation would be premature.

What to watch

The next checkpoints are concrete. An FHFA announcement is promised before the week is out, and its substance will determine whether this is a story at all. On the data side, the national ISM manufacturing index for October is due in early November. If it moves in the same direction as the Dallas gauge, the Texas reading will look like the start of a turn. If not, it will look like noise.
