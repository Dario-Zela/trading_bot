Rough-sleeper funding doubled; landowners may dilute free Land Registry plans

In one sentence
The government is adding £432m to its £442m rough-sleeping drive, for £874m in all, but staffing and available rooms, not money, will decide whether it can deliver by Christmas.

The government is doubling the money behind its drive to get people sleeping rough in England into accommodation by Christmas. Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced on Monday that a further £432m will be confirmed in Wednesday's Budget. That takes the total to £874m, so "doubled" is slightly generous: the new money is a little less than the base it is added to.

The numbers behind "double"

The base is the £442m package announced in August. That package combined £340m from funding set out in July with £102m of uncommitted housing-ministry budgets. It was meant to pay for emergency accommodation, outreach teams and practical support ahead of winter, and to deliver more than 1,000 permanent homes for former rough sleepers over three years.

The new £432m is also meant to extend support beyond March 2027 and to the end of this Parliament. So some of the extra money is a longer-term commitment rather than extra capacity for this winter. Reports of the announcement say housing associations will be asked to identify at least 2,000 potential homes and bedspaces. Outreach teams and bedspaces are already operating in Coventry, with Plymouth and Camden next.

Can it deliver by December?

The scale of the task is set by the latest official count: 4,793 people were sleeping rough in England in 2025, a record. The pledge also echoes the Covid-era Everyone In scheme, which placed about 37,000 people in hotels and temporary accommodation from March 2020. That scheme worked because it used empty hotels and had a clear public-health reason to act fast.

This plan has no equivalent stock of empty rooms, and the early reporting describes no fixed length of stay that councils must offer. It also notes that not every rough sleeper will accept an offer. Burnham has been frank about the limits. In an interview with the Big Issue he said: "I'm not naive. You can't say there will be nobody sleeping rough after Christmas." He has also said government money alone may not be enough.

The arithmetic is mixed. A target of 2,000 identified homes and bedspaces would cover only part of a count near 4,800, though that count is a one-night snapshot and many more people pass through rough sleeping over a year. The binding constraint is probably staffing and the supply of suitable rooms, not the size of the budget. Doubling the budget does not by itself add either within eight weeks.

Landowners and the Land Registry

A separate UK policy story on the same day concerns property. The Guardian reports that campaigners say landowners are lobbying against government plans to make Land Registry data free to access. The Land Registry holds ownership records for England and Wales. The campaigners warn the lobbying could dilute the plans. The reporting gives no figures, dates or named landowner groups, and we could not independently confirm further detail.

The link to housing is indirect. Free ownership data would make it easier to see who holds land and property, which matters for debates about housing supply. Whether the plans survive intact is a question for later; no timetable has been published.

What to watch

The Budget on Wednesday should show how the £432m is profiled between this winter and later years. The first test of the Christmas pledge will come in the autumn rough-sleeping counts and in council reports on how many offers of accommodation have been made and accepted.
