Storm Boelo cuts power to thousands as new wind warning issued

In one sentence
Storm Boelo has cut power to thousands of UK homes and Met Office wind warnings remain in force, though no confirmed total outage count has been published.

Storm Boelo, the second named storm of the UK season, brought strong westerly winds and heavy rain to the north of the country over the weekend of 24–25 October. It left thousands of homes without electricity, and the Met Office has said more power cuts are possible while its wind warnings stay in force.

The outage figures reported so far are patchy. No final count attributable to Boelo alone has been published. LBC, citing the National Grid outage map, reported more than 3,700 homes without power in the South West, including Cornwall, on Saturday morning. The same report said about 31,000 homes in the region had been cut off at one point on Friday, and that engineers had restored supply to more than 60,000 homes since the stormy spell began. That Friday peak came before Boelo's main winds arrived, and the coverage overlaps with the preceding Storm Austen. It should not be read as Boelo's toll alone.

What the warnings cover

The Met Office's amber wind warning ran across Scotland and northern England down to Carlisle from 9pm Saturday to 9pm Sunday. A second amber warning covered north-east Northern Ireland from 9pm Saturday to 10am Sunday. A wider yellow wind warning for the north of the UK ran from midday Saturday to 9pm Sunday. Yellow rain warnings applied to Wales and parts of western and southern England on Saturday.

The strongest gust recorded in the reports was 76mph at South Uist in the Outer Hebrides. Inverbervie in north-east Scotland logged 71mph and Aberdaron in north-west Wales 68mph. The Met Office said "very strong westerly winds associated with Storm Boelo are expected", and its Alex Burkill said there was "more stormy weather to come as we go through this weekend". Rain on higher ground in Scotland could exceed 100mm in a day. Flooding was reported in Gourock, Inverclyde, and Scotland's Resilience Room was activated.

The Met Office had said cuts could recur across the northern amber-warning areas.

Transport and the wider cost

Transport took much of the disruption. LBC reported 56 UK flights cancelled, about 2% of the 2,619 scheduled, including 15 at Heathrow and five at Manchester. TransPennine Express advised against all but essential travel on Saturday and Sunday, and Avanti West Coast advised against travel north of Preston on Sunday. Irish Sea ferry crossings were cancelled or disrupted, and events including London's Diwali on the Square and the Paisley Halloween Festival were called off. Irish News reported that fourteen of CalMac's 30 services were cancelled. LBC's headline teaser also said a gardener at Logan Botanic Garden near Stranraer died after a falling tree struck them. The article body did not give details, so that report should be treated with caution.

Weather, not yet pattern

Storm damage of this kind is routine in a UK autumn, and one named storm does not make a trend. Wind-driven outages of a few thousand homes, restored within days, are well within what the distribution network is built to absorb. The more telling measures would be how long the longest outages last and how many customers are affected across the whole season. Neither is yet available.

The political framing is sharper than the engineering one. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: "We potentially are facing a winter of greater flooding." That is a statement about risk, and the weekend's rain and wind do not by themselves test it. Whether storms like Boelo point to a wider strain on grid resilience or insurance losses will only show in the season's tally.

What to watch

The immediate markers are the Met Office's updates to the warnings, National Grid's restoration figures for the northern amber areas, and whether a third named storm follows before the end of the month. A sustained run of storms would give the resilience question evidence it currently lacks.
