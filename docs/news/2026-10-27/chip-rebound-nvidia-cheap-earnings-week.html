Chips rebound as Nvidia trades below the S&P 500 average, with big-tech earnings ahead

In one sentence
Chips rebounded Monday, and Nvidia's forward multiple has been reported below the S&P 500 average despite record profit forecasts, leaving big-tech capex guidance this week as the test.

Semiconductor stocks recovered on Monday and pulled the Nasdaq 100 higher, according to Benzinga. The more interesting number is one that has been building for months: Nvidia, the largest company in the index, now trades on a lower forward price-to-earnings multiple than the average S&P 500 stock. That is true even though analysts' profit forecasts for it are at records.

The rebound is the trigger. The question it raises is whether investors doubt how long the earnings run will last.

A discount that keeps reappearing

A forward multiple divides the share price by the profit analysts expect over the next twelve months. A stock on 20 times forward earnings costs $20 for each $1 of expected profit.

The discount is not new, and the available figures vary by date and source. A March report citing FactSet put Nvidia's forward multiple at 19.7 times, against 20.3 times for the S&P 500, and described it as the first time in 13 years that the chipmaker had been cheaper than the index. A June report had it at 19.34 times against 20.77 times. A Goldman Sachs trading-desk note, covered by Yahoo Finance, said the multiple had fallen to match the market's after a selloff tied to Middle East war fears.

I could not find a current October multiple or an S&P 500 comparison figure. Benzinga's report, as relayed to this desk, gives neither a multiple nor a method. The comparison also depends on the measure. A cap-weighted index multiple, a simple mean and a median can differ by several points. The record-forecast claim likewise has no consensus figures or dates attached. The direction of the claim is well supported. The size of today's gap is not.

The mechanics still point one way. If forecasts rise faster than the share price, the multiple falls. A stock can get cheaper in that sense while rising in absolute terms. A falling multiple alongside rising estimates means the market is paying less for each dollar of profit. That is the pattern of an investor who expects the profits to fade, not one who expects them to compound.

What the discount may be pricing

There are three readings.

The first is that the estimates are too high. Nvidia's profits depend on customers' spending on data-centre capacity. If that spending is cyclical, a forward multiple built on peak earnings looks low only because the denominator is about to shrink. Michael Burry has echoed Ken Griffin's warning that GPU capacity has been overbuilt, according to Benzinga. Commentators also point to financial strain at smaller "neocloud" lessors of GPU capacity. The reporting offers no figures or named companies, so this is a concern and not yet evidence. Stress among lessors would matter because they are Nvidia customers.

The second reading is that the estimates are right and the stock is simply cheap. In that case the discount would close as results arrive.

The third is about size. A stock that large is held through index funds whatever its price. Its weight in the index says nothing on its own about whether the multiple is justified.

A week of earnings

The test arrives this week. Benzinga says 36% of S&P 500 earnings are due, with Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon the main focus. That 36% figure has not been independently checked. I could not locate the underlying FactSet or Benzinga tally.

The relevant numbers are not the headline profits. They are each company's capital-spending plans. Those budgets are, in effect, Nvidia's revenue. Higher guidance would support the estimates behind the low multiple. Restraint would suggest the doubters have a point.

Europe followed the US move, though the evidence is slight. ASML, the Dutch chip-equipment maker, goes ex-dividend today, per fiscal.ai. Its share price adjusts mechanically for the payment, which makes any move in the stock harder to read. The dividend amount and record date are unverified.

What to watch

The cleanest signal will be the capex guidance from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon. If those are raised, Nvidia's forecasts look safer and the discount looks harder to defend. If they are held or trimmed, the low multiple may turn out to have been the market's early read. Nvidia's own results, due later in the quarter, will then show which reading was right.

For investors who track the index, or who rank stocks by relative valuation, the choice is between trusting the estimates and trusting the discount. This week's results will move that choice, though they are unlikely to settle it.
