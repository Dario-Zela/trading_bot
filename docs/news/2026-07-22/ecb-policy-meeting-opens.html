ECB opens two-day policy meeting in Frankfurt

The European Central Bank's headquarters on the Frankfurt skyline. — Daniel Vorndran (DXR), Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
In one sentence
ECB's two-day Frankfurt meeting opened with markets split on whether June's surprise rate hike was a one-off or the start of something longer, as Iran-linked oil prices climb again.

The European Central Bank's Governing Council convened in Frankfurt on Wednesday for the first of a two-day policy meeting, with a rate decision and press conference from President Christine Lagarde due Thursday afternoon. Unlike most of the ECB's recent gatherings, this one carries genuine suspense — a rarity for a central bank that spent much of the past two years signalling its moves well in advance.

The reason is oil. The war in the Middle East, and Tehran's near-total blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, have kept energy markets on edge since late February, upending an inflation picture that had briefly dipped below the ECB's 2% target before the conflict erupted. In June, the Governing Council responded with its first rate increase in nearly three years, lifting the deposit facility rate a quarter point to 2.25%, with the main refinancing and marginal lending rates rising in step to 2.40% and 2.65%. Staff projections at the time put 2026 headline inflation at 3.0%, an upward revision blamed squarely on energy costs.

Where inflation stands now

Eurostat's confirmed June reading showed euro-area inflation at 2.8%, down from 3.2% in May — the first month-on-month decline this year. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, eased to 2.4% from 2.6%, and energy inflation itself cooled to 8.5% from 10.8%. That is progress, but it leaves headline inflation more than three-quarters of a point above target, with the Governing Council having explicitly declined to rule out further tightening.

The case for a pause, and the case against

Most analysts still expect the Council to hold rates steady on Thursday, treating June's move as sufficient for now rather than the opening of a sustained tightening cycle. Lagarde herself has pushed back on the idea that June was mere insurance, telling Les Échos the bank raised rates because the data pointed to "a genuine inflation problem," not a precautionary gesture.

But the calm cooling in the inflation data has been complicated by renewed Iran-US tensions in recent days, which have pushed crude back up toward $87 a barrel — reviving talk in some trading desks of a surprise increase Thursday. The consensus view still places the next hike, if one comes, at the September meeting rather than this one. Lagarde has been careful not to prejudge it either way: "Forward guidance is not in the cards," she said at the ECB's June forum in Sintra, emphasising that each decision will be made meeting by meeting rather than along a pre-set path.

What's next

The substantive news lands Thursday at 13:45 CET, when the rate decision is published, followed by Lagarde's press conference at 14:30 CET. July is a non-projection meeting — no fresh staff forecasts accompany it — so the Council's language on the balance of risks, rather than any new numbers, will be the thing worth parsing. The next full projection round, and with it a clearer read on whether September brings a second hike, arrives in September.
