Bitcoin hits $66,000 on Clarity Act hopes

A physical bitcoin token, used to represent the cryptocurrency in reporting on its price moves. — Mike Cauldwell (Casascius) / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Bitcoin rallied to $66,000 on optimism over the CLARITY Act, but the bill still lacks the Senate votes needed to pass.

Bitcoin climbed above $66,000 on Tuesday, its highest level in more than a month, as traders bet that Washington is finally close to giving the crypto industry the rulebook it has wanted for years. The gain — roughly 3.5% in 24 hours — came on the heels of news that President Trump had agreed to an ethics provision that Senate Republicans hope clears the last real obstacle to passing the CLARITY Act before Congress leaves for its August recess.

"In one sentence: Bitcoin rallied to $66,000 on optimism over pending crypto legislation, but the bill still needs Senate votes it doesn't yet have."

What the bill would actually do

The CLARITY Act is not a rule about any single coin or exchange — it's an attempt to settle, once and for all, which US regulator has jurisdiction over what. It would draw a statutory line between the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, set registration and disclosure requirements for exchanges and trading platforms, and preserve antifraud authority while giving digital-asset firms a clearer path to operate onshore rather than in regulatory limbo. The House already passed its version in July 2025, 294–134, with 78 Democrats crossing over. The Senate Banking Committee advanced its own draft in May by 15–9. Since then it has stalled — twice missing informal deadlines, most recently before the July 4 recess — over unresolved disputes on stablecoin oversight, anti-money-laundering compliance and, most stubbornly, ethics restrictions on officials with crypto holdings.

That last item is what moved the market this week. On the evening of July 20th, Trump agreed to what a White House official described as the most sweeping ethics provision of its kind, covering restrictions on the president, the vice president and members of Congress. The language went to Senate Republicans within hours. Bitcoin, along with ether, XRP and dogecoin, took off the following session on the assumption that the deal removes the last major sticking point.

The arithmetic Congress still has to solve

It doesn't, quite. Republicans hold 53 Senate seats; the CLARITY Act needs 60 votes to break a filibuster, meaning at least seven Democrats have to cross the aisle. So far exactly two — Ruben Gallego of Arizona and Angela Alsobrooks of Maryland — have indicated support, both with conditions attached. Democratic leadership has been explicit that seeing an ethics framework is not the same as endorsing it; several senators said this week they hadn't even reviewed the final language before markets started pricing in a deal. Senators Josh Hawley and Rand Paul, meanwhile, are expected to vote no from the Republican side, tightening the math further. The Senate's recess begins around August 8th, and people involved in the negotiations describe that date as a hard deadline: miss it, and the bill's prospects for 2026 "deteriorate materially," in the words of one Senate aide quoted in trade press this week.

A market ahead of the vote count

Bitcoin ETFs pulled in $727 million over five consecutive trading days through Tuesday, a flow pattern consistent with funds positioning for a policy catalyst rather than reacting to one that's already landed. Jeff Park, an alpha strategist at Bitwise Capital, was blunt about the gap between price action and legislative reality: "The White House has crafted an ethics proposal that they're comfortable with, which to me is still very doubtful" as a solution to Democrats' underlying concerns, he said, noting that the momentum in the news cycle has come almost entirely from the Republican side. Park's preferred trade reflects that skepticism — he favours assets with the most direct regulatory upside, such as ether, alongside equities like Circle and Robinhood that stand to benefit from a formal market-structure framework, over spot bitcoin itself.

What to watch

The next concrete marker is whether Majority Leader John Thune schedules a floor vote before the recess; absent that, the calendar effectively resets the debate to September at the earliest. Democratic reaction to the specific ethics text — expected to circulate more widely in the coming days — will show whether the seven crossover votes are plausible or aspirational. Given that Tuesday's rally was built substantially on the expectation of passage rather than passage itself, a delay or a defeat would leave bitcoin's $66,000 level without the fundamental support the market has just assigned it.
