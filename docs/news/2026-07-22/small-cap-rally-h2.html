Small-cap stocks rally as second half of 2026 begins

The New York Stock Exchange trading floor, where small-cap names have driven some of the market's sharpest moves this year. — Eduard Hueber / Asymptote Architecture, via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
The Russell 2000 hit a record high after its best first half since 1991, but the gains trace mostly to individual M&A and FDA catalysts, not a clean rotation away from mega-cap tech.

Small-cap stocks have spent 2026 doing something they have not managed in decades: leading. The Russell 2000 closed out the first half of the year up roughly 22%, its strongest six-month start since 1991, and pushed to a fresh record high of 3,024.37 as the third quarter opened. Through July 10, the index's trailing 12-month total return stood at 20.7%, nearly double the S&P 500's 11.3% over the same stretch.

That is a reversal of form. For most of the past three years, gains in the broad market have been supplied by a handful of giant technology companies — Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Tesla — a group whose combined weight in the S&P 500 has swollen to somewhere between 34% and 40%, depending on the measure used. Nvidia alone accounts for roughly 7.5% of the index, a concentration level with few historical precedents. The small-cap move raises the question of whether money is genuinely rotating out of that trade, or whether this is a summer flare-up that fades once the next earnings season reasserts mega-cap dominance.

What's actually moving

The rally is not a uniform drift upward so much as a collection of sharp, idiosyncratic moves. Benzinga's tally of July's biggest small-cap gainers reads more like a catalyst list than a macro trend: Crinetics Pharmaceuticals surged 123% after Vertex Pharmaceuticals agreed to acquire it for $85 a share on July 6. Rapid7 climbed 46% on a mix of cybersecurity-sector strength and a leadership change, even after accounting for the bounce it remains down 22% for the year. Sable Offshore rose 32% after closing a refinancing — roughly $345 million in convertible notes and $107 million in equity — used to repay an Exxon Mobil loan. REGENXBIO gained 28% on FDA alignment over a resubmission for its Hunter syndrome gene therapy, triggering an AbbVie milestone payment, while Orthofix Medical added 28% after the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services reversed a May reimbursement cut for bone-growth stimulators.

Those are company-specific events, not a single macro narrative, and several of the standout names remain down double digits for the year even after the recent pop. That matters for how durable the picture is: a rally built on M&A, FDA decisions and short squeezes in individual names is a different animal from one driven by a systematic re-rating of the asset class.

The broader case

Set against that, there is a structural argument getting real traction with strategists. Small caps continue to trade at a meaningful discount — the S&P 500 near 21 times earnings against roughly 16 times for small-cap benchmarks — and about 40% of Russell 2000 constituents carry floating-rate debt, versus under 10% for S&P 500 companies. That made small caps unusually sensitive when the Federal Reserve was expected to cut rates. It has cut the other way for now: futures markets in early July priced roughly a 75% chance the Fed holds its 3.50%–3.75% range steady, with a 25% chance of a hike, after May's CPI print came in at 4.2% year-over-year and the Fed's preferred inflation gauge at 4.1%. Small caps have rallied anyway, which analysts reading the move charitably attribute to earnings growth, an active M&A pipeline, an accelerating IPO market and investors hunting for the next layer of AI beneficiaries beyond the names that already trade at stretched multiples.

What to watch

The Russell 2000 dipped 0.18% on the session after its record close, a pause rather than a reversal, but it is a reminder that the index has now run far enough to invite profit-taking. The next real test is second-quarter earnings season, now underway, which will show whether small-cap revenue and margin growth back up the valuation-discount argument or whether the rally has been running ahead of fundamentals. A Fed decision that leans toward a hold — or, per current pricing, even a hike — would also bite hardest at the floating-rate balance sheets that make small caps a leveraged bet on rate policy. Until there is clearer flow data showing sustained rotation out of mega-cap tech rather than a cluster of one-off catalysts, the honest reading is that small caps are having a genuinely strong year, not that concentration risk in the S&P 500 has been resolved.
