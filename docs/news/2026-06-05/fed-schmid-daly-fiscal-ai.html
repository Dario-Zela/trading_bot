Fed's Schmid warned that $1 in every $7 of federal spending now goes to interest payments and called the situation in need of a solution, while Fed's Daly noted AI productivity gains remain sectorally concentrated and that tariffs — not technology — are driving near-term inflation.

Fed leadership publicly voicing concerns about fiscal sustainability signals growing anxiety about the structural limits of monetary policy. While the Fed cannot set fiscal policy, such commentary influences Congressional budget debates and investor conviction that rate or inflation outcomes may be constrained by unsustainable deficit dynamics.

Key facts:
- Federal interest payments represent approximately one-seventh (~14%) of federal spending, per Schmid at Hochatown Economic Forum
- Schmid reaffirmed the 2% inflation target should remain the Fed's clear, unmudded message
- Schmid characterized the extraordinary spending in 2008 and 2020 as responses to 'existential' crises, implying them harder to fault in hindsight
