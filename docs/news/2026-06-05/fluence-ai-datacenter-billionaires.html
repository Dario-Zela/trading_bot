Fluence Energy's AI data-centre push with Siemens and Nvidia continues to attract institutional attention

A large-scale AI data centre campus; high-density GPU clusters create the volatile power-draw patterns Fluence's SmartStack is designed to absorb — ess-news.com
In one sentence
Fluence's SmartStack battery platform anchors a new 136 MW AI data-centre blueprint with Siemens and Nvidia, sending FLNC up 40% and drawing billionaire hedge-fund buying.

Energy storage company Fluence Energy has spent the past week at the intersection of two trends investors cannot stop talking about: the insatiable power appetite of AI infrastructure and the need for grid stability to feed it. The catalyst was a June 1 announcement that Fluence, Siemens, and Nvidia had jointly published a reference architecture for AI data centres — a deployable blueprint that puts Fluence's SmartStack battery platform at the heart of every power path from the utility grid to the GPU rack.

The reference design

The architecture is built around Nvidia's DSX Vera Rubin NVL72 compute platform and covers facilities up to 136 megawatts in total capacity, of which 100 MW supports IT load. The design addresses a specific headache: AI workloads draw power in sharp, irregular bursts. A cluster of Nvidia's latest GPUs can swing between idle and full throttle in milliseconds, a pattern that plays havoc with grid voltage and frequency.

Fluence's SmartStack handles that instability. Containerised and modular, the system provides voltage and frequency ride-through, black-start capability — allowing a facility to restart without grid power — demand response, and what Fluence calls "AI load smoothing," absorbing spikes so the utility connection sees a steadier draw. The utility tie comes in at a nominal 34.5 kV, and the overall design meets Tier III concurrent-maintainability standards, meaning any component can be serviced without taking the facility offline.

Siemens contributes prefabricated medium- and low-voltage skids engineered to shrink on-site construction time. Thermal management — increasingly critical as rack densities climb well beyond air cooling's limits — comes from nVent Electric, which has deployed more than 2 GW of liquid cooling capacity globally. Jeff Monday, Fluence's chief growth officer, framed the company's role plainly: "The Smartstack platform delivers critical grid-support functions, including demand response and AI load smoothing services, transforming the grid into an accelerator for compute."

Why the money is moving

The announcement triggered a roughly 40 percent jump in Fluence's share price on June 1, lifting FLNC from around $20 to $28.37 in a single session — a move large enough to catch attention well beyond energy-sector specialists.

The pop landed on ground already prepared by a solid set of quarterly numbers. Fluence's fiscal second quarter, reported in May, showed revenue of $465 million, up 8 percent year-over-year, with a record $5.6 billion backlog and $2 billion in first-half order intake. The company also disclosed master supply agreements with two unnamed hyperscalers — a signal that the design is already making its way from reference document to commercial pipeline.

Institutional positioning shifted noticeably even before the Siemens-Nvidia announcement. D.E. Shaw more than tripled its stake in the fourth quarter of 2025, adding 4.5 million shares — a 296 percent increase. Two Sigma added nearly 1.15 million shares over the same period. Citi raised its price target from $15 to $26 in May, though it maintained a Neutral rating. Barclays kept an Equal Weight stance with a $16 target, a gap that reflects genuine uncertainty about how fast AI data-centre revenue will materialise at scale. Insider Monkey's ranking of the ten best renewable-energy stocks favoured by billionaire investors included Fluence in the cohort, citing the same storage-plus-AI convergence.

The US Energy Information Administration projects electricity demand in 2026 and 2027 to hit successive records, driven largely by data centre build-out and electrification. Whether Fluence translates that macro tailwind into durable earnings depends on how many hyperscalers move from reference architecture to signed purchase orders. The next quarterly order intake figure, due in August, will be the clearest early read.
