Boeing shares climb on reports Singapore Airlines and Qantas are weighing wide-body orders

A Qantas Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner on the tarmac at Melbourne International Airport — the type the carrier is reportedly evaluating for its next wide-body tranche. — Mitchell R Hope / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Boeing shares gained ~4% after reports Singapore Airlines is eyeing 50+ wide-body jets and Qantas is weighing 20 more — but neither order is confirmed.

Boeing shares rose roughly 4% on Thursday after Reuters reported that Singapore Airlines and Qantas are both in preliminary discussions to buy wide-body jets — a pair of separate sourced stories that landed on the same day and briefly made the manufacturer look like the obvious beneficiary of recovering long-haul demand. The stock touched a session high of $221.60 before pulling back. Neither order is confirmed, neither airline named a preferred manufacturer, and both Boeing and Airbus declined to comment.

The two situations are distinct. Singapore Airlines is in early talks to acquire at least 50 large passenger jets — either the Boeing 777X or the Airbus A350-1000 — with purchase options for dozens more aircraft beyond that initial tranche. The airline's 777-300ERs now average more than 16 years of service, and any replacement order would target capacity additions beginning next decade. That timeline matters: with Boeing's 777X first delivery pushed to 2027 after running roughly six years behind its original schedule, a commitment today would land in a queue already stacked with prior customers.

Qantas is considering something smaller in scope: roughly 20 additional wide-body jets, either Boeing 787 Dreamliners or the standard Airbus A350 variant. The Australian carrier's fleet modernization plan begins in fiscal year 2027, when it intends to start retiring older A330 and A380 aircraft. It already holds firm orders for 12 Boeing 787s and 24 Airbus A350-1000s — including 12 ultra-long-range variants designed for non-stop flights from Sydney or Melbourne to London and New York under the so-called Project Sunrise. The 20-aircraft figure being discussed would augment that program, not replace it. A Qantas spokesperson offered the standard non-answer: "We're in regular contact with aircraft manufacturers as part of our ongoing fleet planning and always update the market when we have something to announce."

The signal problem

For trading purposes, sourced-but-unconfirmed aircraft order reports carry a predictable pattern: they move sentiment sharply and fade quickly if no announcement follows. Boeing chatter on Stocktwits jumped 300% over the past 30 days heading into Thursday's session — yet the platform's aggregate reading on the stock remained bearish. That disconnect is instructive. The reports do tell a genuine story about airline planning cycles: carriers don't open manufacturer conversations casually, and both Singapore Airlines and Qantas have clear fleet aging pressures that make the discussions credible on their face. But discussions are not commitments, manufacturers routinely run multi-year competitive processes before an order is placed, and Airbus is competing for both deals. Boeing's lift from these reports reflects sentiment, not confirmed demand.

The substantive question for Boeing investors is whether the 777X can clear its remaining certification hurdles in time to fill the Singapore Airlines slot — and whether Airbus, with a less complicated near-term delivery book, prices itself aggressively for the Qantas 787-class competition. Watch for any formal request-for-proposal announcement from either carrier, and for Boeing's next production milestone on the 777X program, where a GE9X engine seal issue remains under analysis by GE Aerospace.
