Watson battles through US Open qualifying to set up all-British clash

Heather Watson, pictured during an earlier Grand Slam qualifying campaign, is chasing her first US Open main-draw place since 2019. — Carine06 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Heather Watson beat sixth seed Claire Liu to push on in US Open qualifying, as Raducanu and Draper's injury withdrawals leave British tennis thin in New York.

Heather Watson is used to doing this the hard way. Five years after her last appearance in the main draw of the US Open, the 34-year-old from Guernsey went a set down to sixth-seeded Claire Liu in the opening round of qualifying, clawed her way back, then had to defend a double-break lead in the decider before closing it out 4-6, 6-2, 6-4. It was the kind of match that qualifying exists for — scrappy, tense, decided by whoever wants it marginally more in the closing games.

Watson now faces Spain's Angela Fita Boluda, ranked 212th in the world, in the second round, needing two more wins to reach the main draw at Flushing Meadows for the first time since 2019. It is her fifth attempt since then to qualify, a run of near-misses that says as much about the depth of the modern women's tour as it does about Watson's own form.

A weakened flag

Her win matters more than it otherwise might because British tennis arrives in New York short-handed. Emma Raducanu, the 2021 US Open champion, announced earlier this month that a stress fracture in her right leg — the same injury pattern that kept her out of Wimbledon — would rule her out of the tournament entirely, extending a wretched injury-blighted year. Jack Draper, the world number seven and a semi-finalist in New York in 2024, confirmed his own withdrawal over persistent left arm bone bruising that has now cost him every Grand Slam of 2026.

"These last months have felt like nothing but adversity and setbacks," Draper wrote on Instagram. "Most days I've spent in doubt, yet hopeful that I can keep taking positive steps forward."

With British tennis's two biggest current draws sidelined, the burden of representing the nation at the year's final major shifts to a qualifying contingent led by Watson, along with Francesca Jones, Harriet Dart and Katie Swan on the women's side.

Brothers in arms, on opposite sides of the net

The men's qualifying draw threw up its own oddity on the same day: Toby Samuel and Billy Harris, both British, will now meet each other in the second round after winning their openers — guaranteeing an all-British name in the third round regardless of Watson's own fortunes. Jacob Fearnley also advanced, beating Spain's Roberto Carballes Baena 7-6(3), 6-3 to set up a meeting with France's Luca Pavlovic.

It leaves a curious shape to Britain's push in New York: no seeded stars, but a wider-than-usual spread of names still standing after round one, all trying to convert three qualifying wins into a place among the 128.

Watson's next match against Fita Boluda is scheduled for Wednesday, with the winner needing one further round to reach the main draw, which begins on Sunday.
