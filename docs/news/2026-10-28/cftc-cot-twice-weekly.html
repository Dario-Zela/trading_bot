CFTC plans Commitments of Traders report twice a week

In one sentence
Bloomberg reports the CFTC plans to publish its Commitments of Traders positioning data twice a week, but the agency has not confirmed it and no timeline or rationale has been given.

The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission plans to publish its Commitments of Traders report twice a week instead of once, according to a Bloomberg report relayed by Benzinga. The report is the main public record of who holds what in US futures markets. A faster cadence would shorten the wait for a data series that many traders treat as a slow-moving gauge of positioning.

The CFTC has not, as far as this desk has been able to confirm, published a notice, rule or statement on the change. The Bloomberg account is the only source so far, and it has not been checked against a primary CFTC document. Everything below about the plan itself is therefore provisional.

What the report is today

The Commitments of Traders report, known as COT, breaks down open interest in US futures markets by category of trader. The usual schedule, from background knowledge rather than a current CFTC page checked today, is a Friday afternoon release carrying positions as of the preceding Tuesday. A trader reading it on Friday is therefore looking at a snapshot more than three days old, and nearly a week old by the time the next one arrives.

That lag shapes how the data gets used. It suits slow questions: whether speculators are stretched long in crude, or whether asset managers are crowded into Treasury futures. It suits fast questions poorly, because positions can change materially between the Tuesday snapshot and the Friday release.

What is not known

The reported plan comes without the details that would let anyone judge it:

  • Timing. No effective date has been reported.
  • Process. It is not clear whether the change needs a formal rulemaking, with a comment period, or can be done administratively.
  • Rationale. No reason for the change has been reported.
  • Design. It is unclear whether a second release would cover every market and every report format, or whether the snapshot dates would change. A second weekly release could mean a mid-week snapshot, or a re-cut of the same data. Those are different products.

The design question matters most. The value of the data lies in how close the snapshot date sits to the publication date, not in how many times it is published. A Friday release of Tuesday data followed by a Tuesday release of the previous Friday's data would be a modest improvement. A release that cut the lag to a day or two would be a larger one.

Who would notice

Futures traders, macro funds and systematic strategies that use COT positioning as an input are the obvious beneficiaries. More frequent data could change how quickly positioning signals are priced. If a crowded trade becomes visible sooner, it may also unwind sooner, which would shrink the edge for anyone who relies on the weekly rhythm. Whether that happens depends on the design questions above, and on how the market reacts to a signal it can see more often.

Market-structure watchers and regulators have their own interest. Positioning data is also a monitoring tool, and a shorter reporting cycle would give outside observers an earlier view of concentration in individual contracts.

What to watch

The next data point is a CFTC notice, press release or Federal Register filing confirming the plan and setting out the schedule, scope and start date. Until one appears, the Bloomberg report is best treated as a signal of intent, not a settled change. Any reply from the agency to the report would also say whether the twice-weekly cadence is a firm proposal or still under discussion.
