UK-wide drugs shortage leaves Northern Ireland patient in 'horrific pain'

In one sentence
A Coleraine MS patient's loss of nabilone is the human face of a UK-wide stock-out the department says will last until December, amid 157 drugs in short supply.

A woman in Coleraine, County Londonderry, who has multiple sclerosis (MS) says she is in "horrific pain" after the drug she has relied on for 17 years ran out. Her case is not an isolated one. Northern Ireland's Department of Health says both licensed strengths of the drug, nabilone, are out of stock with wholesalers across the UK and are not expected back until December.

The patient, Jill Kenning, was diagnosed with MS in 2008 after sight problems, and later developed numbness down the left side of her body. According to BBC News NI, which first reported her account, she was prescribed nabilone after a severe relapse that required a four-week hospital stay. She has taken it for 17 of the 18 years since.

Nabilone is a synthetic cannabinoid. This article draws only on the BBC's reporting and the department's statements. It has not independently established the drug's clinical role in MS.

The patient's account

Kenning ran out of nabilone the previous Tuesday, according to the BBC. Since then she has spent most of her time in bed. She describes constant eye pain that she compares to being "stabbed with a hot poker".

Her health trust told her none was available and advised her to contact her neurologist. She has since been prescribed alternatives, but says they have not worked, and she has needed hospital stays of several nights. "I'm not only fighting for me," she told the BBC, "I'm fighting for all the other patients that are on this drug." That quotation is as relayed in a republished copy of the BBC story; the original page could not be checked directly.

One patient's experience cannot show how many people are affected. The department has not, in the material reviewed, said how many people in Northern Ireland are prescribed nabilone.

A UK-wide problem

The claim that this is systemic rests on the department's own statement. It says both licensed doses are out of stock with wholesalers across the UK. The shortage began with the 1mg capsules in July. The 0.25mg strength became unavailable more recently. That sequence suggests a supply fault that has widened, though the department has not explained its cause. It described the information as "commercially sensitive".

The department says it is working with the UK Department of Health and Social Care and with health trusts. It has issued guidance to clinicians on alternatives, including unlicensed imports of nabilone where available. It acknowledged that supply disruptions "can be concerning for patients and the healthcare professionals who care for them", and told patients worried about alternatives to contact their specialist teams.

The wider picture

The BBC cites the MIMS industry tracker, which put the number of UK drugs in short supply at 157 this week, up from a low of 107 in January. The trend has been upward through the year.

Those figures show a general rise, not that nabilone's problem is typical. Shortages differ in cause, in duration and in the availability of substitutes. A patient whose condition is stable on a long-standing drug is in a different position from one who can switch easily.

The case also follows other recent Northern Ireland supply problems. BBC News NI reported on 15 October that supplies of two major HRT patch brands had been disrupted, according to a search summary of that coverage that was not checked against the original.

What to watch

The department's December estimate is the date to test. Open questions are whether the wholesalers' stock position changes before then, how many patients depend on nabilone, and whether unlicensed imports reach people in practice. The cause of the shortage remains undisclosed.

Patients on the drug are advised to speak to their specialist team and not to change treatment alone.
