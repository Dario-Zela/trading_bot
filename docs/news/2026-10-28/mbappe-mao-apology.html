Mbappé apologises for 'ill-judged' Mao remark after China backlash

In one sentence
Mbappé apologised for an 'ill-judged' joke about Mao after Chinese sponsors dropped him and state broadcaster CMG pulled his matches and ads.

Kylian Mbappé has apologised for what he called an "ill-judged" remark about Mao Zedong. The apology came after a backlash in China that has already cost him commercial partners and put his club under pressure. The episode shows how little it takes for a throwaway line outside football to hurt a footballer in a market that sports brands court heavily.

The remark

The trigger was a short clip from an interview ahead of Mbappé's appearance on the French online talk show The Bridge. The clip circulated on X. Asked for his favourite meme of himself, the Real Madrid forward described a video of "a guy... I think he was a Chinese dictator." The clip then cut to an altered image with his face on Mao's body. Footage has since been reported removed from the show's channel, though the original wording is known mainly through the clip that spread online.

Mao is still a revered figure in China's official narrative, and flippant references to him are a well-worn route to trouble for foreign brands and celebrities. Citroën, for one, had to eat its words after a Mao joke in an advert in 2008.

The apology and the fallout

In a statement on Tuesday 27 October, Mbappé called his comment "ill-judged". He said he understood it had hurt people in China and in Chinese communities worldwide. He apologised to anyone his words had upset, said that was not his intention, and said he has deep respect for the Chinese people and their history. He closed by saying he was sincerely sorry.

By then the reaction had been quick and concrete, according to reports of the episode:

  • Beverage maker Dongpeng, dairy company Mengniu and cheese maker Milkground ended their agreements with him, each citing opposition to actions that hurt national sentiment.
  • China Media Group suspended broadcasts of La Liga and other games featuring him, and removed his advertisements. It demanded an apology from both player and Real Madrid.
  • The China Football Association said it had "lodged a stern protest" with Real Madrid and called for a public, formal apology from the player and the club.
  • La Liga said the remarks were made in a personal capacity, did not represent the league, and that it would draw up a communication protocol for players and clubs.

The social platform RedNote, where Mbappé had built a following as part of a push into China that began in 2024, also moved. It said its content partnership with him ended on 31 December 2024, that it would remove related promotional material, and his account was barred from taking new followers.

What it says about the market

The cost is the point of the story. The commercial damage is visible in the sponsor exits and the broadcast suspension, but nobody has yet put a figure on it, and how lasting it proves will depend on whether the apology satisfies Chinese authorities and fans. Real Madrid has not, in the reports available, been shown to have issued the statement the CFA asked for. That is the open question. China is a market where clubs and players sell shirts, streams and endorsements, and where the audience, official bodies included, expects respect for national symbols.

For a player whose off-pitch brand is built carefully, the lesson is old but keeps being relearned: a remark made in French to a French audience is not heard only by one. What to watch next is whether Real Madrid responds to the CFA's demand, and whether China Media Group restores La Liga coverage that includes him.
