UK and EU officials to discuss freedom of movement

In one sentence
UK and EU officials are to discuss freedom of movement, a reminder that the hardest question in the post-Brexit reset is being negotiated again, with no date or outcome yet stated.

Officials from the United Kingdom and the European Union are due to hold talks on freedom of movement, according to BBC Politics. The subject is among the most politically sensitive in the effort to repair relations with Brussels. The report as it reached this desk gives no date, no list of participants and no stated outcome, so the meeting is best read as a sign of where the negotiation has got to, not as a breakthrough.

Why the question keeps returning

Freedom of movement between the UK and the EU ended on 31 December 2020, when the Brexit transition period closed. The Trade and Cooperation Agreement that took effect afterwards contained no general right for citizens of either side to live and work in the other's territory. Ending that right was the clearest promise of the 2016 referendum campaign, and it has been guarded ever since. The government's stated line is that any new arrangement will not be a return to free movement.

Brussels has never treated the point as closed. Its interest is mostly in young people: study, work and travel rights for under-30s, which Brussels sees as a way to rebuild the personal ties the 2020 settlement cut.

The narrower scheme already on the table

The nearest thing to movement on the issue is the youth experience scheme. At the May 2025 UK-EU summit both sides agreed to work towards a "balanced" scheme. The EU Council authorised the European Commission to open negotiations the following month. The label was chosen with care: officials are widely reported to have preferred "youth experience" to "youth mobility" to head off accusations that free movement was returning by the back door.

The central disputes have been over design. Reporting has said the EU pressed for visas of up to four years while the UK preferred about one, with a two-to-three-year compromise floated. Brussels has also reportedly offered an "emergency brake" to let London limit numbers. A cross-party Trade and Business Commission report in December 2025 proposed a two-year scheme, extendable by one year, with an initial cap of 44,000 visas reviewed annually. It also noted that public support falls by about ten percentage points if the scheme runs longer than two years.

By May 2026, the PIE News reported, disagreements over the scheme were holding back the wider reset, though the EU's ambassador to the UK still hinted that it could be launched at a coming summit once "precise terms" were settled. This desk could not find reporting after that date confirming whether those terms have been agreed. The status of the scheme should be treated as unresolved unless the BBC's account says otherwise.

What the talks signal

The significance of a meeting lies in the agenda. A discussion limited to the youth scheme would be a continuation of existing work. One that reached residence or broader work rights would reopen a question both governments have tried to keep contained. Nothing in the available reporting says which of the two is planned, and the absence of a date or a named delegation suggests the exploratory stage.

The terms of the relationship have held since 2020. Any change, even a capped scheme for the young, would be the first real alteration to them. That is why the wording matters as much as the numbers: ministers will want a scheme they can describe as limited, and Brussels will want one wide enough to be worth having.

What to watch is whether either side publishes a date and an agenda, and whether the next UK-EU summit is used to announce agreed terms on duration, annual caps and eligibility. Until then, freedom of movement remains a question being negotiated, not settled.
