Reform official accused of likening volunteer English teacher to a Nazi collaborator

In one sentence
A 78-year-old volunteer who teaches asylum seekers English says a Reform councillor likened her to a wartime collaborator; her husband has complained to Essex County Council.

A 78-year-old woman who volunteers teaching English to asylum seekers says a Reform UK county councillor compared her to a wartime collaborator at a parish council meeting in Essex. Her husband has made a formal complaint. Reform and the councillor have not responded to requests for comment.

Mair Godley, who says she is "nearly 79", has taught English at Wethersfield, a former military base where the Home Office houses hundreds of asylum seekers. It is the larger of two such sites in England; the other is at Crowborough, Sussex. The Guardian reported her account on Wednesday.

The meeting

The exchange took place on 14 October at a meeting of Wethersfield parish council. The councillor, named as Carl Johnson, had come to update members on local matters including potholes and Operation Vanguard. That scheme puts private security guards on the streets of Essex town centres.

Mair's husband, Nick Godley, 77, is a former chair of the parish council. He pressed Johnson on the potholes. He also asked whether Operation Vanguard was needed to protect residents from asylum seekers, noting that crime and antisocial behaviour in nearby Braintree were low.

According to the Godleys, Johnson then asked whether anyone in the room had been helping migrants and "facilitating the invasion". Mair Godley said she had volunteered to teach English at Wethersfield. Johnson, they say, replied that this was like helping Germany in the Second World War. He also reportedly said he did not want his daughter to be raped and murdered by asylum seekers.

Draft minutes seen by the Guardian say the discussion became heated and that Johnson was asked to leave to "restore order". Nick Godley called Johnson's behaviour "shocking" and said a member of the public had suggested he should go. Mair Godley said she felt violated, and that she told Johnson at the meeting he was not wanted there.

The wider context

Reform took control of Essex County Council after the May 2026 elections, ending 25 years of Conservative control. Its councillors now hold much of the day-to-day relationship between the party and local communities, including parish councils like this one.

The week before the meeting, Johnson had written on Facebook that people entering the country illegally received free clothing and accommodation while "the rest of us" paid. He described this as treason. The language reported from the meeting, "invasion" and wartime collaboration, echoes that post. That is consistent with the Godleys' account, though it does not prove what was said in the room.

The evidence for the central allegation is so far the Godleys' testimony and draft minutes. The minutes, as described, confirm the row and the request that Johnson leave. The reporting does not say they record the specific remarks.

What to watch

Nick Godley has lodged a formal complaint with Essex County Council about Johnson's comments. A council spokesperson said complaints are only discussed publicly once they have been investigated and a breach has been found. That points to a slow process.

The next developments would be a response from Johnson or Reform, whether the parish council's final minutes keep the draft wording, and whether other residents or volunteers come forward with similar complaints. An apology or sanction would turn a local dispute into a test case for how Reform handles its councillors' conduct towards people who work with asylum seekers. Silence would leave the question open.
