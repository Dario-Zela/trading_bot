Former Met officer who worked with Couzens found guilty of rape

In one sentence
Mark Tyrrell, a former Met officer from the same unit as Wayne Couzens, was convicted of rape at Southwark Crown Court and will be sentenced on 16 December.

A former Metropolitan Police officer who served in the same unit as Wayne Couzens has been convicted of rape. The case revives a question the force has been trying to answer since 2021: whether it can now stop officers like this from staying in the job.

Mark Tyrrell, 57, of Meltham, West Yorkshire, was found guilty at Southwark Crown Court on Tuesday 27 October. The jury convicted him of one count of rape, three counts of sexual assault and one count of perverting the course of justice. The last charge relates to his attempt to delete messages he had sent the woman, who cannot be identified for legal reasons. He will be sentenced at the same court on 16 December.

The verdicts

The jury did not convict on everything. Tyrrell was cleared of a further count of rape, four counts of assault by penetration and eight counts of sexual assault. He told jurors that sex with the woman had always been consensual and that "there was never any forcefulness to it", according to the BBC's report of the trial.

Prosecutor Catherine Farrelly KC told the court that when the woman resisted, "he simply chose to take what he wanted".

Tyrrell had already admitted misconduct in public office, and had admitted perverting the course of justice by persuading the woman to lie to police about why he was in her bedroom. He resigned in September 2023 after being arrested and suspended. The reports available do not give the dates of the offences.

The Couzens connection

Tyrrell served in the Met's Parliamentary and Diplomatic Protection Command, the unit in which Couzens and David Carrick also served. Couzens abducted, raped and murdered Sarah Everard in March 2021 and received a whole-life order at the Old Bailey that September. Carrick, another officer from the command, was later exposed as a serial rapist.

The shared unit is a fact, not proof of a shared failure. Nothing in the reporting suggests that Tyrrell and Couzens were connected in any way beyond having served in the same command. But the pattern is the sort that independent scrutiny has already examined. The Baroness Casey review of the Met, published in March 2023, concluded that the force's vetting and conduct systems had failed to remove officers who should not have been serving. That review is background here, not a finding about Tyrrell's case.

What the Met says

Chief Superintendent Sara Leach said: "We remain appalled that someone like this was serving within our organisation."

The statement is a reasonable test of the force's position, because it concedes the central point. Tyrrell was a serving officer when the allegations arose, and he left only after his arrest and suspension. Whether earlier warning signs were missed, and whether his supervisors had any reason to act sooner, has not been established in the coverage seen so far.

What to watch

Two things will show how far this case reopens the wider argument. The first is the sentencing on 16 December, which will set out the judge's account of the offending and may add detail about how it came to light. The second is whether the Met or the policing watchdogs say anything about how Tyrrell was vetted and supervised during his service. Without that, the conviction is a serious criminal case but not yet evidence about whether the post-Everard reforms are working.
