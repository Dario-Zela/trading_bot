Administration approves three wind projects stalled by the Pentagon

In one sentence
The Pentagon has cleared two stalled onshore wind projects, reportedly to win Democratic votes for a bipartisan permitting bill, though a third approval could not be confirmed.

The Trump administration has begun clearing onshore wind projects that the Pentagon had held up, and the clearances appear to be tied to a bipartisan permitting bill. Reporting from Bloomberg, carried by Insurance Journal, says the Pentagon advanced two projects on Thursday, 22 October, one in Maine and one in South Dakota, after a national security review. The Punchbowl News report that prompted this piece describes three approvals. The two-project figure is the one this article could confirm. Punchbowl's original could not be retrieved, so the discrepancy is unresolved.

A backlog behind a security review

The Pentagon's reviews have become a choke point for onshore wind. A person familiar with the matter told Bloomberg that roughly 300 projects are suspended pending Pentagon approval. Industry groups have put the number of stalled projects at about 155 across 24 states, with the Pentagon citing concerns that drones could hide among turbines. In May, 55 House Democrats led by Rep. Seth Magaziner called the process a "de facto moratorium" on wind permits.

The Bloomberg report does not name the Maine and South Dakota projects, their developers or their capacity.

The link to the permitting bill

The clearances partly fulfil an administration promise seen as critical to a bipartisan overhaul of permitting. The package was brokered by White House officials and Sens. Martin Heinrich (D-NM), Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI), Shelley Moore Capito (R-WV) and Mike Lee (R-UT). It aims to speed approvals of energy and infrastructure projects, including solar, wind, and oil and gas.

An anonymous White House official described the clearances as a gesture of good faith and an initial batch, with more expected "in the coming days and weeks." Bloomberg's account says the administration's promise would cover about 30 of the roughly 300 suspended projects.

This is the sense in which the approvals look like a bargaining chip. Democrats had earlier said permitting talks were dead over the administration's wind halt, and the White House has signalled that its offer on stalled wind and solar projects could be withdrawn if Democrats do not agree to a deal. The sourcing for the trade is anonymous, and no lawmaker has been quoted confirming that votes were exchanged for approvals. The pattern is nonetheless consistent: a Pentagon review described as a security matter has moved in step with the legislative calendar.

Reaction and what comes next

Whitehouse was measured. "It's a start, but we are going to need to see a lot more from the administration on wind and solar," he said in a statement.

The Senate is expected to take up the bill after the November midterm elections. It faces opposition from some environmental groups and from progressive House Democrats. Two cleared projects out of roughly 300 are a small share. The test is how many of the promised batch of about 30 are cleared before the Senate returns, and whether Democrats count them as enough.
