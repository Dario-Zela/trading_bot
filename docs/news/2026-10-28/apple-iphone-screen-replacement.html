Apple to replace iPhone Duos affected by screen problem

In one sentence
Apple says it will replace some iPhone Duo folding phones with inner-screen faults, but has given no numbers, cause or formal repair programme.

Apple has said it will replace some units of its new folding phone, the iPhone Duo, after owners reported problems with the inner screen. The announcement came days after the device went on sale.

The company's statement, issued on Monday, asks affected owners to contact AppleCare for a replacement. Apple described the problem as affecting a small number of devices. It did not say how many, and it did not say what causes the fault.

What Apple has and hasn't said

The wording matters. Apple has not announced a formal repair programme or a recall. Coverage so far describes a commitment to swap faulty units case by case through its support channel. That differs from the published, serial-number-checked programmes Apple has run for earlier screen faults, such as the free display replacements for some iPhone X units in 2018.

Reports of the fault centre on the foldable inner display, and some outlets describe screen cracks. Repair specialist iFixit has suggested one possible cause: a stress hotspot created by the central support bar. Apple has not confirmed that explanation, and it remains an outside hypothesis.

One explainer cites a $999 charge for out-of-warranty screen replacement. The text retrieved for this article did not confirm that figure, so it should be treated as unverified.

Why the scale is the question

A replacement offer for a "small number" of units is routine for a company this size, and it costs little if the number really is small. The picture changes if the fault turns out to be built into the design rather than a manufacturing slip. Foldable screens are the part of these phones most prone to wear, and a first-generation hinge and display are an obvious place to look for a systematic weakness.

The timing adds pressure. An IDC analyst quoted in the coverage said Apple's loyal customer base and its retail network, which can swap devices on the spot, should cushion the damage. But a further 28 markets get the phone on 30 October, and the analyst said Apple needs to clarify its warranty position quickly.

What to watch

Three things will show how serious this is: whether Apple publishes a formal programme with eligibility checks, whether it gives a number or a cause, and whether complaints keep arriving once the phone reaches the wider launch markets on 30 October. If the reports stay at a trickle, this will read as an early-production hiccup. If they grow, the question becomes whether replacing units is enough.
