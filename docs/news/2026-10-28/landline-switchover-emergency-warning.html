Landline switchover could cut people off in emergencies, minister warned

In one sentence
MPs have told security minister Dan Jarvis that the move to digital landlines, due to finish by January 2027, could leave people in remote areas unable to call for help in a power cut.

The copper telephone line that has run into British homes for a century has one property the replacement does not: it carries its own power. A landline served from the exchange keeps working when the mains goes down. Its internet-based successor generally does not, because the box in the hall needs electricity. The change is being sold as a like-for-like upgrade. On Tuesday MPs told a minister that for some people it is not.

What MPs told the minister

Security minister Dan Jarvis, who fronts the government's Be Ready resilience campaign, was pressed by MPs from remote constituencies about the move from analogue to digital phone lines. Torcuil Crichton, the Labour MP for the Western Isles, said: "we have major problems with connectivity because of this move from analogue to digital phone lines." He argued that in a storm-driven power cut, people can lose their phone, their broadband and their mobile signal together.

Markus Campbell-Savours, Labour MP for Penrith and Solway, described an elderly person in an isolated Lake District valley who could be left unable to raise an alarm. Julian Lewis, a Conservative, made a different point: the copper network "could have given us useful levels of resilience in an emergency", and he asked what the fallback would be if digital services failed in a cyber attack. MPs from the West Country raised similar worries.

Jarvis called telephone networks "a vital part of our critical national infrastructure". He said he would "look very closely" at Lewis's point and would raise the telecoms concerns with the Department for Culture, Media and Sport. He said the West Country MPs' concerns would be taken up at a resilience summit in Cornwall next month.

Why the copper line was different

The old public switched telephone network (PSTN) fed a small current down the wire from the exchange, enough to run a basic handset. Digital voice runs over broadband, so the router and any in-home equipment need mains power. Without it, calls stop, and that includes 999.

The usual remedy is a battery backup unit. Retail providers have offered packs that keep a landline working for an hour or more. MPs told the minister that this is "simply not adequate" for a multi-day outage of the kind a winter storm can cause in the Highlands, Islands or the Lakes. A battery also does nothing if the street cabinet or mast further up the chain loses power. That is a separate resilience question for the network operators.

What the rules and the deadline say

Landline operators aim to finish the move to internet-based networks by the end of January 2027. That is later than the original end-2025 target, which BT said in 2024 it would miss after providers paused migrations of vulnerable customers following incidents where telecare devices failed. Reports at the time linked some of those failures to deaths.

The government's line is that the analogue network is increasingly unreliable and that the switch will make services more dependable. Be Ready advice says customers who have not yet migrated should contact their provider. It also says vulnerable customers, such as telecare users, must be given a free backup battery unit.

Secondary sources say Ofcom requires providers to keep 999 access available for at least an hour in a power cut, and to offer free backup to people who depend on their landline. Reports differ on whether that free offer covers everyone or only at-risk users. The report of Tuesday's exchange did not mention Ofcom, BT or Openreach, and none of them is quoted. The exact scope of the obligation should be checked against Ofcom's guidance.

What to watch

The test is whether the safety duties are enforced before the copper is retired rather than patched afterwards. Two dates matter: the Cornwall resilience summit next month, and the end-January 2027 target. Jarvis has promised to raise the issue with DCMS. He has not announced any change to the timetable.
