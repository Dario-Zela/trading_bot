UK INFLATION · By M. Halloway

UK inflation rises to 3.5%, above forecasts, as Gulf war lifts fuel prices

UK CPI inflation rose to 3.5% in September, above forecasts, on Gulf-war fuel costs, leaving the Bank of England to weigh an energy shock against its easing path.

UK consumer price inflation rose to 3.5% in the latest reading, above what forecasters had expected, with fuel and energy costs the main drivers. The Guardian attributes the energy component to the Gulf conflict, which has pushed up oil and petrol prices.

The overshoot creates a difficult trade-off for the Bank of England. Energy-led inflation is a supply shock that monetary policy can do little to offset directly, but it still complicates a rate path that markets had been pricing as a gradual easing. Raising rates to contain a price rise that originates in a war-driven fuel spike risks hurting growth, while cutting into a stubborn headline figure risks losing credibility on inflation expectations.

That repricing is already reaching government debt. Gilt pricing is being reassessed as traders weigh a slower path to lower rates against the fiscal cost of higher borrowing. The channels matter beyond the bond market: gilt yields feed into fixed mortgage rates, the cost of government borrowing and, through interest-rate expectations, sterling.

The full article sets out the component breakdown and the consensus figure the reading beat. Watch the Bank's next rate decision and the tone of its inflation commentary, and whether energy prices stay elevated. If they do, the gap between the rate path markets expected and the one the Bank can justify will be the story to follow.
