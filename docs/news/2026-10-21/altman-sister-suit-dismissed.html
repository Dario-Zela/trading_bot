Sam Altman wins dismissal of sister's lawsuit

In one sentence
A Missouri federal judge dismissed Ann Altman's abuse suit against her brother Sam on procedural grounds after she skipped a court-ordered medical exam, without ruling on the allegations.

A federal judge in Missouri has thrown out a lawsuit brought by Ann Altman against her brother Sam Altman, the chief executive of OpenAI. The ruling was reported on 20 October. It did not decide whether her allegations are true. It ended the case because she did not comply with a court order.

Ann Altman sued in January 2025. She accused her brother of sexually abusing her as a child at the family home in Missouri between 1997 and 2006. She said it began when she was three and he was 12. Sam Altman, now 41, has denied the claims. After the suit was filed, he, his mother and his two younger brothers said in a joint statement that the allegations were "entirely untrue" and that she has "mental health challenges".

What the judge decided

Judge Zachary Bluestone dismissed the case on procedural grounds. According to reports, Ann Altman did not attend a court-ordered independent medical examination. That is a routine step when a plaintiff claims psychological harm, because the defendant is entitled to have the claimed injury assessed by someone other than the plaintiff's own doctor. Reports say she missed it on three occasions, and that she had been warned that skipping it could end the case. The judge described what he saw as a pattern of intentional delay.

One report says the dismissal was with prejudice, meaning the claim cannot be refiled. Other coverage does not say either way, so that point is not settled here. The judge also refused Sam Altman's request for attorneys' fees, saying the dismissal was itself a sufficient sanction. He approved Altman's request to withdraw his own counterclaim, which accused his sister of making false statements that damaged his reputation. Reports differ on the exact day of the order, Monday or Tuesday.

The earlier ruling

The dismissal follows an earlier setback for the plaintiff. In March, Bluestone found that her original sexual assault and battery claims had expired under the statute of limitations. He let her refile under a Missouri law covering childhood sexual abuse, and the amended complaint is the one now dismissed.

So the case has now been narrowed twice on process. First, some claims were found to be filed too late. Then the surviving claim failed because the plaintiff did not meet a discovery obligation. Neither ruling addresses what happened in the Altman household. A dismissal like this shows how strictly courts enforce deadlines and court orders, even in cases involving serious allegations. It does not show that the allegations were found false, and it does not show that they were found credible.

What comes next

The main open questions are procedural. Ann Altman can ask the US Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit to review the order, and the court record will show whether she does. Her former lawyer, Santos Perez, withdrew from the case in October. He said afterwards: "I continue to believe in Annie." Whether she has new counsel, and whether the prejudice question is clarified in the order itself, will decide whether this is the end of the litigation.

The ruling has no direct bearing on OpenAI's business or governance. It is a private family dispute involving a prominent executive, and the court's reasoning is ordinary civil procedure.
