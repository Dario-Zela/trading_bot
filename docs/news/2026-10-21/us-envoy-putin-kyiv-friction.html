US envoy to meet Putin as Washington and Kyiv send mixed signals

In one sentence
A US envoy meets Putin after Zelensky says intelligence sharing is restored, leaving open whether Washington's two-track diplomacy with Moscow and Kyiv is coordinated.

A US envoy is due to sit down with Vladimir Putin in Russia on Wednesday, a day after Ukraine's president said American intelligence sharing with Kyiv had been restored. Washington is therefore talking to both sides at once. Whether those conversations are one coordinated effort or two parallel ones is the question Kyiv and its European partners are now asking.

Oslo, then Russia

Volodymyr Zelensky travelled to Oslo on Tuesday to meet Steve Witkoff, Donald Trump's special envoy. Jared Kushner joined remotely, reportedly because he was unwell. Norway's prime minister, Jonas Gahr Støre, said Ukraine had asked for both men to attend, and that the talks would bring together Ukraine, European allies and the United States to work on a peace proposal to put to Moscow.

Afterwards Zelensky said intelligence sharing was working again. Reports quote him as saying "Now it works" and "We are thankful that intelligence works", and describe the early warnings as important when ballistic missiles are about to strike. He also said he had discussed access to SpaceX's Starlink satellite internet with Witkoff, and called the talks productive: "We've been very concrete, and the organization was very positive."

The reports give no figures or timeline for the restoration, and no detail on what had been curtailed or when. Nor did the coverage reviewed here report any formal outcome from Oslo.

Norway's prime minister said Witkoff would travel on to Saint Petersburg. According to AFP, a US official said Kushner would take part in the Putin meeting virtually. The Washington Post describes the trip as an effort to press Putin towards three-way talks.

The friction behind the talks

The Oslo meeting followed a row of more than a week. Trump struck a deal with Putin allowing Russia to sell some diesel into global markets. Ukraine had refused to stop hitting Russian oil refineries, and Trump suggested Zelensky should step aside. Zelensky accused Trump of trying to push Ukraine into surrender. The New York Times, as relayed by Ukrainian outlets, reported that Trump has been trying to persuade Kyiv that holding on to the Donbas is "not worth the effort".

So the picture is mixed. Intelligence sharing is back, a practical sign of cooperation, while the president's public language and the Russian diesel arrangement point the other way. The diesel deal also touches markets directly; diesel prices remain high as Washington presses refiners and Europe on supply.

The evidence does not yet show a breakdown in the Oslo track. Disagreement over refinery strikes, territory and Zelensky's own future is real, but Kyiv sent its president to Oslo and left the talks sounding positive. The friction may be bargaining as much as rupture. That reading rests on one day's statements and could change quickly.

Coordinated or around Kyiv?

The sequence suggests some coordination. Witkoff met Zelensky first and then heads to Putin, and Norway describes the Oslo format as one meant to produce a proposal to present to Russia. Ukraine asked for the envoys to attend. That is not the pattern of a channel run behind Kyiv's back.

But the reporting also leaves room for doubt. No account reviewed here says what Witkoff will carry to Putin, or whether Kyiv has agreed to it. The NYT account of pressure over territory suggests the proposal's content, not its choreography, is what Kyiv should worry about. And fighting has not paused: as the Oslo talks went on, Russian forces hit energy facilities in Kyiv with jet-powered drones.

What to watch

The first marker is the outcome of Witkoff's meeting with Putin, including whether Moscow accepts the idea of three-way talks. The second is what Washington says to Kyiv and European capitals afterwards; Axios reported after an earlier Putin meeting that Witkoff briefed Ukrainian and NATO officials. If that briefing happens and the intelligence flow holds, the coordination reading gains weight. If Trump's rhetoric on the diesel deal and Zelensky's position hardens again, it loses it. Energy, defence and European risk assets will price whichever way it goes.
