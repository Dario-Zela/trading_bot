Premier League window closes with record £125m Fernandez move to Man City

Enzo Fernández, whose £125m move from Chelsea to Manchester City matched the British transfer record on deadline day. — Bryan Berlin (Berlination) / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
In one sentence
Man City's record-equalling £125m deal for Enzo Fernández closed a window where Premier League clubs spent £3.48bn, even as Everton and Chelsea's linked deals for Balogun and Camara both collapsed.

The Premier League's summer transfer window shut at 11pm on Monday with Manchester City completing a £125m signing of Enzo Fernández from Chelsea — a fee that equals the British transfer record set by Liverpool's capture of Alexander Isak barely a year ago. Two other headline moves, Folarin Balogun's switch to Everton and Lamine Camara's to Chelsea, collapsed within hours of each other in scenes that summed up a window long on money and short on certainty.

Fernández's move reunites him with Enzo Maresca, his former Chelsea manager who took over at City in June after Pep Guardiola's departure. Chelsea bought the Argentine from Benfica for £107m in January 2023, also a British record at the time, and sell him now for an £18m profit having decided City's opening bid was too good to knock back. The 25-year-old midfielder signed a five-year contract.

The deal that ate itself

The more instructive story of deadline day was the one that didn't happen. Chelsea had lined up Monaco's Lamine Camara as Fernández's direct replacement, with a written agreement in place on a six-year deal. Everton, meanwhile, had Folarin Balogun arriving at Finch Farm to complete a £40m move from Monaco. When Balogun's medical threw up complications and the striker had a change of heart about the switch, Monaco reversed course and briefly agreed to sell Camara to Chelsea instead. Then Everton talked Balogun back round — and Monaco pulled the plug on Camara a second time, just minutes before the window closed. Chelsea called the club's conduct "deeply unprofessional."

The upshot: two clubs spent deadline day chasing deals that depended on each other, and both walked away with nothing. It's a reminder that even at the top end of a market flush with broadcast and sponsorship money, supply is still finite — sellers can renegotiate their way out of an agreement right up to the final whistle, and buyers with no fallback plan pay for it in empty squad slots.

Spending outpaces the usual math

Premier League clubs spent a combined £3.48bn this window, up from £3.14bn last summer and another record, according to deadline-day tallies. Chelsea's £117m purchase of Morgan Rogers from Aston Villa and City's £116m deal for Elliot Anderson from Nottingham Forest both briefly held the British record before Fernández's fee matched it outright. None of these numbers map cleanly onto matchday revenue or broadcast deals, which is the recurring puzzle of modern football economics: valuations move on scarcity and bidding-war psychology as much as on a player's measurable output, and clubs continue to treat British transfer records as a threshold to clear rather than a ceiling to respect.

The next test of that logic comes quickly. The Premier League's profitability and sustainability rules require clubs to justify this spending against revenue over a rolling three-year period, and with several clubs already close to the limits, expect scrutiny of amortised transfer fees — and possibly disciplinary cases — before the January window reopens.
