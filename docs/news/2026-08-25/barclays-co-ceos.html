Barclays names Mike Joo and Adeel Khan investment bank co-CEOs

In one sentence
Barclays hired BofA's Mike Joo as investment bank co-CEO alongside Adeel Khan, scrapping a four-person leadership structure from 2024.

Barclays is putting an American in charge of dealmaking for the first time in its history, hiring Bank of America's Mike Joo to co-run its investment bank alongside the incumbent, Adeel Khan, from February 2027.

The appointment, confirmed in a Barclays statement and reported first by Bloomberg, is less a changing of the guard than an admission that the previous one didn't work. Joo does not replace Khan — the two will sit side by side on the group executive committee, Joo running investment banking from New York, Khan continuing to oversee global markets from London. What Joo does replace is a four-person leadership structure that chief executive C.S. Venkatakrishnan installed in 2024 and which, by most accounts inside the bank, spread decision-making too thin and accountability thinner still.

A reshuffle, not a rescue

That 2024 structure paired Khan with three others — Taylor Wright and Cathal Deasy running investment banking, and Stephen Dainton overseeing markets alongside Khan. Dainton has since retired. Wright and Deasy, brought in specifically to revive the dealmaking franchise, are staying on until Joo arrives but are expected by people close to the bank to leave shortly after — Bloomberg reports the two had clashed over strategy, one of the frictions the new setup is designed to end.

The timing is notable because the business Joo is inheriting is not obviously broken. Barclays' investment bank posted a return on tangible equity of 16.1% in the second quarter of 2026, up nearly four points on the year, and the group lifted its full-year income guidance to roughly £31.5 billion from an original £30 billion target. Equities income was up 44% in dollar terms, advisory fees up 51%, and equities now make up about 32% of markets revenue versus 22% in 2023. By the numbers Barclays already beat the 12% RoTE target it set itself for 2026, two years early.

Why change a winning hand

Venkatakrishnan's own framing is that the reshuffle is about consolidating gains, not correcting a failure. "Since 2023, the Investment Bank has delivered strong performance, growing revenues and returns," he said, adding that Khan and Joo "will form a strong partnership to deliver an even stronger, more integrated service to our clients." The subtext, according to people familiar with the bank's internal discussions, is that four co-heads made for a diffuse chain of command precisely as the unit was trying to compete for mandates against Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley — banks that do not run their investment banking arms by committee.

Joo brings a résumé built entirely on the US side of that competition: eight years at Credit Suisse, where he co-headed Asia debt capital markets, then two decades at Bank of America rising to co-head of global investment banking, with an earlier stint at Goldman Sachs where he started his career in 1995. His hire continues a pattern of European banks — Barclays among the most aggressive — recruiting senior Wall Street talent to close a dealmaking gap that has persisted since the financial crisis, even as Barclays' own trading and equities businesses have outgrown it.

The appointment still needs regulatory approval before it takes effect. Between now and February, the more immediate signal to watch is what happens to Wright and Deasy — their exit, if and when it comes, will show how much of this reorganisation is genuinely about structure versus a changing of personnel Barclays isn't yet saying out loud.
