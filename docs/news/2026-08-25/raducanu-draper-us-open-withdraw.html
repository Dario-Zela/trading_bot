Raducanu and Draper withdraw from US Open through injury

Emma Raducanu, pictured practising in 2025, withdrew from the US Open with a stress fracture in her right leg. — Hameltion / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Emma Raducanu and Jack Draper are out of the US Open through injury, leaving nine other Britons — led by Katie Boulter — to carry home hopes.

Emma Raducanu and Jack Draper will watch this year's US Open from home, both ruled out by injuries that have turned 2026 into a season to forget for British tennis's two brightest hopes.

Raducanu, the 2021 champion at Flushing Meadows, withdrew after a stress fracture in her right lower leg — sustained during the grass-court season — failed to heal in time. It is the second successive Grand Slam she has missed; the same injury forced her out of Wimbledon in July. The 23-year-old has spent recent weeks in a protective boot, and while she posted footage on Instagram of a training break in Switzerland, her medical team advised against attempting a return in New York. It caps a wretched year that also included a foot injury before the season began and a two-and-a-half-month lay-off with a post-viral illness. Her ranking, already down to 49, is expected to slip further, and she was left out of Great Britain's Billie Jean King Cup Finals squad for September.

Draper's absence is arguably the heavier blow. The world number seven reached the US Open semi-finals in 2024 and was seeded to go deep again this year, but a long-running bone injury to his left arm — first flagged more than a season ago — has restricted him to sporadic appearances all year. His withdrawal means he will finish 2026 without having played a single Grand Slam match, a striking reversal for a player who looked, twelve months ago, like Britain's clearest men's contender since Andy Murray.

A thinner contingent presses on

Their absences leave a smaller British group to carry hopes at Flushing Meadows. Nine Britons began campaigns this week: Katie Boulter goes into the main draw off the back of a solid US hard-court swing, including a win over Katie Volynets in Cincinnati, and will be chasing a repeat of her 2023 third-round run — her best Grand Slam result. Francesca Jones starts in qualifying in form, having won back-to-back WTA 125 titles in Rome, and is joined there by Harriet Dart, Heather Watson, Jodie Burrage and Katie Swan. On the men's side, Cam Norrie, Arthur Fery and Jan Choinski all have direct main-draw places inside the world's top 90, while Jacob Fearnley, Billy Harris and several others attempt to qualify.

The Lawn Tennis Association, which confirmed both withdrawals, framed the story as one of depth rather than crisis: British tennis has more players inside the world's top 250 than at almost any point in the professional era. But depth is not the same as star power, and losing two seeded, box-office names before a ball is struck changes how the tournament reads from a UK vantage point — fewer marquee matches, less certainty of a run into the second week.

Attention now turns to whether Boulter or Norrie can push into the second week to offset the absences, and to Raducanu and Draper's fitness updates ahead of the Asian hard-court swing in September — the next point at which either might realistically return to competition.
