HSBC to wind down German transaction-services unit, cutting 320 jobs

HSBC's German operations, which have shrunk steadily since the sale of its private-banking arm to BNP Paribas in 2025. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
HSBC will shut its German transaction-services arm and cut 320 jobs by 2028, the latest step in a $2bn cost drive that has already sold off German private banking and Malta.

HSBC is closing its German transaction-services business, cutting roughly 320 jobs by 2028 in the latest retreat from a market the London-based bank has been steadily exiting for two years.

The unit, run out of Düsseldorf, provides securities processing, custody and administration services to institutional clients. HSBC said 300 roles at HSBC Transaction Services GmbH and a further 20 at HSBC Service Company Germany GmbH will be phased out, with the wind-down handled in what the bank called a "socially responsible" manner — European-speak for a process built around voluntary exits and notice periods rather than abrupt layoffs. A spokesperson said the bank is "focused on strengthening its leadership position and market share in the areas where it has competitive advantages," which, read plainly, means German custody banking no longer qualifies.

A pattern, not an isolated decision

Düsseldorf is not the first German business HSBC has walked away from this cycle. The bank completed the sale of its German private-banking arm to BNP Paribas in October 2025, moving roughly 120 staff and their clients to the French rival. Around the same time, HSBC sold its French retail banking business and has since said it will cut 348 jobs — about a tenth of its French headcount — through a voluntary redundancy scheme. Malta has followed a similar arc: after a strategic review, HSBC's continental European arm signed a definitive agreement in December 2025 to sell its 70% stake in HSBC Bank Malta to CrediaBank for €200m, a deal expected to close by the end of this year.

The throughline is chief executive Georges Elhedery, who since taking over in September 2024 has pushed a restructuring built around concentrating capital in Asia and the Middle East while shedding lower-return Western European businesses. HSBC set out to cut $1.5bn from its annual cost base by the end of 2026; having hit that target early, it raised the goal to $2bn in July, funded from the same $1.8bn restructuring budget already earmarked for 2025 and 2026.

Why transaction services, why now

Custody and securities processing are scale businesses — profitable mainly for banks with enough assets under administration to spread fixed technology and compliance costs thin. HSBC's German transaction-services book, built up over decades through acquisitions including the old Trinkaus & Burkhardt franchise, never reached that scale domestically once the private-banking clients that fed it were sold off. Winding it down removes a cost centre that had become harder to justify once its natural client base moved to a competitor.

The move also reflects a broader retreat by global banks from continental European transaction banking, where margins have been squeezed by low-rate-era pricing pressure even as regulatory and reporting costs have risen. Rivals with genuine scale in custody — BNY, State Street, Deutsche Bank's own securities-services arm — are the more likely destinations for clients displaced by HSBC's exit, rather than domestic German challengers.

What to watch

The Malta sale to CrediaBank is due to close by year-end, and HSBC has flagged Bahrain and South Africa as further candidates for divestment under the same review. Elhedery's next test comes with third-quarter results in late October, when analysts will look for confirmation that the raised $2bn savings target is being banked without the revenue leakage that often follows aggressive European retrenchment.
