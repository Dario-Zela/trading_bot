New Manchester City signings say club is 'desperate' to return to winning ways

In one sentence
Rúben Dias says City's £458m summer rebuild has brought in players 'desperate to win' as the club looks to move past a trophyless Premier League stretch under new boss Enzo Maresca.

Manchester City spent last season chasing shadows. A second successive year without the Premier League title — the first such stretch of Pep Guardiola's decade in charge — was patched over with an FA Cup and EFL Cup double, but by May the mood at the Etihad was one of transition rather than triumph. Guardiola left in the summer after ten years and 20 trophies. Enzo Maresca arrived from Chelsea. And City tore up most of the midfield that had carried them through the most dominant period in English football history.

Rúben Dias, handed the captaincy by Maresca and now the dressing room's senior voice, says the players brought in to replace that generation understand exactly what they signed up for.

"So many people have left and so many winners have left, and now new people have joined with a lot of ambition and people that are desperate to win."

A record-breaking rebuild

The scale of the turnover explains why Dias felt the need to say it out loud. City broke their transfer record twice in one window — first for Newcastle's Elliot Anderson, then for Chelsea's Enzo Fernández, who arrived for £125m to succeed 2024 Ballon d'Or winner Rodri in central midfield. Rodri himself left for Barcelona in a £51.3m deal after seven trophy-laden years and 298 appearances. Bernardo Silva and John Stones, two more fixtures of the Guardiola era, departed as their contracts ran down. In total City spent a Premier League-record £458m, recouping more than £300m in sales, and added Ayyoub Bouaddi and Iliman Ndiaye alongside Anderson and Fernández to rework an attack and midfield that had looked tired by the end of last season.

"They are joining and full of ambition and with all the dreams in the world," Dias said. It's the kind of line a captain is contractually obliged to produce about his new teammates, but the context gives it some teeth: City are trying to replace a Ballon d'Or winner and two decorated internationals inside a single window, while simultaneously bedding in a manager who has never won a major trophy in England.

Maresca's imprint

Dias was equally keen to talk up the early impact of Maresca, who spent time as Guardiola's assistant during the 2018-19 treble season before spells in charge of Leicester and Chelsea. The Italian's methods, by Dias's account, are already visible in training.

"There's obviously many things from training session to some details about the way we press, details about the way we defend, and that make all of these Enzo's idea. I feel like everything is making a lot of sense. Everyone is buying into the idea. Everyone's excited about it and there is a lot of joy in training, which is massively important."

City have made the ideal start on the pitch to back up the words — four straight wins across the Premier League and Champions League to open the season, the kind of form that buys a new manager time and quiets any grumbling about a squad rebuilt on the fly. Dias himself, asked about adapting to a more prominent leadership role after Silva's departure, kept it simple: "It goes down to trying to be the best version of yourself, and in terms of that, nothing has changed."

What's next

Four wins from four is an easy sample size to point to; it says little about how City cope once the fixture list toughens and Maresca's ideas meet a Premier League defence that has had time to study them. The manager's methods, and the new-look midfield's chemistry, face a sterner examination in October, when City's Champions League group games and a run of league fixtures against sides who finished above the bottom half start to bite. Dias's armband, and his claim that the dressing room has bought in, will be judged against results rather than quotes by then.
