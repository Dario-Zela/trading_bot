HSBC cuts Stellantis to Reduce as US inventory glut returns

A Jeep dealership lot — the kind of unsold-inventory overhang HSBC says is building up again across Stellantis's U.S. dealer network. — Christopher Ziemnowicz / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
In one sentence
HSBC downgraded Stellantis to Reduce, citing a return of the U.S. dealer inventory pileup that forced deep price cuts and production halts in 2024.

HSBC has cut Stellantis to Reduce from Hold, slashing its price target to €4 from €5.50 and pointing to a problem the automaker thought it had put behind it: too many unsold cars sitting on U.S. dealer lots. Shares in the Jeep, Ram and Chrysler parent fell 2.68% intraday on the call, trading near their 52-week low of €4.83, a long way from the €10.49 high the stock touched a year ago.

In one sentence: HSBC downgraded Stellantis to Reduce, citing a return of the U.S. dealer inventory pileup that forced deep price cuts and production halts in 2024.

The numbers behind the call are specific. HSBC analyst Renato Neves flagged U.S. dealer inventory running at 93 days' supply in June, roughly 120,000 vehicles more than a year earlier — a build-up the bank warned could force Stellantis to repeat the steep discounting and factory curtailments it used in 2024 to work through a similarly bloated lot. HSBC also pointed to 19 vehicle recalls covering 2.5 million units so far in 2026 as a compounding drag on the brand's standing with buyers.

A turnaround still finding its footing

The downgrade lands awkwardly for chief executive Antonio Filosa, who took over in June 2025 and has spent the year since trying to stop the bleeding. The company posted a $26.3 billion net loss for 2025, driven largely by charges tied to the very inventory and recall problems HSBC is now revisiting. Filosa answered with a restructuring built around 60 new models by 2030, and the first-quarter numbers gave the plan some credibility: adjusted operating income turned positive, EPS of $0.246 beat estimates of $0.083, and U.S. sales rose 4% year-on-year to 305,902 units even as the broader American light-vehicle market fell 6-6.5%. Ram led that gain; Jeep, historically the group's best-selling U.S. brand, has been slower to recover.

HSBC's note suggests that improvement was more cyclical than structural. Even with volumes ahead of the market, dealers are still carrying more metal than they can move at current prices — which is exactly the condition that preceded the 2024 clear-out. The bank cut its 2026 adjusted operating income forecast for the company by more than half, well below Stellantis's own low-single-digit margin guidance. The stock has now lost close to 39% of its value since Filosa's appointment, even as he has talked up North America as the engine of the recovery.

A glut at one of the world's three largest automakers is not a company-specific curiosity. Dealer inventory is one of the more reliable early-warning signs for consumer demand, and Stellantis's overhang sits alongside a U.S. new-car market that HSBC's own data show contracting industry-wide this year. If discounting returns to clear lots, it pressures margins across the sector, not just at Stellantis — rivals competing for the same buyers typically have to match the price cuts.

Stellantis reports first-half results on July 30, the next point where the inventory question gets tested against hard numbers rather than analyst estimates. Investors will be watching dealer-days-supply figures and any signal on pricing discipline as closely as the headline earnings line.
