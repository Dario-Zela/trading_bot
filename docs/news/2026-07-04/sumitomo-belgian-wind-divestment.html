Sumitomo to divest stakes in three Belgian offshore wind projects

Turbines and support vessels at the Bligh Bank site off Belgium's coast, location of the Nobelwind project. — Askjell Raudøy / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Sumitomo is selling its stakes in three Belgian offshore wind farms — two to existing partner JERA Nex BP, one to a Belgian investor consortium.

Sumitomo Corporation is stepping back from Belgium's offshore wind coast. The Japanese trading house has agreed to sell its stakes in three wind farms off the Belgian coast — Nobelwind, Northwester 2 and Northwind — in a pair of deals that hand tighter control of the North Sea projects to a smaller set of owners.

The larger transaction sends Sumitomo's holdings in Nobelwind and Northwester 2 to JERA Nex BP, the London-based joint venture between Japan's JERA and oil major bp that already co-owned both projects and operates them from a hub in Ostend. Sumitomo is selling its 30% stake in the 219-megawatt Northwester 2, taking JERA Nex BP to full ownership, and its 39.02% stake in the 165-megawatt Nobelwind, lifting the venture's holding there to 80.1%. Separately, Sumitomo's stake in the 216-megawatt Northwind project is going to Publiwind, a consortium of Belgian investment firms and public institutions — a sale that keeps that asset in Belgian hands rather than consolidating it with an existing international operator. Combined, the three projects account for roughly 600 megawatts of capacity, enough to power several hundred thousand Belgian homes.

Consolidation, not retreat from renewables

Sumitomo frames the sales as part of its Medium-Term Management Plan 2026, a resource-reallocation exercise rather than a verdict on offshore wind itself. The company has held stakes in these projects since construction — Nobelwind since 2017, Northwester 2 since 2020 — and says both delivered stable operations and profitability throughout. One of the two deals is expected to book its profit or loss in Sumitomo's fiscal 2025 accounts, with the remainder landing in fiscal 2026 forecasts once the Northwind sale to Publiwind clears regulatory approval.

That framing matters because it distinguishes this from a Japanese pullback on European renewables. JERA Nex BP is not a new entrant buying its way into the market; it is an incumbent shareholder tightening its grip on assets it already built and runs. A JERA Nex BP executive described the deal as reinforcing "an attractive portfolio of assets that we operate and know very well," language that reads as continuity rather than a fresh strategic bet. The Northwind sale to a Belgian consortium, meanwhile, suggests domestic capital has appetite for operating wind assets once the original industrial developer exits — a pattern likely to recur as Belgium's first wave of offshore projects, built in the early 2010s, reaches the point where original investors look to rotate capital elsewhere.

Neither party has disclosed the value of either transaction, which limits how much can be read into the price Sumitomo accepted versus what it paid to build these positions in the first place. Regulatory clearance for the Publiwind sale is the next concrete marker, expected to complete within Sumitomo's current fiscal year; how briskly that approval moves may say more about Belgian appetite for further ownership consolidation in its offshore fleet than the transaction value itself would.
