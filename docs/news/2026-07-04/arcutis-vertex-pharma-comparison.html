Arcutis or Vertex: which pharma stock wins in 2026?

Growth story or cash machine: Arcutis and Vertex represent opposite ends of pharma's risk spectrum. — LadyofProcrastination / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Arcutis's revenue nearly doubled and Vertex kept printing $3bn-plus in free cash flow — but neither valuation looks obviously out of line without a catalyst to force convergence.

Two pharmaceutical companies reported very different kinds of good news this year. Arcutis Biotherapeutics, a small dermatology specialist, nearly doubled its revenue. Vertex Pharmaceuticals, a cystic-fibrosis giant forty times its size, kept doing what it has done for years: printing cash. The contrast has become a stock-picker's parlor game — growth story versus cash machine — but the numbers show less of a clean trade-off than the framing suggests.

Arcutis closed 2025 with total revenue of $376.1 million, up 91% from $196.5 million the year before, driven almost entirely by ZORYVE (roflumilast), its topical treatment for psoriasis, atopic dermatitis and seborrheic dermatitis. Net product revenue from ZORYVE alone rose 123% to $372.1 million. The net loss narrowed sharply too, to $16.1 million from $140 million in 2024. Momentum carried into 2026: first-quarter net product revenue hit $105.4 million, up 65% year over year, and the company posted its first quarter of positive operating cash flow — a modest $2.2 million, but a milestone for a business that has spent most of its life burning through financing rounds. Arcutis has reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $480 million to $495 million in net product revenue.

Vertex, by contrast, does not have a growth-versus-profitability story to sort out — it has both, just at a slower pace. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $12 billion, up 10%, with net income of $4 billion, a 32.7% margin, and free cash flow of $3.2 billion. The first quarter of 2026 brought $2.99 billion in revenue, up 8%, with non-GAAP earnings of $4.47 a share beating the Street's $4.33 estimate even as headline revenue came in just under forecast. The core cystic-fibrosis franchise — Trikafta/Kaftrio at $2.35 billion and the newer Alyftrek at $424 million — still supplies the bulk of sales, but newer entrants Casgevy (gene therapy) and Journavx (a non-opioid pain drug) contributed more than a quarter of the quarter's revenue growth despite generating just $72 million combined. Vertex ended the quarter with $13 billion in cash after $344 million of buybacks and reaffirmed full-year guidance of $12.95 billion to $13.1 billion.

What the multiples actually say

The market prices this divergence unevenly. Arcutis carries a market capitalization near $3.3 billion against Vertex's $134 billion — a scale gap that dwarfs the growth gap. On forward earnings, Arcutis trades around 143 times projected profit, reflecting a business barely past breakeven, versus roughly 26 to 31 times for Vertex, whose earnings are large, stable and unlikely to disappoint by much. But flip to price-to-sales, a fairer gauge for a company not yet consistently profitable, and the premium narrows: Arcutis trades near 8 times sales against Vertex's 10.5 times. By that measure, the market is not obviously overpaying for Arcutis's growth, nor is it obviously discounting Vertex's stability.

What the comparison does not offer is a catalyst that would force one valuation toward the other. Arcutis's re-rating depends on sustaining triple-digit ZORYVE growth long enough to reach durable profitability, a multi-year proposition with patent litigation against Teva and competition from AbbVie and Pfizer still unresolved. Vertex's re-rating, if any, depends on non-CF drugs like Casgevy and Journavx scaling from rounding errors into real revenue lines — a slower, less dramatic process that shows up one earnings call at a time.

The next test for both arrives with second-quarter results: Arcutis's ability to hold ZORYVE's revenue trajectory without the seasonal dip that hit its first quarter, and Vertex's non-CF portfolio proving it can move the needle rather than just the narrative.
