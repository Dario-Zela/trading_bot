Boeing, Lockheed and Palantir backed both Trump anniversary initiatives, report finds

Lockheed Martin's headquarters in Bethesda, Maryland — one of 14 federal contractors CNBC found sponsoring both America250 and Freedom 250. — Coolcaesar / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
CNBC found 14 major federal contractors, including Boeing, Lockheed and Palantir, quietly backed both of Trump's rival 250th-anniversary initiatives.

Washington will spend all of 2026 celebrating one anniversary through two rival parties, and corporate America has apparently decided the safest move is to show up to both.

A CNBC analysis published this week found 14 major companies with federal contracts or pending business before Washington — Boeing, Lockheed Martin and Palantir among them — have donated to both America250, the congressionally chartered nonprofit that has spent nearly a decade planning the country's 250th birthday, and Freedom 250, the rival, White House-aligned outfit that sprang up after President Trump returned to office wanting his own stamp on the festivities. Also on the dual-donor list: Northrop Grumman, Oracle, RTX, Deloitte, Exiger, John Deere, SAP, Scotts Miracle-Gro, Phorm Energy, UFC and United Airlines.

None of this is illegal, and CNBC's reporting found no evidence the sponsorships were tied to specific government decisions. But for a set of companies whose fortunes hinge on contracts, regulatory approvals and the occasional merger clearance, backing both horses in a political turf war reads less like civic generosity and more like a hedge.

A birthday with two party planners

America250 traces back to a bipartisan commission Congress created in 2016 to plan the semiquincentennial. Freedom 250 is newer and squarely Trump's: a public-private partnership the White House launched in December that has run the summer's highest-profile events, including a UFC card outside the White House in June, a Great American State Fair on the National Mall, and Friday's record-chasing fireworks show that organizers billed as the largest pyrotechnic display in history, drawing crowds capped near 150,000 on the Washington Monument grounds alone.

Freedom 250 is structured as a subsidiary of the National Park Foundation, and its sponsorship tiers run from $500,000 to more than $10 million, according to fundraising materials — with top-tier donors offered VIP seating, private receptions, a "historic photo opportunity" with the president, and in some cases speaking roles at July Fourth programming. That fee schedule has drawn its own scrutiny: a House Natural Resources Committee report released Thursday, built on whistleblower interviews and internal documents, alleges some donors who believed they were giving to the bipartisan America250 were instead handed Freedom 250's wire instructions — routing and account numbers included — redirecting their money to the Trump-aligned entity. Freedom 250 spokesperson Danielle Alvarez called the allegations "categorically false."

Betting on both outcomes

Set against that backdrop, the logic of a defense contractor writing checks to both organizations gets easier to read. Boeing, Lockheed and Palantir all carry substantial exposure to federal procurement decisions that outlast any single administration's messaging priorities. Sponsoring only the president's preferred vehicle risks nothing if he prevails politically, but it looks partisan if the pendulum swings; sponsoring only the bipartisan legacy body risks looking like a snub to a White House that awards contracts. Funding both spends comparatively little to protect against either outcome.

"The concern is that this celebration appears to offer access to the president while some of those companies have business before his administration," Bruce Freed, president of the Center for Political Accountability, told CNBC. Freed, who has spent years advising companies on political spending disclosure, added that for a firm carrying "federal contracts, regulatory issues or merger interests, being in the room with the president can be worth far more than the sponsorship itself."

Of the 14 companies named, only John Deere responded to CNBC's request for comment, and it sidestepped the question of dual sponsorship entirely, saying it was proud to celebrate Americans whose work helped "build power, feed and sustain" the country. Boeing, Lockheed, Palantir and the rest stayed quiet.

Why the ethics question outlasts the party

The stock-market read on this is thin by design — nobody is suggesting a fireworks sponsorship moves a defense budget line. The relevance is further downstream: this is a live case study in how corporations manage political risk when a norm-breaking structure emerges alongside a traditional one, and it hands ethics watchdogs and possibly the Government Accountability Office a fact pattern worth testing. If the House Natural Resources Committee's wire-fraud allegations gain traction, or if a future GAO or inspector-general review examines whether donor tiers to Freedom 250 correlated with contract or regulatory outcomes for sponsors, the reputational cost to companies that hedged here could exceed the sponsorship checks by a wide margin.

For now, the marker to watch is procedural rather than financial: whether the Natural Resources Committee's interim report triggers a formal referral, and whether any of the 14 companies face direct questions in a congressional hearing rather than a press inquiry they can decline to answer.
