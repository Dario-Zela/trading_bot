TotalEnergies sells 8.5% Marjoram gas stake to Inpex

In one sentence
TotalEnergies is selling its non-operated stake in Malaysia's developing Marjoram gas field to Inpex for $350m, tidying its portfolio rather than retreating.

TotalEnergies is selling out of a Malaysian gas project that has not yet produced a single cubic foot of gas. The French major has agreed to hand its interest in the Marjoram field, off the coast of Sarawak, to Japan's Inpex for $350m, in a deal that fits a pattern rather than breaking one: Total keeping the fields it runs, and letting go of the ones it doesn't.

In one sentence: TotalEnergies is selling its non-operated stake in Malaysia's developing Marjoram gas field to Inpex for $350m, tidying its portfolio rather than retreating from the country.

What's actually changing hands

The numbers look contradictory until the structure is explained. Total is selling an 85% interest in Block 2E, the offshore licence that hosts Marjoram — but because Marjoram straddles Block 2E and adjacent acreage under a unitisation agreement, that 85% block interest works out to only an 8.5% economic share of the Marjoram accumulation itself. Petronas Carigali, Malaysia's state operator, holds the other 15% of Block 2E and remains a partner regardless of who owns Total's slice.

Marjoram was discovered in 2014, a deepwater sour-gas find that Shell is developing jointly with the neighbouring Rosmari field as a single project — Rosmari-Marjoram — under Shell's operatorship. First gas is targeted for the end of 2026, with output expected to reach around 800 million cubic feet a day once the project, including a purpose-built solar-powered platform, is fully on stream. Total will have no say in how fast that happens either way: it was never the operator, and its holding was always a minority one.

Pruning, not retreating

Nicolas Terraz, Total's exploration and production chief, framed the sale as routine housekeeping: "This agreement is fully aligned with our strategy of actively managing our portfolio and prioritising material positions." That is corporate language for a simple idea — Total would rather hold assets it operates and controls than sit as a passive minority investor waiting on someone else's project timeline.

The sale looks less like a retreat from Malaysia than a reallocation within it. Total still holds interests in 17 offshore blocks there, including two it operates directly, and recently added the SapuraOMV Upstream assets to become the country's third-largest gas producer. It has a $2.2bn renewable-energy joint venture with Masdar in the works and a carbon-storage project with Petronas and Mitsui in the Malay Basin. Set against that footprint, walking away from an 8.5% slice of someone else's gas field barely registers as a strategic decision — it is a rounding exercise.

For Inpex, the calculus runs the other way. The Tokyo-based producer has been building out Southeast Asian gas positions under its Vision 2035 plan, and a stake in a field that is about to start producing, rather than one still being explored, is exactly the kind of lower-risk addition that strategy calls for.

The transaction is expected to close in the coming months, subject to customary approvals. The next marker worth watching is less the paperwork than the reservoir: first gas from Rosmari-Marjoram, pencilled in for late 2026, will be the point at which Inpex's bet — and Total's decision to cash out ahead of it — gets its first real test.
