Moderna and Merck's mRNA vaccine clears Phase 3 in world first for cancer therapy

Moderna's Cambridge, Massachusetts headquarters, where the mRNA cancer vaccine program was developed — Matthew Hill / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Moderna and Merck's personalized mRNA vaccine plus Keytruda hit both endpoints in a melanoma Phase 3 trial, the first such win for an mRNA cancer therapy, and Moderna stock jumped 132%.

For twenty years, the pitch for personalized cancer vaccines has been consistent and consistently unproven: sequence a patient's tumor, find the mutations that make it foreign to the immune system, and build a shot that teaches T cells to hunt those mutations down. Wednesday, that pitch cleared its highest bar yet. Moderna and Merck said their jointly developed vaccine, paired with Merck's blockbuster immunotherapy Keytruda, met both the main and a key secondary goal of a Phase 3 trial in melanoma — the first time any mRNA cancer therapy has succeeded at that stage of testing. Moderna's stock, battered for years since its Covid-vaccine peak, surged as much as 177% intraday before settling to close up roughly 132%, its best single-day move on record.

A vaccine built from your own tumor

The therapy, known as intismeran autogene (also called mRNA-4157 or V940), does not work like a conventional vaccine. There is no shelf-stable, one-size-fits-all dose. Instead, doctors sequence a patient's surgically removed tumor, identify up to 34 "neoantigens" — the specific mutated proteins that mark that cancer as abnormal — and encode them into a single custom strand of mRNA. That mRNA is wrapped in a lipid nanoparticle, the same delivery technology behind Moderna's Covid shot, and injected to train the immune system to recognize and attack any cancer cells left behind after surgery. Manufacturing takes about six weeks per patient.

In the Phase 3 trial, called INTerpath-001, 1,137 patients with high-risk, surgically resected stage IIB–IV melanoma were randomized to receive either the personalized vaccine alongside Keytruda or Keytruda alone. Earlier Phase 2b data from the same combination had shown a 49% reduction in the risk of recurrence or death, and a 59% reduction in the risk of the cancer spreading to distant organs, compared with Keytruda by itself. The companies said Wednesday's interim analysis confirmed that signal held up in the larger, randomized Phase 3 population: recurrence-free survival, the trial's primary endpoint, was met, as was the secondary endpoint of distant metastasis-free survival. No new safety concerns emerged. The trial continues to track overall survival, and full data will be presented at a coming medical meeting rather than released in full Wednesday.

Why the sector has been waiting for this

Melanoma was a logical proving ground: it carries an unusually high mutation burden, giving the vaccine plenty of neoantigens to target, and Keytruda is already a standard treatment for high-risk cases after surgery. But the significance reaches beyond one skin cancer. "For many years, the idea of creating an mRNA treatment designed specifically for an individual patient's cancer was aspirational," Moderna chief executive Stéphane Bancel said. "We are now helping turn that vision into a reality." In a separate interview, Bancel went further, telling CNN the result was "as big a day for humanity" as November 16, 2020 — the date Moderna announced its Covid vaccine had worked in late-stage trials. Dean Li, president of Merck Research Laboratories, said individualized neoantigen therapies "have the potential to redefine how patients with completely resected stage IIB-IV melanoma are treated."

The result matters for the entire mRNA field because it is the first proof, at the most rigorous stage of clinical testing, that the platform behind the Covid vaccines can be redirected at cancer rather than infectious disease. Moderna and Merck have said the same neoantigen approach could eventually be tested in kidney, bladder and pancreatic cancers. A rival personalized vaccine, BioNTech and Genentech's autogene cevumeran, is following a similar path through earlier-stage trials in pancreatic and colorectal cancer — meaning Wednesday's result also validates the broader category two of the biggest mRNA players are racing to own.

Markets reprice mRNA's second act

The stock reaction was the sharpest signal of how far expectations had fallen. Moderna shares had been trading roughly 64% below their August 2021 peak, as Covid-vaccine demand collapsed and the company burned cash on a pipeline investors had grown skeptical would ever pay off. Wednesday's rally added tens of billions of dollars to Moderna's market value, pushing it toward roughly $62 billion, while Merck — a much larger company at close to $371 billion — rose only modestly, since Keytruda's role in the combination was already well understood by the market. William Blair upgraded Moderna to Outperform, citing what analysts called a clear path to revenue beyond infectious disease for the first time. The stakes are particularly high for Merck: Keytruda, its top-selling drug, faces a patent cliff in 2028, and a proprietary combination therapy that only works with Merck's own immunotherapy gives the company a way to defend that franchise once generic competition arrives.

What happens next

Wednesday's release was a topline announcement, not a full data set — the companies have not yet disclosed the actual percentage improvement in recurrence-free survival from the Phase 3 trial itself, only that it was statistically significant. That fuller data set, due at an upcoming medical conference, will be the next real test of the market's enthusiasm. Regulatory submissions are expected to follow; Moderna and Merck previously sought accelerated approval based on Phase 2b data in 2024 and were turned down, meaning full Phase 3 results will need to carry the case this time. If they hold up, a commercial launch could follow as soon as next year, in what would be the first approved personalized cancer vaccine on the market — and the moment mRNA stopped being a pandemic technology and became an oncology one.
