Prince Harry and Meghan reported to be moving back to the UK

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex at Sandringham in December 2017, before their 2020 departure from royal duties. — Mark Jones / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Harry and Meghan reportedly plan to relocate to a private home outside London within weeks, but Buckingham Palace says their non-working royal status is unchanged.

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex are coming home — sort of.

Prince Harry and Meghan are planning to relocate from California to a private residence outside London within weeks, according to reports first published by The Telegraph and confirmed by outlets including CNN, NBC News and France 24. Their children, Archie, 7, and Lilibet, 5, have reportedly already been enrolled at British schools and are expected to start classes in September. It would be the family's first UK base since they gave up the lease on Frogmore Cottage, their Windsor home, in 2023.

The couple will keep their Montecito mansion and a holiday property in Portugal, and insiders describe the UK move as a new base rather than a permanent return. Still, it marks a striking reversal for a couple who left Britain in 2020 declaring their intention to "step back" from royal life and build a new one on the other side of the Atlantic — a rupture so sharp it produced a Netflix docuseries, a bestselling memoir, and years of public sniping between Montecito and the Palace.

What's driving it

The timing points to King Charles. Harry and Meghan brought Archie and Lilibet to see the King in July — the children's first reunion with their grandfather since the late Queen's Platinum Jubilee in 2022 — and multiple reports say Charles was only told of the relocation plan this past Sunday, suggesting it wasn't discussed during that visit but decided soon after. The King is being treated for an undisclosed cancer, and Harry has spoken publicly and pointedly about the uncertainty that creates: he told the BBC in February 2024 that he didn't know "how much longer my father has." People briefed on the plans say Charles "welcomes the opportunity" to see more of the family privately.

Still on the outside

Whatever warmth is driving the move, Buckingham Palace was quick to draw a line under it. The Palace confirmed the relocation but stated plainly that there is no change to the Sussexes' status: they remain non-working members of the royal family, a position unchanged since the terms Harry and Meghan agreed with the late Queen in 2020. There's no indication of a return to official duties, a shared calendar of engagements, or any softening of the arrangement that saw them give up their HRH working titles and military patronages.

That distinction matters for how the move should be read. A change of postcode is not a change of standing. Harry and Meghan will be neighbours again, not colleagues — private citizens who happen to live nearer to Windsor, seeing family at Christmas and school pickups rather than State Banquets.

What's next

The location of the new home and the children's school is being kept private, so the next visible marker will simply be September — Archie and Lilibet starting term at a British school for the first time. Beyond that, the question the tabloids will chase is whether closer geography softens the estrangement further, or whether "non-working" turns out to be the Palace's final word on the subject.
