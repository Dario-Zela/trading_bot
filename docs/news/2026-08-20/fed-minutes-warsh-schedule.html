Fed minutes show split on rate path as Warsh keeps leaner meeting schedule

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who took the gavel in May 2026, revived his proposal for a six-meeting annual schedule in the July minutes. — Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
In one sentence
Fed held rates 9-3 in July with three officials pushing for a hike, as staff cut their growth call and Warsh's six-meeting plan stayed on ice for 2026.

The Federal Reserve released minutes on Wednesday from its July 28–29 meeting, and the headline number was a hold: the Federal Open Market Committee voted 9–3 to keep the federal funds rate at 3.5%–3.75%. The more telling number is the 3. Three regional bank presidents — Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari and Dallas's Lorie Logan — dissented in favour of a quarter-point increase, the largest dissent bloc of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's young tenure. None came from the Board of Governors in Washington.

The minutes show the disagreement ran deeper than the vote tally. Several participants who ultimately backed the hold said price pressures looked "broad based" across goods and services and argued for a more restrictive stance; a couple of the dissenters went further, suggesting financial conditions may not yet be tight enough to bring inflation back to the Fed's 2% target. Total PCE inflation ran at 4.1% in May, core PCE at 3.4% — both comfortably above target, with tariff pass-through, Middle East-linked energy costs and AI-buildout demand cited as the drivers keeping the number sticky.

Set against that hawkish undercurrent, the Fed's own staff trimmed their growth call. The economic outlook prepared for July came in "a touch weaker" than June's, even as the unemployment rate — 4.2% in June — was seen holding near its longer-run estimate and payroll growth stayed above last year's pace. Staff also flagged "notable" financial vulnerabilities, pointing to elevated asset valuations and hedge-fund leverage near all-time highs across strategies.

A leaner calendar, for now

Warsh, who took over from Jerome Powell in May, also used the meeting to revive an idea he floated earlier in the summer: cutting the FOMC's schedule from eight meetings a year to six, spaced roughly every two months. He argued the change would let more data accumulate between decisions and give the Committee — and its staff — more room for "strategic monetary policy issues" rather than reactive tweaks. The Committee reached no conclusion, and Warsh confirmed the 2026 calendar is unchanged: meetings resume September 15–16, then October 27–28 and December 8–9.

The net effect of the minutes was to leave traders where they largely already stood. Futures pricing after the release put the odds of a September hold near 65%, per CME FedWatch data, while the probability of at least one hike before year-end held around 70% — up slightly on the week, a reflection of how many officials are on record worrying about inflation even while voting to wait. With growth cooling by degrees and price pressures broad enough to keep three presidents pushing for tighter policy, the Fed's message is less a signal than a placeholder: no move yet, but no one is ruling one out.

The next real test comes with July's jobs and inflation data ahead of the September 15–16 meeting, which carries a fresh Summary of Economic Projections — the first chance to see whether Warsh's committee is any closer to consensus, or simply keeping the argument on hold.
