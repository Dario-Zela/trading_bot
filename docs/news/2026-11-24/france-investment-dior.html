'Choose France' pledges near €15bn; Dior board backs Arnault holding reshuffle

In one sentence
The 'nearly €15bn' Choose France figure looks like a recycled earlier total, since the 2026 summit in June was put at €93bn, while the Dior-Agache merger is real but dates from September.

The headline figure does not hold up as a new event. The "nearly €15bn" total attributed to the 'Choose France' drive matches an earlier edition of the summit. The 2026 edition was held on 1 June at Versailles, and the French government put it at €93bn across 71 investments. The €93bn was dominated by a SoftBank commitment of up to €75bn for AI data centres. Searches turned up no November 2026 announcement of €15bn.

What the record shows

Reuters-based coverage described a record €15bn ($16.2bn) of foreign pledges from an earlier Choose France summit, with Microsoft pledging €4bn. That coverage does not clearly state its year, so the headline may be a late or recycled report. The 2026 total was more than double the €40.8bn announced at the 2025 summit, according to one report.

There is a November event with a similar name. The government's "Choose France – Édition France" was held on 23 November. It is aimed at French companies investing in France, and no €15bn pledge figure was found for it. The two events should not be confused. Pledges of this kind also tend to mix firm commitments, multi-year plans and re-announcements, so a total is a weak guide to new money even when it is correctly dated.

The Dior reshuffle is real, and older

The second thread is better documented, though it is not news this week. Christian Dior's board was told in late September of a plan to simplify the Arnault family's control of LVMH. The sources differ on whether that was 23 or 24 September.

The steps, as reported:

  • Financière Agache would be absorbed by its parent, Agache.
  • Agache would then merge into Christian Dior.
  • Dior would be renamed Agache and converted into a limited partnership with share capital (an SCA).

Today, Agache owns 100% of Financière Agache. That company controls 96% of Dior's equity and 97.1% of its voting rights. The merger would include a tender offer for the 2.44% of Dior shares that Agache does not own. After it, Agache would hold 49.76% of LVMH directly. One source puts the family's overall stake at 50.33% of capital and 66.27% of voting rights.

On 7 October, Dior replaced two independent directors ahead of the change. The conversion and merger are expected at the end of December 2026, subject to shareholder approval. The exchange ratio and final terms remain subject to an auditor's review and a formal information document. A board approval in late November would fit that timetable, but it was not independently confirmed.

What to watch

The date and breakdown of the "nearly €15bn" need confirming against the Élysée or Bercy before it is treated as new money. On Dior, the next data points are the information document, the terms of the tender offer and the shareholder vote ahead of the end-December completion.
