FTSE 100 rises on banks and gold miners as UK PMIs beat; midcaps outperform

In one sentence
UK flash PMIs beat forecasts and the FTSE 100 rose on banks and gold miners, with midcaps ahead, but the survey and index figures were not verified, so the day's driver stays unclear.

UK business activity surveys came in ahead of forecasts on Tuesday, and the FTSE 100 closed higher. Banks and gold miners did most of the lifting, and UK midcaps did better than the blue-chip index. The pairing of lenders and gold miners is an awkward one, because the two groups usually respond to different things.

This report rests on headline-level sourcing. Investing.com reported that the flash purchasing managers' indices beat consensus and that the FTSE 100 rose, led by banks and gold miners. A Reuters report is cited for the midcap outperformance. Searches did not turn up the underlying S&P Global/CIPS release or a closing-level report for the day. The PMI readings, the consensus figures, the index closes and the size of the midcap lead are therefore not given here. They are left out rather than estimated.

What moved, and what is not yet known

The flash PMIs are the first read on monthly activity in manufacturing and services, and markets treat them as a timely check on the economy. A reading above forecast usually helps domestically exposed shares and can lift the pound. The size of the beat and the level of the headline index relative to the 50 line, which separates expansion from contraction, decide how much the survey matters. Neither is established here.

Banks are the most direct way to trade a stronger UK economy in the FTSE 100. Better activity supports loan demand and credit quality, and it can shift expectations for interest rates, which affect lenders' margins. That fits a day on which the data beat expectations.

Gold miners fit less neatly. They tend to rise when bullion rises, and bullion tends to rise on falling real yields, a weaker dollar or demand for safe assets. A risk-on session driven by a growth beat does not obviously produce any of these. The two groups leading together leaves two readings open: a growth day with a separate metals tailwind, or a more mixed session in which money moved into both cyclical and defensive positions. Without the day's gold price move, which was not verified, the data cannot separate them.

Midcaps and the Nvidia backdrop

The second thread is that the FTSE 250 and other UK midcaps beat large caps, which Reuters attributes to a global risk-on rally sparked by gains in Nvidia. That is consistent with a growth-led reading. The FTSE 250 is more tied to the domestic economy than the FTSE 100, which earns most of its revenue abroad, so a domestic activity beat should help it more. A global technology rally would add to that, since risk appetite tends to lift smaller, more economically sensitive companies.

The Nvidia move is covered separately in this edition, in the piece on the chipmaker's jump as Jensen Huang returns. The specific size of the move and the list of global indices that rose with it were not confirmed for this article.

The midcap lead is the best evidence in this cluster for a domestic-growth reading. It is only suggestive, because the size of the outperformance is unknown and a single session can reflect rotation rather than a change in view. The gold miners point the other way, or at least sideways.

What it means

One flash survey does not mark a turn in UK activity. Flash PMIs are revised when the final figures arrive, and a single beat against forecasts says little about the trend. A more useful test is whether the beat is repeated in the final PMI releases and in the hard data that follow.

Rates are the second test. Bank shares are sensitive to expectations for Bank Rate and gilt yields. The 10-year gilt yield has been holding above 5% ahead of the Bank of England's Financial Stability Report, a theme covered in this edition's gilts preview. A stronger activity reading would not usually make the Bank's task easier if it also keeps pricing for rate cuts in check. Sector news may also be at work: the NatWest and Metro Bank items elsewhere in this edition show that banks have their own stories apart from the macro data.

The next checks are the Financial Stability Report and the final PMI readings. A second beat, with banks and midcaps again leading and gold quiet, would support the growth reading. If gold miners keep leading on their own, the earlier session was more likely about safe-haven demand.
