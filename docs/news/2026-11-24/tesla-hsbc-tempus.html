HSBC puts Tesla at $212 without SpaceX; AstraZeneca commits $200m to Tempus AI

In one sentence
HSBC's $212 Tesla target excludes any SpaceX tie-up and it also warned on XPeng, while AstraZeneca's reported $200m commitment to Tempus AI looks like a multi-year spending floor.

HSBC has set a $212 price target on Tesla and says the figure leaves out any tie-up with SpaceX. That is an unusual thing for a broker to say out loud. Merger talk around Elon Musk's companies is now common enough that an analyst has to state whether it is in the number.

A target with a carve-out

The note, as reported by GuruFocus, describes $212 as a standalone valuation. The bank has not assumed a combination with SpaceX, and it has not added a premium for the chance of one. The detail matters because HSBC has been among Tesla's more cautious followers. Its most recent target that could be confirmed was $157, with a Reduce rating, set in October on worries about production shortfalls and a weak energy business. The sources available did not explain the move to $212, and HSBC's current rating was not confirmed. Neither was the $212 figure itself, which rests on the triage report.

SpaceX is a separate question for the same bank. HSBC has initiated coverage of SpaceX at Hold with a $115 target, saying the stock is already fully valued. That is below the $135 IPO price and the roughly $118 where the shares recently traded. Other brokers have gone the other way. Wedbush has argued for a Tesla–SpaceX merger, and one outside forecast puts completion in 2027.

The carve-out can be read two ways. HSBC may be drawing a line between what it can model and what it cannot. Or it may be telling clients that part of the market's Tesla valuation is a bet on a deal that has not been announced. The note does not say which share of the price is deal speculation, so the extent of it is still an inference.

XPeng: a warning despite the rally

HSBC also warned on XPeng even though the Chinese EV maker's shares have rallied. The summary available gave no target, rating change or reasoning. HSBC had been positive on the stock before: in August 2025 it raised its XPeng target to $29.60 with a Buy. The latest warning is therefore a change of tone, though its size cannot be measured from what was reported. It matters mostly to holders of XPeng and of its Chinese peers.

AstraZeneca and Tempus AI

The second thread is a purchase commitment. AstraZeneca has committed $200m to Tempus AI, the diagnostics and precision-medicine company, according to Simply Wall St. Other reports describe the structure. Benzinga, citing a filing, says the two companies extended their master services agreement to the end of 2031. A new five-year commitment period starts on 1 January 2027, and AstraZeneca would spend $200m on Tempus products and services, about $40m a year. The reported minimum is $32m a year. AstraZeneca could terminate at any time but would owe the shortfall against the commitment.

If that is the deal, it is a spending floor and not an equity stake. It is also distinct from the April 2025 agreement among Tempus, AstraZeneca and Pathos AI, which was also reported at $200m, in data-licensing and model-development fees for an oncology foundation model. The two are easy to confuse, and the date of the renewal was not clear in the sources. The terms above should be treated as reported, not confirmed against a primary filing.

What links them

The two items are different kinds of news. One is a broker's view and the other is a customer's contract. Both turn on how much of a price is backed by commitments and how much by scenarios. A minimum annual spend from AstraZeneca is a contracted figure. A Tesla–SpaceX merger is a hypothesis. HSBC's note separates the two for Tesla. Whether other brokers follow will show up in their next target updates. The next hard data on Tempus will be its reported revenue once the commitment period begins in January 2027.
