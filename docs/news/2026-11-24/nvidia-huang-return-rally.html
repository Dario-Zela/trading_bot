Nvidia jumps by close to $1tn as Huang returns; Trump claims credit

In one sentence
Nvidia added about $950bn in value, a record one-day gain, after its board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO; Trump says he 'convinced' the board, a claim unverified.

Nvidia's market value rose by close to $1tn on Monday, the largest one-day gain in market-capitalisation history. The company announced that morning that Jensen Huang, its co-founder, was back as chief executive. The rally spread across technology shares. It also left the White House claiming credit for a decision the board says it reached itself.

The reinstatement premise first looked doubtful, because Huang has led Nvidia since he co-founded it in 1993. It is not a stale item. It follows a short, disorderly interregnum. CNBC reports that Huang resigned as CEO on 12 November. NBC News describes him as briefly removed. The two accounts differ on whether he jumped or was pushed, and Nvidia's own statement was not available to this desk. The return itself, and the market's reaction to it, are well documented.

The move

Nvidia shares closed up 31.5% on Monday 23 November, according to CNBC, its best day since May 2003. NBC News puts the gain in value at about $950bn. The stock had fallen more than 40% over the previous month and still sits roughly 30% below its 6 October record.

The rest of the market followed. CNBC reports that the Nasdaq Composite rose 6.51%, its best day since 9 April 2025. NBC reports that the S&P 500 gained 4.7% and the Dow added about 1,450 points. The S&P climbed back out of correction territory. The Nasdaq 100 is still around 15% below its last record. The tech names moved as follows:

  • Micron rose 19.04%.
  • Meta rose 6.55%.
  • Alphabet rose 6.34%.
  • Neocloud stocks rose more than 20%: CoreWeave gained 22.32% and Nebius 20.28%.

Asian equities rebounded sharply on Tuesday, in a Reuters-sourced account of the session.

The rally recovers ground lost in a bad week. The Nasdaq fell 10% in the week to 20 November, and all three main US averages entered correction territory. The Nasdaq turned negative for 2026. The BBC reported that Nvidia fell nearly 14% on Friday after Reuters reported, without confirmation from the BBC, that the company was under an SEC investigation over its accounting practices. NBC cites the same investigation reports. Neither outlet gives the agency's findings, and none are public.

Why the market cared

The size of the move says as much about the preceding fall as about the return. Nvidia is the main proxy for AI-infrastructure spending, so a leadership vacuum there weighed on everything that depends on it: chipmakers, cloud providers, memory suppliers. Evercore ISI's Rich Ross put it directly:

"The return of Jensen Huang removes the single biggest factor weighing on AI, Semis, Tech, Growth and Risk."

The reasons given for Huang's exit are serious but unproven. NBC and CNBC report that he admitted to infidelity, and that one woman he allegedly had a relationship with was reportedly a suspected Chinese asset. CNBC attributes the intelligence assessment to reports. These are allegations in press accounts. This desk has not seen the board's reasoning or any primary document.

Some strategists were wary of the scale of the bounce. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky observed that "extreme one-day gains like today are more often associated with markets that are trending down, not up." A single session that recovers a month's losses does not settle the underlying questions. Those questions are the SEC reports, the AI-valuation worries that had already unsettled San Francisco, and the stock still sitting well below its peak.

Trump claims credit

President Trump told CNBC that he spoke directly with Nvidia's board and "convinced" it to reinstate Huang. He said Huang "did nothing wrong." He also said the previous week's sell-off "probably" influenced his involvement, though the "bigger impact was the long term."

These are the president's statements, not confirmed facts. The board's stated reason was a series of discussions and meetings with Huang, which leaves open how much weight, if any, the White House call carried. No Nvidia comment on Trump's account has been found. The claim drew criticism over political interference in the governance of a listed company, and the point matters beyond this case. Nvidia's fortunes already depend on Washington through export licensing for its chips to China. A chief executive reinstated with a president's public endorsement, and with a president who ties the decision partly to market levels, creates a different set of dependencies for the company and its investors.

What to watch

Three things will show whether Monday was a recovery or a spike. The first is a primary statement from Nvidia, or a regulatory filing, that sets out the circumstances of the resignation and return, and any response to Trump's account. The second is whether the SEC reports are confirmed, denied or clarified. The third is whether the Nasdaq holds the gains. It remains about 15% below its high, and Nvidia has roughly 30% to recover to reach its October record.
