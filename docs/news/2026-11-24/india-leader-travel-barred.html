Indian opposition figure says he was stopped from flying to the UK

In one sentence
Saurav Das, a leader of India's Cockroach Janta Party, says his passport was flagged at Delhi airport as he tried to fly to Oxford; no authority has explained.

A leader of India's "Cockroach Janta Party" says he was stopped at Delhi airport on Sunday night, when he tried to fly to London to speak at the University of Oxford. Saurav Das, a co-convener of the youth-led opposition movement, says immigration officers told him his passport had been "flagged". He has produced no documents. As far as published reports show, no Indian or British authority has said why.

What Das says happened

Das posted his account on X on 23 November. He was preparing to board a 1am flight to London, he wrote, when he reached the immigration desk. "When I went for the immigration check, I came to know that my passport had been flagged," he said. He had been invited by the Oxford University Students' Union to join a panel on what he calls the "cockroach movement".

He went further. He said the Modi government had also blocked him from speaking at Cambridge, the London School of Economics and the University of Edinburgh. Those claims rest on his word alone. The reports reviewed give no dates or details for the earlier episodes.

Das also took a sardonic line, saying he wished he were a businessman accused of bank fraud, and he named Nirav Modi and Vijay Mallya. Both left India while facing fraud allegations. The point is a familiar one in Indian politics: defaulters have sometimes departed freely, while critics find themselves stopped. Das added that the episode would not halt his work and that he plans to travel to London, Paris and elsewhere.

The party's founder, Abhijeet Dipke, backed him. Dipke suggested that "someone in power" was jealous of the Oxford invitation. That is an inference, not evidence of a motive.

What is not known

The central questions are open. A flagged passport can mean several things. It might reflect a lookout circular, a pending legal case, or an administrative problem. Reports say a spokesperson for India's home ministry referred the BBC to airport authorities and Delhi police. No reply from either appears in the coverage reviewed. The grounds, the issuing authority and any right of appeal remain unstated.

Only Das's side of the account is public. Another party leader, Ratna Singh, said she and Das had both been invited by the Oxford Union and that her own visa application had been rejected a few days earlier. Rejection of a UK visa would be a matter for British officials, which is a different thing from an exit block in Delhi. The reports do not say why it was refused.

Background

The Cockroach Janta Party emerged this year as a protest movement, and its leaders have clashed with the Delhi authorities before. Police detained Dipke ahead of a protest demanding the resignation of the chief election commissioner. Das, Dipke and a third leader, Ashutosh Ranka, were also held on an Air India flight after it landed in Delhi on 10 October. Those episodes show a pattern of friction with police. They do not show that this passport flag was political.

What to watch

The test is whether any authority explains the flag. Das and Dipke are due to speak at an Oxford Union panel on the party on 1 December. Whether Das can travel by then, or appears by video, will show if the flag can be lifted or contested. A reply from the home ministry or immigration bureau would also settle whether this was a legal order or an error.
