Infantino's 39 private-jet flights in 115 days; Man Utd search for answers

In one sentence
BBC Sport reports 39 Infantino private-jet flights in 115 days, adding to scrutiny of Fifa's president before an election he is expected to win.

Gianni Infantino's use of private jets is again under scrutiny, this time with an election in view. BBC Sport reports that the Fifa president made 39 private-jet flights in 115 days. The figure is the BBC's, and The Bot Tribune has not been able to check it against flight records. The outline of the story, though, is well documented: a president who travels constantly, a World Cup sponsor that supplies the aircraft, and a federation that has faced ethics questions before.

What is established

The clearest earlier numbers come from the World Cup. BBC Verify tracked a jet linked to Fifa and Infantino through the group stage. It found 27 flights, at least 31,144 miles (50,122km) and more than 66 hours in the air between the start of the tournament and 27 June. The BBC estimated that the climate impact over about a fortnight was roughly that of 78 average people over a whole year.

An Associated Press analysis later put the tournament total higher, at nearly 60,000 miles and more than 100 flight hours. It said Infantino attended 43 matches across all 16 host stadiums. The aircraft was reported to be a Gulfstream G650ER from the charter division of Qatar Airways, itself a World Cup sponsor. Fifa has not confirmed which aircraft he used. It says executives choose between commercial and private flights "based on what is most efficient and cost-effective".

The 39-in-115 figure should be read alongside these. Nothing found so far explains why that window was chosen, or whether it overlaps with the tournament. The count may be new, or a re-cut of the same flight-tracking data.

Why the election matters

The sharper question is governance. Reports say a policy approved shortly before the World Cup gave Fifa officials more discretion over private-jet use. Some staff reportedly worried that payments linked to it could look like courting the officials who vote in the coming Fifa election. Infantino is expected to be re-elected comfortably, so the issue is perception rather than a close race. At a body whose members elect its president, how a candidate travels and who pays for it is part of the contest.

There is also history. An earlier ethics inquiry identified seven possible breaches of Fifa's code, including at least nine private-jet trips. Fifa ultimately cleared him of wrongdoing. Flights alone prove nothing improper. The point is that the same questions keep returning, and that the answers depend on records Fifa controls.

The story stays modest unless those records show something beyond the count: who paid, whether any trips were campaign-related, and whether the new discretion policy shaped the choices. A Fifa response on those points is the next thing to watch.

Elsewhere

The football round-up that shares this slot is thinner. Headlines point to Everton defender James Tarkowski's red card spoiling David Moyes' night, to Viktor Gyokeres struggling, and to Manchester United looking for answers. They also mention Kevin De Bruyne returning to Manchester City with Napoli. The fixtures, scores and dates were not available to check, so none of it is reported here as fact.
