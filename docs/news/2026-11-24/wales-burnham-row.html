Welsh first minister says Burnham does not treat Wales as an equal nation

In one sentence
Wales's first minister, Rhun ap Iorwerth, says Burnham does not treat Wales as an equal nation, reviving a demand for parity with Scotland that talks have not yet settled.

The first minister of Wales has accused the prime minister of failing to treat Wales as an equal nation. The charge turns a long-running grievance about devolution into a direct dispute between Cardiff Bay and Downing Street. The remarks were reported by BBC Politics. The full text of the first minister's comments was not available when this article was written, so the precise wording and the occasion are not set out here.

The first minister's case

The office is held by Rhun ap Iorwerth of Plaid Cymru. Since Andy Burnham became prime minister, ap Iorwerth has pressed the same argument in public several times. He has urged Burnham not to bypass the Senedd and not to treat Wales as a region of England. A Welsh government minister has made a related point: Burnham, the minister said, should first stop describing Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland as regions.

The demand is for parity with Scotland. Ap Iorwerth has called for a "New Deal" to redress what he describes as the historic unfairness of the fiscal and constitutional settlement. He has said Wales should be brought on to an equal footing with Scotland "as a bare minimum". Scotland's devolved powers are broader than those of Wales, which is the gap he wants closed.

What Burnham has said

Burnham has offered reassurance. He has said he wants to put Wales at the centre of any government he runs, pushing power "down and out of Westminster and Whitehall". At the end of October he said the four nations must "move at the same pace together" on devolution. He has also promised that Welsh demands over the Crown Estate will get a fair hearing.

After an October meeting, ap Iorwerth said both sides agreed on the urgent need to revise Wales's fiscal and borrowing powers. So the relationship has not been wholly hostile. The new accusation suggests that warm words have not yet been matched by action, at least in the first minister's view.

The second thread: the 10-year plan and No 10 North

A second BBC Politics item concerns Burnham's 10-year plan and a "No 10 North" operation. That item was not read in full, and the triage note gave no trigger for the scrutiny it expects. The link to Wales is plain enough, though. Ap Iorwerth has dismissed the "Number 10 of the North" idea as something that will mean very little to people in Wales. A scheme built around northern England can look to Cardiff like a sign that the prime minister's devolution thinking is centred on English regions rather than on the nations.

What to watch

The row matters mainly if it produces concrete results. The tests are whether the government changes Wales's fiscal and borrowing powers, whether it settles the Crown Estate question, and whether it moves towards the parity with Scotland that ap Iorwerth wants. Burnham has not been quoted in this reporting saying Wales is unequal, and the first minister's claim is an interpretation of his conduct. Downing Street's response is the next thing to look for. So is any timetable for the fiscal talks agreed in October. Without these, the dispute will stay at the level of rhetoric.
