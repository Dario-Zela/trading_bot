Met police seize 893kg of cocaine hidden in boxes of oranges

In one sentence
The Met seized 893kg of suspected cocaine hidden in orange boxes, arresting eight people after raids from a Leyton wholesale market to a lorry stopped en route to Dover.

Police in London have seized 893kg of suspected cocaine in an operation that began among the crates at one of the capital's biggest wholesale produce markets. The drugs were packed into boxes of oranges, a disguise that works on the same logic as the cargo it imitates: fruit moves in volume, on tight schedules, and in the sort of crates nobody opens twice.

The Metropolitan Police announced the seizure on 23 November. It describes the haul as one of the biggest drug seizures in its history. Eight people were arrested and all remain in custody.

What happened

Officers arrested five men, aged 27, 33, 41, 43 and 58, at New Spitalfields Market on Sherrin Road, Leyton, on the evening of Sunday 22 November. All five were held on suspicion of conspiracy to supply class A drugs. A sixth man, 44, was arrested nearby in Leyton with a further 100kg of suspected class A drugs.

The inquiry then moved outward. A warrant at a property in Cheshunt, Hertfordshire, led to the arrest of a 48-year-old man and the seizure of £450,000 in cash. A lorry was stopped near Maidstone and taken to Dover to be searched. About 250kg of suspected cocaine was found, and the 50-year-old driver was arrested on the same suspicion.

The Met's total of 893kg is for the whole investigation, not for a single crate-stack at the market. The force has not said how that figure divides between the market, the Leyton arrest and the lorry. It has also not said where the oranges, or the drugs, came from.

A familiar disguise

Cocaine in fruit is not new. Banana shipments have carried tonnes of it into British ports: in 2021 about 2.3 tonnes was found in a consignment bound for Portsmouth. A 2022 case in the south-west of England turned up 49kg in a container of South African oranges. What stands out here is the destination. The seizure was made in the wholesale market itself, and the lorry search points to a road leg through Kent as well.

That suggests the group was moving product through ordinary produce distribution rather than relying only on the port. The Met has not confirmed that reading, and the investigation is continuing.

What the Met says

Detective Superintendent Lindsey Billany, who leads the investigation, said the operation showed the Met's "focus on bringing down the drug traffickers whose trade fuels violence on London's streets." She called it "a warning to organised crime groups importing class A drugs."

The force says it disrupted serious and organised crime groups more than 21,000 times last year, a 63% increase on 2024.

What to watch

The next marker is charging decisions. The eight remain in custody, and prosecutors will have to decide who faces which charge. Any court appearance should show whether the Met can tie the market, the Leyton arrest, the Cheshunt cash and the lorry into one conspiracy. It may also reveal where the shipment entered the country.
