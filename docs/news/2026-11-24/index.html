STANDING WATCH · UK-EU · By C. Tanaka

UK-EU reset: Burnham rules out rejoining without a referendum

The new Prime Minister, Burnham, has ruled out any return to the European Union without a referendum, calling such a move "unthinkable" in the absence of a public vote. The position, set out by the government and reported by both the Guardian and BBC political desks, pairs that constitutional line with a "pragmatic approach" to the EU in day-to-day dealings.

The distinction matters. The government is not changing any law. It is stating where it will not go: a rejoin, however it is pursued, would need the public's explicit consent first. Day-to-day cooperation with Brussels, by contrast, is presented as a matter of practical deal-making rather than a return to membership.

For investors and businesses, that gives a clearer boundary to price against. A referendum becomes the precondition for any future rejoin, so long-run UK-EU policy risk is tied to whether and when a vote could be held, not to ministerial signals alone. Watch for how the government defines "pragmatic" in practice, and whether any party presses for a vote on the question.
