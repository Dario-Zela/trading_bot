Angola prices its De Beers stake at $180m; Glencore's cash flow recovers

In one sentence
Angola's reported $180m valuation of a 10% De Beers stake implies about $1.8bn for the whole, but the figure is single-sourced and unconfirmed as Anglo American's exit talks continue.

Angola has put a price of $180m on a 10% stake in De Beers, according to IntelliNews. If the figure holds, it is the first concrete number attached to a minority holding in the diamond group as Anglo American works towards leaving the business. So far it rests on that single report. The basis for the number, and whether it is an offer, an internal valuation or something else, has not been established.

What the number implies

The arithmetic is simple, and it is ours rather than the report's. $180m for 10% implies about $1.8bn for the whole of De Beers.

That sits well below other marks in circulation. Reuters has reported that Anglo carries De Beers at $4.9bn after $3.5bn of impairments over two years. At that book value, 10% would be worth roughly $490m. A Russian-language report from AK&M on 16 November described a Global Diamond Consortium paying Anglo $1bn for its 85% stake, with $750m upfront. No other outlet in our checks corroborated that, and it implies a different valuation again.

The figures cannot be reconciled without knowing what each one measures. A minority holding with no control, a stake priced as part of a consortium, and a book value after write-downs are three different things. Until the basis of Angola's number is clear, it is best read as a marker of the buyer's expectations, not a transaction price.

Where the talks stand

Angola's mines ministry said in mid-November that it wants a 10% holding in De Beers as part of Anglo's proposed sale of its 85% majority stake. It has said this would come through a consortium. Botswana, which holds a right of first refusal, has backed the deal and plans to raise its own stake to 30%. In July Angola said it wanted a holding large enough to influence strategy. Anglo's exit is described as under negotiation, with talks continuing.

For Anglo's shareholders, the question is how much a diamond business, hit by weaker demand and competition from lab-grown stones, can fetch from producer-country buyers. Those buyers may value the stake for its strategic and fiscal importance as well as for its cash flows. A government buyer pricing a stake low would set a reference point that Anglo might resist.

Glencore's cash flow

The same week brought a separate valuation question for another miner. Glencore, the Baar-based miner and commodity trader with exposure to copper, coal, zinc, nickel and cobalt, is reported to be seeing its cash flow recover. That has prompted the usual debate about whether a cyclical, trading-heavy business deserves a premium multiple. We could not find a dated Simply Wall St piece to confirm the details of the rebound. Published discounted-cash-flow estimates of Glencore's fair value vary widely, which shows how sensitive the answer is to commodity price assumptions.

The link between the two stories is price discovery in mining assets. De Beers is being sold at a discount to its recent carrying value, while a diversified miner's multiple depends on how much cash flow the market trusts to recur.

What to watch

The next test is whether Anglo, Botswana or Angola confirm a price or the structure of the consortium. A second source for the $180m figure, and its basis, would settle whether it is a real anchor for the exit.
