BoE holds at 3.75% as hawkish dissent persists

The Bank of England's Threadneedle Street headquarters, where the MPC's 6-3 vote masked a widening hawkish minority. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
The BoE held rates 6-3, but dissent for a hike jumped from two to three votes on energy-driven inflation risk — a gap markets are largely ignoring.

The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% on July 30, extending its hold to a fifth straight meeting. But the vote that got it there was not the placid affair the headline number suggests: three of the nine-member Monetary Policy Committee voted for an immediate quarter-point hike to 4%, up from two dissenters at the June meeting. The direction of travel inside the room is now more interesting than the number outside it.

The three hawks — Megan Greene, Huw Pill and Catherine Mann — all pointed to the same culprit: energy prices that refuse to behave. Ofgem's price cap rose 13% for the July-to-September quarter, driven by Gulf-conflict-linked wholesale gas costs, a move the Bank itself estimates will add more than 0.7 percentage points to July's inflation reading alone. Pill warned that "profound uncertainty surrounding the energy price outlook is likely to be prolonged and of unknown duration," and argued a hike now would send a clear signal on upside risk. Greene went further, arguing that with inflation above target for roughly five years running, "a proactive hike in Bank Rate may reduce the probability that second-round effects set in" — a reference to the risk that one-off energy costs bleed into wages and broader prices.

A dovish governor, a hawkish committee

Governor Andrew Bailey worked to contain the reading of the vote almost as soon as it landed, telling reporters not to "leave this room thinking that the Bank of England is edging towards a hike." He noted that headline inflation had cooled faster than expected, to 2.6% in June — the lowest print since March 2025 — and framed the energy spike as a one-off pass-through rather than the start of a trend.

Markets took Bailey's framing over the vote count. Two-year gilt yields fell 11 basis points to 4.34%, their steepest drop since mid-June, as traders pared back hike bets following the press conference. Sterling barely moved, edging up 0.08% to $1.3376. Priced-in odds of a September move sit in the low double digits — a small fraction of the committee that just tripled its hawkish flank.

That gap between the market's calm and the committee's growing unease is the story. Two dissenters became three in six weeks, on the back of an energy shock the Bank itself expects to push inflation to "a little under 3%" in the third quarter and above 3.25% by the fourth. Should the August CPI print, due before the next meeting, come in hot, the MPC would need only two more votes to flip the majority — not a large ask when the dissent is already trending in one direction.

The next decision lands September 17. Between now and then, one more CPI release and the passthrough of Ofgem's higher cap into household bills will do most of the talking.
