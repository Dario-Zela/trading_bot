Disney tops profit estimates as shares jump

In one sentence
Disney beat EPS estimates and lifted Dow futures, but the beat came against recently lowered forecasts and a slight revenue miss.

Disney shares rose roughly 3-4% on Wednesday after the company's fiscal third-quarter earnings beat Wall Street's forecasts, and the print was strong enough to nudge Dow futures up around 200 points in early trading — a reminder of how much weight a single mega-cap earnings call can still throw around a broad index.

The headline number: adjusted earnings per share of $2.06, comfortably ahead of the $1.86 analysts had penciled in, and up from $1.61 a year earlier. Operating income came in at $5.56 billion against a $5.24 billion forecast. Revenue rose 7% to $25.25 billion — though that was about $150 million short of the consensus estimate, a detail that got less attention than the EPS beat but matters for judging how clean this quarter actually was.

The strength was concentrated in two places. Disney's Experiences segment — theme parks, cruises, consumer products — posted record operating income of $3.31 billion on revenue of $9.97 billion, up 10%. Domestic parks and experiences revenue climbed 11% to $7.12 billion, with attendance up 3% and per-visitor spending up 4%. Streaming was the other bright spot: entertainment streaming revenue (Disney+ and Hulu) rose 11% to $5.53 billion, and operating income in that segment more than doubled, to $712 million from $329 million a year ago. Disney also raised its fiscal 2026 buyback target to at least $9 billion, from $8 billion, and booked a $100 million tariff refund.

A beat, but against a lowered bar

Context cuts against the cleanest version of this story. Disney stock had drifted to roughly a 15-month low heading into the print, and analysts had been trimming price targets in the days before — Barclays cut its target from $135 to $110, Citigroup from $145 to $135. A beat against expectations that had just been marked down is a different animal from a beat against a stable consensus; some of Wednesday's pop is relief, not revelation.

Still, the underlying detail is harder to wave away than the past few quarters' beats. Streaming operating income doubling, rather than merely growing, points to something closer to a genuine profitability inflection at Disney+ and Hulu after years of losses. The 4% rise in park per-capita spending, against a backdrop of tariff and inflation worry elsewhere in retail, is a small but real data point on discretionary consumer spending holding up. Neither is proof of a broader trend on its own — this was one company's quarter, not a macro signal — but it is more substantive than a routine mega-cap beat-and-pop.

This was also the first full quarterly report under CEO Josh D'Amaro, who succeeded Bob Iger in March. D'Amaro used the results to lay out plans to fold consumer products into the entertainment division starting in fiscal 2027's first quarter, part of a push to make Disney+ what he called the company's "digital centerpiece." The next test of whether Wednesday's numbers reflect a real turn or a favorable one-off comes with the fiscal fourth quarter print, due in November, and with it the first read on holiday-season park bookings and streaming pricing under the new structure.
