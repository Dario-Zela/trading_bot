Jos Buttler becomes T20 cricket's all-time leading run-scorer

Jos Buttler, England's World Cup-winning wicketkeeper-batter, now T20 cricket's all-time leading run-scorer. — Simon Walker / No 10 Downing Street (Open Government Licence)
In one sentence
Jos Buttler passed Kieron Pollard's 14,803 T20 run record with three sixes off Marco Jansen for Manchester Super Giants, reaching 14,833.

Jos Buttler needed 22 runs when he walked out at Old Trafford on Wednesday evening to chase down Welsh Fire's total. By the time he'd finished, he owned the biggest number in T20 cricket.

Buttler's unbeaten 51 off 20 balls — six of them sixes — carried Manchester Super Giants to a nine-wicket win and carried the England wicketkeeper-batter past Kieron Pollard's all-time T20 run tally of 14,803. The record fell mid-over: the first of three consecutive sixes off South African seamer Marco Jansen took Buttler through, and the third completed his half-century and sealed the match in the same stroke. He finished on 14,833 T20 runs.

"To become the highest run-scorer in the history of T20 cricket is a fantastic and proud moment," Buttler said afterwards. "Records are made to be broken, and one day someone else will surely surpass this one."

What makes the number notable isn't just its size. Pollard reached his mark across roughly 700 T20 matches; Buttler got there in 522 appearances and 492 innings, a rate of accumulation that says as much about the modern schedule as it does about his hitting. The current top five — Buttler, Pollard, Chris Gayle (14,562), Alex Hales (14,449) and David Warner (14,284) — is now entirely stocked with players whose bulk of scoring came in T20 franchise cricket rather than international white-ball fixtures.

A record built in the leagues, not the Test arena

That's the real story here. A decade ago, the milestones that mattered in cricket belonged to Test batting — centuries at Lord's, series averages, career aggregates built over five-day matches. The sport's all-time run-scoring record in its shortest format now belongs to a competition most Test purists still regard as a sideshow: the Hundred, a 100-ball English domestic tournament that didn't exist until 2021, played out at Old Trafford in front of a crowd there for a Wednesday-night franchise fixture, not a national team.

Buttler, 35, has built his career accordingly — captaining England to a T20 World Cup, but earning the bulk of his professional minutes, and his money, in the IPL, the Hundred and franchise cricket's ever-expanding calendar. His 87-run stand with Tim Seifert (62 not out) on Wednesday was itself a snapshot of that world: two international wicketkeeper-batters, on contract to a city franchise, doing the accumulating that once belonged to Test opening partnerships.

The next data point arrives quickly. Buttler is currently the Hundred's second-highest run-scorer this season with 277 runs from six innings, and franchise T20 leagues — the IPL, the Big Bash, MLC, the Hundred itself — will keep adding matches to his ledger long before any international summer does. The record, on current trends, won't stand still for long.
