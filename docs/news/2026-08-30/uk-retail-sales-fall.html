UK retail sales fall for the first time in three months

Shoppers and traffic at Oxford Circus, London's principal shopping street. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
UK retail sales fell 0.5% in July as June's weather- and promotion-driven boost faded, with online spending down 3.9% and core sales missing forecasts.

UK retail sales fell 0.5% in July, the first monthly decline since April, as the promotional pull-forward and hot weather that flattered June's numbers gave way to a quieter high street. The Office for National Statistics said the drop was in line with economist forecasts, but a closer read of the data — particularly a 0.9% fall in core sales excluding fuel, worse than the 0.5% expected — suggests the underlying picture is softer than the headline number implies.

In one sentence: UK retail sales fell 0.5% in July as June's weather- and promotion-driven boost faded, with online spending down 3.9% and core sales missing forecasts.

A rebound that borrowed from itself

June's 0.7% rise now looks less like momentum and more like a loan against July. Retailers pulled promotional activity forward into June, and unusually hot weather pulled shoppers out early too — both a way of saying the same sale happened once, not twice. Non-food store sales fell 1.3% in July, with clothing the weakest link: volumes there dropped 2.7% on the month as households that had already bought their summer wardrobes in June's heatwave had little reason to return to the shops.

Department stores added to the drag, hurt by stock availability problems rather than demand, according to the ONS release. Fuel sales rose in July but remain below February's level, and food stores continued to perform well, benefiting from resilient supermarket trading — the one category holding up as the discretionary end of the high street cooled.

The online number worth watching

The most striking figure in the release is not the headline 0.5%. Online sales values fell 3.9% in July, having risen 2.5% in June, and the share of retail spending done online slipped to 28.3% from 29.2%. Year-on-year, online sales are still up 6.5%, so this is not evidence of a structural retreat from e-commerce. But the scale of the monthly swing — more than seven times the size of the overall retail move — shows how much of Britain's recent "resilient consumer" narrative has been propped up by a single volatile channel, and how quickly that channel can reverse once a promotional cycle ends.

Taken together with the three-month trend — sales volumes up 1.1% on the three months to April, and up 3.0% on a year earlier — the July dip looks, for now, more like digestion than reversal. Economists broadly agree that one month's data does not make a trend, though the annual growth rate of 1.6% undershot the 2.2% consensus, a gap wide enough to unsettle anyone hoping the consumer had turned a durable corner.

Why the Bank of England will be watching

The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 30 July, its fifth consecutive hold, in a decision made before this data landed. Retail sales feed directly into the Monetary Policy Committee's read on demand, and a miss on core sales gives the more dovish members of the committee something to point to ahead of the September 17 meeting, where markets currently expect another hold. The retail figures alone are unlikely to move that call — one soft print rarely does — but they add to a small pile of evidence, alongside gilt yields sitting at their highest since 1998, that the domestic economy is more brittle than the spring's rebound suggested.

The next test comes with the August retail sales release, due in September, alongside the ONS's revised GDP figures. If the online decline persists and clothing sales stay weak into the autumn shopping season, the case for a September or November rate cut strengthens. If July turns out to be the correction after an inflated June, the Bank's holding pattern continues undisturbed.
