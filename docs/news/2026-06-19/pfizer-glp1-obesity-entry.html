Pfizer accelerates GLP-1 push to challenge Lilly and Novo Nordisk in obesity market

In one sentence
Pfizer's monthly-dose obesity drug berobenatide posted 15.9% weight loss in Phase 2b trials — solid, but trailing Lilly's Zepbound and Novo Nordisk's Wegovy ahead of a targeted 2028 launch.

Pfizer presented Phase 2b data for its obesity drug berobenatide at the American Diabetes Association's Scientific Sessions in New Orleans on 6 June — results promising enough to keep the programme on track and complicated enough to lay bare exactly how steep the climb ahead will be.

Berobenatide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist picked up when Pfizer acquired obesity biotech Metsera for up to $10 billion last autumn, delivered roughly 15.9% non-placebo-adjusted weight loss at 32 weeks in adults with obesity, with no apparent plateau at the trial's close. In a parallel study of patients with obesity and type 2 diabetes, it trimmed blood sugar by 2.2% on a placebo-adjusted basis. The drug's central pitch is a dosing schedule that transitions from weekly injections during an induction phase to monthly maintenance shots — a meaningful convenience gap over the weekly regimens patients currently tolerate for Ozempic and Wegovy.

Analysts received the data with measured enthusiasm. Guggenheim, BMO, and Leerink each described results as "solid but undifferentiated." The comparison is difficult to escape: Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Zepbound) has achieved roughly 22% weight loss at 72 weeks, and Novo Nordisk's high-dose Wegovy lands around 20.7%. Berobenatide's 15.9% comes from a shorter timeframe, and Pfizer argues the absent plateau suggests the drug has more to deliver in Phase 3 — a reasonable claim that Phase 3 will have to prove.

A late entry into a $66 billion market

The commercial logic for the push is difficult to dispute on paper. The global GLP-1 market reached approximately $66 billion in 2025. Eli Lilly's combined Mounjaro and Zepbound franchise generated $36.5 billion that year — more than half the company's total revenues. Novo Nordisk's Wegovy alone added roughly $11 billion in the same period. That scale has turned both companies into the pharmaceutical sector's dominant growth stories, and a credible third entrant would apply meaningful pressure to their valuations and pricing power.

Pfizer's route in is not confined to berobenatide. The company also holds an early-stage oral small-molecule GLP-1 programme in-licensed from Chinese biotech YaoPharma, alongside an oral GIPR antagonist candidate and a broader set of metabolic assets from the Metsera portfolio. Pfizer plans more than 20 obesity-related trials in 2026, with ten of them Phase 3 studies targeting berobenatide for chronic weight management and comorbidities including knee osteoarthritis and obstructive sleep apnea.

The structural headwinds are significant. Pfizer is aiming for a first berobenatide approval no earlier than 2028 — a window in which biosimilar semaglutide will already be entering the market, and in which Novo Nordisk and Lilly are already compressing list prices under pressure from compounding pharmacies and each other. Eli Lilly further complicated the competitive picture in April 2026 by launching Foundayo (orflorglipron), an oral small-molecule GLP-1, opening a new convenience front before Pfizer's own oral programme reaches that stage.

Monthly dosing may prove sufficiently attractive to earn a loyal patient cohort — particularly among those who struggle with the injection fatigue of weekly regimens. Whether that advantage is broad enough to justify a late-2020s market entry, against two entrenched rivals with manufacturing scale, brand recognition, and efficacy data accumulated over years, is a harder question.

The critical next data points are Phase 3 enrolment milestones and, eventually, 52- and 72-week weight-loss readouts that allow direct head-to-head comparison with weekly market leaders. If berobenatide can close the efficacy gap at those longer timeframes, the convenience argument gains real force. If it cannot, Pfizer's obesity bet may ultimately rest on price — and in a category being repriced downward, that is the thinner foundation.
