Nippon Steel to spend $3bn on US Steel's Mon Valley Works

In one sentence
Nikkei reports Nippon Steel will spend about $3bn at US Steel's Mon Valley Works, above the $2bn–2.5bn range US Steel gave in June, to replace a roughly 90-year-old hot mill.

Nippon Steel's takeover of US Steel is starting to show up in capital budgets. Nikkei Asia reports that the Japanese group plans to spend about $3bn at Mon Valley Works in Pennsylvania. The centrepiece is a new hot roller, built with Nippon's latest technology, to replace equipment about 90 years old.

The headline figure is higher than the numbers US Steel has put on the project so far, so it needs some care.

The numbers

In June, US Steel said the Mon Valley programme would cost $2bn to $2.5bn over about three years. That was more than double the "no less than $1bn" Nippon committed to the site in August 2024. The Nikkei figure of roughly $3bn is above the top of that range. The triage material does not say whether it covers a wider scope, other works at the site, or a newer estimate. Nor does it say whether the project has been enlarged since June. The Nikkei article was not available for direct checking, so the $3bn is a single-source number.

The age of the old equipment is also reported differently. Company-linked accounts in June described the mill being replaced as 87 or 88 years old. Nikkei's "about 90" is a rounding of that, not a conflicting claim.

What is being built

As described in June, the new hot strip mill would be built at the Edgar Thomson plant in Braddock. It would replace the ageing mill at the Irvin plant in West Mifflin, which is due to be decommissioned. A hot strip mill takes slabs from the steelmaking side and rolls them into coils of sheet steel. Those coils feed the automotive, appliance and construction supply chains. Capacity is planned to rise to up to 3.5m tons a year, from about 2.2m tons today. Construction was expected to begin later in 2026 and last about three years.

A company-commissioned report put the economic impact for Pennsylvania at $1.4bn to $1.7bn. It projected up to 6,381 jobs over the build and as much as $58m in state and local tax revenue. These are sponsor-supplied estimates and should be read as such.

Why it matters

The plant spending is part of a wider Nippon pledge of about $11bn in US investment by 2028. For regulators and politicians who scrutinised the deal, a new mill at a specific site is easier to verify than a headline commitment. For buyers of flat-rolled steel, a modern mill promises better product consistency and more capacity. Neither benefit arrives before the three-year build is done.

This is still one plant and one report. The next checkpoints are a US Steel or Nippon confirmation of the $3bn figure and whether it supersedes the $2bn to $2.5bn range. Evidence that construction has started at Braddock would also be telling.
