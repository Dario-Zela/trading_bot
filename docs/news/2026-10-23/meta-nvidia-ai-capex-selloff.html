Meta falls 6% on AI spending fears despite strong earnings; Nvidia warning hits chips

In one sentence
Meta fell about 6% after a revenue beat as capex rose and free cash flow turned negative; a reported 20% SOXL drop after a Nvidia warning remains unconfirmed.

Meta Platforms reported a revenue beat on Wednesday and lost about 6% of its market value on Thursday. The cause was not the quarter's results but the bill for the artificial-intelligence build-out behind them. A separate Benzinga report says a leveraged semiconductor fund fell roughly 20% after a Nvidia warning. That second leg is the weaker part of the story, and this article treats it that way.

The quarter

Meta's third-quarter revenue came in at $64.6bn, ahead of the roughly $63.3bn analysts expected and up about 26% on a year earlier, according to Newsquawk. The spending side was what drew attention. The company raised the floor of its 2026 capital-expenditure guidance, narrowing the range to $135bn–$145bn. Free cash flow was negative, at about $2.1bn, which coverage describes as the first negative quarter since Meta listed in 2012.

The market response was swift. Shares fell about 6–7% on 22 October, with sources differing on the exact figure, and were reported near $650. Reports also noted that the pricing of Meta's five-year credit default swaps rose to record levels. That is a measure of the cost of insuring against default, and it suggests the concern extended to the company's balance sheet as well as its share price.

The reaction fits a pattern. After the third quarter of 2025, Meta fell more than 11% when it signalled that 2026 spending would be notably larger than 2025's. Its second-quarter 2026 report in July also met a falling share price. A stock that rises on a revenue beat and then falls on a capex line has told investors where the scrutiny now sits.

Contagion, and a claim to check

Meta was not alone. Other large spenders slid, with Microsoft, Oracle, Alphabet and Amazon each down between 2.2% and 4.6%, according to market reports. CoreWeave, which carries heavy debt against its data-centre build, fell about 10% as worries about the cost of capital resurfaced. The Nasdaq had its worst day since June.

The chip leg is harder to pin down. The triage source, Benzinga, attributes a roughly 20% loss in SOXL, the 3x-leveraged semiconductor ETF, to a Nvidia AI warning. Searches turned up no Nvidia statement, no filing and no second outlet confirming either the warning or the size of the fund's decline. The details are therefore unconfirmed: what Nvidia said, when it said it, and over what period SOXL lost the 20%.

The arithmetic is plausible, though. A fund that aims for three times the daily move of a semiconductor index turns a drop of about 7% in the index into a drop of about 20%. Such products also reset daily, so losses over several sessions can compound in ways that do not map neatly onto the underlying index. That feature is the reason a single warning can produce a wipeout of this size in a leveraged vehicle while the unleveraged sector falls by a third as much.

What it changes

The significance, if the pattern holds, is in how the AI build-out is being priced. Through much of the past two years, strong results offset questions about spending. The Meta reaction suggests the reverse: spending that outruns free cash flow is now enough to override a revenue beat. The negative free cash flow and the widening credit spreads indicate that investors are weighing financing, not just demand.

That raises the stakes for the suppliers. Nvidia and its peers depend on the hyperscalers continuing to raise capex, so any hint of a ceiling travels quickly down the chain, and leveraged funds amplify the move.

The next checks are concrete. Nvidia's own disclosures would settle what, if anything, it warned about. The remaining big-tech reports this season will show whether the Meta reaction is a company-specific verdict or a sector-wide repricing of capex. Meta's 2027 spending guidance, which it has so far declined to quantify, is the number the market is waiting for.
