BBC investigation finds Sudan's RSF deliberately targeted hospitals

In one sentence
A BBC investigation documents at least 43 attacks on el-Fasher's medical sites and, citing doctors' testimony, argues the RSF targeted them deliberately; the RSF rejects it.

The BBC says it has gathered enough evidence to argue that the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), the paramilitary group fighting Sudan's army, did not simply damage hospitals in the besieged city of el-Fasher but set out to hit them. The finding, published on 22 October, moves the reporting from isolated allegations towards a documented pattern. The case for intent rests largely on testimony, however, and not every party agrees with it.

What the BBC counted

The investigation documents at least 43 separate attacks on hospitals and healthcare sites in el-Fasher, the capital of North Darfur, between May 2024 and October 2025. Some sites were hit repeatedly. The Saudi Maternity Hospital was struck up to 25 times on some days.

The methods varied: shelling, drone strikes, precision-guided rockets and ground raids. The BBC's data also records 120 health workers injured, 55 killed and 10 abducted or forcibly disappeared between April 2023 and October 2025.

Several incidents stand out:

  • South Hospital, 8 June 2024. After days of attacks, RSF fighters raided the hospital. Staff were beaten, and a doctor was left for dead.
  • Relocated facilities. The Specialised Children's Hospital and the main dialysis centre were shelled again after they moved.
  • Patients in transit. On 15 March 2025 a drone killed nine people carrying wounded to a temporary ward. On 17 August 2025 another hit a donkey cart carrying patients, killing two of them and the driver.
  • 25 October 2025. The RSF stormed the Saudi Maternity Hospital on the day it took the city. The World Health Organization has said hundreds were killed there.

The case for intent

The argument for deliberate targeting is cumulative. Six doctors described a systematic pattern and not incidental damage. Facilities were attacked again after they relocated, and makeshift surgical sites were hit five times in 2025. Dr Muhamed Suliman, a surgeon in el-Fasher, said the aim was to force the hospitals to close. "It was intense," he said. "It was deliberate. It was planned."

Doctors also told the BBC they believed fighters targeted hospitals because army or allied fighters were among the patients. Under the laws of war, treating wounded combatants does not strip a hospital of protection.

The RSF-backed health minister, Dr Alaa Eldin Nugud, called the findings propaganda and said South Hospital had been a military base. The BBC said it found no independent evidence for that claim. He blamed the army and its allies for a smear campaign.

Reaction and limits

Volker Türk, the UN human rights chief, called the findings "deeply disturbing" and consistent with his office's own monitoring. His office said the attacks may amount to war crimes. The WHO described el-Fasher as "one of the clearest and most severe examples" of the war's effect on health care.

The picture is not uniform. Médecins Sans Frontières has said it does not know whether hospitals are being intentionally targeted, only that the warring sides take no precautions to spare them. The army is also accused of striking medical sites: it denied responsibility for the March 2026 bombing of a hospital in al-Daein, which the WHO condemned.

Intent is the hardest element to prove in any war-crimes case. Witness accounts of a pattern, however many, are not the same as orders or communications showing a decision to attack. The BBC's tally gives prosecutors and UN investigators a catalogue to test, but it does not settle that question.

What to watch

The next step is whether UN investigators or the International Criminal Court, which has been examining Darfur, take up the incidents. The ICC's Darfur work has so far centred on atrocities in earlier phases of the conflict. The RSF has said it would investigate violations in el-Fasher, but its response to this report has been to reject it.
