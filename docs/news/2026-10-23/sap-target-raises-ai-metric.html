SAP draws analyst target raises as it publishes AI governance framework

In one sentence
BMO and Berenberg raised SAP targets as it detailed AI governance; the target moves look routine, and only the new AI business-impact metric could matter if tied to revenue.

SAP drew another round of analyst target increases this week, with BMO Capital Markets lifting its price target on the German software group to $265 from $248 and Berenberg also raising its own. The timing coincided with SAP detailing its approach to AI governance, including a new measure of the business impact of its AI products. The target changes look routine. The new metric is the only part of the news that could bear on the numbers investors care about.

The target changes

BMO kept its Outperform rating on SAP's US-listed shares. According to the note's summary, the analyst described the company's cloud conversion story as still positive. That is a continuation of an existing thesis, not a change of view: the shift of SAP's customers from on-premise licences to subscription cloud contracts has been the main driver of its valuation for several years.

Berenberg's raise is harder to pin down. The triage brief gave no new level, and the figures that turn up in public search results are undated and do not agree with one another, so none is reproduced here. The direction is clear, but the size is not confirmed.

Target raises of this kind are common after company events, and they tend to move a stock briefly or not at all. Brokers often adjust models to reflect a rolled-forward valuation date or a slightly higher multiple, and that does not require new information about earnings.

What SAP published

Governance material on AI is not new for SAP. The company has maintained a Global AI Ethics Policy since 2022. It runs an AI Ethics Office, an internal steering committee and an external advisory panel, and requires an impact assessment for each AI use case. It also holds ISO 42001 certification for AI management systems. The latest version of the policy on SAP's site is dated June 2026.

What was reported as new this week is a metric for AI business impact. The details have not been confirmed against SAP's own release, and no definition or reported figure could be found in available sources. That matters, because a governance document is a compliance item, while a metric is something analysts can model.

Why the metric matters

The market's central question about enterprise software is whether AI features lift revenue for incumbents or erode their seat-based pricing. A disclosed measure of how much business AI generates or influences for SAP's customers would speak directly to that. It would need to be tied to something concrete, such as AI-related cloud backlog, attach rates or customer outcomes, before it could shift forecasts.

Without that link, the target raises stay what they appear to be: a reaction to a favourable news flow, not a change in the earnings outlook.

What to watch

The next test is SAP's fourth-quarter and full-year results, when the company reports its cloud backlog and gives 2027 guidance. If the AI impact metric appears there with numbers attached and a bridge to cloud revenue, the case for a re-rating strengthens. If it stays a qualitative measure, the brokers' raises will have been the whole story.
