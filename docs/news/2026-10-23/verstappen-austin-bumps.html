Verstappen says Austin track bumps are out of control

In one sentence
Verstappen has criticised the Circuit of the Americas surface before the US Grand Prix, but no track or FIA response has been confirmed.

Max Verstappen has taken aim at the Circuit of the Americas surface again. The F1 champion's complaint, reported by BBC Sport, comes before this weekend's US Grand Prix in Austin, Texas. It is a familiar grievance at a circuit with a long record of bump complaints.

The details of this latest criticism are thin. The BBC report, as relayed to this desk, gives no corner-by-corner account of where the surface is worst. It also gives no word on whether the circuit or the FIA has responded. Until one does, this is a driver grumbling before a race, not a story about the track.

A long-running complaint

The history is why the comments carry some weight. After the 2023 race, Verstappen said the Austin surface was not up to Formula One standard. Lewis Hamilton called it the bumpiest track he had driven, though he allowed that some bumps add character. Esteban Ocon said the bumps were on the limit for car reliability. The track's other tenants have complained too. MotoGP riders have been vocal about the same asphalt.

The circuit has responded before. It resurfaced parts of the lap, including the heavy braking zone at the uphill Turn 1, ahead of the 2024 race. That work was a reaction to the F1 and MotoGP criticism. Resurfacing has not ended the complaints, and the ageing of the remaining asphalt keeps coming up as a factor.

There is also a structural reason the bumps persist. Austin is built on shifting Texas ground, and the current generation of F1 cars runs stiffly and low. Any ripple in the surface goes straight to the driver's spine and the car's floor.

What would make it news

A pre-race driver complaint is routine. It becomes a story only if something follows, such as a track or FIA response. That could be a change to the surface, a revised kerb or a formal statement from the circuit operator. Nothing of that kind has been confirmed. The specifics of Verstappen's remarks, including which corners he meant, are unverified here.

The next data points are practice and qualifying in Austin. Drivers' radio messages and any reports of car damage will show whether the surface is a problem on track or only in the press room. A formal FIA note to teams would be the clearest sign that the complaint is being treated as more than noise.
