Tank barrel production returns to the UK after 20 years

In one sentence
UK large-calibre gun barrel making is being rebuilt, with tank guns tied to Rheinmetall's Telford plant due in 2027, but no contract values or volumes are public.

Britain is restarting the manufacture of large-calibre gun barrels, a capability it lost when the Royal Ordnance Factory at Nottingham closed. Tank barrels are the latest piece to come back onshore. It is a small industrial fact, but a concrete one: the first test of whether the UK can rebuild an armour supply chain it let lapse.

The BBC Business report that prompted this piece describes tank barrel production returning to the UK after about 20 years. That report was not available in full here, and the operator, volumes, customer and start date it gives could not be checked. The detail below comes from earlier reporting on the programme.

What is being rebuilt

The tank-gun element is tied to Rheinmetall's planned large-calibre barrel facility in Telford, which the company calls the "Gun Hall". Reporting describes a plant making 155mm artillery barrels and 120mm tank guns, including for the Challenger 3 main battle tank. Production is scheduled to begin in 2027. The investment is cited at more than £400m in economic value and more than 400 jobs over a decade. The plant stems from the Trinity House Agreement between the UK and Germany, signed by defence secretary John Healey and his German counterpart Boris Pistorius.

A parallel strand is already under way in steel. A contract of about £61m, placed with BAE Systems and Sheffield Forgemasters for 155mm artillery barrels, is restarting the forging of large-calibre barrel blanks. That process had been dormant in the UK for close to two decades. The first four barrels have reportedly shipped as development forgings, destined for Ukraine for final machining and assembly.

The gap became visible in Ukraine, where donated Challenger 2 tanks and AS90 howitzers wore out their barrels faster than Britain could replace them from domestic supply.

The "20 years" caveat

The gap is described inconsistently. The Forgemasters forging restart is put at close to 20 years. Some coverage of the Telford plant says the UK will have domestic capacity again for the first time since 2016, and others say for the first time in over a decade. The 20-year figure is best read as applying to forging, not to every stage of barrel making. Which stage the BBC headline refers to is not established here.

Read-across for listed companies

The listed exposure is indirect. Rheinmetall is German-listed. BAE Systems is the FTSE name most connected to the artillery-barrel work. No contract value has been published for the tank-barrel line, and no volumes are public, so any earnings effect is unquantifiable. The £61m artillery contract is small against BAE's annual revenue, which runs to tens of billions of pounds. The significance is strategic: onshore supply for a high-value component reduces dependence on foreign suppliers and on Ukraine-era stopgaps.

What to watch

Three things will show whether the restart is real capacity or a headline. The first is the 2027 production start at Telford. The second is whether the MoD places multi-year orders that give suppliers a reason to invest. The third is the pace at which Sheffield's development forgings turn into qualified production barrels.
