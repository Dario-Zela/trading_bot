Powerful Tibet earthquake kills at least 16

USGS map graphic of the magnitude-7 earthquake in northern Tibet — USGS / NBC News
In one sentence
A magnitude-7 earthquake in northern Tibet on 22 October killed at least 16 people, injured dozens and collapsed at least 339 homes, with rescue work under way.

A magnitude-7 earthquake struck northern Tibet late on Thursday, killing at least 16 people, injuring dozens and bringing down hundreds of homes in a high, thinly populated stretch of the plateau. Rescue work was continuing as the toll was being counted.

What is known

The quake hit at 10:33 p.m. local time on 22 October. The US Geological Survey placed the epicentre about 191km (119 miles) east-northeast of Nagqu, at a depth of 18km. China's Earthquake Networks Center, cited by the state news agency Xinhua, gave a shallower 10km. Chinese reports locate the epicentre in Baqên County, in Nagqu prefecture. Differences of this kind are routine in the first hours, as agencies refine their readings. Shallow quakes tend to shake the ground harder.

Officials cited by NBC News said at least 339 homes had collapsed, according to the Nagqu media office. Casualty figures differ between outlets. NBC reported 32 injured, 11 of them seriously. Other reports gave a higher figure, up to 54. Police and fire rescue teams were putting up tents for people left without shelter. One report cited more than 1,600 local personnel in the search. Xinhua said communications were down in part of the affected area and that rail service through the region had been halted.

State broadcaster CCTV described the quake as the biggest in the region in five years.

Why the plateau shakes

Tibet sits on the collision zone where the Indian plate pushes under Eurasia, the process that built the Himalaya and keeps the plateau seismically active. The most recent comparable disaster was in January 2025, when a magnitude-7.1 quake near Shigatse killed 126 people and damaged hundreds of houses. Tremors from that event were felt in Kathmandu.

This quake struck farther north, in a sparsely populated area of herding communities, which may limit the number of people exposed. But the same remoteness slows rescue. Roads are long, winter is approaching, and night-time temperatures at that altitude fall well below freezing, so shelter for displaced residents is urgent.

What to watch

The death toll is likely to be revised as teams reach outlying settlements and communications are restored. Aftershocks are the other risk, since they can bring down damaged structures and hinder searches. Questions about building quality will follow if rural housing proves to have collapsed at a disproportionate rate. Chinese authorities typically release updated casualty and housing-damage figures within a day or two, and those numbers will show how severe the damage was.
