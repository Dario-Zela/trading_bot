Ukraine: Trump says Musk has restored Starlink access

In one sentence
The WSJ reports Trump phoned Musk over Starlink limits in Ukraine and Trump says access is restored, but who imposed the cap and whether it is lifted remain unconfirmed.

Ukraine's military relies on a commercial satellite network owned by one man, and this week the terms of that access appear to have been settled by a phone call between two others. The Wall Street Journal reports that President Donald Trump called Elon Musk and asked him to lift the restrictions on Starlink in Ukraine. Trump now says access has been restored. That is the only account of the restoration so far. It comes from the president, and the reports reviewed for this article do not include confirmation from SpaceX or Kyiv.

What was restricted

According to the Journal, as relayed by Ukrainian outlets, the restriction took effect around 14 October. It cut the speed at which military Starlink terminals could stay connected, reportedly to about 160 km/h (100 mph). Fast-moving equipment lost its link as a result. Sources told the paper that some Ukrainian attack and interceptor drones were left close to useless. Ukrainian officials said the change let Russian missiles get past their air defences. President Volodymyr Zelensky warned publicly that Ukrainians could die unless unrestricted access came back. The European Commission urged SpaceX to restore service.

The Journal's reporting is secondhand here, through Ukrainska Pravda, Kyiv Post and others. The original article was not available to read directly.

Who decided

That question is not settled. The Journal's sources say Musk ordered the restriction. Two of them say he and Trump discussed it by phone shortly after it became public, and one says they talked before the decision was made. The paper also reports that both men were unhappy with Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries, which Trump has linked to high energy prices.

Other accounts point the other way. Some US officials say Musk acted on his own and that Trump did not order any punishment of Ukraine. Reuters had earlier reported that an order restricting Starshield, a related system, came directly from the White House.

The principals have said little. Musk wrote on X: "Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS." On 19 October Trump said he did not know who was responsible but "would like to find out." The call to Musk and the claim of restoration came afterwards.

Why it matters

The episode does not depend on who is right about the origin. Either a private owner cut a wartime service on his own judgment, or a head of state worked through that owner to apply pressure. In both cases Ukraine's communications turned on a decision that no government in Kyiv, or in Europe, could make or contest through any formal process. The fix came the same way, by one person asking another.

The point carries beyond Ukraine. Any government that builds military or emergency communications on commercial satellite services is exposed to the owner's mood and to the owner's relationship with Washington. Starlink has been central to Ukrainian operations since 2022, and this is not the first dispute over its use.

What to watch

Three things would give the story more weight. One is confirmation from SpaceX or the Ukrainian military that the speed cap is gone and terminals work at full speed. Another is whether Washington or Musk explains who ordered the cap and under what authority. The third is whether Kyiv and its European backers, who have urged action on the dependence, start funding alternatives.
