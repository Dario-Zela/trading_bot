Detective raped by a colleague says the Met's handling was 'like being raped again'

In one sentence
Det Con Linda Williams, raped by PC David Carrick in 2004, says the Met's handling of her compensation claim felt like being raped again, and she wants Sir Mark Rowley to resign.

A serving Metropolitan Police detective who says she was raped by a colleague two decades ago has told the BBC that the force's handling of her later compensation claim felt "like being raped all over again". Det Con Linda Williams, 60, has waived her anonymity. She is calling for the commissioner, Sir Mark Rowley, to resign.

The colleague was PC David Carrick, who was jailed for life in 2023 for sexual offences against her and 11 other women. Her account moves the focus from the assault itself to what happened when she sought redress from the institution that employed both of them.

The assault and the delay

Williams was a rape investigation officer in the early 2000s. In 2004, she says, Carrick raped her while she was on a three-month attachment with him in south London, far from her usual base. He suggested she stay at his home rather than drive back late at night. She declined. According to her account, he replied: "It's what I do. I'm going to carry on."

She says she "never told a soul" because she believed reporting would have ended her career. She came forward in 2021, after seeing news that Carrick had been charged with raping another woman. Hertfordshire Police investigated her complaint. Carrick was jailed for life in 2023.

The civil claim

Williams has post-traumatic stress disorder. She sued the Met for failing to protect her from Carrick and for making her feel unable to report. The case ran for four years and has now settled. The Met paid compensation "without acceptance of liability". The BBC does not give an amount.

She says the process left her feeling suicidal. What she found hardest was the force's legal position on her injuries. A Met document said a key factor in assessing damages was that the "abuse itself was isolated to a single episode". It also argued that her psychological harm was "not such as to significantly impact her on a day-to-day basis" for at least 15 years. She says the Met is "90% down to the Met Police, 10% to Carrick" when it comes to her PTSD. She also says she would not report a rape to the Met today.

The Met's response

The force says it "tried to reduce further distress and re-traumatisation" and aimed to handle her claims "as quickly and compassionately as possible". It says it recognises "the unique and additional impact" on her and calls her public service "commendable". A spokesperson said Sir Mark has "repeatedly acknowledged" her courage. He has asked to meet her again. She is not convinced it would change anything.

The wider record

Rowley took over in 2022 with the Met in crisis after Sarah Everard's murder by a serving officer. After Carrick's sentencing he said: "We have failed and I'm sorry. He should not have been a police officer." Two months later, Baroness Casey's review described the force as institutionally misogynistic. Williams says he met and apologised to her, but that the apology lacked detail. She says he lacks empathy and is more focused on public perception than on victims and officers. She describes a culture that has not changed enough, citing a "very sexist" environment in which she was locked in the boot of a police car for a shift.

This is one officer's account. The Met's position is that it tried to handle the claim with care, and the settlement carries no admission of fault. The report does not establish a pattern in how the force treats officers who report colleagues. That would need data on complaint outcomes and civil claims, which the Met has not published in this context.

What to watch is whether Rowley's offer to meet her leads anywhere, and whether the Met says if its litigation approach in such claims will change.
