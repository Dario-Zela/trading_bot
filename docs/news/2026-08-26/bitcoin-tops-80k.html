Bitcoin tops $80,000 for first time since May as crypto rally accelerates

Bitcoin traded above $80,000 this week for the first time since May, touching an intraday high of $81,240. — Satheesh Sankaran / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Bitcoin hit $81,240 this week — a three-month high — as Treasury bond buybacks and CLARITY Act momentum drove an eight-day streak of ETF inflows.

Bitcoin traded above $80,000 this week for the first time since May, briefly touching $81,240 before easing back — a three-month high that market participants are reading less as a speculative pop and more as the product of two converging forces: a US Treasury Department newly committed to propping up the long end of the bond market, and a crypto industry closing in on the federal rulebook it has wanted for a decade.

The token's advance this month has been unusually steady by crypto standards. It gained more than $16,000 over the past week alone — among the largest weekly point gains bitcoin has recorded — without the single violent up-day that typically defines its rallies. Spot bitcoin ETFs, the exchange-traded funds that let institutional and retail investors hold bitcoin exposure through ordinary brokerage accounts, have taken in money every trading day for more than a week, with the run extending toward eight straight sessions and pulling in roughly $2.7 billion for the month — the strongest month for the funds since April.

A bond-market move that spilled into crypto

The more surprising catalyst sits nowhere near a crypto exchange. On August 19, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the department would double the size of its buybacks of longer-dated government bonds — lifting individual purchase operations from a $2 billion ceiling to a $4 billion floor and roughly doubling the frequency of long-end operations, from two per quarter to four. The expanded buying, which starts September 9 and runs through early November, is designed to absorb some of the supply pressure that has kept 30-year Treasury yields hovering near 1998 levels.

The immediate bond-market reaction was sharp: the 10-year yield fell more than five basis points and the 30-year dropped nine on the announcement day, before partially reversing later in the week. But the buyback also functions as a signal that the Treasury is willing to lean against tightening financial conditions — the kind of liquidity-friendly backdrop that has historically coincided with rallies in risk assets, bitcoin included. Presto Research analyst Min Jung called the move up through $80,000 "a catch-up trade," noting bitcoin had lagged other risk assets for weeks before the buyback news broke. Arctic Digital's Justin d'Anethan was more bullish, describing the pattern on bitcoin's daily and weekly charts as a "bullish engulfing candle" that "seems to hint at a radical trend change."

The regulatory leg: CLARITY Act momentum

The second driver is closer to home for crypto markets specifically. On August 19, President Trump hosted a roster of industry executives — Coinbase's Brian Armstrong, Gemini's Tyler and Cameron Winklevoss, Kraken parent Payward's Arjun Sethi, Robinhood's Vlad Tenev and Ripple's Brad Garlinghouse among them, alongside SEC Chair Paul Atkins and CFTC Chair Mike Selig — at the White House to push for passage of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act. The bill would settle a long-running jurisdictional fight between the SEC and CFTC over which tokens count as securities versus commodities, the kind of ambiguity that has kept some institutional money on the sidelines.

Trump called it "a very, very powerful structured legislation which will keep us ahead of China, keep us ahead of everyone else," and pressed for what he termed "a fair version" of the bill. Senate Majority Leader John Thune has since filed a procedural motion setting up an initial vote for September 15 — still short of passage, and complicated by an unresolved dispute between the parties over ethics and conflict-of-interest provisions Democrats want attached and illicit-finance safeguards that remain contested. The bill has stalled before; this is its second attempt at a floor vote this year. But the mere scheduling of a date gave traders a concrete near-term event to price toward, which analysts said fed directly into the short-covering that amplified the week's gains.

A rally with an asterisk

The advance is not without caveats worth stating plainly. Bitcoin's push to $81,240 came with a pullback the same day, back to around $78,800, as some of the move proved to be short-position liquidations rather than fresh buying — traders who had bet against bitcoin being forced to close those bets as the price rose, mechanically adding to the upward pressure. And for context that gets lost in "three-month high" headlines: bitcoin remains roughly 36% below the approximately $126,000 peak it hit in October 2025, meaning this week's move is a recovery within a longer drawdown, not a return to prior highs.

Still, the composition of the buying is what separates this rally from bitcoin's more purely retail-driven runs. BlackRock's IBIT fund alone has accounted for roughly 62% of the month's ETF inflows, and the eight-session streak represents money moving through regulated, custodied vehicles rather than exchange order books — a distinction that matters to anyone trying to judge whether a rally has legs or is simply riding a squeeze. Total US spot bitcoin ETF assets now sit near $98.6 billion, having crossed that threshold only in the past several sessions.

The next two data points worth watching sit roughly three weeks apart. The Treasury's expanded buyback operations begin September 9, and the Senate's procedural vote on the CLARITY Act is scheduled for September 15 — assuming the underlying disputes get resolved before then. Whether bitcoin holds above $80,000 through both dates, or gives the week's gains back to profit-taking, will say more about the durability of this rally than the initial break through the round number did.
