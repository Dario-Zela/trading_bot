Premier League clubs scramble before the 1 September transfer deadline

The City Ground, Nottingham Forest's home, where a club-record deal for Liam Delap has been agreed. — Arne Müseler / www.arne-mueseler.com (CC BY-SA 3.0 DE)
In one sentence
Nottingham Forest have agreed a club-record £50m deal for Chelsea's Liam Delap, testing whether mid-table money can now outbid the traditional order before Tuesday's deadline.

Nottingham Forest do not, as a rule, out-negotiate Chelsea for young English strikers. That they appear to have just done so — agreeing a club-record deal worth up to £50m for Liam Delap less than a week before the transfer window shuts — is the kind of detail that makes deadline week interesting beyond the usual scramble of loan swaps and medicals booked in airport hotels.

The deal, first reported by Fabrizio Romano and since confirmed by Sky Sports and ESPN, sees Forest pay around £45m up front with add-ons taking the total towards £50m. A medical is expected on Wednesday. For a club that finished seventh last season and whose entire summer has been shadowed by an ownership row between Evangelos Marinakis and outgoing manager Nuno Espírito Santo, it is a statement signing — the largest fee Forest have ever paid, for a player Chelsea signed just fourteen months ago.

A striker nobody wanted enough

Delap's season at Stamford Bridge has been one of the summer's quieter disappointments. Signed from Ipswich Town for around £30m last year with real fanfare — an England-eligible number nine still only 22 — he managed just two goals in 41 appearances under Enzo Maresca and, more recently, Xabi Alonso, and was left out of the matchday squad entirely for Monday's 3-2 win over Fulham. Chelsea, mid-way through a wider squad clearout under Alonso, stand to turn a profit of roughly £20m on a player who barely got a run of games.

That is precisely the kind of player mid-table clubs used to get on loan, not buy outright for a club record. Forest's own manager was notably circumspect when asked about it before Monday's game. "I can tell everybody that he doesn't play today," Oliver Glasner said. "Everything else we shall see in the next days." Romano's reporting suggests the deciding factor was more human than tactical: Delap, after speaking to Glasner directly, decided he would rather be first-choice at the City Ground than fighting for minutes in west London. Forest, who lost Taiwo Awoniyi to Coventry for £9m earlier in the window, needed a focal point up front — and were prepared to pay elite-level money to get one who is still young enough to improve.

Arsenal's £128m gamble

The bigger number in this window belongs to Arsenal, who are pushing to sign Julián Álvarez from Atlético Madrid in a deal reportedly worth up to £128m — a British transfer record, if it lands. The 26-year-old Argentine had set his heart on Barcelona, but Atlético have refused to sell him to a direct La Liga rival, and Barcelona are understood to have told Álvarez they will not extend their own pursuit beyond this week. That leaves Arsenal as, in Sky Sports' phrase, "the only get-out" for a player who was reportedly left "devastated" by his own supporters' reaction to the saga.

Unlike Forest's move for Delap, this is not mid-table ambition outbidding tradition — it is the usual order reasserting itself, with the reigning Premier League champions using financial muscle to prise a wanted man from a club that would rather not sell him at all. Atlético are said to be holding firm on their €150m valuation, and ESPN describes the mood around the negotiation as "extremely tense" with barely a week of window left to resolve it.

United's persistent itch

Manchester United, meanwhile, are still working the phones rather than signing contracts. A left-back has been on their list for weeks — Luke Shaw has started 43 of United's last 44 league matches, an underline of both his importance and the lack of any credible deputy — and Newcastle's Lewis Hall remains the preferred target. Newcastle, by most accounts, do not want to sell, which has pushed United to sound out alternatives including Barcelona's Alejandro Balde, whom they would rather take on loan than buy outright, and Racing Santander's Jorge Salinas. None of it, as of Wednesday, has produced an actual bid Newcastle would entertain. A new central midfielder remains on the same list, competing for the same finite transfer budget.

What the week tells you

Three clubs, three different relationships with the market: Forest paying up for certainty, Arsenal paying up because the alternative is watching a rival sign a target they already wanted, and United still hoping a cheaper route to the same problem appears before Tuesday. The Delap fee is the one worth remembering after deadline day passes — not because £50m is enormous by modern standards, but because it was Nottingham Forest, not Manchester City or Arsenal, willing to pay it for a 22-year-old the traditional powers had already decided wasn't worth persisting with. Whether Glasner turns that gamble into goals will say more about the transfer window's real winners than any of the numbers being quoted this week.

The deadline falls at 11pm on 1 September. Delap's medical, Álvarez's fee negotiation with Atlético, and United's left-back search are all likely to resolve — one way or another — before then.
