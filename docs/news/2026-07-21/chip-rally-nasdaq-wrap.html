Chip rally lifts Nasdaq 100 as Micron jumps 5%, but Dow and small caps lag

A Micron Technology semiconductor fabrication facility in Taichung, Taiwan. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Chip stocks rebounded from last week's selloff, lifting the Nasdaq 100 and S&P 500 even as the Dow and small caps fell.

Chip stocks pulled Wall Street's growth names higher on Monday, but the rest of the market didn't come along for the ride. The Nasdaq 100 added 0.4% to 28,715 and the S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,482, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.2% to 52,025 and the small-cap Russell 2000 gave back 0.2% to 2,957.

The split tape had a clear author: semiconductors. Micron Technology jumped 5.7%, Broadcom gained 3.4%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 2% after a bruising prior week. Technology was the best-performing S&P sector, up 0.9%, followed by energy and communication services. Materials, real estate, utilities and healthcare all closed lower.

A rebound, not a resolution

Monday's gains were less a fresh rally than a partial retracing of losses. The chip sector had just come off a bear-market decline — the Philadelphia SE Semiconductor Index finished last week more than 20% below its late-June record, and Micron alone had shed roughly 13% in a single session, erasing an estimated $138 billion in market value. Intel and AMD fell 9% and 7% respectively in that stretch.

The trigger for that selloff was less about chip demand than about who captures the profits from it. Meta signaled it may have built more AI computing capacity than it currently needs, raising questions about whether the industry's capital spending has outrun near-term demand. At the same time, custom silicon from hyperscalers and a new Cerebras–OpenAI partnership chipped away at the assumption that Nvidia-style GPUs are the only game in town, and reports that SK Hynix was slowing its high-bandwidth memory expansion added to the unease. None of that thesis reversed over the weekend; Monday's bounce reads more as bargain-hunting after a steep drop than a verdict that the concerns were wrong.

Narrow leadership, broad caution

That is the tension in Monday's numbers. A market genuinely regaining conviction would tend to lift cyclicals and smaller companies alongside the mega-cap winners — instead, the Dow and Russell 2000 both closed in the red, and defensive-leaning sectors such as utilities and healthcare lagged. Rotation into a handful of chip and AI-infrastructure names, rather than broad participation, is a pattern investors have seen repeatedly this cycle: strength concentrated in stocks whose earnings are tied directly to AI capital spending, with everything else treading water or worse.

Chris Zaccarelli of Northlight Asset Management framed the backdrop for the coming week bluntly: "Our expectation is that earnings will continue to be good. It's just a little bit harder to tell if they will be better than expected because there's such a high bar at this point." Elsewhere in markets, the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up 4 basis points to 4.61%, crude held near $82.60 a barrel, and bitcoin added 1.5% to roughly $65,300 — none of it suggesting a broad shift in risk appetite beyond the chip trade itself.

The next test arrives fast. Alphabet, Tesla, Intel and IBM all report earnings this week, and Intel's results in particular will be read as a referendum on whether the chip rebound has legs or was simply an oversold bounce. A broadening of gains beyond semiconductors — into the Dow's industrial and financial names, or into small caps — would be the clearer signal that conviction, not just chip earnings, is driving the market. Until then, Monday's advance is best read as a sector story wearing an index-level headline.
