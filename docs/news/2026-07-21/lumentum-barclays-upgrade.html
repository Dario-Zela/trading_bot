Lumentum extends 2026 rally as Barclays sees more room to run

An optical transceiver module of the kind used to link servers and switches inside AI data centers — the market Lumentum supplies. — OUSENT (Cherizhang) / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
In one sentence
Barclays upgraded Lumentum to Overweight after a pullback, saying AI-optics demand remains intact — shares jumped over 9%.

Lumentum Holdings shares jumped more than 9% on Monday after Barclays upgraded the optical components maker to Overweight from Equal Weight, arguing that a three-month slide in the stock had priced in doubts about artificial-intelligence infrastructure spending that the underlying business doesn't support.

"Nothing has changed. The underlying fundamentals remain strong for transceiver/laser demand, and we like the risk/reward on the pullback," Barclays told clients in a note accompanying the upgrade.

The firm kept its price target at $1,000, implying roughly 36% upside from Friday's close. That's a notable stance given where the stock already sits: Lumentum has more than doubled in 2026, up around 108% year to date and 677% over the past twelve months, as data-center operators buy up the optical transceivers and lasers that move data between GPUs at high speed. But the run hasn't been a straight line — LITE had fallen more than 11% over the prior three months, underperforming its optics peers by roughly 40% over the same stretch, according to Barclays' figures.

What spooked investors

The pullback traces largely to timing questions around co-packaged optics, or CPO — technology that fuses optical engines directly onto a chip package rather than routing signals through separate pluggable transceivers, cutting power draw as AI clusters scale toward hundreds of thousands of accelerators. Nvidia committed to a multiyear partnership with Lumentum in March, including a roughly $2 billion investment and a multibillion-dollar purchase commitment tied to CPO components. Investors have since fretted about whether that rollout arrives on the schedule Nvidia has sketched out, or slips.

Barclays' read is that the worry outran the evidence. JPMorgan's Samik Chatterjee reached a similar conclusion earlier this year, citing supply-chain checks suggesting Nvidia's large-scale CPO deployment remains on track and may even be running ahead of schedule. Lumentum's own disclosures point the same direction: an optical circuit switch backlog above $400 million and a separate multi-hundred-million-dollar CPO order slated for delivery in the first half of calendar 2027, according to comments from chief executive Michael Hurlston.

Wall Street's broader positioning already leaned bullish before Monday. Of 25 analysts covering the stock, 20 rate it Buy or Strong Buy against five Holds, with an average price target of $1,104.89 — more than 50% above Friday's close. Barclays' move brings one of the more cautious holdouts into the bull camp, and does so on a straightforward premise: that a stock trading off short-term nerves is a different animal from a stock whose end demand has actually softened.

The next real test lands August 11, when Lumentum reports fiscal fourth-quarter results. Revenue growth has been running north of 60% year over year through the AI buildout; whether that pace holds — and whether management offers firmer detail on CPO shipment timing — will settle whether Monday's rally reflects durable demand or another swing in a stock that has spent 2026 moving in both directions at speed.
