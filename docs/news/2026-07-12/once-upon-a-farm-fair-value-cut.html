Once Upon A Farm's fair value cut 10% after analyst reset

Jennifer Garner, co-founder and brand spokesperson of Once Upon A Farm, which listed on the NYSE in February 2026. — ColliderVideo, via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0)
In one sentence
Analysts cut Once Upon A Farm's average price target 10% to $24.38 after weak 2026 guidance, a company-specific reset rather than sector contagion.

Analysts tracking Once Upon A Farm PBC have trimmed the average 12-month fair value estimate for the stock to $24.38, down from $27.13 — a roughly 10% reset compiled by Simply Wall St. from the models of firms including TD Cowen and Barclays. The company, the organic baby-food maker co-founded by Jennifer Garner and now-CEO John Foraker, went public on the New York Stock Exchange on February 6 this year, pricing its IPO at $18 a share and closing its debut day up 17%.

The reset traces to a specific event rather than a vague vibe shift. Once Upon A Farm's first earnings report as a public company, on May 7, showed solid 2025 numbers but a 2026 sales outlook of $302 million to $310 million — growth of 25% to 29%, a sharp deceleration from the 53% expansion the company posted the year before. Adjusted EBITDA guidance of just $2 million to $4 million left little room to argue the business is turning a corner on profitability. Shares fell roughly 7% on the print and have since traded as low as $14, well off the $27 high they touched earlier in the year.

TD Cowen and Barclays both cut targets in the reset that produced the new $24.38 consensus — TD Cowen's move accounting for roughly $8 of the reduction, Barclays for about $5 — while continuing coverage rather than dropping the stock. Evercore ISI and Deutsche Bank have also lowered targets since the print, to $23 and $17 respectively, with ratings that read more "wait and see" than "sell." Simply Wall St.'s own discounted-cash-flow inputs shifted alongside the target cuts: the model's forward price-to-earnings multiple came down to 41.7 times from 52.4 times, even as projected revenue growth and margins ticked up slightly. In plain terms, the earnings power hasn't obviously worsened — analysts have simply decided they should pay less for it.

Company problem, or sector problem?

That distinction matters for reading across to the rest of consumer staples. Once Upon A Farm's cut lands alongside a broader bout of analyst caution in packaged food: Stephens downgraded Campbell Soup to Equal Weight from Overweight in June and cut its target to $21 from $28, citing margin pressure from heavier promotional spending, and Simply Good Foods has drawn similar scrutiny over declining Atkins sales. But the through-line at those companies is margin compression in mature, competitive categories — not the growth-deceleration story driving Once Upon A Farm's reset. A five-month-old IPO burning through its growth premium is a different animal from a decades-old soup brand losing pricing power. The honest read is that this is a company-specific repricing of a newly public growth story that arrived amid, rather than because of, a jittery moment for food stocks generally.

The next real test comes September 2, when Once Upon A Farm reports second-quarter results. Whether net sales growth tracks toward the low or high end of that 25-to-29% guidance range — and whether adjusted EBITDA clears even the low end of its $2 million to $4 million range — will do more to settle the valuation question than any further round of target trims.
