Ranulph Fiennes's family do not know where he is; UK warmth reaches 26C

In one sentence
Relatives of the 82-year-old explorer Sir Ranulph Fiennes say they cannot find him, while police say he is safe and cared for, and the UK enjoys a brief 26C late-September warm spell.

The family of Sir Ranulph Fiennes say they do not know where he is. The explorer is 82. He has not been seen in public for about two years. The King has reportedly been alerted.

His stepson Alexander says the last time he saw Fiennes in person was in September 2024, at the explorer's home. Friends told the Telegraph that letters and Christmas cards sent afterwards came back unopened.

A rift, and a care home

The reports describe a family rift involving Fiennes's second wife, Louise Millington-Cotes. She is said to have declined to put the King in touch with her husband. Accounts also say she has limited his contact with friends and relatives. They allege he has been moved between care homes, some under assumed names. Those are allegations in press reports, and the search results carried no response from Millington-Cotes.

There has been one glimpse. Arabella Pepper, his former sister-in-law, saw him in July 2025 after admitting herself to a care home where she had been told he was staying. She described the relief on his face. He asked her to tell everyone he loved them and was longing to see them.

Cheshire Police say they are aware of the concerns. They say they are satisfied he is living in an appropriate setting with suitable care, and that there are "no immediate safeguarding concerns". The gap between that statement and the family's account is the heart of the story. Officers say he is safe, and the people who know him best say they cannot check.

Fiennes made his name on polar crossings, the Transglobe Expedition and an ascent of Everest in his sixties. He is a man whose whereabouts were once tracked by satellite and celebrated in the press. He is now being searched for by his own relatives.

Meanwhile, a warm spell

Elsewhere, the British weather is doing something unseasonable. Tuesday is forecast to be the warmest day of the week, with up to 26C possible in the south-east of England. That is well above typical late-September highs. Cloud is expected to build in the north as a cold front moves south-east. Wednesday turns unsettled, with rain and cooler air from the north-west. Thursday looks dry and bright again, particularly in the south.

Forecasters describe a warmer-than-average but changeable end to the month, with more settled conditions possible as October begins. Nothing here is extraordinary. It is a last taste of summer, and it will be brief.

What to watch

The weather will have moved on by Thursday. The Fiennes question will not. The next test is whether the family, or the King's office, is given access to him, or whether police widen their inquiries beyond the welfare check already made.
