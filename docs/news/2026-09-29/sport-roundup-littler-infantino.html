Littler out at the World Grand Prix; Infantino raises his cash pledge; Leicestershire get a partial reprieve

In one sentence
Littler, the world number one, lost a first-round tie at the World Grand Prix; Infantino signalled more Fifa cash for members; Leicestershire cut their penalty to 19 points but face relegation.

Luke Littler, the world number one and defending champion, is out of the World Grand Prix in Leicester after a 2-1 first-round defeat by Luke Woodhouse. Elsewhere in sport, Fifa's president has signalled he will pay national associations more from the governing body's reserves. Leicestershire's cricketers have won back some points but not their safety.

Littler undone by his own average

Littler took the opening set, then drifted. He averaged 82.41, well short of the standard that made him favourite, and Woodhouse levelled the match before winning the deciding set 3-1 in legs with a 75 checkout. Woodhouse now plays Nathan Aspinall, the 2022 runner-up, in the last 16.

One early exit does not make a slump. The tournament's double-in, double-out format is brutal on the best player in the field, and a single set can turn on one missed double. But the result matters commercially in a small way. Littler is a headline draw for bookmakers and broadcasters, and a defending champion leaving on the first night takes a big name out of the later rounds and shifts outright prices towards his rivals. The triage material describes his market pull, but no odds movement or sponsor reaction has been reported, so any wider financial effect remains conjecture. His form will be judged again at the season's next major events.

Infantino opens the reserves

Gianni Infantino has told Fifa's six continental confederations that he would back "the greatest level of additional funding that can responsibly be delivered". His letter follows demands from Uefa and Concacaf that Fifa pay $10m (£7.5m) to each of its 211 members from its reserves, which are put at about £4.4bn.

The pressure comes from the collapse of his Fifa Forward Enterprise plan, which proposed selling stakes in the World Cup and other competitions to private investors and drew a fierce backlash. Infantino described the withdrawn proposal as one "intended to enable Fifa to invest more in football". No new figure has been named. The Fifa Council takes up the question when it meets on 15 October, and that is when the size of the pledge should become clear.

Leicestershire's partial reprieve

Leicestershire were docked 27 County Championship points on 16 September for breaching pitch regulations in August's win over Glamorgan. The pitch was blamed on a Bollywood film shoot. The penalty has now been cut to 19 points, after the Cricket Regulator asked the Cricket Discipline Panel whether it had accounted for the 19 points already lost by forfeiting the result of that match. The extra eight-point sanction was waived on the grounds that losing the match points was punishment enough.

That is not enough to save the club. With the 19-point penalty in place, Leicestershire sit 17 points behind Essex and will be relegated from Division One if it stands. They have chosen to pursue a full appeal rather than accept the reduced sanction, so the club's status stays unresolved until that process ends.

What ties them together

All three stories turn on rules and money more than on results. Littler's exit is a commercial jolt only if the market treats it as one. Infantino's cash comes with a vote attached on 15 October. Leicestershire's fate rests on an appeal whose outcome decides whether a partial win means anything.
