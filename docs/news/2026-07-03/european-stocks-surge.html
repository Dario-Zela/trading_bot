European stocks rally, oil slips as defense and luxury shares lead

Rheinmetall's KF51 Panther — the German arms maker led Thursday's rally, up more than 6%, on the back of the UK's new £298 billion defense investment plan. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
European defense and luxury stocks rallied together Thursday — one on a UK rearmament pledge, the other on falling war-risk premiums — as oil hit a five-month low.

European equities closed higher Thursday in a session with an odd pairing at the top of the leaderboard: arms makers and handbag makers. The Euro Stoxx 50 added 1.37% to close near 6,370, with defense contractors and luxury-goods houses supplying most of the lift, while crude oil kept sliding — Brent fell below $71 a barrel, its lowest level since late February.

Rheinmetall led the defense bloc, up 6.07%, with Thales adding 2.05%, Saab 3.32% and BAE Systems 1.95%. The push came a day after London published its Defence Investment Plan — an extra £15 billion ($20 billion) layered onto existing budgets, part of a £298 billion package over four years that lifts UK defense spending to £79.1 billion, or 2.7% of GDP, by 2029. Roughly £5 billion of that is earmarked for drones and autonomous systems, £8.2 billion for the UK-Italy-Japan Tempest stealth-fighter programme, and £64 billion for renewing the nuclear deterrent, including new Dreadnought and SSN-AUKUS submarines.

LVMH, meanwhile, gained 1.6%, extending a firmer run across the luxury names that had been among the hardest hit by the Middle East conflict that broke out in February. The Gulf has been a fast-growing market for the sector even through a broader, years-long slump tied to soft Chinese demand, which is why luxury stocks have moved almost in lockstep with war-risk headlines since. Thursday's driver was oil: shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has climbed above 10 million barrels a day with US naval support, Iranian exports have topped 40 million barrels since Washington lifted its blockade, and record Russian seaborne volumes have added to a general glut. All of that, plus signs of progress in indirect US-Iran talks, pushed Brent to its lowest since before the war started and WTI toward $68.

Same rally, two different engines

The pairing looks contradictory only at first glance. Luxury is rising because investors read the oil slide as confirmation that Gulf demand and shipping risk are normalizing — a straightforward de-escalation trade. Defense is rising for close to the opposite reason: European governments are locking in higher structural spending regardless of how the Iran conflict resolves, a commitment now large enough — Germany's Rheinmetall-led buildout, the UK's £298 billion plan — to keep order books full for years. The two sectors are pricing different variables, which is exactly why they can rally on the same day.

The near-term test is whether the peace track holds. Doha talks were already delicate, and they now face a delay after the death of Iran's former Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei, whose funeral begins July 4. Any relapse would send oil — and luxury shares — back the other way; the defense trade, tied to multi-year budget lines rather than headlines, would be far less exposed.
