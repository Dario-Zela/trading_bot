SpaceX and Marvell crash the most-searched list, Micron stays on top

A SpaceX Falcon 9 lifts off — the company's own stock became one of the most-searched tickers on Benzinga Pro within weeks of going public. — Mussi Katz / Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
In one sentence
Micron stayed the most-searched ticker on Benzinga Pro for a second month, while newly public SpaceX and surging Marvell cracked the June top 12.

Every month, Benzinga Pro tallies which stock tickers its subscribers type into the search bar most often — a rough proxy for what retail and professional traders alike are paying attention to. The June list, out this week, has a familiar name at the top and two new arrivals that tell their own story about the month that was.

Micron Technology held the number-one spot for a second straight month, no great surprise for a stock that closed at an all-time high of $1,213.56 on June 25 after reporting fiscal third-quarter revenue up 346% to $41.5 billion and gross margins near 85%. Micron shares are up roughly 300% for the year — the kind of chart that keeps a ticker pinned to the top of anyone's search history.

The more interesting story is who joined the list. SpaceX debuted on the Nasdaq on June 12 under the ticker SPCX, in what became the largest IPO in history — a $75 billion raise on a $1.77 trillion valuation, with Elon Musk retaining more than 82% of the voting power. Shares priced at $135, opened at $150, and closed their first day up about 19%. Benzinga's data team called SpaceX's entry at number three "one of the highest ever initial entries" on its leaderboard — a striking result given the stock had been public for less than two-thirds of the month it was being measured.

A search list is not a scoreboard

Marvell Technology rounded out the notable new entries at number five, having tripled in value this year on the strength of AI-chip demand. The stock jumped more than 25% in a single session in early June after Nvidia's Jensen Huang, speaking at Computex Taipei, called Marvell a "trillion-dollar candidate" — Nvidia had put roughly $2 billion behind the company. The rally kept going: UBS raised its price target to $340 from $230 on June 30, citing Marvell's position in CXL data-center interconnect technology.

The rest of the top 12 — SPY, Tesla, Nvidia, Intel, SanDisk, Apple, a small-cap called Freecast, Oracle and Broadcom — reads like a snapshot of where trader attention concentrated during a chip-heavy month. Nebius, IREN, Qualcomm and Rocket Lab all fell out of the rankings from May.

What the list doesn't do is tell anyone whether searching a ticker preceded buying it, selling it, or just gawking at the chart. Benzinga doesn't publish a search-to-trade correlation, so the ranking works better as a mood board for retail attention than as a signal to act on. SpaceX's first full month of public trading, and whether its search volume converts into sustained volume on the tape, will be a better test of that question than its debut-month novelty.
