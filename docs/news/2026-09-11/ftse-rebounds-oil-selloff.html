FTSE 100 claws back ground after oil-driven inflation selloff

The London Stock Exchange building in Paternoster Square, City of London. — London Stock Exchange / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
FTSE 100 tried to recover Friday from a 1.3% oil-driven slide, but hotter UK inflation has cut the odds of a September BoE rate cut from 70% to 25%.

London's blue-chip index edged higher on Friday, clawing back a sliver of the ground lost in Wednesday's rout, as traders tried to decide which story matters more right now: oil-driven inflation risk, or hope that the Bank of England still has room to cut.

The FTSE 100 fell 1.31% on Wednesday to 10,670.06 — its sharpest one-day decline in weeks — as Brent crude broke above $100 a barrel for the first time since July. The FTSE 250 dropped 0.99% and the broader FTSE All-Share lost 1.27%. By Friday the index was attempting a modest recovery, but the move looked tentative rather than a change of mind.

What actually moved the market

The proximate cause was the Strait of Hormuz. Vessel transits through the strait fell to seven over one 10-day window, against a running average of 14, after an Iranian ballistic missile attack on a US Navy warship drew a retaliatory strike that destroyed five Iranian tankers. Brent rose 3.36% on the day to $101.21 — its highest close since May — and has since traded as high as $108 amid separate Houthi strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure in Abha, Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait.

The sector split on Wednesday told its own story. BP rose 1.3% and IG Group gained 1%, energy and volatility plays doing what they do when crude spikes. On the other side, Auto Trader fell 4.7%, Burberry 4% and Melrose Industries 3.5% — consumer and industrial names that are first to be marked down when higher energy costs threaten margins and household spending. That is a market repricing input costs and demand simultaneously, not just reacting to a headline.

The rate-cut question gets harder

The FTSE's problem is that oil is complicating the Bank of England's job at the worst possible moment. UK headline CPI jumped to 3.4% in the year to August, up from 2.9% in July, with services inflation — the metric the Monetary Policy Committee watches most closely — accelerating to 4.6%. Markets had been pricing roughly a 70% chance of a quarter-point cut at the MPC's 17 September meeting; after that inflation print, that probability fell to around 25%. Governor Andrew Bailey held Bank Rate at 3.75% in July for a fifth straight meeting, on a 6-3 vote, and support for a hike rather than a cut has been building rather than fading.

A sustained run above $100 oil makes that arithmetic worse still: higher energy costs feed directly into the inflation print the Bank is trying to bring down, at exactly the point traders had hoped for relief. That is the tension behind Friday's hesitant bounce — investors are not so much betting on a cut as hoping the geopolitical premium in oil proves temporary enough that it doesn't force the MPC's hand.

What's next

The Bank's 17 September decision is now the pivot point for the FTSE's next real move, not Friday's drift. If Hormuz traffic stays constrained and Brent holds above $100 into that meeting, a cut looks increasingly unlikely — and equities will be left leaning on a rate-relief narrative that the data no longer supports. Watch shipping-traffic figures out of the strait and any signal from Saudi Arabia on infrastructure damage; both will move oil, and oil, for now, is moving everything else.
