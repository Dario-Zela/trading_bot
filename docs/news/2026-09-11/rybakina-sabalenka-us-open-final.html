Rybakina to face Sabalenka in blockbuster US Open final

Elena Rybakina, who beat Coco Gauff in the semi-final to reach the US Open final and clinch the world No. 1 ranking. — Hameltion / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Elena Rybakina beat Coco Gauff to reach the US Open final against two-time champion Aryna Sabalenka, with the world No. 1 ranking already secured but the bigger prize still open.

Elena Rybakina will play for the US Open title on Saturday having already won the argument that mattered most to her ranking: by reaching the final, the Kazakh guarantees she becomes the new world No. 1 on Monday, regardless of what happens against Aryna Sabalenka. What she has not yet settled is the one that matters more to her career — whether she can beat the two-time defending champion when the trophy is actually on the line.

Rybakina battled past Coco Gauff 3-6, 6-4, 6-4 under the lights at Arthur Ashe Stadium on Thursday, dropping the opener before Gauff's first serve started to leak and her forehand followed it into the net. It is the kind of match Rybakina has made something of a signature this season — untroubled by an early setback, sharpening exactly when the opponent expects otherwise.

"Coco, she's a great player. It's always tough to play against her," Rybakina said afterward. "Even if sometimes her first serve doesn't go, the second one is so heavy." Asked about the ranking she is about to inherit, she was characteristically unmoved: "It's just a number right now... mostly you think about the titles in the end of the day."

A rematch with the incumbent

Sabalenka needed less convincing to reach her own final. She dismantled No. 3 seed Jessica Pegula 7-5, 6-2, surrendering only a single break point across the entire match and closing it out despite Pegula, by her own admission, playing some of her best tennis of the tournament. "I thought her level was insanely high," Pegula said. "She played insane." Sabalenka called it simply the tennis she needed: "I was so desperate for the win, I wanted it badly."

The win stretches Sabalenka's US Open winning streak to 20 matches and puts her a victory away from a third straight title in New York — a feat no woman has managed since Serena Williams won three in a row between 2012 and 2014. It also sets up a third Grand Slam final between the two players, after finals at the Australian Open in 2023 (Sabalenka) and this year (Rybakina), meaning Saturday's match doubles as a decider in their major-final rivalry.

What's actually at stake

Strip away the mathematics and the shape of the match is simple: Rybakina has already banked the No. 1 ranking no matter the result, but a win would let her pair the top spot with the biggest title of her career and take the outright lead in their head-to-head at Grand Slam finals. A Sabalenka win would deny Rybakina the double, hand the Belarusian a third straight US Open crown, and set up a swift bid to reclaim No. 1 before the season's remaining tournaments run out. Overall head-to-head still favours Sabalenka 10-7, but Rybakina has won the two meetings that mattered most this year, beating her in the Australian Open final and at the WTA Finals.

That tension — dominant incumbent against an ascending challenger who now, officially, outranks her — is what makes Saturday more than a formality. Sabalenka has treated Flushing Meadows as her personal address for two years running; Rybakina arrives having already forced her off the top of the rankings without needing to beat her to do it. Only one of those facts survives the final.

The women's final is scheduled for Saturday at Arthur Ashe Stadium. A Sabalenka win would put her within one tournament of tying the Grand Slam-final head-to-head at 2-2 and reopen the race for No. 1 before the year-end WTA Finals in Riyadh; a Rybakina win would make her the first player since Sabalenka herself to hold both the top ranking and the US Open title simultaneously.
