Shell reshuffles US retail and power assets in $715m-plus deals

Shell is reshuffling its US retail and power generation assets in a series of deals worth over $715 million. — Michael Rivera (Mjrmtg) / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
In one sentence
Constellation is buying Shell's 609MW Rhode Island gas plant for $715m as Shell swaps US power assets and takes full control of a 320-site fuel retailer.

Shell spent the second week of September reshaping two very different corners of its US business at once — one deal handing a New England power plant to a rival generator, the other pulling a Tennessee fuel retailer fully in-house. Neither move alone would be more than a footnote. Together they sketch a company sorting its US assets into what it wants to run itself and what it would rather sell to someone who wants it more.

The larger number belongs to Constellation Energy, which agreed to buy the Rhode Island State Energy Center (RISEC) from Shell Energy North America for $715 million, or roughly $580 million after first-year tax benefits. RISEC is a 609-megawatt combined-cycle gas plant in Johnston, Rhode Island, running since 2002 and selling power and capacity into the ISO New England wholesale market. Constellation chief executive Joe Dominguez called it "a high-performing asset that perfectly complements Constellation's extraordinarily successful customer business in New England," and the company says the deal is immediately accretive to operating earnings, clearing its internal 10% unlevered-return threshold without touching the $5 billion share-buyback programme it has authorised through the end of 2027.

A two-way trade, not a retreat

Shell isn't simply exiting US power generation. In the same announcement it disclosed it is buying full ownership of Hunlock Creek Generating, a 169-megawatt gas facility in Pennsylvania comprising a 125-megawatt combined-cycle unit and a 44-megawatt peaker. Shell has effectively been trading a merchant asset in a capacity market it doesn't want to hold against one that fits closer to its existing US supply book — RISEC's output had already been flowing to Shell since 2019 under an energy-conversion agreement that terminates once Constellation completes the purchase. Both transactions still need customary regulatory sign-off, with completion targeted for the first quarter of 2027.

For Constellation, the purchase extends a buying spree that has already reshaped the company this year, most notably its $29.1 billion acquisition of Calpine, which analysts expect to lift 2026 non-GAAP earnings per share by roughly 20%. Adding a New England gas plant to a fleet built heavily around nuclear generation gives Constellation more flexible capacity in a region where power demand — driven in part by data-centre growth — has been outrunning new supply.

The retail side of the ledger

Shell's other September move sits well away from power markets. Equilon Enterprises, operating as Shell Oil Products US, is raising its stake in Tri Star Energy from 33% to 100%, buying out the remaining interest from the Parman Corporation and Kimbro Oil Company. Tri Star, a Nashville-based operator founded in 2000, brings 320 company-run fuel and convenience sites across Tennessee and neighbouring states, plus supply agreements covering another 552 dealer-owned locations, under brands including Twice Daily, Sudden Service and Little General. Financial terms weren't disclosed, and the deal is expected to close by year-end pending regulatory approval — a faster timeline than the power-plant swaps.

Taken together, the three transactions read less like a single strategic pivot than routine portfolio triage at a company with US assets in fuel retail, gas-fired generation and everything adjacent to both. Shell is consolidating where it already has scale — more than doubling its company-owned US convenience footprint through Tri Star — while handing off a merchant power asset to a buyer for whom New England capacity is a better strategic fit. Whether that logic extends to other regional plants in Shell's US generation book is the next thing worth watching, particularly as regulators in Rhode Island and Pennsylvania work through the approvals that both power deals still need before their expected first-quarter-2027 close.
