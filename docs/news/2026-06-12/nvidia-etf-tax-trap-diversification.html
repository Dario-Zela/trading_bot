Nvidia insiders with concentrated mega-gains explore ETF structures to sidestep capital-gains lock-in

Nvidia's headquarters campus in Santa Clara, California — Kevin McCarthy / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
NVIDIA insiders locked into massive unrealized gains are turning to exchange funds and Section 351 ETF structures, but each route carries meaningful trade-offs in time, access, and IRS eligibility.

The math that made Nvidia insiders wealthy is the same math that now keeps them stuck. Shares accumulated in the early-to-mid 2010s have appreciated more than 400-fold on a split-adjusted basis over the past decade, and selling even a slice of those holdings triggers a federal capital gains bill — up to 20% on long-term gains, and as high as 37% on short-term — before state levies arrive. In California, where much of the company's workforce is concentrated, add another 13.3%. For holders sitting on positions worth tens or hundreds of millions of dollars, the resulting tax drag can easily consume a third of proceeds. The rational response, for many, has been to hold.

The aggregate scale of that paralysis is significant. Nvidia insider selling has exceeded $3.3 billion in recent years — a figure that represents only what holders chose to monetize at full cost. Jensen Huang, whose personal stake in the company represents the overwhelming share of his estimated net worth, holds tens of billions on paper. AllianceBernstein published research late last year describing the broader phenomenon as "unlocking the golden handcuffs" — a phrase that captures the structural bind: concentrated mega-gains are wealth, but not quite liquidity.

A growing corner of the financial industry is now pitching structured alternatives.

Two tools, two very different strings attached

Traditional exchange funds are the longer-established route. Investors pool appreciated shares with others doing the same, receive a pro-rata interest in a diversified basket, and defer the capital gains event. The vehicle accepts concentrated single-stock positions — exactly the profile of a long-tenured Nvidia employee — and minimum investments typically start around $100,000. The cost: a mandatory seven-year holding period before withdrawing diversified shares without triggering recognition, and access limited to accredited investors and qualified purchasers.

The newer entrant is the Section 351 ETF conversion, which allows holders to contribute appreciated securities into a newly created ETF as a non-taxable event. The original cost basis carries over — taxes are deferred, not forgiven — but the structure offers immediate liquidity through ordinary exchange trading. Thirty-nine such ETFs launched in the United States between 2021 and 2025, seeding roughly $8.7 billion in investor assets. Tidal Financial Group, Cambria, and Alpha Architect are among the active facilitators.

The crucial limitation: the IRS imposes a strict diversification test. No single security can exceed 25% of contributed assets; the top five combined cannot exceed 50%. The rule was written precisely to prevent a concentrated position from being restructured tax-free without meaningful diversification — meaning a Nvidia insider whose portfolio is 90% a single stock cannot use a 351 conversion directly. Those who already hold a broader basket, and want to restructure it efficiently, have more options.

Donor-advised funds, hedged equity overlays, and direct indexing round out the menu, each with distinct eligibility walls and cost trade-offs ranging from roughly 0.40% to 1.50% annually across vehicle types.

The practical upshot is that most pure Nvidia insiders remain pointed toward the traditional exchange fund — accepting the seven-year lockup as the price of diversification. Whether demand for these instruments accelerates will depend in part on whether proposed changes to federal capital gains rates advance in the current Congress. Any upward revision from the current 20% ceiling would sharpen the incentive considerably, and make the seven-year handcuffs look like a bargain.
