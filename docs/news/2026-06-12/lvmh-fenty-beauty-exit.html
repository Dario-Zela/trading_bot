LVMH in exit talks over Fenty Beauty stake as digital-luxury valuations cool

Rihanna at a Fenty Beauty launch event in 2018; LVMH has been her co-owner in the brand since its founding the previous year. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
LVMH is exploring the sale of its 50% Fenty Beauty stake to Jay-Z's MarcyPen — priced at up to €2.5bn — as it clears room for a potential Armani bid.

LVMH is exploring the sale of its 50 percent stake in Fenty Beauty — the cosmetics brand it co-founded with Rihanna in 2017 — in a move that crystallises a broader repricing of high-profile, founder-led beauty assets across the luxury sector.

Jay-Z's investment platform MarcyPen Capital Partners is reportedly the frontrunner to acquire the stake. The firm was formed in 2024 when Marcy Venture Partners merged with Pendulum Opportunities and now manages roughly $1.1 billion in assets. A deal would leave Rihanna's 50 percent stake intact, keeping ownership of the brand firmly within Black creative entrepreneurship even as the corporate architecture shifts.

JPMorgan has valued LVMH's half of the business at between €1.5 billion and €2.5 billion. Investment bank Evercore has been engaged to run the process. No official statement has come from either LVMH or Rihanna's camp — the silence customary at this stage, and given the headline sensitivity of the asset, probably deliberate.

A portfolio reset with a long list

The Fenty exit does not stand alone. LVMH is in the middle of what analysts have called its most significant portfolio restructuring in nearly four decades. Marc Jacobs was sold for $850 million to a joint venture between WHP and G-III. The group is also reported to be weighing exits from Make Up For Ever and Fresh, the US brand founded in 1991. LVMH stock — down roughly 23 percent year-to-date through mid-June — underscores the pressure on management to defend returns.

The deeper logic may be Armani. Following Giorgio Armani's death in September 2025, LVMH emerged alongside L'Oréal and EssilorLuxottica as a named preferred buyer for a stake in the Italian house, with a full transfer expected to unfold over three to five years at a potential valuation approaching €10 billion. A deal at that scale demands balance-sheet headroom, and trimming the beauty division is one way to find it.

Fenty Beauty itself is not a distressed asset. The brand generated an estimated $450 million in net sales in 2024, having famously crossed $100 million in its first 40 days on shelves back in September 2017. The question LVMH is implicitly answering is not whether Fenty is valuable — it plainly is — but whether a minority stake in a founder-controlled brand fits the portfolio the group wants to own as it reaches for larger, more structurally complex acquisitions.

The timing also speaks to a broader moment in celebrity beauty. Rhode, Hailey Bieber's skincare line, sold to e.l.f. Beauty for $1 billion earlier this year; Rare Beauty has reportedly fielded interest from acquirers. The initial wave of valuation euphoria that greeted direct-to-consumer beauty launches is giving way to harder questions about margin structure, brand longevity beyond the founder's cultural moment, and whether the strategic uplift of celebrity association translates to durable returns.

What to watch: whether MarcyPen moves from reported frontrunner to confirmed buyer, and how LVMH characterises the exit publicly — as portfolio discipline or as a vote of confidence in a brand mature enough to stand without a luxury conglomerate's balance sheet behind it. The framing will say something about what the group believes it got out of nine years as Rihanna's co-founder.
