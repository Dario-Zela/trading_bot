Strike's Mallers calls Bitcoin below $63,000 'the only honest signal'

Bitcoin has declined more than 50% from its October 2025 peak above $126,000, trading around $62,800 in early June 2026. — Dmar198 / Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
In one sentence
Strike's Jack Mallers reframes Bitcoin's 50%-from-peak slide as honest price discovery, but the argument is narrative management rather than a trading signal.

Bitcoin has shed more than half its value since peaking above $126,000 in October 2025, and as of early June it was trading around $62,800—territory that has prompted the usual conference-circuit reassurances. At BTC Prague on Wednesday, Jack Mallers offered one of the more carefully constructed versions.

Mallers, who founded payments app Strike and also serves as co-founder and chief executive of Bitcoin treasury company Twenty One Capital (Nasdaq: XXI), told the Prague audience that Bitcoin trading below $63,000 is not a sentiment problem. It is, in his framing, "the only honest signal"—an unmanipulated readout of actual financial conditions in a system where other markets have been distorted beyond legibility.

"Bitcoin is the closest thing we have to the monetary reflection of truth," Mallers said. "Active 24/7 traded indicator of how the world is doing."

The move is rhetorical and deliberate. Bitcoin's critics read the 50%-from-peak decline as a risk-asset rout—no different from any other high-beta unwind. Mallers's construction inverts that: Bitcoin is behaving honestly because other markets are not.

His evidence for the disconnect: University of Michigan consumer sentiment has sunk below the floors of 2008, 2000, and the recessions of the early 1980s. The S&P 500, meanwhile, trades near all-time highs. The two data points, he argued, cannot both describe the same economy. One market is reporting fiction, and in his telling it is not the one that trades around the clock without a circuit breaker.

The liquidity mechanism

The cause of the sell-off, Mallers argued, is not crypto-specific panic but a global cash scramble. Nations are simultaneously funding wars, artificial-intelligence buildouts, and expanding deficits. Individuals are behind on credit-card bills and rent. The result is forced liquidation across every asset class. "You sell what you can, not what you want," he said.

The market data from early June supports the broad picture, if not the specific interpretation. Bitcoin briefly touched $61,165 on June 4, triggering roughly $3 billion in leveraged-position liquidations in a single session. Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $2.7 billion in outflows over the following week. Strategy—the corporate Bitcoin accumulator formerly known as MicroStrategy—completed its first Bitcoin sale since 2022, an event that attracted attention disproportionate to its size because the institutional-conviction narrative had been doing structural work.

The "honest signal" framing serves a specific constituency: Bitcoin holders who need a story that is neither panic nor denial, and who want the price weakness to confirm the thesis rather than threaten it. Mallers holds direct financial exposure through both Strike and Twenty One Capital, a circumstance that does not invalidate the argument but does clarify the incentive structure around it.

For traders, the speech offers softly bullish sentiment from a connected advocate—no new data on fundamentals, no strategy shift, no institutional catalyst. The more actionable indicators to watch are the Federal Reserve's next rate signal—May CPI came in at 4.2% against a 2% target—and whether Bitcoin ETF flows reverse as geopolitical risk fades following reports that a deal to lift the Strait of Hormuz blockade is expected to be finalized by the weekend.
