Summerville completes £60m West Ham exit to Al Hilal

Crysencio Summerville playing for West Ham United before his £60m move to Al Hilal. — Egghead06 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
West Ham, relegated last season, sold winger Crysencio Summerville to Saudi side Al Hilal for £60m, the club's third-biggest sale ever.

Crysencio Summerville's move to Saudi Arabia was, by the standards of this transfer window, entirely predictable — a Premier League winger of proven output, playing for a club with no European football to offer, priced at a level Gulf money finds easy to meet. Al Hilal have completed a £60m signing of the Netherlands international from West Ham United, the two sides confirming a deal worth an initial £55m plus £5m in add-ons on a four-year contract.

The context matters more than the fee. West Ham were relegated to the Championship at the end of the 2025/26 season, and clubs outside the top flight rarely hold onto players a Saudi Pro League side wants badly enough to pay for. Summerville managed seven goals and five assists in 34 appearances last season as West Ham went down — modest by the standards of a £60m sale, but he arrived at the World Cup in the form of his career, contributing two goals and two assists across all four of the Netherlands' matches in the tournament.

A tidy profit, a familiar destination

West Ham paid Leeds United around £25m for Summerville just a year ago; selling him on for up to £60m turns relegation into a rare bright spot on the balance sheet, and ranks as the club's third-largest sale ever behind Declan Rice's move to Arsenal and Mateus Fernandes' £85m switch to Tottenham earlier this window. Between the two departures, West Ham have banked more than £150m — the kind of parachute-adjacent windfall that softens a relegation season considerably.

Summerville wasn't short of suitors closer to home. Roma had a £39m bid rejected on 20 July, and Manchester United monitored the situation without ever pushing hard, tied up as they were in selling Marcus Rashford first. None of that competition, in the end, mattered — Al Hilal's fee and wages were not something a Championship-bound winger's market was going to top from clubs still balancing their own books.

He joins a squad already stacked with European names: Ruben Neves, Karim Benzema and Theo Hernandez are all on Al Hilal's books under manager Simone Inzaghi, part of a recruitment strategy that has made the Riyadh club the default landing spot for players whose stock is high but whose leverage, for one reason or another, has run out.

The pattern is now familiar enough to barely qualify as news on its own — another capable Premier League player heading to Saudi Arabia for a fee that looks generous set against a relegation campaign. What's worth watching is whether West Ham reinvest the Rice-Fernandes-Summerville money into a squad built to bounce straight back, or whether this becomes another selling-club rebuild with no clear timeline back to the top flight.
