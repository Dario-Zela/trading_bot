PlayStation Network suffers global outage despite Sony's all-clear status page

A PlayStation 5 console and DualSense controller — PSN access to online play, the Store and account services was disrupted worldwide on July 24. — Osh33m / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Sony's PSN status page showed all systems normal for hours while a global outage, traced to an AWS connectivity fault, locked out millions of players.

Sony's PlayStation Network went down worldwide on Friday, locking millions of players out of online games, the PlayStation Store and basic account functions for the better part of a day. For several of those hours, the company's own status page told a different story: every light green, every service listed as operational, while the outage trackers filled up and gamers on three continents stared at login screens that refused to load.

The outage began during Friday morning in the US — reports spiked around 7:40am ET, according to outage-tracking data, with PSN going down alongside Reddit, Hulu, DoorDash and Apple Pay in what turned out to be a shared point of failure. Down Detector logged a sharp jump in complaints from the US and UK, and PlayStation LifeStyle described players from multiple regions unable to sign in, download games, join multiplayer sessions or complete purchases. Some initially guessed the cause was a surge of traffic from the Marvel Tokon open beta, or even speculated — without evidence — about a DDoS attack. The real culprit turned out to be more mundane and more revealing: a connectivity problem in Amazon Web Services' US-WEST-2 region in Oregon, the same infrastructure knot that has now tripped up major consumer platforms multiple times this year.

The status page said otherwise

What set this outage apart wasn't the scale — Sony's network has gone down before, including a lengthy outage in February 2025 — but the gap between what the company was telling the public and what the public was experiencing. Push Square reported that Sony's status page read "all services are up and running" even as the complaints kept climbing, a claim that only caught up to reality once the company updated the page to mark every PSN service as "experiencing problems." Insider Gaming noted "radio silence" from Sony for hours, with no public acknowledgement of a cause even as the EA Sports FC 26 World Championship was postponed because of the disruption. The BBC said it approached PlayStation for comment and did not receive an immediate response.

By 3:41pm ET, Sony's status page showed everything restored, roughly five and a half to six hours after the first reports. No public explanation followed connecting the outage to AWS, even after Amazon had already confirmed its own connectivity issue.

The episode is a small case study in a larger problem: dashboards built to reassure customers and regulators are only as good as the monitoring behind them, and when a platform's status page lags the lived experience of its users, it stops functioning as information and starts functioning as PR. For a company just as dependent on third-party cloud infrastructure as anyone else, the next test will be whether Sony's public reporting catches up before — not after — the next outage, AWS-driven or otherwise.
