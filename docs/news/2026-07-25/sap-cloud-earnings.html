SAP's cloud backlog swells to €22.9bn on strong Q2

SAP's headquarters in Walldorf, Germany. — Vladislav Bezrukov / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
SAP's cloud backlog hit €22.9bn, up 27%, as 22% cloud revenue growth capped a strong week for continental tech earnings.

SAP reported second-quarter cloud revenue growth of 22% on Thursday, with the backlog of contracted-but-unrecognised cloud revenue swelling to €22.9bn — a figure that gives Europe's largest software company an unusually clear view of its own future.

The current cloud backlog — SAP's term for cloud contracts signed but not yet booked as revenue — rose 27% year-on-year, outpacing even the strong cloud revenue growth itself. Cloud ERP Suite revenue, the more mature and closely watched segment of that business, climbed 25% to €5.5bn, or 88% of total cloud revenue. Total group revenue rose 9% to €9.9bn, a slower 11% at constant currencies, while non-IFRS operating profit grew 7%.

Why the backlog matters more than the quarter

A single quarter's revenue print says something about demand right now. A backlog growing faster than revenue says something about demand later. SAP's current cloud backlog represents contracts customers have already signed for cloud subscriptions to be delivered over the coming 12 months — money not yet on the books but effectively locked in. When that figure grows faster than in-quarter cloud revenue, as it did this quarter, it means the pipeline of committed future business is expanding even as SAP converts a record amount of it into billed revenue.

That distinction separates SAP's release from a garden-variety earnings beat. Enterprise customers migrating core financial and operations systems — SAP's S/4HANA line — onto the cloud are typically signing multi-year agreements, not month-to-month subscriptions. A backlog of this size and growth rate is a proxy for how many large enterprises have already decided, contractually, that they are not turning back.

"We delivered another quarter of strong current cloud backlog growth," chief executive Christian Klein said in the results statement. "Customers are choosing SAP to enable accurate and compliant AI outcomes grounded in their most critical business processes and data."

Chief financial officer Dominik Asam struck a similar note, pointing to "sustained current cloud backlog and free cash flow growth against a volatile macroeconomic backdrop" — a pointed reference to a year in which tariff disputes, Gulf tensions and interest-rate uncertainty have weighed on corporate capital spending more broadly.

A profit outlook clouded by dealmaking, not demand

The one soft spot was profitability. SAP trimmed its non-IFRS operating profit outlook for the year, citing more than €100m of expected dilution from July's acquisitions of data-management firm Dremio and AI startup Prior Labs — deals aimed at deepening SAP's AI stack rather than any weakness in the core business. Margin pressure also reflects continued R&D spending and the cost of running AI models embedded in its Business AI Platform, which SAP has positioned as central to its "Autonomous Enterprise" pitch to customers.

Investors read through the deal-related noise: SAP shares rose more than 9% on the results, one of the stronger single-day moves for a DAX heavyweight this year, and helped lift the broader European tech complex. The result lands in the same week ASML posted a strong beat and raised its own 2026 sales forecast, adding to a run of solid continental earnings that has, for now, pushed back against fears that Europe's tech sector would lag its US counterparts through this reporting season.

The next test comes with SAP's full third-quarter update in October, when the market will look for confirmation that the backlog is converting into revenue at the pace implied by this quarter's numbers — and whether the Dremio and Prior Labs integrations start paying for themselves.
