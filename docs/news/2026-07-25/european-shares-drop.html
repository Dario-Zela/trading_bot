European shares post steepest one-day drop in two weeks

The Frankfurt Stock Exchange building; Germany's DAX fell 1.56% as European shares posted their worst session in over two weeks. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
The STOXX 600 dropped 1.3% — its steepest fall in two weeks — as weak tech earnings, $100 oil and a hawkish ECB collided before markets steadied into Friday.

European equities suffered their worst single session in more than two weeks on Thursday, as a run of disappointing earnings collided with a jump in oil prices and a European Central Bank that sounded more hawkish than markets had bargained for.

The pan-European STOXX 600 closed down 1.3% at 638.5 points. Germany's DAX fell 1.56% to 24,763.12, France's CAC 40 dropped 1.64% to 8,299.09, and London's FTSE 100 held up comparatively well, off 0.73% at 10,639.17 — a resilience partly explained by the index's heavier weighting toward energy producers, which were among the day's few gainers.

The selling had three distinct sources, and all three landed on the same day. STMicroelectronics, the French-Italian chipmaker, tumbled 17.7% after guiding third-quarter revenue below expectations, dragging the STOXX 600's technology subindex down 2.9%; Dutch peer BE Semiconductor lost 7.3% on its own results. Nestlé fell nearly 8% — its steepest one-day decline since 1989 — despite lifting its full-year sales outlook, a sign the earnings damage was not confined to chipmakers. Sentiment toward tech was further soured by Alphabet, which raised its 2026 capital-spending guidance to $195–205 billion from $180–190 billion and fell nearly 5% in after-hours US trading despite beating revenue estimates; Tesla dropped more than 5% on similar AI-spending concerns. Investors read both as evidence that AI infrastructure costs are outrunning any near-term payoff, a worry rippling well beyond Silicon Valley.

Oil and rates squeeze from both sides

Crude added a second layer of pressure. Brent touched $100 a barrel for the first time since May after Houthi forces struck tankers off Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea, lifting European energy shares 1.5% and sending TotalEnergies up 2.5% on its strongest quarterly results in nearly three years. The same oil spike fed directly into the ECB's calculus. The central bank held its deposit rate at 2.25%, as expected, but President Christine Lagarde left little doubt that a September increase is now live, telling reporters that "the longer energy prices stay high, the more likely they are to drive up broader inflation through indirect and second-round effects." Some Governing Council members reportedly favoured hiking on the day; the decision to hold was unanimous, but traders are now pricing a quarter-point move in September.

The combination — cracks in tech earnings alongside a central bank leaning toward tighter policy — put two of the market's chief anxieties in the same session: slowing growth on one side, sticky inflation on the other. That squeeze eased slightly as the week closed. By Friday, Brent had slipped back below $100 and London, Paris and Frankfurt indices steadied, though the FTSE lagged its continental peers as the energy trade that had cushioned it began to unwind.

The next test comes from the earnings calendar itself: Microsoft and Apple report in the coming days, both carrying the same AI-capex scrutiny that hit Alphabet, while the ECB's September 11 meeting will show whether Thursday's hawkish signalling turns into an actual rate move.
