Lloyds replaces Everyday Offers with an in-app Rewards hub as fintech competition bites

Lloyds Banking Group signage — the group's Rewards hub now covers Lloyds, Halifax, and Bank of Scotland customers. — Tiia Monto / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Lloyds replaced its Everyday Offers cashback scheme with a gamified Rewards hub on 9 June — a defensive digital move as Monzo and Revolut now claim half of UK adults.

Lloyds Banking Group has quietly retired 'Everyday Offers' — its years-old cashback incentive programme — and replaced it on 9 June with a more theatrical successor: Lloyds Rewards, an in-app hub that bundles discounts, cashback, and prize draws into a single destination inside the mobile banking app.

It is, on the surface, a modest product move. The context around it is rather less quiet.

A hub with prizes attached

The new hub, accessible to customers of Lloyds, Halifax, and Bank of Scotland, leans heavily on gamified mechanics that Monzo, Revolut, and their cohort have spent a decade refining. Fifty customers each month can win £2,026 — a figure that is not as arbitrary as it first appears; it matches the year. Entry requires a minimum £1,000 deposit and app activation. June also carries a rotating special: a trip to Disneyland Paris, with one entry earned per £20 of debit card spending, no cap. For anyone who sets contract renewal reminders for their broadband, energy, or mobile through the hub, there is a separate £5,000 draw on offer. And for customers who open the app at least three times in a month, 50 are picked to receive £50 — a straightforward engagement incentive dressed as a reward.

Cashback at Sainsbury's, B&M, and IHG hotels rounds out the proposition. Apple product discounts make their familiar appearance — a reliable fixture of British retail bank loyalty programmes, and still a reliable conversion driver.

Paul Wilkinson, Rewards Director at Lloyds, offered the expected framing: "We've created Lloyds Rewards to expand the ways customers can make their bank account work harder, and their money go further."

Pressure from the neobanks

The timing speaks to a competitive environment that has not been gentle on incumbent banks. Monzo and Revolut together now count more than half of UK adults as customers — a figure that would have seemed fanciful when either launched in 2015. Monzo runs its own cashback scheme offering between 2% and 10% back at selected retailers. Lloyds' answer, historically, was Everyday Offers: functional, but not memorable.

The new hub is an attempt to be stickier. Prize draws create reasons to open the app; frequent app visits create exposure to cross-sell. That is the playbook, even when it goes unstated.

There is also a consolidation logic worth watching. Lloyds is reported to be considering retiring the Halifax brand — an announcement potentially this summer — which would migrate millions of Halifax current account holders under the Lloyds umbrella. Building a rewards infrastructure that already spans all three brands without differentiation reads, in that light, as preparation for a simpler future. Whether it was designed that way, or simply ended up that way, is a detail the bank has not volunteered.

Two signals will clarify whether Lloyds Rewards is strategy or stagecraft. Lloyds' half-year results later this summer would be the natural moment to surface any adoption data — a meaningful uptick in digital engagement would give the launch genuine weight. And the Halifax brand decision, if confirmed, would make the shared rewards infrastructure look rather more deliberate in hindsight than the press release implied.

For now, Lloyds has a new sign above the loyalty counter. Whether customers bother walking through the door is another question entirely.
