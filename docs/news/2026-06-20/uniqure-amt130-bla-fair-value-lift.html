uniQure's fair value lifted 34% as analysts price in an AMT-130 BLA path — contingent on regulatory clearance

Fluorescence micrograph of a neuron bearing an mHTT protein inclusion — the molecular hallmark of Huntington's disease that AMT-130 is designed to silence. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
uniQure's modelled fair value rose 34% after the FDA agreed to accept three-year AMT-130 data for a BLA — but the confirmatory trial design is still unresolved.

Analysts raised uniQure's modelled fair value by 34% after FDA signalled it would accept three-year Phase I/II data as the basis for an AMT-130 BLA — but the confirmatory trial design remains open, and at least one major bank is still sitting on Neutral.

Something shifted in uniQure's valuation story on 17 June 2026, and it did so sharply. Modelled fair value estimates for the Amsterdam-headquartered gene therapy company moved from roughly US$39.05 to approximately US$52.56 — a 34.5% step-up — as analysts repriced the regulatory pathway for AMT-130, its investigational one-time gene therapy for Huntington's disease. Price targets across several research desks followed upward: Goldman Sachs moved its target to US$46 from US$14, Cantor Fitzgerald went to US$61 from US$18 (upgrading to Overweight), and Barclays also lifted to Overweight. The stock itself surged more than 70% intraday on the news.

The trigger was a Type B meeting with the US Food and Drug Administration in which the agency told uniQure that three-year data from the company's Phase I/II study would be acceptable as the primary basis for an accelerated-approval Biologics License Application. That was a meaningful reversal: as recently as March 2026, the FDA had told uniQure that Phase I/II data compared against an external control were insufficient, and had strongly recommended a prospective, randomised, double-blind, sham-surgery-controlled study before any filing. The reversal reopened a near-term filing window that markets had largely written off.

The clinical data underlying the BLA plan are substantive, if limited in scale. In the high-dose cohort at three years, AMT-130 showed 75% slowing of cUHDRS (composite Unified Huntington's Disease Rating Scale) progression against propensity-matched external controls from the Enroll-HD database, with a p-value of 0.003. The programme carries RMAT designation — the first in Huntington's disease — as well as Breakthrough Therapy and Fast Track status. AMT-130 works by using an AAV5 vector to deliver an engineered microRNA into the caudate and putamen via stereotactic intracerebral infusion, selectively reducing huntingtin mRNA.

The caveat analysts are not hiding

The enthusiasm is conditional. The confirmatory trial design — a post-approval requirement for any accelerated-approval path — is not yet agreed. The FDA has indicated it would prefer a concurrent standard-of-care control arm rather than a sham surgical procedure, which would ease the ethical burden of trial enrolment but introduce its own methodological questions. Goldman Sachs, for all that it tripled its price target, held its rating at Neutral, citing "remaining execution risks around confirmatory study design, durability data, and eventual regulatory review outcomes." The model changes behind the fair value lift reflect assumptions about revenue growth (uprated to 137.4% from 122.8%) and a higher forward earnings multiple — assumptions that snap back hard if the BLA review goes poorly or the confirmatory study design collapses into dispute.

The company expects written FDA meeting minutes within 30 days, which should clarify confirmatory trial parameters. The BLA submission itself is targeted for Q3 2026. That is the next hard date: if the filing lands on schedule and receives Priority Review, a first-approval decision could conceivably arrive before the year is out. Until then, the 34% jump in modelled fair value reflects a thesis, not a result.
