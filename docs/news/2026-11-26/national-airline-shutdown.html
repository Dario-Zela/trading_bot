Another national airline shuts down and cancels all flights

In one sentence
Several small carriers have stopped flying in recent days; Norse Atlantic is the one with UK exposure, after it grounded its 12-jet fleet and disrupted PIA's London and Manchester services.

The seed headline for this piece names no carrier, and the one outlet it cites, TheStreet, runs a recurring "another national airline" series. TheStreet's pages could not be opened here, so the specific airline behind the headline could not be confirmed. What can be said is that several carriers have stopped flying within days of each other. The one with the clearest UK exposure is Norse Atlantic of Norway, which cancelled all flights on Wednesday. It is not a flag carrier, so the "national" label does not fit it.

Norse Atlantic: the case with UK exposure

Norse Atlantic, a Norwegian low-cost long-haul operator, stopped flying on 25 November and halted bookings, according to One Mile at a Time and other travel outlets. Passengers were reportedly checking in when they learned the airline was grounded.

The company had warned of trouble in advance. It said: "The Company's financial situation is critical," and that it "is dependent on securing financing to meet its short-term liquidity needs." It has said it is exploring strategic alternatives, including a possible takeover, ACMI (aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance) contracts and short-term financing, and has given no assurance that any of these will work. It has not formally announced liquidation.

The fleet is 12 Boeing 787s, none of them owned. The aircraft would presumably go back to their lessors. Two of them flew for Pakistan International Airlines under a wet-lease arrangement, and PIA cancelled the London–Islamabad (PK786) and Manchester–Lahore (PK710) services, citing an "unexpected operational suspension" by its partner. These are the UK-facing casualties that have been confirmed so far. No passenger count has been published.

The business had been shrinking. The long-haul low-cost model it was built on had already failed, and it lost an IndiGo lease. One Mile at a Time links that loss to airspace closures and high fuel costs.

The wider run of exits

Norse is one of several. Slovakia's AirExplore, a charter carrier, said it ceased operations on 10 November and applied to surrender its air operator's certificate. Armenian Airlines has told management it intends to surrender its certificate again, with high jet fuel prices given as the reason. TheStreet has also covered Congo Airways, whose future remains unclear after it stopped flying.

The common thread is cost. Jet fuel and airspace restrictions are squeezing carriers with thin balance sheets and leased fleets. These are mostly small or niche operators, and none is a large flag carrier. That keeps the failures in brief territory, but the pattern bears watching.

What to watch

Three things will show how far this goes. The first is whether Norse finds financing or a buyer, or files for insolvency. The second is how PIA re-covers its UK routes. The third is whether Norse's lessors move to repossess the 787s. UK passengers with Norse-operated tickets should look to their booking channel. Package holidays carry ATOL cover, and card payments may offer Section 75 protection.
