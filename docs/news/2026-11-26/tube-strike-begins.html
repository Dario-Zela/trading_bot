London commuters face disruption as tube strike begins

In one sentence
RMT drivers began the first of eight 24-hour Tube strikes over a four-day-week plan, with two more walkouts due in the run-up to Christmas.

A strike by London Underground drivers began on Thursday, closing or cutting back service across the Tube network and pushing commuters onto buses, the Elizabeth line and the Overground. It is the first of eight 24-hour walkouts that the Rail, Maritime and Transport union (RMT) has called between now and February 2027.

What is closed

National Rail's disruption notice says the Circle and Piccadilly lines have no service all day. Other lines run normally until about 10:00 and are then reduced. From noon every line is disrupted, and the Waterloo & City line stops altogether. After 21:00 no Underground line runs. Recovery will be slow. The Circle, Piccadilly and Waterloo & City lines stay shut until noon on Friday, and normal service is not expected on all lines until about 18:00.

The Elizabeth line, DLR, Overground, buses and trams are running as normal, but are expected to be much busier than usual.

The dispute

The row is over a proposal from Transport for London to let drivers compress their hours into a four-day week. The RMT calls the plan damaging. In a statement it said: "London Underground must withdraw these damaging proposals and engage seriously with the union." London Underground's Nick Dent says drivers "can now choose to remain on a five-day week with a guarantee of remaining at the same depot". The rival drivers' union Aslef is reported to have accepted the roster changes. That leaves the RMT the only union opposing them.

The drivers' action is separate from a Piccadilly line dispute involving the TSSA union. That one concerns the dismissal of an employee for gross misconduct, which the union says was unfair.

The cost to the city

No one has yet published a figure for what this strike will cost London's economy. The most concrete evidence comes from last year. Dee Corsi of the New West End Company said West End sales fell by as much as 20% year on year on certain days during the 2025 strikes. She added that about half of West End visitors arrive by Underground, and that "any disruption to the network has an immediate and significant impact on local businesses."

The calendar is what worries hospitality. Allen Simpson of UKHospitality said the timing "will be particularly damaging, with two falling in the crucial trading period just before Christmas." The two dates are 17 and 19 December.

What follows

The RMT has announced further 24-hour stoppages, each starting at noon, on 17 and 19 December, 4 and 6 January, and 17 and 23 February. National Rail's notice lists these six; a reported eighth date in late February is not on that list. The next checkpoint is whether talks produce a withdrawal or a change to the four-day proposal before the pre-Christmas dates. If they do not, the December strikes will show whether this dispute amounts to more than a local nuisance for London's retailers and restaurants.
