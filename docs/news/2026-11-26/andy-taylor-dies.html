Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor dies aged 65

In one sentence
Andy Taylor, the guitarist whose hard-rock edge drove Duran Duran's early-1980s peak, has died aged 65 after a long fight with prostate cancer.

Andy Taylor, the guitarist whose riffs gave Duran Duran's early-1980s hits their muscle, has died at 65 after a long battle with prostate cancer. The band said he died on Monday 23 November. Press reports say he died peacefully at home, surrounded by family.

Taylor's cancer became public in November 2022, when Simon Le Bon read out a letter from him at the band's Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction. Taylor had been diagnosed with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer. He wrote that it was not immediately life-threatening but that "there is no cure."

The sound under the sheen

Duran Duran were sold in their heyday on cheekbones, yachts and synthesisers. Taylor was the corrective: a hard-rock-leaning player who gave the polish some bite. On Rio, the 1982 album that carried 'Hungry Like the Wolf' and the title track, his guitar kept the songs moving. It is much of the reason they still sound like rock records with a dance-floor habit, and not just the products of a fashion moment.

He belonged to the band's most commercially successful lineup. That was Taylor, John Taylor, Nick Rhodes and Simon Le Bon, until he left in 1986.

The band's tribute was brief. "Andy was there at the very beginning of our story," the statement said. Le Bon wrote that there was "a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart. It'll never close up; I won't let it." NME quoted the band as saying his playing "drove the songs that took us around the world."

Power Station and after

Taylor's most visible detour came in 1985, when he co-formed the supergroup Power Station with Robert Palmer, drummer Tony Thompson and bassist Bernard Edwards. Their cover of 'Some Like It Hot' was a US hit. The project suited his taste for heavier, funk-edged rock, and it also marked the point at which he and Duran Duran began to part ways.

His wife, Tracey, spoke for the family. "To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist," she said, and went on to describe him as a devoted husband, father and grandfather. The family has asked that donations be made in his name to Prostate Cancer UK instead of flowers.

What survives

The more lasting question is what happens to the catalogue's story. The classic five-piece era is now permanently closed. Reports say Taylor rejoined the band in 2023 to work on the album Danse Macabre, which came out that November. That chapter, brief as it was, now reads as a farewell.

The Hall of Fame speech in 2022 remains the clearest picture of how he faced his illness: plain, unsentimental and unsparing about the prognosis. Further tributes from fellow musicians are likely in the days ahead. The family has not yet said whether there will be a public memorial.
