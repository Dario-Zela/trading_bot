Jonathan Ross 'horrified' by Walliams allegations and Brand conviction as more allegations reach police

In one sentence
Jonathan Ross says he is 'horrified' by the Walliams allegations and Brand's conviction, as police investigate two further alleged rapes by Brand and Walliams denies wrongdoing.

Jonathan Ross has said he is "horrified" by the allegations against David Walliams and by Russell Brand's conviction. In a statement issued on Tuesday, he said he was once on friendly terms with both men and was "genuinely unaware of their conduct" at the time. The statement lands in an industry still asking who knew what, and when.

What Ross said

Ross, 65, said he was as horrified as everyone else by the revelations about both men. He also addressed an awkward piece of his own history: a joke at the 2004 Comedy Awards that referred to Walliams and a 15-year-old. Ross said the awards' scriptwriting team wrote the line. He understood it to be a gag about Walliams dating younger women. He called it "tasteless" and said he would not make it today.

The statement followed an ITV News and Guardian investigation, reportedly three years in the making. It sets out multiple allegations of sexual misconduct against Walliams spanning more than two decades. The ITV documentary David Walliams: Power & Silence aired on Tuesday night and is available on ITVX and YouTube. One woman describes an encounter at 15 as sexual assault. Several say he groomed them as teenagers. One says he contacted her at 15 after a Little Britain fan event, with a sexual relationship starting when she was 17. Other allegations concern conduct linked to Britain's Got Talent and his dealings with the publisher HarperCollins.

Walliams denies wrongdoing. In a letter to the journalists, he admitted he "behaved thoughtlessly" towards some casual partners but wrote: "I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law."

The Metropolitan Police said it was aware of the media reports. It said it had received no related reports of sexual assault or other offences and would assess any that arrived. It has also contacted the media organisations involved in civil proceedings, so that potential victims know how to report to police.

Brand: conviction and the police

Brand, 51, was convicted on 5 November of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others, in offences between 1999 and 2009. He had denied the charges. The trial involved allegations from six women.

Police are also investigating two further alleged rapes, one involving a girl who was 16 at the time. Met Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley told the BBC that new victims commonly come forward after convictions of well-known figures. "I clearly wouldn't be surprised if many more came forward," he said, adding that a conviction makes people feel that coming forward is possible and that they will be believed. The Reuters report cited here did not give the number of complaints received since the verdict. Reports of the sentence were inconsistent across the sources checked, so none is stated here.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham praised the victims' courage. Asked about a 2014 post of support for Brand, he said he had not known the "full extent" of the behaviour and called the crimes "horrific".

The industry question

Ross's choice of words matters. Presenters who shared a stage, a green room or a Saturday-night slot with these men are now being asked what they saw. "Unaware" is a defensible answer for some and a thin one for others, and it will be tested case by case. Ross has his own history here: he was caught up in the 2008 Sachsgate row, in which he and Brand left lewd messages on actor Andrew Sachs's answerphone.

The Walliams story is at an earlier stage than Brand's. It rests on journalism and a denial, not a court finding, and police say they have received no related reports so far. Two things will show whether anything beyond statements follows: whether the Met's assessment turns into an investigation, and whether the broadcasters and publishers named in the allegations say what they knew and did.
