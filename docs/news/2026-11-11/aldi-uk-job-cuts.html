Aldi cuts hundreds of UK head office and logistics roles

In one sentence
Aldi's UK arm is cutting hundreds of head office and logistics jobs in a global efficiency drive, a sign discounters are trimming overheads to fund price cuts as sales growth slows.

Aldi's UK arm is cutting hundreds of jobs at its head office and in logistics. The discounter has framed the move as part of a wider international review of how it works, and store staff are not affected. For a grocer whose pitch is the lowest shelf price, the cuts show how far it is looking to its own overheads to protect that position.

The figures reported so far are partial. The Guardian, whose report is the main source, says Aldi is consulting its technology team on the loss of up to 150 roles. That follows 103 redundancies in finance and administration earlier this year and almost 20 among buying directors. Aldi also said it had slightly reduced the number of regional managers, affecting a handful of well-paid positions. No total for the logistics cuts has been published, and the company has not disclosed severance terms. The programme's name and scale are also unconfirmed. "Global cost trimming" is the wording used by the reports, not an Aldi label.

Back office moves offshore

Part of the story is where the work is going. Finance, procurement and administration functions are being outsourced to India and other countries under a deal with the services group Genpact, according to the Guardian's account. That echoes February 2025 trade-press coverage, when Aldi proposed putting up to 350 roles at risk at its Atherstone head office in buying, finance and back-office teams. The Grocer reported at the time that the cuts stemmed from a long-planned shift to lower-wage countries. Aldi did not comment on that reporting then.

The pattern suggests a multi-year restructuring in stages, not a one-off reaction to a bad quarter. One affected worker called the latest move "shortsighted". Aldi's own statement said it continually reviews the working processes of its international business to operate more simply and efficiently.

Why the squeeze matters

The backdrop is slowing growth. UK sales at Aldi rose only 0.4% in the 12 weeks to 1 November, according to Worldpanel by Numerator. Aldi has also said it invested £300m in lowering prices on 900 products in the UK in 2026. Price cuts of that size have to be paid for somewhere. When sales growth flattens, the options are thinner margin or lower cost, and head office and distribution are the lines a discounter can trim without touching the shop floor or the price tag.

That reading is an inference from the sales and price-investment figures, not something Aldi has said. The company has not tied the job cuts to the price programme.

The discounters' own cost base matters because the wider sector is under the same pressure. Other pieces in this edition, including Poundland's search for a rescue deal, show how little room low-price retailers have when costs rise.

What to watch

The next markers are the outcome of the technology-team consultation and any further consultations on payroll roles, which the Guardian says could begin in the coming months. A confirmed headcount for logistics would show how deep the cuts go. Aldi's next UK sales update from Worldpanel will show whether the price investment is winning back volume.
