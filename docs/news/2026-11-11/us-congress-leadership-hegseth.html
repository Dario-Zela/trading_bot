Thune stays Senate GOP leader, Jordan beats Scalise for House minority leader; Hegseth impeachment bid blocked for now

In one sentence
House Republicans picked Jim Jordan over Steve Scalise, 106–73, as minority leader, Thune kept the Senate job, and the House shelved Hegseth's impeachment 211–204.

House Republicans have chosen Jim Jordan, the Ohio hardliner who chairs the Judiciary Committee, to lead them in opposition. They passed over Steve Scalise, the sitting majority leader. In a closed-door, secret-ballot vote on Tuesday, Jordan won 106 to 73. Senate Republicans, meeting separately, kept John Thune as their leader. The same day the House voted to shelve an attempt to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth.

A conference picks its temperament

The House vote follows a midterm election in which Republicans lost control of the chamber. Reporting on the vote put the party's losses at more than 40 seats. Mike Johnson is stepping aside as the party's leader. That left a contest between two men who have long sat at the centre of the conference, each with a different claim on it. Scalise has been the party's number two for years and runs the floor. Jordan is better known as a confrontational investigator and a favourite of the party's more combative wing.

The margin was wide, though not overwhelming. The 179 votes cast split 106 to 73. A leader who loses the majority generally has less to manage and more to oppose, and the conference appears to have decided that Jordan suits that task. His committee has been the House Republicans' main vehicle for oversight fights. When Democrats take the gavels in January, the minority's tools will be messaging, procedural resistance and whatever pressure can be brought on the new majority's investigations.

This is also a precedent worth noting. In 2018, after the last midterm loss of this kind, Jordan challenged Kevin McCarthy for the same post and lost 159 to 43. Eight years on, he has won the same job against a more senior rival.

Thune holds the Senate

The Senate result was quieter. Thune was re-elected leader by acclamation, according to his spokesperson. Some senators had asked for the vote to be delayed, but no challenger emerged. That sets his position for the next Congress, although the tallies and circumstances in the Senate conference are less documented than the House count.

The contrast is part of the story. The Senate conference reaffirmed its leader without a contest, while the House conference replaced its own number two for the top job after a rout.

Hegseth impeachment put off

The House also dealt with Republican Thomas Massie's resolution to impeach Hegseth. Massie, a Kentucky Republican, had filed eight articles, centred on the Iran war and alleged violations of war powers law. The House voted 211 to 204 to refer the resolution to committee, which in practice shelves it. Massie was the only Republican to vote with all the Democrats against the motion.

That ends the effort for now, not for good. The measure was never likely to succeed in a Republican-controlled House. Reports note that the delay may only postpone the matter. Democrats take control of the House in January, and they will decide whether to revive the accusations. They will do so facing a Republican minority led by Jordan.

What comes next

The immediate test is January, when the new Congress is sworn in and the leadership change takes effect. Whether Democrats revisit Hegseth's conduct, and how Jordan's conference responds, will show more clearly what the 106 to 73 vote bought. Details of the tallies come from reporting by PBS NewsHour, ABC News, NBC News and The Hill, rather than from conference records.
