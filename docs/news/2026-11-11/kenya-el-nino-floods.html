Kenya braces for El Niño floods

In one sentence
Kenya's weather service forecasts above-normal short rains under a likely very strong El Niño, making this a test of whether early warnings turn into early action.

Kenya's weather service says the coming short rains are likely to be wetter than normal across the whole country, and the El Niño behind that outlook is forecast to be very strong. Flood warnings are now out for East Africa. The harder question is whether anyone acts on them early enough to matter.

The forecasts are firm, but the most recent material found for this article dates from early September, so what follows does not capture the latest official position.

What the forecasts say

The Kenya Meteorological Department (KMD) has put the probability that the 2026 El Niño turns out very strong at 81%. It puts the chance that the event lasts into the first months of 2027 at 97%. Its national outlook for the October–December short rains, issued on 26 August, gives some areas an 85–90% likelihood of above-normal rainfall. Its 10 September update warned of floods, flash floods and landslides. Some counties were expected to see enhanced rain as early as September, with wet conditions possibly running into early 2027.

The Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET) took a more mixed view in July. It said strong short rains should improve pasture in pastoral areas, but it also flagged flood risk. The World Food Programme has made the same point about timing: millions of Kenyans are still recovering from a severe drought, and floods would hit communities that have little left to absorb them.

Not a repeat of 1997–98, necessarily

The 1997–98 El Niño is the benchmark for flood damage in Kenya, and comparisons to it were always going to be made. KMD has cautioned against drawing them automatically. El Niño does not guarantee heavy rain everywhere. The Indian Ocean Dipole, the temperature gradient across that ocean, can strengthen or dampen its effects in East Africa. This year a positive dipole is reported to be adding to the El Niño signal, which is why the seasonal outlook leans so wet.

A seasonal forecast gives odds for a three-month period. It does not say which river will burst, or when. That gap is where preparedness is tested.

From forecast to action

The government has activated a national framework. It is coordinated by the National Disaster Risk Management Authority and overseen by an Ad Hoc Cabinet Committee on El Niño Preparedness and Response, and the deputy president has publicly said the country is ready.

The things that decide outcomes are mostly local and unglamorous: whether drains and culverts are cleared before the rains, whether families on floodplains and steep slopes are moved in time, whether stocks of food and medicines are in place before roads are cut, and whether funding is released on a forecast rather than after the damage. Aid agencies and insurers are watching the last point most closely. Money that arrives after the floods pays for relief. Money that arrives before them can reduce the losses.

No death toll, damage estimate or confirmed rainfall totals for this season were found, and none are reported here.

What to watch

The next markers are KMD's monthly and weekly updates as the short rains build, and the speed of any evacuation orders and emergency releases in flood-prone counties. If the wet season runs into early 2027 as forecast, the same systems will be tested again.
