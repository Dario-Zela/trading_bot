The Monaco summit that will decide the future of Carrick and Man Utd

In one sentence
United's owners and executives meet in Monaco amid reports that Carrick will be recommended for dismissal, with Pochettino, Howe and Emery named as options; nothing is confirmed.

Manchester United's owners and senior executives are due to gather in Monaco on Wednesday to decide whether Michael Carrick keeps the job he was given permanently only in May. If the reporting is right, the real question is who replaces him, and whether a club that has not held a managerial succession together since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013 can get this one right.

The meeting was arranged in advance to use the international break. Co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe will attend, along with chief executive Omar Berrada and technical director Jason Wilcox, according to reports relaying BBC Sport. Berrada and Wilcox are expected to recommend that Carrick be dismissed. Ratcliffe has the final say but is expected to consult the Glazer family. No outcome had been confirmed at the time of writing. If Carrick goes, he would reportedly be told in person in Manchester on Thursday.

A short tenure, a poor start

Carrick, 45, signed a two-year contract in May 2026 after taking over from Ruben Amorim, who was sacked in January. By the account in the reports, the contract has not bought him patience. United have won one league match this season and sit in the relegation zone, which is described as the club's worst start after ten games in four decades. Those figures come from a single chain of reporting and should be read as such.

A dismissal would cost money. Carrick could receive up to £5m in compensation, a sum that is small by Premier League standards but hard to justify for a man appointed on a permanent basis six months earlier. It is also the second time in a few years that United have turned to a former player to steady things, and the second time the steadying has not lasted.

The shortlist

Three names appear in the reporting: Mauricio Pochettino, Eddie Howe and Unai Emery.

  • Pochettino signed a new contract as head coach of the United States men's national team only months ago, but has said openly that he wants to return to Premier League management. Some outlets say the Glazers regard him as their top pick. That is a claim about a preference, not a decision.
  • Howe is available without a fee, having left Newcastle United in the summer. Earlier reporting described him as fully open to the job if it came up.
  • Emery won the Europa League with Aston Villa last season and is the third name in the BBC-sourced options. The reporting says little about how seriously he is being considered.

The list is a signal as much as a plan. All three are experienced, Premier League-literate coaches with established methods. That is a different profile from the two most recent appointments, a young and unproven coach followed by a stopgap. It suggests the club wants to buy certainty after a stretch of buying potential.

The pattern that matters

Since Ferguson retired, United have gone through David Moyes, Louis van Gaal, José Mourinho, Ole Gunnar Solskjær, Ralf Rangnick on an interim basis, Erik ten Hag, Amorim and Carrick. The change of ownership structure under Ratcliffe, with Berrada and Wilcox brought in to run football operations, was meant to end the improvisation. The Monaco meeting is the first real test of that structure under pressure.

Two features of the process are worth noting. The recommendation is coming from the executives, not the owner, which is how a modern football club is supposed to work. But the final call still rests with Ratcliffe, and the Glazers are still consulted, so the old ownership layer has not disappeared. If the Glazers' reported preference for Pochettino carries the day over the executives' own judgment, that would say something about where power actually sits.

Timing adds pressure. United want a new manager in place ideally before the Liverpool match after the international break, which leaves little room for a drawn-out courtship, and particularly little if the preferred candidate is under contract with a national federation.

What to watch

The first signal is whether Carrick is told on Thursday, as reported. The second is who United approach first, and whether compensation or contract issues, notably Pochettino's deal with US Soccer, slow the process. Howe's availability makes him the simplest option; Pochettino's is the more complicated one. Whoever is in the dugout against Liverpool will show how much of the Monaco meeting was a decision and how much was a hope.
