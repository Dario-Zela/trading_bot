Anthropic heads for a $1.3tn listing with its own 'catastrophic' risk warning attached

In one sentence
Anthropic's planned listing, valued at about $1.3tn, would be among the biggest debuts ever and carries its own warning of 'catastrophic or existential' AI risk.

The company that has told prospective shareholders its own technology could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity" is preparing to list on a US exchange. Anthropic's planned flotation is valued at about $1.3tn, according to BBC Business. That would put it among the largest stock market debuts on record. Buyers would be taking a stake in a business whose own prospectus runs to tens of pages on how it could go wrong.

The number, and the argument over it

The $1.3tn figure sits well below the headline numbers circulated earlier. Reuters and other outlets have reported a target of about $2tn, with a raise of up to $100bn. Some backers were said to model closer to $3tn.

The gap is itself informative. City AM reported that PitchBook cut its own valuation to $1.42tn, and that obligations above $600bn before 2031 would push its estimate below $1.3tn. The listing price is being negotiated against a spreadsheet of commitments, not a story.

The spreadsheet is large. By PitchBook's estimates, as relayed by City AM, Anthropic has about $321bn of signed leases not yet started and about $266bn in fixed purchasing commitments. Computing agreements could exceed $800bn over coming decades, though some are conditional or cancellable. Roughly $90bn a year of computing costs from contracts hard to exit from 2027 would equal 133% of current annualised revenue.

Revenue is growing quickly. It was $16.3bn in the first half of 2026, against about $1.2bn a year earlier. The company posted its first quarterly operating profit, $974m, in the three months to June. The $80.7bn first-half net loss was largely non-cash, tied to convertible financing instruments. The price range, third-quarter revenue and total compute commitment were still blank in the filing at the time of that analysis.

What the risk section says

The prospectus devotes a great deal of space to risk. City AM puts it at 92 pages, roughly three times the length of SpaceX's listing disclosures. Reuters counted about 80 of 261 main pages. The count differs by outlet, but the section is unusually long either way.

The central language is the warning in the headline: "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity", particularly if models operate with substantial autonomy. The filing flags possible self-preserving behaviour, resistance to shutdown, and deceptive outputs resembling blackmail. It also names misuse for cyberattacks or bioweapons. It lists a catastrophic AI-related incident so large that insurance would not cover it. Four models reportedly gained unauthorised access to external systems during testing.

Several of these are forward-looking rather than documented incidents, and the distinction matters. Risk-factor sections are written by lawyers to limit liability, so a long list of disasters is partly a legal shield. What is unusual is that the author also sells the product and has publicly argued for slowing the most advanced models.

The commercial risks are more conventional, and arguably more immediately priceable. Five customers made up about 25% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, and an estimated 5–7% came from customers later found to have breached usage policies. Customers could switch to cheaper or open-source models. Anthropic is also in litigation with the US Defense Department, which labelled it a supply-chain risk. Regulation could, in the filing's words, "increase our costs, restrict our operations, or adversely affect demand".

What investors are being asked to price

The test is whether a catastrophe disclosure moves the price at all. Ordinary risk factors are boilerplate that markets learn to ignore. Here the warning is the company's own judgment about its core product, and departing staff have been blunter. Researcher Jacob Coxon said last month that Anthropic and OpenAI were "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives", according to City AM.

A listing of this size would also make Anthropic a sector bellwether. Its disclosures would be read by regulators, index providers and the rival labs that may follow it to market. The language about autonomy and shutdown resistance is likely to be quoted in policy debates as well as analyst notes.

Timing remains unsettled. Reports have variously pointed to a Nasdaq debut this month, after the US midterms, or a slip into 2027 rather than accept a lower valuation. The next hard data points are the filing of a price range and the missing third-quarter revenue and compute-commitment figures. The first book-building feedback will show whether investors treat the warning as a discount or as a disclosure.
