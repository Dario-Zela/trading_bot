Acquitted 'China spy' says he was a fall guy; El Salvador claims Haitian gang HQ destroyed

In one sentence
An acquitted UK defendant calls himself a security-services fall guy and Bukele says Haiti's gang HQ is destroyed, but both claims are unverified.

Two unrelated claims, both made by interested parties and neither independently confirmed, led the BBC's world and politics feeds on Wednesday. In Britain, a man acquitted in a case billed as a "China spy" prosecution says he was a fall guy for the security services. In the Caribbean, El Salvador's president says his forces have destroyed a Haitian gang's headquarters.

In both cases the public record is thinner than the headlines suggest. The Tribune has been unable to retrieve the court, charges, dates or the defendant's name for the UK case, and has found no independent account of the Haiti strike.

An acquittal and an accusation

The defendant was acquitted in a UK prosecution described as a China-spying case. He now alleges he was made a "fall guy" for the security services. That is his account, not a finding by any court or inquiry. Nothing seen so far shows what the security services say in reply, and the claim cannot be tested without the trial record.

It lands in territory that is already sensitive. In September 2025 the Crown Prosecution Service dropped Official Secrets Act charges against Christopher Cash and Christopher Berry weeks before their trial. The Director of Public Prosecutions said prosecutors could not obtain government evidence describing China as a national security threat. The Home Secretary denied any ministerial interference, and MI5's director general, Ken McCallum, voiced frustration at the outcome. Separately, a jury at the Old Bailey has convicted two men, a former police superintendent and an immigration officer, of assisting Chinese intelligence by spying on Hong Kong dissidents.

Those cases are not this one, and nothing retrieved links them to the acquitted man's claim. They show why such allegations draw attention. China-related prosecutions in Britain are rare, politically charged and dependent on evidence that agencies and ministers control. If the man's claim were substantiated, it would suggest the security services shaped a prosecution, and that would affect trust in such cases. Until the verdict, the court record and any official response are checked, it remains an assertion by a defendant who has reason to make it.

El Salvador's claim in Haiti

President Nayib Bukele says Salvadoran forces destroyed a Haitian gang headquarters. The BBC is the only listed source, and the claim comes from Bukele himself, not from independent or on-the-ground reporting.

The context is a deployment of more than 2,000 Salvadoran troops and military hardware, ordered after four Salvadoran aviation technicians disappeared following a gang attack on 18 October. They have not been found. Bukele has said the aim is to recover them, not to occupy Haiti. The UN says only about 200 of the Salvadoran soldiers fall under the jurisdiction of the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force. Haiti's prime minister, Alix Didier Fils-Aimé, says foreign forces operate under Haitian authority.

Earlier this month Bukele said Salvadoran helicopters had fired on gangs attacking Chadian soldiers of the force, and posted: "The gangs no longer have the upper hand." Haitian police, working with the force and Salvadoran troops, found the burned remains of a truck they said the missing men's group had used, in Croix-des-Bouquets.

Bukele has a record of publicising operations on social media, and his state of emergency at home has drawn criticism from rights groups. The UN has urged that all operations respect human rights law. Médecins Sans Frontières reported 30 people injured, including women and children, in the eight days after 18 October, and warned that more international forces could worsen the situation. Haiti has had no elected government since 2021, and elections have been pushed back to July 2027.

What to watch

For the UK case, the questions are the court record, the charges, and whether the security services or the Crown Prosecution Service respond to the allegation. For Haiti, they are Haitian police or UN confirmation of what was struck and who was inside, and whether the four missing technicians are found.
