OpenAI fires workers for mishandling 'sensitive information'

In one sentence
OpenAI fired three researchers over what it calls mishandling of sensitive information tied to external model evaluations, and has not said what was involved.

OpenAI has fired three researchers after an internal investigation concluded they had mishandled sensitive information. The company has said little beyond that, and the thinness of the public record is part of the story.

An OpenAI spokesperson told the BBC: "Our investigation confirmed that these individuals mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures, violating our policies and breaking the trust essential to our work." The dismissals were reported as taking effect on 1 October.

What is known

The people named in reports are Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni. Each had regularly posted about AI safety on X in the weeks before the firings. According to those reports, the alleged mishandling involved work with an external organisation that evaluates AI models. The reports do not say what information was involved, how it left the company's control, or whether any of it became public.

The reports also say the three were not dismissed for raising safety concerns. That is the company's account of the decision. It has not been tested independently, and the former employees' side was not available in the reports reviewed for this article. Their public posting history is context, not evidence of motive.

Why the details matter

There are two plausible readings, and the public facts do not separate them.

The first is routine. A lab that shares unreleased models with outside evaluators has to control what travels in each direction. Firing people who step outside those procedures is how a company shows evaluators, customers and regulators that the procedures mean something.

The second is more awkward. Labs are under pressure to ship quickly while also opening their models to outside scrutiny. Evaluation work sits where those pressures meet. If staff who work with outside evaluators are being dismissed over how they handled information, other researchers may become more cautious about what they share, and with whom. Nothing published so far shows that is happening. It is a risk worth watching, not a finding.

OpenAI's own framing points to the first reading. The company describes a breach of procedure, not a dispute over safety.

Why it matters beyond one lab

OpenAI is a vendor to a large share of corporate software, and its governance affects customers and investors who rely on it. An information-handling lapse at a leading lab is not a market event on its own. What would matter is how the company handles disclosure from here. Questions of that kind sit alongside the concern raised by central bank officials about AI as a market risk.

What to watch

Three things would clarify the picture: whether OpenAI says what category of information was involved, whether the evaluation organisation comments, and whether the three researchers respond publicly. Absent any of those, the dismissals remain an HR outcome with an unexplained cause.
