Coroner warns of future deaths at mental health unit where patient was killed

In one sentence
A coroner has warned of future deaths at an east London NHS mental health unit after finding staff falsified observation records before a patient was killed.

A coroner has warned that more patients could die at an east London mental health unit. The warning follows the strangling of a patient by another patient, on a ward where staff were found to have slept, used their phones and falsified records of patient checks.

Hugo Flint-Cahan, 34, was killed in January 2023 at the Newham Mental Health Centre. Rolando Torres-Pena, 22, pleaded guilty to manslaughter by diminished responsibility and received a hospital order with no time limit. A six-day inquest in September 2026 then examined how the ward was being run that night. Graeme Irvine, senior coroner for east London, concluded that neglect "more than trivially contributed" to the death.

What the coroner found

Mr Irvine has issued a prevention-of-future-deaths report. Coroners use these to require a named body to respond when they believe further deaths are likely. This one sets out 14 concerns, according to reporting by the BBC and others.

The central finding is that staff did not carry out "timely and thorough observations" of patients on the night. Observation levels on inpatient wards are set according to risk, and the checks are meant to be a basic safeguard. Staff then falsified the records, the coroner said, "in the safe knowledge that staff on duty would not report or escalate the deception."

The report also records that staff colluded to take unauthorised breaks of about two hours. Staff were found to have been asleep and on their phones for long periods. The reporting I reviewed gives these as the coroner's findings. I have not seen the full report.

A familiar pattern

The coroner said the findings are "strikingly similar" to those of a 2021 inquest involving the same trust. Remedial measures reported to that earlier court, he said, "do not appear to have been implemented effectively."

That is the basis for the future-deaths warning. A single lapse can be treated as an aberration. A repeat of the same failure after assurances had been given suggests the fix did not hold.

The trust's response

East London NHS Foundation Trust (ELFT) called the failings "wholly unacceptable" and said it has undertaken a "significant programme of work" to improve inpatient services. Dr David Bridle, its chief medical officer, said the trust would ensure "the learning from the coroner's findings informs our continuing work."

James Cahan, Mr Flint-Cahan's cousin and the family's solicitor, said the public deserves "a clear explanation of how this was allowed to happen." He described findings of dishonesty on this scale as extraordinary.

What happens next

ELFT and NHS England must respond to the coroner in writing by 19 November 2026. The responses are normally published. The test will be whether they describe how observation practice is checked independently, for example by audits that do not rely on staff-completed records, given that the records themselves were falsified. The 2021 inquest shows that assurances alone have not been enough.
