Tokenised finance edges into the mainstream: BlackRock in Hong Kong, bitcoin as collateral, a first digital gold trade

In one sentence
BlackRock has won Hong Kong approval for a tokenised HKD money market fund; the bitcoin-collateral and JPMorgan-HSBC gold stories remain thinly sourced.

BlackRock has won Hong Kong approval for a tokenised money market fund denominated in Hong Kong dollars, taking an on-chain cash product into a regulated Asian market. Two other items were bundled with it in the day's tokenisation news: a US pilot that lets crypto serve as derivatives margin, and a reported digital gold trade between JPMorgan and HSBC. Of the three, the BlackRock fund has the best sourcing. The other two are thinner than their headlines suggest.

The fund

The product is the BlackRock HKD Digital Liquidity Fund. Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission authorised it, according to Ledger Insights. Hubbis describes it as BlackRock's first tokenised fund in Hong Kong and in Asia-Pacific. It also describes it as the first Hong Kong-domiciled money market fund with a constant net asset value offered to both institutional and retail investors locally.

The fund holds short-dated Hong Kong dollar paper such as government bills and deposits, which is conventional cash-management fare. The difference is the plumbing. According to Dealroom, investors can subscribe and redeem in ordinary fiat or in digital cash, including tokenised deposits and stablecoins. Standard Chartered Hong Kong acts as custodian, administrator and trustee. One named settlement option is HKDAP, a Hong Kong dollar stablecoin issued by Anchorpoint Financial, a venture Standard Chartered leads. Serrari Group ties the fund to the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's Project Ensemble, the city's tokenisation testbed.

Two caveats. The triage headline put the fund at roughly $100m, and none of the sources checked confirmed a size. That figure is not used here. The coverage found also describes an authorisation, not a confirmed trading start date, and no primary BlackRock or SFC statement was located. The description of the fund as an expansion of BlackRock's existing tokenised range is reasonable, but the reporting reviewed does not itself spell out the earlier products.

Why a second market matters

A tokenised money market fund is a familiar instrument in a new wrapper. Putting units on a ledger and allowing stablecoin or tokenised-deposit settlement can make cash management faster and easier to automate, for example by moving collateral outside banking hours. What the Hong Kong approval adds is regulatory standing. A pilot in a sandbox is one thing. An authorised fund open to retail as well as institutional money, with a bank as trustee, is a product another regulator has examined and allowed.

That is the stronger reading of the news. It is an inference from the structure of the approval, not a statement from BlackRock. The open question is scale. Authorisation says nothing about how much money arrives, and the evidence that these funds have outgrown pilot size will be asset figures, which are not yet available for this fund.

Bitcoin as collateral: a narrower story

The second thread is a report that bitcoin now counts as collateral "from the CFTC to the ECB". The US half is plausible. From memory, and not re-checked for this piece, CFTC staff allowed futures commission merchants to accept bitcoin, ether and USDC as margin in a pilot in December 2025. The date, asset list and terms should be confirmed against CFTC documents before being relied on.

The ECB half is unsupported. No primary ECB source was found showing that the central bank accepts, or is formally moving to accept, bitcoin as collateral. The only listed source is a Yahoo Finance video with no transcript or named official. A narrow US pilot should not be read as a global shift.

The gold trade

The third thread is a CryptoProwl report that JPMorgan and HSBC completed a first digital gold transaction. A search found no corroboration of a JPMorgan trade. What it did find was HSBC's earlier launch of tokenised physical gold for institutional clients. HSBC created a digital twin of loco London gold held in its vault, with each token representing 0.001 troy ounce, according to Finextra. The reported joint trade has no size, platform or date attached. It stays unverified until one of the banks confirms it.

What to watch

The tests are concrete. For BlackRock, they are a launch date, the fund's assets after its first quarters, and whether HKDAP or tokenised deposits actually carry the settlement. For the collateral story, they are the CFTC's pilot documents and any ECB statement. For gold, they are a bank press release. The Hong Kong fund is the only one of the three that already has a regulator's name on it.
