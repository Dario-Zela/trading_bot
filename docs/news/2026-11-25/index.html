SCIENCE · PLANETARY DEFENCE · By R. Okonkwo

ESA's Hera arrives to survey the crater left by DART's asteroid strike

The European Space Agency's Hera probe has arrived at the Didymos asteroid system to survey the aftermath of NASA's DART mission, which deliberately crashed into it. DART struck the moonlet Dimorphos on 26 September 2022 at roughly 6.1 km/s, and NASA measured that the impact shortened Dimorphos's orbit around its parent asteroid by about 32 minutes. DART was destroyed in the collision, so Hera is the first spacecraft to examine the crater and the changed orbit. Launched on 7 October 2024, Hera carries two CubeSats, Milani and Juventas, to characterise the system in detail. Its measurements of the crater's size and the momentum the impact transferred will show whether crashing a spacecraft into an asteroid is a dependable way to deflect one, a question central to planetary defence. The results to watch are the survey's first crater measurements and any revised estimates of how much the impact changed the system.
