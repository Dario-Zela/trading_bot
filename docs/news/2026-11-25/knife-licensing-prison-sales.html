Knife-seller licensing planned as Tories pitch sale of London's Victorian jails

In one sentence
The government will license all knife sellers, which it calls a world first, while the Conservatives propose selling London's Victorian prisons to raise £1.4bn for new places.

The government says it will license anyone who sells knives in the UK. Ministers describe it as the first scheme of its kind in the world. The same day, the opposition Conservatives set out a plan to sell London's Victorian prisons to pay for new jail places. The two announcements are unrelated in substance, but both are about how Britain deals with crime. Both are also promises about the future, not changes already in force.

A licence to sell

According to reporting on the Home Office announcement, the scheme would cover businesses and private individuals alike. Shops and online vendors would need a licence to sell any knife, from butter knives to machetes, or face fines. Overseas sellers would have to route sales through a UK-based intermediary responsible for meeting the rules. Officials say the scheme is expected to cover all knives, though they are still considering some limited exemptions.

The scheme will need primary legislation. It follows a consultation that ran from 16 December 2025 to 24 February 2026, launched in the wake of the Southport killings. That consultation proposed that the police administer the system, drawing on their experience of firearms licensing. It also proposed import licensing, on the argument that rules on domestic sellers alone would push buyers towards overseas suppliers. The ideas trace back to an independent review of online knife sales, which recommended seller registration.

The "world first" label comes from the government and has not been independently verified. It also needs qualifying. Scotland already runs a knife dealers licensing scheme for non-domestic sales, so the novelty lies in the breadth: every seller, private individuals included, and every type of knife. Several details are still open. These include the start date, the fee levels, the penalties and the final exemptions. The Home Office has yet to assess the full cost and administration of the scheme. Police officers had warned during the consultation that a new licensing duty would fall on already stretched forces.

Selling the Victorian estate

The Conservatives, meanwhile, are pitching a different kind of sale. Nick Timothy, the shadow justice secretary, announced a plan to sell off London's Victorian prisons, naming Wormwood Scrubs, Brixton, Wandsworth and Pentonville. He said the sales could raise £1.4bn for "modern, purpose-built prisons elsewhere". Reports say the party would also pledge 50,000 new prison places and an additional £2bn a year for the Ministry of Justice.

The idea is not new. George Osborne and Michael Gove proposed closing inner-city Victorian jails and selling the sites for housing in 2015, and the plan was abandoned in 2019 when a shortage of places became acute. Critics of the earlier scheme questioned who owned the land, and whether sales would yield enough to cover replacements. A Labour spokesperson said the Conservatives were responsible for the state of the prison estate, and pointed to what the government calls the largest prison building programme since the Victorian era.

The arithmetic is the weak point. Selling an old prison only works if the sites can be emptied without losing capacity, and capacity is the scarce thing. The £1.4bn is a headline sum, and the plan would also have to meet the cost of building and staffing 50,000 places.

What to watch

For knife sellers, the next marker is the legislation that will give the scheme its detail. Fee levels, exemptions and the role of the police will show whether the "world first" is a workable regime or a broad promise. For the Conservatives, the test is whether the £1.4bn figure survives costing, and whether voters see a plan that was shelved in 2019 as more credible this time.
