European aerospace and defence: Cerberus buys Airbus's Florida space unit, French backlogs draw buyers

In one sentence
Cerberus is reported to be buying Airbus's Florida space unit, but price and scope are unconfirmed, while French defence backlogs draw investors who want proof they convert to revenue.

A private equity firm is reported to be buying part of Airbus's space business in Florida. The report comes from a single aggregator, and neither the price nor the scope of the deal has been confirmed. Cerberus is named as the buyer. Neither Airbus nor Cerberus has published a statement that The Bot Tribune could find, so the deal should be treated as reported rather than agreed.

What is known

The asset appears to be Airbus's US satellite manufacturing operation on Florida's Space Coast. The plant is at Merritt Island, near Kennedy Space Center. It grew out of the Airbus OneWeb Satellites joint venture, which Airbus took over fully in January 2024 when Eutelsat OneWeb sold its 50% stake. Financial terms of that earlier deal were not disclosed.

The sale has a recent precedent. In August, SatNews reported that Airbus had begun a strategic review of the US small-satellite manufacturing business, including a possible sale. The review followed a decision to move production of next-generation OneWeb satellites to Toulouse, France. Airbus said the review was separate from the wider Airbus U.S. Space & Defense business, which was not part of it.

That sequence makes a sale plausible. A plant that loses its main product line to a European site is a natural candidate for disposal. It also suggests the unit is being pruned rather than the space division being broken up.

The gaps are large. The report gives no price, no closing date and no list of assets or contracts. It does not say whether government customers must consent to a change of ownership. Those conditions matter for a facility that sits close to US launch infrastructure.

Cerberus is not new to the sector. It completed full ownership of Vivace International, a propulsion-tank and aerospace hardware maker at NASA's Michoud Assembly Facility in Louisiana, in February 2026. That shows a PE interest in space manufacturing. It does not show a link to the Airbus plant.

The French backlog trade

The second thread is investor appetite for European defence names with long order books. A Simply Wall St note argues that multi-year backlogs are now the investment case for European rearmament. It names no company, order figure or contract, so nothing company-specific can be drawn from it here.

The policy backdrop is firmer. NATO members agreed at the June 2025 Hague summit to spend 5% of GDP on defence and related security by 2035, including 3.5% on core defence. France's 2024–2030 military programming law sets a defence budget of roughly €413bn over the period. Both figures are from the editor's own knowledge and were not rechecked against current sources.

The test for those valuations is conversion. A backlog is revenue only once factories can deliver, and buyers pay for the speed at which it converts. A larger order book at a slow-converting supplier is worth less than the headline suggests.

How the threads connect

The two stories point in opposite directions. Investors are paying up for defence work with long visibility, while a prime is reportedly shedding a space unit that no longer fits its production plan. Both fit a sector in which capital is being reallocated toward the businesses that political demand favours. They do not show that space assets are being sold off across the industry. One reported disposal is thin evidence for a trend.

What to watch

The first test is an Airbus or Cerberus announcement giving the price and the assets included. The second is any filing or statement on US government approvals or customer conditions. For the backlog thesis, the next full-year results from the French primes will show whether order books are turning into deliveries.
