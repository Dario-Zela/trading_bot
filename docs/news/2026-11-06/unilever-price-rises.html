Unilever raises prices to offset higher commodity costs

In one sentence
Unilever's Q3 shows 2.3% price growth and 2.9% volume growth as it passes on higher commodity costs, a limited but encouraging read for staples margins.

Unilever is passing higher commodity costs on to shoppers. The question for investors is how much, and whether volumes survive it. The FTSE 100 maker of Dove, Hellmann's and Marmite has been signalling price increases for months, and its latest quarterly figures put numbers on the pattern. The Supply Chain Dive item that prompted this piece gave no figures, so the detail below comes from the company's own statements as reported.

What the company has said

In May, Unilever warned that it expected full-year cost inflation of about €750m to €900m (roughly £658m to £789m). It tied the pressure to the Iran war, which has raised logistics and factory costs. Executives said that if inflation persisted, price rises could reach the top of a 2% to 3% range. Home care lines linked to crude oil were expected to be hit hardest. The increases were to arrive in select markets and categories rather than across the board.

The third-quarter trading statement, published on 28 October, shows how that played out. Underlying sales growth was 5.2%, made up of 2.9% from volume and 2.3% from price. Turnover was €13.3bn, up 6.7%. The company described the step-up in pricing as planned and attributed it to higher commodity costs, mainly in Home Care and Personal Care. Segment figures reported from the release put Personal Care price growth at 2.4%, Beauty & Wellbeing at 2.1% and Foods at 1.3%. A Home Care figure was not available in the material reviewed.

The pricing is also modest next to the last inflation cycle. In 2022 Unilever raised prices 11% in a single quarter and said it had yet to pass on the full effect of input costs. In 2021 the figure was 4.1% for the quarter to September. The 2.3% reported now sits at the low end of the range management floated in May.

Volumes are the test

A price rise only supports margins if shoppers keep buying. On that measure the quarter was reassuring. Volume grew 2.9%, led by emerging markets, and Unilever raised its full-year volume outlook to at least 3%. It also expects underlying sales growth of around 5% in the second half. Those numbers do not show a consumer pushing back, at least not yet.

The 2022 episode is the caution. Analysts then noted that supermarket own-label products took share from Unilever's brands in Europe and the US as prices rose. A quarter with 2.3% pricing says little about how shoppers react if increases need to go further. Foods, at 1.3%, is the category with the least pricing so far.

What it says about staples margins

The read-across is real but limited. Unilever has shown that a large staples group can pass through a mid-single-digit cost shock with price rises of roughly 2% to 3% and without losing volume. This is one company, in one quarter, with a mix tilted towards emerging markets. It does not show the same holds for food and household makers selling mainly into European and US supermarkets. Margin figures, which would settle the question, were not in the material reviewed.

The rise in UK interest rates adds a second pressure on household budgets. Shoppers in Britain are now absorbing both higher borrowing costs and shelf prices that are being pushed up.

The next data point is Unilever's full-year results, due on 11 February 2027. They should show whether the €750m to €900m cost estimate held, how much of it was recovered through pricing, and whether volume growth stayed above 3%.
