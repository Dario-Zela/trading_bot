Watchdog says Andrew received up to £2,000 a month from sub-letting

In one sentence
A figure of up to £2,000 a month is now attached to Andrew's sub-letting income, though the NAO's own report gave no amount and the Public Accounts Committee inquiry may settle it.

A figure has now been put on what Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor made from sub-letting: up to £2,000 a month. It is a ceiling, not a confirmed average. The sum is small beside the questions it raises about how a former royal came to hold property on the Windsor estate.

The public-spending watchdog, the National Audit Office, set out the background in a report published in early June. The NAO's own account does not state an amount. It found that Andrew sub-let three cottages on the Royal Lodge estate and kept the income. He did so while holding the main house on a lease from the Crown Estate at a peppercorn rent. The £2,000-a-month figure is the one attached to the latest coverage. It could not be traced to a published line in the NAO report, and the watchdog's own wording on the amount remains "undisclosed".

The arrangement

The terms go back to 2003. Andrew committed to spend about £7.5m on refurbishing Royal Lodge. In return he paid a premium and a nominal annual rent, and the property was effectively his for the length of a long lease. The NAO found that three cottages within the grounds were let on to tenants by Andrew directly. The sub-letting ended in April 2026, when he left Royal Lodge for the King's Sandringham estate.

Palace sources have suggested the tenants were staff or retired staff and that the rent only covered running costs. That account is unverified. If it holds, an upper limit of £2,000 a month would be a modest return. Property agents' estimates of market rents for comparable cottages on the Windsor estate have run higher, at £2,500 to £5,000 a month each. That gap between a possible market rent and the sums reportedly charged is part of what any inquiry will want explained.

What the watchdog said

The NAO has been careful in its language. Its director, Lee Summerfield, said the office's role was "to set out the facts". Buckingham Palace said the report was "in line with the Royal Household's commitment to transparency". The Crown Estate has told MPs that allowing the sub-lets represented "best value" at the time. The report does not allege wrongdoing by Andrew.

The same report touched on other royal property arrangements, including the rent the King pays for his daughters' palace homes. The princesses' rent is meant to be 60% of the open-market rate.

What comes next

The NAO report was intended to feed a Public Accounts Committee inquiry. The questions for MPs are fairly narrow. They are what Andrew actually received, whether the Crown Estate knew or should have known, and whether the lease terms gave value to the public. A firm number from that inquiry would show whether the £2,000 ceiling is borne out. Without one, the figure shows how much of the arrangement is still undisclosed.
