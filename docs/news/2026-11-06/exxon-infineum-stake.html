ExxonMobil reported to be weighing an exit from its Infineum stake

In one sentence
The FT reported that Shell and Exxon are exploring a sale of Infineum, their 50/50 additives venture, with a reported $8bn ask; neither company has confirmed it.

ExxonMobil is reported to be weighing the future of its half of Infineum, the lubricant and fuel-additives joint venture it shares with Shell. The aggregator item that prompted the latest round of attention, from Simply Wall St, frames this as an Exxon-only portfolio review. The better-sourced version points somewhere else: both owners are said to be looking at a sale of the whole business. Neither company has confirmed anything.

What has been reported

The underlying account comes from the Financial Times, which reported on Thursday 22 October, citing people familiar with the matter, that Shell and Exxon are looking to sell Infineum. The newspaper's report was carried by Alliance News the following day. According to that account:

  • Infineum has asked Bank of America to run the process.
  • The owners are seeking a valuation of about $8bn.
  • The process was requested only in recent days and is at an early stage.
  • It may not result in a transaction.

The $8bn figure is an asking price relayed by anonymous sources. It is not an assessment of value. Neither parent publishes separate earnings for the venture, so there is nothing in the public record against which to test it. No buyer has been named. The Simply Wall St item itself supplies no figures, no deal terms and no primary source.

The business

Infineum was formed in January 1999 by combining the two companies' additives divisions, and is owned 50/50. It is based in Abingdon, Oxfordshire, with business centres in the US, China and Singapore and six manufacturing plants, plus two operated by partners in France. It makes specialty chemicals for engine oils and fuels, and describes its work as covering transport, e-mobility and decarbonisation. The ownership split should be checked against current company filings.

It is a mature, steady business, and it sits awkwardly with the strategies of both parents, which are concentrating capital on upstream production, LNG and, for Exxon, a handful of growth chemicals projects. An additives supplier whose products are tied to the combustion engine also faces a long-run question about electric vehicles, which is one reason a sale now, rather than later, would be easy to understand.

Not the first attempt

The owners have been here before. In 2008 they explored a sale of Infineum, with private equity groups including Apollo, Carlyle and KKR reported among the interested parties. The process was shelved after the strategic review concluded that the shareholders should keep the business. Offers fell short of expectations and bidders struggled to arrange financing in the leveraged loan market. The episode is a useful reminder that early-stage reviews of this kind often end without a deal.

What to watch

Three things would move this from rumour to story. The first is a statement or filing from Exxon or Shell, most plausibly alongside quarterly or full-year results. The second is the appearance of named bidders; private equity and strategic chemicals buyers are the obvious categories, and the size of any financing package would show how much appetite there is at $8bn. The third is whether the sale is of the whole venture or of one parent's share. The framing of the Simply Wall St item suggests the latter, but the FT's account describes a joint disposal. Until a primary source says which, this remains a possibility rather than a decision.
