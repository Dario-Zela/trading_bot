Bank of England raises Bank Rate to 4%, its first hike in over three years

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate from 3.75% to 4% by 8–1, its first rise since 2023, but only Bailey's 'another increase is likely' guidance hints at more, and Taylor says this is not a hiking cycle.

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate from 3.75% to 4% at its November meeting, its first increase since August 2023, when rates peaked at 5.25%. The move ends the easing cycle that had brought borrowing costs down from that peak, and it shifts the policy debate from how far rates will fall to whether they will rise again.

The change reaches households first. Repayments on variable and tracker mortgages are likely to rise, and government borrowing costs track gilt yields, so the Treasury's financing is also exposed. Sterling and gilt pricing are the other channels to watch, though the market reaction is not yet confirmed in this brief.

Two details will decide how far the shift goes: the vote split on the Monetary Policy Committee and whether its forward guidance points to further increases or a single move. The full article will set out the published vote and guidance from the Bank's announcement and minutes. Until then, the decision should be read as a change in direction for policy, not yet as confirmation of a sustained tightening cycle.
