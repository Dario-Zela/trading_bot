Trump sees 'positive momentum' on Iran as oil falls, though Tehran still demands US 'surrender'

After meeting Qatar's prime minister, Trump called new Iran strikes 'highly unlikely' and oil fell, but Tehran still demands US 'surrender', leaving the outcome uncertain.

President Donald Trump said on Monday that further US strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely," following a meeting with Qatar's prime minister, and described what he called "positive momentum" toward ending the conflict. He added that Washington remains ready to act again if needed, so the de-escalation signal is conditional rather than a settled commitment.

Tehran did not match the tone. Iranian officials continued to demand that the US "surrender," and their public rhetoric stayed hard, leaving a clear gap between the two sides' stated positions. Oil prices fell on the news, with markets apparently pricing in the chance of a lower-risk Gulf over the coming weeks.

Qatar's role matters here. It is one of the few channels still running between Washington and Tehran, and a mediator's involvement is often the first visible sign that talks are being considered, even when the parties are far apart. Any real move toward an end to the fighting would bear on Gulf security, commercial shipping through the region and energy prices, all of which are now reacting to every signal from both capitals.

What to watch is whether the Qatari channel produces a concrete step, such as a pause in strikes or a formal meeting, or whether Tehran's language softens. Until one of those happens, the optimism in Washington and the hard line in Tehran remain unreconciled, and the oil market's reaction could reverse quickly if either side hardens.
