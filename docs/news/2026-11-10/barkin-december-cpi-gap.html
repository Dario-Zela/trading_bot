Fed's Barkin sees no December base case as BLS omits October annual CPI

In one sentence
Bloomberg reports the BLS will omit October's annual CPI figures just as Richmond Fed's Barkin says he has no December base case; the BLS omission is unconfirmed.

The Federal Reserve's next rate decision may arrive without the inflation figure most often quoted in its own statements. Bloomberg, as relayed by Benzinga, reports that the Bureau of Labor Statistics will leave out the 12-month headline and core figures when it publishes October consumer price data. The same day's coverage carried remarks from Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin, who said he has no base case for a December move. Taken together, the two items describe a central bank being asked to hold its course with a thinner evidence base.

One caution first. This article rests on a secondary report. The BLS has not been checked directly, and the BLS release calendar and the notice attached to the October release are the documents that would settle it. The Bloomberg claim is treated below as reported, not confirmed.

What is reportedly missing

The annual rate is the number that headlines, central-bank statements and market pricing tend to anchor on. It smooths month-to-month noise and is the figure set against a 2% target. If it is absent for October, the loss is one of convenience and comparability, not of underlying information.

The report as relayed does not say that the underlying October index levels or monthly changes are withheld. That distinction matters, and it is unresolved. If the monthly series is published, anyone with the prior year's index can compute an annual rate. The unofficial figure would carry no official standing, and it would depend on the quality of last year's comparison base.

That base is the likely root of the problem. The BLS did not publish a standard October 2025 CPI after the federal shutdown, and the 12-month October changes were missing from the record. An annual comparison for October 2026 would run against that gap. A omission now could therefore be a knock-on effect of last year's disruption, not a fresh failure. It is also possible the story conflates the two episodes. The date is awkward as well: the October 2026 release would not normally appear as early as 10 November, so the report may be describing an advance notice of the release's contents.

What Barkin said

Barkin, a non-voting participant in the Fed's rate-setting committee this year, told an audience that he has no base case for the December meeting. He placed the policy rate at around, or slightly below, neutral, meaning it is neither clearly restraining nor stimulating the economy. Benzinga's account quotes him calling the latest jobs report 'healthy' while describing the labour market as low-hire, low-fire: few people are being let go, and few are being taken on.

He also said he wants two full months of data before each meeting. These remarks come from a single outlet, and the wording should be checked against a transcript or the Fed's own release before being relied on.

The two-month rule is the connection to the CPI story. A policymaker who wants a longer run of data before acting is made more cautious by a gap in the headline series. A policy rate described as near neutral also means the bar for a move is higher than it was when rates sat well above that level. Neither is a signal of a cut or a hike. Both point to patience.

What it means for pricing

Markets can adapt to a missing annual print, as they did last year, by leaning on monthly changes, core components such as shelter and services, and alternative gauges, including the Fed's preferred personal consumption expenditures measure and private price trackers. Each substitute carries more interpretive risk than the single published figure, and that tends to widen the range of reasonable rate-path views.

The practical consequence is that the rest of the data calendar gains weight. The November jobs report and the monthly components of the October CPI, if they are published, will carry more of the load. The next scheduled test is the December meeting itself, where Barkin's absence of a base case will meet whatever inflation record the BLS has by then released. The BLS release calendar should be the first stop for confirming which October series will actually appear.
