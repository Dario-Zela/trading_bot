Bitcoin 'glory days are over', says Bloomberg expert, as bulls push back

In one sentence
A Bloomberg voice says crypto's best returns are behind it and bulls disagree, but the quote is unverified and the evidence so far is mixed.

Bitcoin's critics have a new refrain. A Bloomberg commentator has declared that crypto's "glory days are over", and bullish holders have answered that the call is premature. The exchange, relayed by Benzinga, is opinion against opinion. It is worth reading only for what the evidence says about each side.

The Benzinga item does not make clear who at Bloomberg made the remark, and the original Bloomberg text could not be located. This article therefore treats the quote as unverified. The arguments below come from earlier, documented Bloomberg commentary and from the price record, not from the disputed exchange.

A familiar call

The thesis has a long lineage at Bloomberg. In 2023, Bloomberg Intelligence strategist Mike McGlone argued that the days of big Bitcoin pumps were over, because volatility had fallen sharply and a sudden jump to a new high looked unlikely. In January 2026 he said he had reversed his long-term view and urged investors to "sell the rallies" across risk assets. Late-2025 coverage by DL News reported his scenarios for 2026, which ran from a fall toward $50,000 to a drop as far as $10,000.

A separate Bloomberg opinion column in 2024 made a structural version of the argument. Its title, "Crypto Is Going Mainstream, Which Means It's Over", suggested that each step toward institutional acceptance removes some of the asymmetry that drew early buyers.

What the record supports

The bearish case has some evidence behind it. Bitcoin peaked near $126,000 in October 2025. By early February 2026, Bloomberg's Odd Lots newsletter was describing a drawdown of about 44% from that peak and arguing this was the industry's worst winter yet. A fall of that size, together with the volatility compression McGlone cited in 2023, fits a maturing asset whose outsized returns are fading.

The weakness is the track record of the calls themselves. The 2023 "no more big pumps" claim preceded the approval of US spot Bitcoin ETFs and a rally to six-figure prices. A forecast of a $10,000 floor, or of a permanent end to outsized returns, would also require a drawdown of roughly 90% from the peak, a magnitude that only the earliest bear markets have matched. Cycle-timing calls rarely come with a falsifiable date, which makes them hard to score.

The bulls' counter-arguments, as far as they can be reconstructed, rest on ETF access, scarcity and past rebounds. None of those has been tested against the current drawdown. Short aggregator snippets report Bitcoin slipping below $76,000 on 6 November alongside roughly $530m of outflows from spot Bitcoin and Ether ETFs. Those figures could not be traced to an original outlet and should be read cautiously.

What would settle it

The debate will not be decided by commentary. Three things would help. The first is whether ETF flows turn from outflows to sustained inflows. The second is whether realised volatility stays compressed or expands. The third is whether Bitcoin reclaims its October 2025 high. A decisive break below the lows of the current drawdown would favour the bears. A recovery of the old peak would reduce the "glory days are over" call to another entry in a long list of mistimed forecasts.
