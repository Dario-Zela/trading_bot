Tommy Walsh, of Ground Force and Homes Under the Hammer, dies aged 69

Tommy Walsh in 2004, at the height of his Ground Force fame. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Tommy Walsh, the builder who became a staple of British makeover TV through Ground Force and Homes Under the Hammer, has died aged 69 after a long illness.

Tommy Walsh, the East London builder who became one of the most recognisable faces of British makeover television, has died aged 69. His agent, Debbie Schiesser, said he died on Monday 9 November after a long illness, according to reports of her statement. The family has asked for privacy and has not disclosed a cause of death.

Walsh was the muscle of Ground Force, the BBC garden-transformation show that ran from 1997 to 2005. He handled the hard landscaping (decking, walls, water features) beside Alan Titchmarsh, who is among those leading the tributes. The format was simple and slightly mischievous: a team arrived while an unsuspecting householder was out, and a garden was rebuilt in two days. Walsh was the one with the tape measure and the cheerful patter.

From the garden to everything else

Born in Hackney on 18 December 1956, Walsh built a long television career on being a tradesman who could talk to a camera. Ground Force led to a run of his own programmes, including Challenge Tommy Walsh (2002–2005), Space Invaders, Tommy Walsh's Eco House (2010), Tommy's Ultimate Workshop, Fix Your House For Free and Flip That House with Tommy Walsh. He also turned up on Celebrity Fit Club and the BBC quiz Pointless.

More recent viewers knew him from daytime. He appeared on Homes Under the Hammer from 2021 to 2024, and on Clean It, Fix It, which listed him from 2021 to 2026.

Illness and tributes

Walsh had a benign chest tumour removed in 2002. He was diagnosed with throat cancer in 2022, had surgery and was reported to have recovered. One report links his death to a long illness following that diagnosis, but the family has not confirmed a cause, and none is stated here.

Tributes followed on Monday, from Titchmarsh and from the Watford club Old Parmiterians. The BBC and the Guardian were named in triage as having reported the death. This account rests on the agent's statement as relayed by other outlets and on Wikipedia, and the original BBC and Guardian copy was not checked.

The end of an era

Makeover television has changed a good deal since Ground Force began. Weekend garden rescues gave way to property programmes and, later, daytime renovation shows. Walsh worked across all of those stages. The through-line was a reassuring competence: he made building look like something an ordinary person might have a go at, even when the budget and the crew said otherwise.

No funeral arrangements have been announced. Any further statement from his family or the BBC is the next thing to watch.
