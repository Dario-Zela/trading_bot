Warren previews Senate Banking agenda on prediction markets and Trump-family crypto

In one sentence
An aide says Elizabeth Warren would focus Senate Banking on affordability, AI, prediction markets and financial-crisis risk, but only if she wins the chair, which is unconfirmed.

Senator Elizabeth Warren's office is sketching out what the Senate Banking Committee would look like under her control: a docket built around affordability, artificial intelligence, prediction markets and the risk of a financial crisis. The preview comes from an aide, as relayed by The Block via Benzinga. It is a statement of intent, not a policy change, and it depends on Warren actually taking the gavel.

What the aide said

According to The Block's account, the agenda would concentrate on four areas: the cost of living, AI, prediction markets and a potential financial crisis. The headline on the source piece also says Warren would scrutinise Trump-family crypto ventures. The summary available to this desk names no specific ventures, dates or actions, and it carries no on-record quote from the aide. Those details are left out here rather than guessed at.

The themes are not new for Warren. As ranking Democrat on the committee she has pressed the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) on whether it can police prediction markets and crypto. A June 2026 letter to CFTC chairman Selig questioned the agency's capacity after staffing cuts, and in July she asked the Government Accountability Office to investigate those cuts. Committee Democrats have also asked Chairman Tim Scott for a public hearing on prediction markets, after Republicans met privately with Kalshi's chief executive. In July she urged Senate leaders to make sure crypto legislation stops a president from profiting from office.

The conditional

Everything hinges on the chair. Democrats won the Senate in the November 3 midterms, according to AP-based reporting, which put them at a majority of at least 51-49. One outlet reported a larger Democratic tally with some races undecided, so the final count is not yet settled. This desk found no reporting that confirms Warren as the next Banking chair, and the new Congress does not convene until January. The preview is therefore a description of what she would do, not a schedule of what will happen.

Why it matters to markets

The Banking Committee holds the hearings and document-request powers that shape how financial regulators behave. For prediction-market operators, a Warren-led committee would be a change from a Republican chair who has been willing to hold private roundtables with the industry. For crypto firms with ties to the Trump family, it would mean a standing venue for oversight questions. Committee oversight does not create law, but it can shape how agencies like the CFTC act and what legislation can pass.

The pending crypto market-structure bill is the nearer test. Warren has said she wants a crypto bill but not the Clarity Act in its current form, and she has said she would lead on crypto legislation if she chairs the committee.

What to watch

The first marker is the Senate Democratic caucus's committee assignments, which would confirm or deny the chairmanship. After that, the question is whether the aide's outline turns into announced hearings and named targets once the new Congress sits in January.
