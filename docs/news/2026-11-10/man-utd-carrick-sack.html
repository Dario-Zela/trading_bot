Man Utd expected to sack head coach Carrick

In one sentence
Manchester United are reportedly set to sack head coach Michael Carrick, but the club has confirmed nothing and no successor is officially named.

Manchester United are reportedly about to dismiss Michael Carrick. The club has confirmed nothing. The story rests on press reports, which the desk has not been able to match to a single, fully verifiable primary account. Those reports describe a head coach whose departure is a matter of when, not whether, and a club that insists, in the words of one summary, that a decision "hasn't been made yet".

That caveat matters. The club has made no announcement, and the reports give no firm date or confirmed successor. What follows is a report of what is being said, not a confirmed sacking.

What is being reported

The account that emerged on Monday evening, 9 November, is consistent in outline. The Athletic is cited as saying that the expectation is that Carrick is out of a job "in a matter of hours or days". TalkSport is cited as reporting that chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox will recommend the dismissal to Sir Jim Ratcliffe at a meeting on Tuesday. The final decision rests with Ratcliffe and the Glazer family, who remain the club's other significant owners. BBC Sport, the outlet named in this edition's brief, is understood to have carried the same expectation. The desk could not retrieve that report directly, so it is not relied on here.

The sporting case, as reported, is not subtle. United are said to sit 18th, in the relegation zone for the first time in four decades, and winless in eight Premier League games. Across all competitions Carrick has reportedly won three of 15 this season.

That is a steep fall. Carrick took over as interim head coach in January after Rúben Amorim left, won 11 of his first 16 games, and was handed the job permanently in May on a two-year contract to June 2028 after United finished third and returned to the Champions League. If he goes, he would be the latest permanent appointment to be undone within months of a vote of confidence.

The succession question

No successor is confirmed. Reports name Mauricio Pochettino, currently in charge of the United States men's national team, as favourite, with a release clause said not to be a major obstacle. Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann are also linked. These are press names, not offers. The same reports suggest Carrick would not take charge of the visit to Liverpool on 22 November, which would imply a change during the international break.

The pattern is what makes the story larger than one man's job. Amorim, appointed in late 2024, lasted roughly 14 months. Carrick was installed as the steady-handed answer and was given a permanent deal in May. A dismissal in November would suggest the club's plan lasted a summer.

The listed-company angle

Manchester United plc trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker MANU (background not drawn from the reports, and worth checking against filings). That gives a coaching decision a second audience. Each change carries a compensation bill, a recruitment bill and the question of whether a club that has missed European football in some seasons and returned in others can sell investors a stable sporting strategy.

The desk has not found reliable pricing data to say how the shares reacted, and does not claim a market move. The logic is simpler. A club that appoints a coach permanently in May, then moves to replace him by November, invites doubt about whether anyone is steering. Revenue depends heavily on Champions League qualification, so a relegation-zone start is a financial problem as well as a sporting one.

What to watch

The first marker is Tuesday's meeting between Berrada, Wilcox and Ratcliffe, and whether the club issues a statement afterwards. The second is whether a replacement is named before the Liverpool game on 22 November. If the club denies the reports, or leaves Carrick in place, the story weakens considerably. If it confirms, attention shifts to the terms of his exit and the choice that follows.
