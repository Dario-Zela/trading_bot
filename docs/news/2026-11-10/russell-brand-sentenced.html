Russell Brand jailed for 10½ years for rape and sexual assault

In one sentence
Russell Brand was jailed for 10½ years for rape and sexual assault, and a judge said his status protected him, while Jemima Goldsmith alleges he abused her too.

Russell Brand was jailed on Monday for 10 years and six months for one rape and three sexual assaults. Judge Joel Bennathan told Southwark Crown Court that the 51-year-old's fame had shielded him for years, and that the people around him had let it. The question since is whether the industry that employed him, and the institutions that should have policed it, will treat that as a verdict on themselves as well.

The sentence

A jury convicted Brand last week of raping a 21-year-old fan at his London home in 2009. It also convicted him of three sexual assaults: an MTV worker at a Soho bar party in 2004, a Capital Radio worker in a corridor in 2005, and a Channel 4 worker during a recording session in 2009. The jury acquitted him on two further rape counts and one count of attempted indecent assault.

Bennathan called Brand a "predator" who had shown "staggering arrogance". According to RTÉ's account of the hearing, he said Brand's "status protected you" until journalists exposed his conduct in 2023, and that his entourage had "indulged your dreadful behaviour". Of the assaults he said: "It was outrageous and laddish, but it was also a criminal offence, and as it happens you haven't got away with it."

Brand must serve two-thirds of the term, at least seven years, before release on licence. He will be on the sex offenders' register for life. He has spent four nights in HMP Wandsworth. The court heard six character references, including one from Georgina Baillie. His barrister, Ian Winter KC, described Brand's wife, Laura Gallacher, as suffering "total devastation". Brand showed no emotion as the sentence was read.

The workplaces

The geography of the offences is the part that bears on the industry. Three of the four women worked in or around mainstream broadcasting, at MTV, Capital Radio and Channel 4. The incidents were put to a jury as things that happened at parties, in corridors and during recording sessions, in places where colleagues, producers and managers were plausibly nearby.

The Metropolitan Police's Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy said the verdict showed that "fame, status and the passage of time do not change the fundamental issue of consent". The judge's remark about the entourage points at a different group, those who were present, or close enough to know, and said nothing.

The government's response is the other half of the story. The triage notes for this piece say a minister warned the media industry that "time is up" on tolerating abuse. That quote could not be confirmed. The coverage reviewed here did not carry it, and the minister's name and title are unverified. It is therefore not attributed here. Even without the quote, the pressure is evident. A judge has put on the record that status protected a serial offender, and that is hard for broadcasters to answer with a statement about changed procedures.

A second public accusation

Hours after the sentence, Jemima Goldsmith, who dated Brand in 2013 and 2014, posted on X that he had subjected her to "violence, coercive control and abuse". She said she had to call police in 2014 to have him removed from her house during a violent incident. She did not press charges or go public then, she said, because she feared the press attention on her then-teenage children.

Her account is an allegation, not a finding of any court, and Brand has not been tried on it. ABC's report did not include a response from him. She said she had not seen or spoken to him since 2014. She also said she had helped the journalists who first exposed him, Ros Urwin, Louisa Compton and Dorothy Byrne, and had offered to testify if the newspaper that published the accusers' accounts was sued. She wrote that she was "glad and relieved" that he had faced justice.

The detail matters because it describes the silence the judge condemned. A woman with money, a public profile and, on her account, a police callout in her own home still judged that staying quiet was safer. That is a harder thing to dismiss as one industry's failure, and it explains why the argument is about tolerance more than about one man.

What next

Metropolitan Police have said they passed prosecutors their investigation into two further rape allegations against Brand. Any charging decision would extend the case beyond Monday's sentence. Brand also retains the usual route of appeal against conviction and sentence. The more immediate test is whether broadcasters and production companies publish what they knew about conduct in the 2000s, and whether the minister's warning, once confirmed, becomes policy or stays a line in a press release.
