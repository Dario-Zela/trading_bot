C3.ai and BigBear.ai diverge on revenue, but C3.ai still looks pricey

C3.ai founder Thomas Siebel, who resumed the CEO role on May 8, 2026, after stepping back for health reasons. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
C3.ai's revenue fell 53% year-over-year while BigBear.ai's held nearly flat — yet C3.ai still trades at roughly 35% above its DCF fair value.

Two of the best-known names in publicly traded enterprise AI are moving in opposite directions this year, and the numbers are stark enough to explain why, without any need for hype.

C3.ai, the Redwood City software vendor founded by Thomas Siebel, reported revenue of $51.6 million for the quarter ended April 30 — down 53% from $108.7 million in the same quarter a year earlier. Full fiscal-2026 revenue came in at $250.3 million, while the company's net loss ballooned to $470.4 million, 63% wider than the year before. Siebel, who had stepped back to executive chairman in 2025 after being diagnosed with an autoimmune condition that left him visually impaired, resumed the CEO role on May 8. He arrived to a company that had already cut headcount from 1,075 to 700 as part of a restructuring meant to strip out $135 million in annualized costs.

BigBear.ai, the Virginia-based rival that sells AI and analytics tools mostly to defense and national-security customers, had a far quieter quarter. Revenue came in at $34.4 million for the three months ended March 31 — down just 1% from $34.8 million a year earlier. That follows a rougher 2025, when full-year revenue fell 19% to $127.7 million from $158.2 million, a decline the company has pinned on federal budget cuts under the Trump administration. Backlog, though, grew 14% quarter over quarter to $281.9 million, helped by a $53 million classified contract, and BigBear.ai is sitting on $431.5 million in cash after converting most of its 2029 notes into equity. Management affirmed full-year guidance of $135 million to $165 million.

The gap between them is closing fast

Put the two most recent quarters side by side and the story sharpens: a year ago, C3.ai was outselling BigBear.ai by roughly $74 million a quarter. Now the gap is about $17 million. That's not BigBear.ai's revenue growing — it's C3.ai's revenue collapsing toward it. Whether that counts as "convergence" is a matter of framing; a more accurate read is that C3.ai's commercial business, hit hard by customer attrition and the disruption around Siebel's medical leave, has fallen off a cliff, while BigBear.ai's government contract book has simply held its ground.

Priced for a recovery it hasn't delivered

What makes C3.ai worth watching beyond the revenue line is the valuation. The stock trades around $9, having fallen 21% so far in 2026 and 65% from its 52-week high of $35.98 — part of an 82% five-year slide. Yet on a discounted cash-flow basis, Simply Wall St pegs fair value at $6.61 a share, implying the stock still carries a 35% premium. On sales, C3.ai trades at roughly 5.6 times revenue against a peer average closer to 1.6 times. In plain terms: a company burning through cash — free cash flow was negative $199.2 million over the past twelve months — and shedding revenue at double-digit rates is still priced as though a turnaround is already underway. BigBear.ai, at roughly $3.27 a share, doesn't carry the same disconnect, if only because expectations for it were never as lofty.

The next test for both comes with C3.ai's fiscal first-quarter update, expected in September, when investors will see whether Siebel's cost cuts have stemmed the bleeding — and whether the market's optimism about a rebound is catching up with, or still running ahead of, the numbers.
