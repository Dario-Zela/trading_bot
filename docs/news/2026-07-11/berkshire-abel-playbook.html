Berkshire's Alphabet and UnitedHealth buys show Abel widening the playbook

Greg Abel, who became Berkshire Hathaway CEO on January 1, 2026, succeeding Warren Buffett. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Berkshire's Q1 filing shows Abel tripling Alphabet and axing 16 stocks — but the UnitedHealth stake he exited was actually Buffett's, complicating the tidy narrative.

Greg Abel has been running Berkshire Hathaway for barely six months, and the trades already look different from Warren Buffett's.

Berkshire's first-quarter 13F filing, released May 15, showed Abel exiting 16 full positions — Visa, Mastercard, Amazon, Domino's, Aon and UnitedHealth Group among them — while nearly tripling the company's stake in Alphabet and opening a fresh $2.65 billion position in Delta Air Lines. The portfolio shrank from 42 holdings to 29 in a single quarter. Berkshire was a net seller of stocks for the fourteenth straight quarter, offloading $24.1 billion against $15.9 billion in purchases.

"They're broadening their scope, and they are getting involved," said Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets, in an interview with Benzinga. Woods argues the moves show Abel isn't confined to "the old traditional Warren Buffett investments" and is instead willing to act on positions the market has, in his words, gotten "ridiculously overdone."

The Alphabet build-out

The clearest evidence for that view is Alphabet. Berkshire held 17.8 million shares worth $5.6 billion at the end of 2025; by March 31 that had grown to nearly 58 million shares, roughly $17 billion — a 224% increase in three months. Then, in June, Berkshire went further, agreeing to buy $10 billion of newly issued Alphabet stock in a private placement tied to the company's $80 billion AI infrastructure raise, split evenly between Class A shares at $351.81 and Class C shares at $348.20. The combined position now stands near $41 billion, about 86.4 million shares and a 9.2% stake — enough to make Alphabet Berkshire's fourth-largest holding, ahead of Coca-Cola. Woods called the scale of it "surprising," noting Berkshire spent 25 years mostly avoiding big technology bets before Apple.

UnitedHealth complicates the story

The UnitedHealth trade is less a case of Abel expanding the playbook than of him closing a chapter Buffett opened. Berkshire built its roughly 5 million-share, $1.6 billion stake in UnitedHealth in the second quarter of 2025 — under Buffett, after the insurer's stock had collapsed more than 50% — and disclosed it that August. By the Q1 2026 filing, the position was gone entirely. Woods's point stands on its own terms even so: exiting a full stake within a few quarters is "something you didn't see very often from Warren," who tended to hold for years or decades once committed. The speed of the exit, more than the original purchase, is the tell.

That distinction matters for how much weight to put on the "new CEO, new strategy" framing. Buying Alphabet aggressively and funding an AI infrastructure raise is a genuine change in emphasis — Berkshire has rarely written a $10 billion check into unprofitable-by-Buffett's-metrics growth capex. Purging 16 positions in three months, though, reads as much like routine portfolio tidying by a new hand at the wheel as it does a strategic pivot, and Woods himself hedges, saying Abel's full impact "will take time to materialize." He remains bullish on insurance names for the back half of 2026 and expects a buying opportunity in Berkshire shares once the post-Buffett transition fully settles.

The next data point is Berkshire's Q2 13F, due in mid-August, which will show whether the Alphabet buying continues at pace and whether Abel keeps trimming the roster or starts rebuilding it.
