NEXA or NGLOY: which is the better value stock?

Anglo American's London headquarters at 17 Charterhouse Street. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Zacks rates zinc miner Nexa Resources (Value grade A, P/E ~8) cheaper than Anglo American (Value grade C, forward P/E ~17.5), though Anglo's premium reflects its pending Teck merger and copper exposure.

Zacks' stock-screening desk ran its usual value gauntlet this week on two miners that could hardly look more different up close: Nexa Resources, a mid-cap zinc producer working Peru and Brazil, and Anglo American, the roughly $53 billion London-listed major mid-merger with Canada's Teck Resources. Both trade in New York — Nexa as NEXA on the NYSE, Anglo American as an over-the-counter ADR under NGLOY — and both carry a favourable earnings outlook. On raw valuation, though, they are not close.

Nexa trades around $12.85 a share, a market capitalisation near $1.7 billion, at a trailing price-to-earnings ratio of roughly 8. Anglo American, at about $24.88 a share and some $52-58 billion in market value depending on the data provider, carries a forward P/E closer to 17.5. Zacks' own scoring system, which sorts stocks into value grades from A to F using multiples like P/E, P/B and cash flow, gives Nexa an A and Anglo American a C. Nexa also holds the stronger Zacks Rank — a #2 (Buy) against Anglo American's #3 (Hold) in the comparison, reflecting sharper upward revisions to analysts' earnings estimates. Put simply: Nexa is cheaper on the numbers and has momentum behind those numbers, which is the combination the Zacks methodology rewards.

What's driving each stock

The two companies are betting on different metals cycles. Nexa is a pure zinc play — it runs Peru's largest underground zinc mine at Cerro Lindo and the largest zinc refinery in the Americas at Cajamarquilla — at a moment when the metal itself is not the story; the company's own execution is. First-quarter net income came in at $118 million, up from $29 million a year earlier, with adjusted EBITDA rising 126% to $283 million as zinc production climbed 18% year-over-year on higher ore grades. Net debt sits at $1.48 billion, roughly 2.2 times trailing EBITDA. The company is mid-build on its Cerro Pasco Integration Project, a multi-year expansion meant to extend mine life beyond 15 years, with phase one construction targeted for completion in the third quarter. Zinc itself faces a soft macro backdrop — forecasters at Fastmarkets and StoneX see a growing global surplus into 2026-27 as smelter capacity comes online in China and Europe — which makes Nexa's cheap multiple look less like a bargain and more like the market pricing in that headwind.

Anglo American's story is corporate, not commodity. Shareholders on both sides approved its merger of equals with Teck Resources in December, structured as a share swap — 1.3301 Anglo shares per Teck share — expected to close in late 2026 or early 2027 and to generate roughly $800 million in annual pre-tax synergies. The company also finished divesting its platinum arm, Valterra Platinum, banking $2.5 billion from the final stake sale to pay down debt. That leaves Anglo American a leaner, copper-and-iron-ore-weighted business trading at a premium multiple, with the market effectively paying up for the Teck deal's execution and for copper exposure — a metal that, unlike zinc, is trading near record highs on tight concentrate supply.

The Zacks screen answers the narrow question of which stock is statistically cheaper relative to its own earnings trajectory, and on that measure Nexa wins clearly. Anglo American's higher multiple is the price of admission to a bigger, more diversified, copper-heavy company mid-transformation — a bet on the Teck merger closing cleanly rather than on this quarter's numbers. Whether that premium is justified depends on how the merger integration lands, a question that will start to resolve as the deal moves toward its targeted close.
