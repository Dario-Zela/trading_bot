AstraZeneca advances oral GLP-1 candidate to Phase 3, entering the crowded obesity market

AstraZeneca's Discovery Centre headquarters on the Cambridge Biomedical Campus, the company's global R&D base — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
AstraZeneca's oral GLP-1 pill elecoglipron posted 10.5% weight loss in Phase 2b and advances to Phase 3, but two approved oral rivals are already on shelves.

AstraZeneca presented Phase 2b results for its oral GLP-1 drug elecoglipron at the American Diabetes Association's scientific sessions this week and announced it is advancing the compound into Phase 3 trials — entering a market where two competitors already have approved oral products on pharmacy shelves.

The announcement puts AstraZeneca (LSE: AZN) in a race against the clock. Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy pill won FDA approval in December 2025, achieving 16.6% mean weight loss in the OASIS 4 trial. Eli Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron) followed in April 2026, showing 12.4% weight loss at its highest dose over 72 weeks. AstraZeneca, by contrast, is only now starting its pivotal programme.

What the Phase 2b data show

Two trials anchored the announcement. In VISTA, 310 adults with obesity or overweight received elecoglipron 75 mg daily or placebo for 26 weeks; the drug arm lost an average of 10.5% of body weight versus 0.6% on placebo. Crucially, the loss had not plateaued by 36 weeks, where it reached 11.8%. Some 88.8% of patients on the drug achieved at least 5% weight reduction — the threshold typically cited as the minimum to confer metabolic benefit.

The SOLSTICE trial enrolled 406 adults with Type 2 diabetes and showed 7.7% weight loss and a 1.9-percentage-point reduction in HbA1c at 26 weeks. Both results comfortably satisfied the bar for advancing to Phase 3.

Jefferies analysts, reviewing the data at the ADA meeting, described efficacy as "higher than we have seen with other key oral small-molecule GLP-1 agents" and called the overall package "competitive efficacy with standard tolerability." The adverse-event profile — predominantly gastrointestinal, mild to moderate — is consistent with the drug class; the company reported no liver safety signals in either trial.

Into an already furnished room

Elecoglipron is a small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist, which means it sidesteps the 30-minute pre-meal fasting window that oral semaglutide (Wegovy pill) requires — a real convenience advantage. But Lilly's Foundayo is also a small molecule with no dietary restrictions, and it already carries an FDA approval. AstraZeneca is roughly two to three years behind where those two competitors stood today.

The Phase 3 programme, branded EMBOLD for obesity and ELUMINATE for diabetes, includes a combination trial pairing elecoglipron with dapagliflozin — AstraZeneca's own Farxiga — a cardiometabolic stacking strategy that could differentiate the drug in the T2D indication specifically. Long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes studies are also planned, though results there would not arrive before the early 2030s.

The global GLP-1 weight-loss drug market was valued at roughly $69 billion in 2026 and is projected to approach $195 billion by 2035. Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly currently control about 87% of prescription weight-management revenue, with semaglutide alone holding a 52% share of the GLP-1 market. AZN shares closed at £134.64 on Wednesday, around 19% below the average analyst price target of £165.14.

The Phase 2b numbers are credible enough to justify the Phase 3 investment. Whether an oral GLP-1 entering the market two or more years after entrenched rivals can carve meaningful share is a different — and as yet unanswered — question. Interim data from EMBOLD, expected in the late 2020s, will be the first real signal.
