Dogecoin falls to multi-year low as Musk's market-moving influence fades

The Dogecoin logo — the Shiba Inu-themed meme coin that Elon Musk's posts once moved by double digits in a single session. — Dogecoin Foundation / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Dogecoin fell to $0.0849, its lowest since the pre-2021 bull run, as Musk's once-reliable ability to move the token by double digits has shrunk to barely a blip.

Dogecoin touched $0.0849 this week — its lowest level since before the 2021 meme-coin frenzy — capping a slide that has erased roughly $48 billion in market value from the November 2024 peak of $61 billion. The token is now 88 percent below its all-time high. Whether the decline says anything deeper about how markets are pricing celebrity influence is a harder question than the price chart makes it look.

For most of DOGE's trading history, the answer to that question was straightforward: when Elon Musk posted, the coin moved. Research covering his early 2021 tweets found average single-day price gains of roughly 25 percent per mention, with individual spikes reaching 50 to 70 percent. A February 2021 tweet calling it "the people's crypto" sent the coin up more than 70 percent inside 24 hours. A December 2021 announcement that Tesla merchandise would accept DOGE added nearly 40 percent.

That arithmetic no longer applies. When Musk reposted an AI-generated video referencing Dogecoin on X in March this year, the coin barely registered a move. His X activity around the Department of Government Efficiency — whose DOGE initialism had briefly given the token a news-cycle tailwind in late 2024 — never reliably translated into sustained demand, and after the agency was wound down ahead of schedule, Musk publicly called the naming overlap a coincidence. Whatever repricing the agency's prominence had induced unwound with it.

The ETF verdict

The more structural test came with the launch of spot Dogecoin ETFs in late 2025. By any comparison to the reception for Bitcoin and Ethereum equivalents, the result was poor: combined net inflows across the new DOGE ETF products reached roughly $12 million total, against the billions that flowed into Bitcoin ETFs within weeks of their approval. Most trading days since launch have logged zero net flows.

That figure matters not because ETF adoption is the only measure of institutional credibility, but because it tested a specific hypothesis: that DOGE's market-cap premium over its fundamentals (the coin has no smart-contract capability and minimal transaction utility) reflected latent institutional demand waiting for a regulated wrapper. The data suggests it did not.

The broader meme-coin sector has corroborated the same verdict. The category peaked at roughly $150 billion in December 2024, collapsed to around $38 billion by early 2026, and has not recovered materially. DOGE has lost market share within even that declining pool.

None of this proves that celebrity-driven volatility has been permanently defanged. A single high-profile Musk statement can still produce short-term price movement; the reflexive 30-percent pumps of 2021 have shrunk to blips that fade within a news cycle, but the mechanism has not disappeared entirely. What the data does not yet support is the stronger claim — that the market has undergone durable structural change rather than a prolonged sentiment hangover following an unusually sharp hype cycle.

The next meaningful test: whether the SpaceX IPO process, anticipated around June 12, produces any renewed DOGE activity given Musk's centrality to that story. If it does not, the case for genuine correlation decay strengthens considerably.
