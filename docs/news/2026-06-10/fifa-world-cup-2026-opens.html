World Cup opens tomorrow with all three host nations in the field

Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, venue for the 2026 World Cup opening match — hosting a tournament inauguration for the third time — Arne Müseler / Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA 3.0 DE
In one sentence
The 2026 World Cup opens 11 June in Mexico City with $60B in global betting projected and $2B already traded on prediction markets before kickoff.

The 2026 FIFA World Cup opens tomorrow at Estadio Azteca in Mexico City — the stadium's third tournament inauguration, after 1970 and 1986 — with hosts Mexico facing South Africa in the Group A curtain-raiser. Forty-eight teams, three host nations, three currencies, and somewhere north of $60 billion in projected global wagering await.

That last figure is the number that earns this a slot here. Research firm H2 Gambling Capital pegs the global sports betting handle at $60 billion across the 39-day tournament, a 71 per cent jump from Qatar 2022. The driver is structural: since that last edition, legal sports betting has spread across 38 US states, putting North America at the centre of both the geography and the liquidity. US sportsbook volume alone could reach $4.3 billion — more than any previous sporting event on domestic record.

Prediction markets front-run the field

The most striking pre-tournament move has come in prediction markets. Polymarket and Kalshi combined for over $2 billion in World Cup trading volumes before a ball was kicked — the first time a non-political event has hit that scale on those platforms. Spain and France sit as co-favourites, each at roughly 16 per cent implied probability; England follows at 11 per cent, Portugal at 10, and defending champion Argentina at 9.

Those figures establish the baseline against which in-tournament repricing will be measured. A first-match upset — Mexico losing at the Azteca, or the United States dropping points — would move the host-nation books sharply. The expanded format compounds the event risk: 104 matches over six weeks, with live betting markets open throughout.

Three currencies, one tournament

All three host nations — USA, Canada, and Mexico — qualified automatically, giving this edition an unusual structural feature: sovereign FX exposure embedded in the tournament timeline. The Mexican peso has been under pressure through the first half of 2026, partly on security-related concerns ahead of the tournament, and further surprises in the group stage add another variable. The USD and CAD are less directly sensitive to result sentiment, though event-driven models tracking tourist flows and consumer spending across host cities will find plenty to watch.

For the British contingent: Scotland are in their first World Cup since 1998, having qualified directly after a dramatic late rally against Denmark. Wales did not make it, eliminated in the UEFA play-offs by Bosnia and Herzegovina on penalties. England open Group L against Croatia on 17 June in Dallas.

The final is set for 19 July at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey. Between now and then, 104 matches will reprice odds dozens of times a day. The opening whistle at the Azteca tomorrow is, among other things, the start of a market.
