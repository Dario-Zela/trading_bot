Barclays raises Flex target on AI server supply-chain exposure

Server racks inside a data centre facility; Flex manufactures power systems and integrated rack infrastructure for hyperscaler buildouts. — Carl Lender / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Barclays raised its Flex Ltd. price target from $174 to $203, citing AI server supply-chain upside — a call that looks more like analyst catch-up than market discovery.

Barclays lifted its price target on contract manufacturer Flex Ltd. (Nasdaq: FLEX) from $174 to $203 on June 4, maintaining an Overweight rating, and argued that the stock's exposure to AI server supply chains remains underappreciated by the market. The 17% target increase came a day after FLEX touched an all-time high of $166.86.

That sequencing matters. By the time an analyst publishes a re-rating, the market has often already done most of the work.

The thesis

Flex is the world's third-largest electronics manufacturing services company by revenue, sitting behind Foxconn and Pegatron but well ahead of most peers. Its Cloud and Power Infrastructure segment — power systems, server racks, thermal management — grew 38% in fiscal 2026 to $6.61 billion, equal to roughly a quarter of the company's $27.9 billion in total annual sales. That unit was the engine behind full-year revenue growth of 8% and a record adjusted operating margin of 6.3%, the sixth consecutive quarter above the 6% threshold.

The AI server buildout driving those numbers is not a secret. Hyperscalers are committing hundreds of billions of dollars to data-center infrastructure, and Flex sits in the supply chain for the physical equipment those centres require — power conversion, rack integration, high-density cooling. The Barclays note, relayed via Insider Monkey, cited the AI and broader business segments as the basis for the upgrade, without specifying a particular financial inflection.

Spin-off already on the table

The more interesting structural move is one Flex disclosed in early May: it intends to spin off the Cloud and Power Infrastructure unit into an independent publicly traded company, targeting completion by Q1 2027. Management frames the separation as a recognition that AI infrastructure demands a focused operating model and a distinct investor base — one willing to underwrite higher growth multiples than a diversified manufacturer typically commands.

SpinCo, as the company calls it, is projecting 65% to 75% revenue growth in fiscal 2027, followed by acceleration beyond 80% in fiscal 2028. Those figures imply the segment will more than double in two years. Flex added to the logic in March by completing a $1.1 billion acquisition of Electrical Power Products, expanding its critical power capabilities for utilities and data centres.

Flex also joins the S&P 500 in this month's quarterly rebalancing — a mechanical event that forces index-tracking inflows and tends to compress the valuation discount that smaller, underfollowed stocks carry.

Catch-up or discovery?

With guidance of $32.3 billion to $33.8 billion in revenue and adjusted EPS of $4.21 to $4.51 for fiscal 2027, Flex is not a hidden story. The stock has moved sharply since the spin-off announcement, the spin-off itself has been covered, and Barclays is one of several banks — alongside KeyBanc and JPMorgan — that have raised targets in recent weeks. When multiple houses update in a cluster following a major disclosure, the re-ratings tend to reflect revised models rather than proprietary insight.

That does not make the call wrong. If the CPI spin-off genuinely unlocks a valuation step-change — and the gap between a contract-manufacturer multiple and a high-growth infrastructure multiple is wide — then there may be room to run even from here. But readers should distinguish between a thesis being correct and a thesis being early. Barclays is arguing the former; the recent price action suggests the market has already begun to agree.

The next concrete data point arrives when Flex provides an investor day for SpinCo, expected before the end of calendar 2026.
