Saipem wins $181m drilling contracts for Scarabeo 9

Scarabeo 9 outside Phuket, Thailand, undergoing sea trials. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Saipem's $181m of Egypt and Suriname work for Scarabeo 9 gives the rig cover through 2027-28, though undisclosed terms limit what it says about pricing.

Saipem has won two drilling contracts for its Scarabeo 9 semi-submersible, worth about $181m combined, and the rig now has work lined up through 2027 and 2028. The Italian contractor announced the awards on 18 November. On its own the sum is a routine backlog update. The clients and the sequencing are the more useful detail.

The two contracts

The first is in Egypt. Burullus Gas Company, a joint venture of Shell, Petronas and the state-owned Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation, has contracted the rig to drill three offshore wells. Operations are scheduled to begin in the first quarter of 2027.

The second is in Suriname. TotalEnergies EP Suriname will use the rig for a campaign of five wells, with an option for five more. The work follows the Egyptian campaign, though no start date has been given.

Saipem has not split the $181m between the two, and reports of the awards give no duration in days or months. That limits what can be said about day rates. Without a term and a separate value for each contract, the figures cannot be converted into a daily price for the rig, which is the number that would say most about pricing in the ultra-deepwater market.

What the rig is

Scarabeo 9 is an ultra-deepwater unit with dynamic positioning (DP3) and a dual RamRig hoisting system, designed to work in water up to 12,000 feet deep. Saipem's stated reading of the awards is that they give "greater visibility" on its utilisation across 2027 and 2028. Rigs earn nothing between contracts, so a sequenced run of campaigns on two continents is the outcome a contractor wants.

The awards also follow an earlier batch. On 27 October Saipem announced two other drilling contracts worth about $121m, one of them using Scarabeo 9 for Eni offshore Ghana, the other using the drillship Saipem 10000 for Eni offshore Cyprus. The Scarabeo 9 appears to have been kept busy in recent months.

What it says about demand

The client list is the informative part. A Shell-Petronas-EGPC venture and TotalEnergies are well-funded operators, and both are committing to multi-well programmes rather than single wells. The Suriname option for five further wells adds upside that is not in the $181m figure, if exercised. Egypt's Mediterranean gas and Suriname's deepwater oil are both areas where operators are still drilling.

That is as far as the evidence goes. One rig and two contracts do not show a trend in offshore demand, and the absence of disclosed day rates means the pricing question stays open. For Saipem investors the practical point is coverage: one of the fleet's most capable floaters is now largely spoken for.

What to watch

The markers are the start of the Egyptian campaign in the first quarter of 2027, whether TotalEnergies exercises the five-well option in Suriname, and whether Saipem's next quarterly update discloses backlog and utilisation figures that allow a day rate to be inferred.
