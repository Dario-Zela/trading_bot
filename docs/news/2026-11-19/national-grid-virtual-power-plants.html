National Grid uses virtual power plants to ease local grid constraints

In one sentence
National Grid and Eversource say customer batteries, thermostats and EVs are increasingly being used to ease specific local grid constraints in the US Northeast, though pilot results are not yet in.

A correction first: the premise that this concerns Britain does not hold. The Utility Dive report is about National Grid's American business, in Massachusetts and upstate New York, and nothing in it describes a UK deployment. The brief's UK framing, the connection queue, data centres and renewables waiting for wires, comes from the triage and is not found in the source. National Grid also runs a British distribution arm, but no UK virtual power plant (VPP) programme turned up in the reporting behind this piece. What the source does show is a useful case study in how a large network operator is starting to treat customer-owned equipment as a substitute for new wires.

From shaving peaks to fixing streets

A virtual power plant is software that bundles many small assets, such as home batteries, smart thermostats, EV chargers and commercial loads, and dispatches them as one resource. In New England these assets have mostly been used to trim system-wide peak demand through the ConnectedSolutions programme. The change reported by Utility Dive, in a Nov. 18 article by Brian Martucci on a Nov. 13 webinar, is that the same assets are being pointed at particular neighbourhoods.

Steve Caldwell, National Grid's vice-president for energy efficiency and clean transportation, said resources enrolled in ConnectedSolutions and its enhanced version, ConnectedSolutions+, are increasingly used for location-specific distribution constraints. Executives from National Grid and Eversource described this as a "bridge to wires": a stopgap in load pockets where demand is growing faster than the grid can be built out.

The scale is modest. According to the figures cited, National Grid has about 100MW of distributed load in New England, roughly 2% of regional peak, and more than 350MW upstate in New York, roughly 5% of regional peak. Eversource's portfolio comprises about 100,000 thermostats and 25,000 commercial and industrial customers. The article does not say how the percentages were calculated or whether the two are measured on the same basis.

What is being tried

The pilots are where the locational idea becomes concrete. ConnectedSolutions+ pays higher incentives to customers on constrained feeders. Eversource has two pilots announced this year: one covering three Greater Boston substations, aimed at shifting load away from peaks, and one at a southeastern Massachusetts substation, aimed partly at absorbing excess midday solar. National Grid, working with Haven Energy, is offering a BackupBattery+ scheme that would give up to 125 income-qualified households batteries at no cost, in areas where they have clear grid or resilience value.

Location is the whole point. Vinnie Campo, Haven's chief executive, said in an earlier interview with Utility Dive: "The value of a battery to the grid can be fundamentally different even one mile apart." That cuts against the standard VPP pitch of aggregating assets wherever they happen to be.

Caveats from the people doing it

Most of the performance claims come from a vendor. The webinar was sponsored by EnergyHub, a DER management software firm, and its chief revenue officer, Erika Diamond, supplied the headline numbers: about 4GW of flexible capacity under management, around 3,500 load-shifting events this summer, and a dynamic load-shaping pilot of 70 events across 12 utilities with up to 90% consistency over the full event. "Things have really changed because we have reached meaningful scale," she said. The article does not independently verify these figures.

The utilities were more guarded. Eversource's Tilak Subrahmanian said: "We are cautiously optimistic, but a lot of it needs to play out still." He acknowledged that DER alternatives to capital projects are not always cheaper, and that behind-the-meter solutions need a very precise definition of the problem. Eversource also cannot yet reliably tell which customers have air conditioning, and a few unmanaged EVs could overload small pole-top transformers, which managed charging might help defer replacing. Smart-meter rollout should help; he expected a "much better position in a year". The article gives no cost data and no results from the new pilots.

What to watch

The test is whether these pilots show, on named feeders and substations, that dispatched customer equipment can defer a specific upgrade at a lower cost, and whether regulators in Massachusetts and New York accept that evidence. Results from the Greater Boston and southeastern Massachusetts pilots, and the first outcome data from BackupBattery+, would be the first hard evidence. For a British reader, the relevance is the method rather than any local deployment: the connection queue question is partly one of software, but only if the aggregated assets can be shown to deliver where the wires are short.
