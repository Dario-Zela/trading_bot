GSK wins FDA approval for Nuvalent-acquired lung cancer drug Nelkarmi

In one sentence
The FDA has granted accelerated approval to GSK's Nelkarmi for previously treated ALK-positive lung cancer, the second approval from its $10.6bn Nuvalent purchase.

The US Food and Drug Administration has granted accelerated approval to Nelkarmi (neladalkib), a GSK drug for previously treated ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer. The decision, reported on 17 November, came ahead of the agency's 27 November target date. The approval is the second US lung cancer clearance to come out of GSK's $10.6bn purchase of Nuvalent, and not the first, as early aggregator coverage suggested. The first was Jideytro (zidesamtinib) for ROS1-positive disease, in July.

The approval and the acquisition are both confirmed by multiple outlets, GSK's own release and trade press, rather than by the stock-commentary sites in the original tip.[^1][^2]

What was approved

The label covers two groups: patients whose only prior ALK inhibitor was alectinib (Alecensa), and patients who have had at least two ALK inhibitors plus platinum-based chemotherapy. The application had priority review, breakthrough therapy and orphan designations.[^3]

The evidence comes from ALKOVE-1, a phase 1/2 trial. The primary efficacy analysis included 222 previously treated patients. According to Fierce Pharma's reading of the label:[^3]

  • Overall response rate was 49% in the alectinib-pretreated group and 32% in the group with two or more prior ALK drugs and chemotherapy.
  • Among patients with measurable brain metastases, intracranial response rates were 55% and 26% respectively.
  • About half of responses (52% and 51%) lasted at least a year.
  • 5% of patients permanently discontinued treatment, with liver toxicity the leading cause.

The reported adverse reactions include hepatotoxicity, musculoskeletal pain, constipation, altered taste, peripheral neuropathy, oedema and fatigue.[^4] These are single-arm figures from an early-phase trial, so they cannot be compared directly with other ALK drugs tested in different populations. Fierce Pharma's article also appears to conflate the 26% intracranial figure with an overall response rate for the later-line group. The overall figure there is 32%.

The approval is accelerated, meaning continued authorisation may depend on confirmatory trials. GSK is running the phase III ALKAZAR study in patients who have not yet received an ALK inhibitor.[^4]

The deal behind it

GSK announced on 9 June that it would buy Nuvalent, a Boston-based company, for $124 a share in cash, about $10.6bn and a roughly 40% premium. The structure was a tender offer followed by a merger.[^1] Coverage called it GSK's largest deal in more than a decade, and framed it as part of the effort to offset the coming patent expiry on its HIV drug dolutegravir. Two kinase inhibitors, zidesamtinib and neladalkib, were then under FDA review. This article did not independently confirm the exact closing date. Fierce Pharma says only that the acquisition happened "earlier this year".[^3]

Two approvals within five months of the announcement is a fast return for a deal of this size. Whether it is a good return is a separate matter. Consensus forecasts, drawn from Royalty Pharma's late-2025 royalty announcement, put Nelkarmi at about $3.5bn in sales by 2035, against $1.9bn for Jideytro.[^3] Those are projections, and the price of the drug was not stated in the coverage reviewed.

What to watch

The commercial test is the later-line setting, where the label is narrow and competition from existing ALK inhibitors is established. The larger prize is the ALKAZAR trial in untreated patients, whose results would determine whether Nelkarmi can move to first-line use. That is where the forecast sales would have to come from. Confirmatory data will also decide whether the accelerated approval converts to a full one.
