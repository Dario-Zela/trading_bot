AST SpaceMobile falls 8% on new offering filing

In one sentence
A new share filing knocked AST SpaceMobile down about 8%, reviving dilution worries for a pre-revenue satellite builder that has repeatedly funded itself with equity.

AST SpaceMobile, which is building a constellation of satellites meant to connect directly to ordinary mobile phones, fell about 8% after a new share-related filing revived worries about dilution. The company has no meaningful revenue yet, so it pays for its buildout largely by issuing securities, and any new supply of shares tends to be read against that history.

The size of the move is the one figure the triage brief supplied, and the filing itself could not be matched to a published price report. The close and the exact day's trading range should be checked against exchange data before anyone leans on the 8% as more than approximate.

What the filings show

The recent paper trail is a mix of resale registrations and larger financings, and the distinction matters for how much dilution is actually new.

  • A prospectus supplement dated 17 November covers 780,052 Class A shares tied to Ligado Networks, certain of its subsidiaries and One Dot Six, for payments under collaboration and spectrum agreements.
  • A separate supplement covers 10,000,000 Class A shares issued to Firefly Aerospace under an intellectual property licence agreement dated 6 November. One analysis notes that those shares were already issued, so the filing mainly makes them resaleable. That adds tradeable supply without adding to the share count.

Neither is a conventional capital raise. A resale registration does not bring cash into the company; it lets holders sell stock they already own or have been issued. Which of these, if either, drove the day's decline has not been established from the available reporting.

A familiar pattern

The company has issued equity and equity-linked paper repeatedly. It entered an at-the-market programme of up to $500m in May 2025, followed by an $800m programme in October 2025, when the shares closed at $67.76. More recently it has reportedly sold convertible notes due 2036, with accounts differing on the coupon (2.00% in one, 2.25% in another) and on a roughly 2m-share direct placement alongside. A separate report put a convertible deal at $1bn, though its dating is unclear.

That context is why the reaction was swift, and why the story is a brief rather than a feature. Dilution is the recurring cost of the model, not a surprise.

Where the stock stands

The shares were already under pressure. They fell 8.7% to $33.69 on 10 November after third-quarter revenue of $42.3m missed estimates and the company pushed its 45-satellite target to mid-2027. Bank of America cut its price target to $45 from $80, keeping a Neutral rating. A further 5.5% fall followed on 13 November, and the stock was down 49.4% over twelve months to that date.

A company whose shares have halved in a year pays more, in dilution terms, for every dollar it raises. That is the arithmetic behind the sensitivity to each filing.

What to watch

The useful tests are the share count and the cash position. The next quarterly report will show how many shares are outstanding and how much the company has drawn from its equity programmes. The satellite launch schedule, now pointed at mid-2027 for the 45-satellite target, sets how long the funding has to last.
