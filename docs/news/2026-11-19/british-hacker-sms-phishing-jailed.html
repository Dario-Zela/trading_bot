British hacker jailed for six years in the US over $11m SMS phishing scheme

In one sentence
A Dundee man in the Scattered Spider group got six years in a US prison for a text-phishing and SIM-swap scheme that stole at least $11.6m in cryptocurrency.

A British man who helped run a text-message phishing operation has been sentenced to six years in a US federal prison. Tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, from Dundee, was the last of five defendants to be sentenced in a case that prosecutors say took at least $11.6m in cryptocurrency from people across the United States. He was sentenced on 17 November, according to reports, in federal court in Santa Ana, California.

What the scheme did

The case is linked to the group known as Scattered Spider. Between September 2021 and April 2023, prosecutors say, the conspirators sent hundreds of phishing texts to staff at telecoms, technology, gaming, cloud-communications and cryptocurrency firms. The messages sent recipients to fake login pages. Credentials typed into those pages were posted to a Telegram channel that Buchanan ran with an accomplice.

With those logins the group got into employee accounts and internal company systems. Those systems held the information needed to find individual customers and their crypto accounts. To get past two-factor codes sent by text, the conspirators hijacked victims' phone numbers through SIM swaps, in which a mobile carrier is tricked or bribed into moving a number to a SIM card the attacker controls.

The point is that the main target was not the end victim. Staff at companies that hold customer data were phished, and the customers' own accounts were reached through them.

The sentence

Buchanan was arrested in Spain in May 2024 and has been in US custody since April 2025. He pleaded guilty in April 2026 to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft. The loss figure has risen since the plea, when the cited amount was $8m; most outlets now report $11.6m, which is the amount the brief's "$11m" rounds down. A restitution hearing is set for 11 December.

The other four defendants have already been dealt with, according to SecurityWeek:

  • Noah Michael Urban: 10 years
  • Ahmed Hossam Eldin Elbadawy (Texas): 45 months
  • Evans Onyeaka Osiebo (Texas): 45 months
  • Joel Martin Evans: 2 years

Buchanan's six years sits in the middle of that range. The sentences are not directly comparable, since roles, plea timing and cooperation differ, and the reports available do not explain how the court weighed them.

Why a US court

Buchanan is a UK national who was caught in Spain, yet he was tried in California because the victims and the stolen funds were American. The case shows that a defendant's location does not put them beyond reach. Separately, two British defendants, Thalha Jubair and Owen Flowers, were sentenced in the UK earlier this year over an attack on Transport for London, which indicates that cases in this area are being pursued on both sides of the Atlantic.

For ordinary account holders, the case is a reminder that text-message codes are a weak second factor when phone numbers can be taken over. Authenticator apps and hardware keys do not depend on the phone number and are harder to defeat this way.

What to watch

The next date is the 11 December restitution hearing, which will fix how much Buchanan owes victims. Reports on the case come from several outlets, but the BBC's own account was not independently checked here, and details such as the judge's name were not available.
