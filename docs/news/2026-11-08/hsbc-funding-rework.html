HSBC reworks its funding structure

In one sentence
A reported HSBC funding-structure change is being framed as a valuation debate, but no instrument, amount or terms have been identified in any filing.

A change to HSBC's funding structure has been reported as a prompt for a fresh look at the bank's valuation. The reports do not say what the change is. No instrument, amount, pricing or terms have been identified, and no regulatory filing has been found that describes it.

That gap matters more than the framing around it. "Funding structure" covers a wide range of events. At one end sits routine liability management: a new senior or subordinated issue, a call of an existing note, a refinancing of maturing debt. Large banks do this continually, and it rarely moves anything. At the other end sits a change to capital composition, leverage, or the terms on which shareholders are paid. That would be a different matter, and it would show up in a primary document.

What would make it a story

The test is straightforward and depends on documents, not commentary. A story would exist if a filing or results statement showed one or more of the following:

  • a change in regulatory capital ratios or the mix of capital instruments behind them;
  • a shift in leverage or in the bank's reliance on wholesale funding;
  • altered terms for dividends, buybacks or coupons on additional Tier 1 securities.

None of these has been confirmed. A refinancing that replaces like with like, at similar cost, changes little in the investment case, and a valuation debate built on it would be arguing from an assumption.

The framing problem

The source of the story is an automated-style valuation write-up from Simply Wall St, a retail investing site. Such pieces typically tie a company event to a view on whether the shares are cheap or dear. That is a legitimate exercise, but it presupposes a fundamental change. Here the premise is the part that is missing. Valuation arguments are only as strong as the event they rest on, and this one has not been described.

It is also worth separating the stock-level debate from the sector backdrop. UK bank valuations are moving on analyst target revisions, as the trimming of Barclays shows, and on a broader risk-off tone in global markets. A move in HSBC shares around this time cannot be attributed to a funding change without evidence that the change is material.

What to watch

The primary sources are HSBC's own announcements to the London Stock Exchange and its regulatory news service, its debt issuance disclosures, and the capital and funding sections of its next results statement. If any of those records a change in capital, leverage or payout terms, the item becomes news and will be reported with the figures. Until then, the accurate summary is limited: a funding change is being discussed, and its content is unknown.
