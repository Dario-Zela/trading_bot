Labubu mania fades, testing Pop Mart's growth story

A Labubu figure from Pop Mart's "The Monsters" series, the character at the centre of the company's growth story. — Pop Mart / TTIME Studio (via Wikipedia)
In one sentence
Pop Mart's reported third-quarter revenue fell 45% to 50% as the Labubu craze cooled, testing whether the company can sell blind-box collectibles beyond one hit character.

Pop Mart, the Chinese toy maker that rode a single plush character to a global retail phenomenon, is now reporting what happens when the character stops being scarce. Preliminary figures for the third quarter, reported on 27 October, put group revenue down 45% to 50% on a year earlier, according to Bloomberg-sourced accounts. A year before, the same quarter had grown by roughly 250%.

The character is Labubu, a toothy, pointy-eared figure from "The Monsters" series by the Hong Kong-born artist Kasing Lung. Pop Mart sells it mainly in blind boxes, where buyers do not know which figure is inside. The model works only while buyers keep wanting to open one more box.

The numbers

The reported decline was uneven. The Americas fell 70% to 75%, Asia-Pacific 65% to 70%, and Europe and other regions 50% to 55%. Mainland China fell 25% to 30%, the mildest drop and still a sizeable one. The figures come from a preliminary update, not full results, and the company has not published a detailed explanation. Pop Mart described 2026 as "a year of adjustment".

The slide did not arrive without warning. Domestic online sales fell 5% year on year in May, according to Moojing data on Tmall, Taobao and Douyin cited by the Korea Times — the first annual decline since 2024 and 25% below the monthly average for the second half of 2025. Morningstar's Jeff Zhang said at the time that "the earlier frenzy surrounding Labubu has cooled". Deutsche Bank's Sammi Xu expected "a clearer downcycle in the second half of the year".

The comparison base matters. Revenue in the third quarter of 2025 had roughly tripled and a half, so some payback was inevitable. The size of the fall, though, exceeded the roughly 30% decline that analysts had pencilled in, according to reports of Jefferies' reaction.

Why a hit is hard to replace

Blind-box economics reward scarcity and the chase for a rare variant. When supply expands to meet demand, the resale premium that fed the frenzy tends to shrink. Bloomberg reported in September 2025 that prices of mini Labubu figures on the resale market had already fallen 24% from their peak. That is the pattern to watch: a collectible trading below its launch excitement gives buyers less reason to gamble on new boxes.

Pop Mart has been trying to broaden the line-up. It has promoted other characters such as Twinkle Twinkle and Dimoo, kept opening flagship stores, including in Hangzhou and Paris, and sold film rights for Labubu to Sony Pictures. A "Labubu 4.0" release is expected in the second half of 2026. None of this has yet offset the drop in the core line. Pop Mart does not break out Labubu's share of sales in the sources reviewed here, so how far other characters have filled the gap cannot be measured from public numbers.

Analysts cut forecasts

HSBC trimmed its 2026 revenue estimate to 33.2 billion yuan from 34.6 billion yuan, and cut its 2027 and 2028 forecasts by 8% and 9%, citing a longer path to stabilisation. Jefferies, per reports of its note, lowered 2027 and 2028 earnings estimates by 9% to 12% and projected fourth-quarter revenue about a third below the prior year. Shares were reported to have fallen more than 6% in early Hong Kong trading after the update and to be down more than 30% earlier in 2026, after a rout of about 40% from the 2025 peak.

The read-across extends beyond one company. Licensed-toy makers that lean on a single character face the same question: whether a brand can outlast the viral moment that built it.

What next

The next test is the full third-quarter and annual figures, and in particular whether fourth-quarter sales hold near the 34% decline Jefferies projects. The "Labubu 4.0" launch is the nearest product catalyst. A weak reception would suggest that the character's pull, not just its scarcity, has faded. Investors are already concerned that the slowdown could run into 2027.
