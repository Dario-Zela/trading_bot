Ducommun posts record revenue and flags a new Airbus agreement as Airbus expands at Mirabel

In one sentence
Ducommun says it posted record revenue and signed a new Airbus agreement, but neither the figures nor the deal terms are disclosed in the available sources.

Ducommun, the Californian aerospace supplier, told investors on its third-quarter call that revenue hit a record and that it has signed a new agreement with Airbus. The detail stops there. No contract value, term or programme has been disclosed in the material available, and the claim has not been checked against a Ducommun or Airbus release.

What the call is said to have shown

The account of the call comes from aggregators, not from a company filing or transcript. It describes record revenue but gives no figure, no prior-year comparison and no margin. A record can mean a great deal or very little. A company growing at 2% sets a record every year, and one recovering from a trough sets one only after a sharp rise. Without the number, the word carries no analytical weight.

The Airbus agreement is in the same position. Ducommun makes structural and electronic components for commercial and defence customers, so a new Airbus agreement could be anything from a modest extension to a multi-year work package. The source does not say which. Until Ducommun's earnings release or an Airbus statement fixes the scope, the agreement is best read as a signal that the relationship is active, not as a measure of its size.

The Mirabel backdrop

The second thread is a Simply Wall St valuation note saying that Airbus's expansion at Mirabel, Quebec, is keeping its valuation in focus. Mirabel is the final-assembly site for the A220, the narrowbody Airbus inherited from Bombardier's C Series programme. That background is not from the source and should be confirmed before it is relied on.

The note supplies no capex figure, no capacity target and no timeline for the expansion. It also does not say that the Ducommun agreement is tied to Mirabel. The two items sit together in one valuation write-up, and nothing in the source shows a causal link between them. Treating them as one story would overstate what is known.

What can be said

The narrow reading is the defensible one. A mid-cap supplier reports a record top line and a fresh agreement with its largest customer type, and a separate note says an Airbus site expansion is part of how investors are valuing the planemaker. Each fact is mildly positive for aerostructures demand. Neither is quantified, and both come through aggregators that summarise other people's disclosures.

That is thin ground for a read-through to the wider supply chain. One company's order book does not show whether Airbus's suppliers are collectively seeing stronger demand, and a valuation note is an opinion about price, not evidence of orders.

What to watch

The next data points are primary documents. Ducommun's third-quarter earnings release and its 10-Q should give the revenue figure, the margin and any language about the Airbus agreement's value or duration. An Airbus or Quebec government announcement would be needed to put numbers on Mirabel. Until those appear, the one firm fact is that Ducommun says it has a new Airbus agreement. Its size is unknown.
