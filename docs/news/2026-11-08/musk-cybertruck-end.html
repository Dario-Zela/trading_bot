Musk appears to signal the end of the road for the Cybertruck

In one sentence
Musk said on X that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production in mid-2027 to make room for the Cybercab at its Texas plant, though Tesla has issued no separate statement.

Tesla's chief executive has said the Cybertruck will stop being built in about eight months. The angle in the brief called his comments hedged, but they were not. Elon Musk said in a post on X on Friday that Tesla "will be winding down production of the Cybertruck to make room for more Cybercab production" at its Texas plant. He put the end of production at the "middle of next year", which would be mid-2027.

The announcement came on Musk's personal account, not in a Tesla filing or press release. I could not find a separate company statement. The details below are drawn from UPI's report of the post. Forbes and the BBC also covered it, but I could not open their articles.

What Musk said

A user on X called the news the "end of an era". Musk replied that the Cybertruck was the "best vehicle Tesla has ever made". He added: "True, but the Cybertruck fleet will be around for a long time." The truck's lead engineer, Wes Morrill, wrote that it "lives on through Cybercab". Musk has called the Cybercab, a two-seat vehicle meant for driverless taxi service, crucial to Tesla's future.

Musk did not say whether the Cybertruck would be built anywhere else. He gave no reason beyond factory space. The Cybertruck, the Model Y and the Cybercab are all built at the Gigafactory outside Austin, so the decision reads as a trade-off over a shared site. Musk did not describe it as a verdict on demand.

The sales record

The numbers point the same way, though they are estimates. Tesla does not report sales by model. Cox Automotive estimated about 20,237 US Cybertruck sales in 2025, down 48% from 38,965 in 2024. At the truck's unveiling, Musk predicted sales of 250,000 units. Reporting on the truck has also cited quality problems and recalls. Pricing runs from around $80,000 for the standard models to about $102,000 for the Cyberbeast, according to the BBC's reporting as relayed by other outlets.

That is a large gap between forecast and result. Even the 2024 estimate is far below the 250,000 figure.

What to watch

The open questions are practical. Tesla could confirm a timetable in its next quarterly report or in a regulatory filing. Those documents would also show whether the company takes any charge for the truck's tooling. An end date also leaves open how many Cybertrucks will be built before then, and whether Tesla will keep supporting owners' vehicles in the meantime. Musk's remark that the fleet will be around for a long time suggests it will, but Tesla has not spelled out any support plans.
