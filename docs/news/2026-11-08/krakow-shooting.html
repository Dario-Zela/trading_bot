Briton among six killed in far-right shooting in Krakow

In one sentence
A licensed gun owner killed six people in Kraków's Main Market Square, including a British citizen, and Poland's interior minister says initial analysis points to far-right views.

A gunman opened fire on pedestrians in the Main Market Square of Kraków on Saturday, killing six people and wounding at least 15 more. Polish officials say early analysis of the attacker's views points to the far right. One of the dead is a British citizen. The attacker was shot by police and detained. Details are still moving, and several figures below come from early reporting that officials have not finalised.

What happened

The attack took place on 7 November in the medieval square that is the centre of Kraków's tourist district. According to Polish reports, the attacker drove a black Subaru to the area. He wore military-style clothing and fired at people on the square. Interior minister Marcin Kierwiński said police opened fire about three minutes after the first report. He said the quick response probably prevented a larger toll. Two officers were shot and underwent surgery. Their condition was described as serious.

Of the six dead, four were women and two were men. Police commander Marek Boroń said one identified victim was a British citizen and two others were Polish women. The remaining victims had not been publicly identified at the time of writing. A Portuguese citizen was among the injured, according to Portugal's foreign ministry. The injured count varies by source. The city puts it at 15, while the interior minister cited 16 people in hospital, a figure that includes the two police officers and the attacker. Kraków has declared official mourning from 8 to 11 November.

The attacker and the weapons

The suspect is a Polish citizen born in 2002, reported to be 24, from the Świętokrzyskie region. Officials have denied that he had any military service or links to the armed forces. Reports differ on his age and some other details, with one outlet giving 25.

Three people were also found dead at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski: two women and a man. Preliminary information suggests they may have been close relatives. Officials have not confirmed how or when they died, or whether their deaths are connected to the Kraków attack.

The weapons are likely to become the central policy question. Reporting says the man held a firearms licence and belonged to a shooting club, as his father did. TVP Info reported two guns registered to the attacker and 17 to his father. Earlier accounts gave different numbers, and the figures need official confirmation. Early reports mentioned an automatic weapon, possibly an AR-15-type carbine. Experts have yet to establish what was used or where it came from. Kierwiński has suggested some weapons may have come from the family home, which is also unconfirmed.

'Far right', and what is still open

The far-right label comes from Kierwiński, who said initial analysis points to far-right, ultranationalist views, and that this was still being verified. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the attack an act of terrorism. Prosecutors, not ministers, decide the legal classification, and that depends on establishing intent. Seven prosecutors are working at the scenes, according to justice minister Waldemar Żurek. No source checked for this article has published the evidence behind the ideological claim, such as writings, online activity or contacts. Until that appears, the characterisation rests on official statements alone.

The case also sits within a Polish debate about gun access. Poland has traditionally had comparatively strict licensing. A licensed sport shooter with access to a large family arsenal would put pressure on how storage and household access are regulated. That is an inference from the reported facts, not something officials have yet said.

What to watch

The next markers are the formal charges and whether prosecutors bring terrorism charges. Also due are the identification and repatriation of the British victim, and consular contact between London and Warsaw. Investigators must also establish how the weapons were obtained and what happened at the home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. Any legislative response on firearms storage and licensing would follow from those answers.
