GLOBAL MARKETS · AI SELLOFF · By M. Halloway · 2026-11-08 · 5 min read

AI-led selloff spreads from Wall Street to Asia and Europe after the midterms, wiping trillions off market values

In one sentence
An AI-led selloff around the US midterms hit Wall Street, Tokyo and Seoul hardest, then partly reversed, raising the question of how much global equity depends on one trade.

A selloff that began in artificial-intelligence shares moved from Wall Street to Asia and Europe within days of the US midterm elections. The week's second half then produced a partial recovery. The pattern is the useful part. A market that fell together on one theme, and bounced unevenly, is a market whose rally leaned heavily on a single trade.

Headline reports put the damage in the trillions of dollars of market value. This desk has not been able to reconcile that total against exchange data, so the figure is best read as an order of magnitude. The index moves below come from published market reports.

From election day to Asia

The first signs came on election day, 3 November. A Reuters-sourced market report described a fresh bout of AI anxiety pressuring global stocks as voting began. London, Paris and Frankfurt all closed lower.

The heaviest damage came in Asia on Wednesday 4 November, after Nvidia and other AI-linked names dragged US indexes down. Japan and South Korea carry large weightings in chipmakers and electronics suppliers to the AI build-out, and both fell hard.

According to Upstox's market coverage, the Nikkei 225 fell as much as 4.8% intraday, a drop of 3,051.76 points to 60,030.10 from a previous close of 63,081.86. South Korea's KOSPI fell as much as 4%, to 5,421.72.

The intraday lows overstate the day's outcome. The Nikkei was down 1.42% at 62,188.63 in the latest reading cited. The KOSPI had turned positive, up 0.62% at 5,683.80. Hong Kong's Hang Seng was up 1.5% at 23,887.73 and the Shanghai Composite up 2.16% at 3,686.17.

So the panic was concentrated in the markets most exposed to AI hardware. China's mainland and Hong Kong benchmarks, with a different mix of stocks, rose.

Wall Street and the question of cause

ITV News reported on 5 November that the S&P 500 had tumbled by more than 3.5% in what it called a wipeout for AI stocks. A fall of that size in a broad index is rare. For it to come from one theme says something about how concentrated US equity returns have become. A handful of AI-exposed companies make up a large share of the index's value, so a repricing of that group moves the whole.

Why the midterms? The coverage offers two explanations, and they are not the same.

The first is political. Upstox reported that vote counts favoured the Democrats, who were on course to take control of Congress for the rest of President Donald Trump's term. It said markets feared tighter regulation and scrutiny of technology and a possible crackdown on AI spending. An AP report carried by WPXI similarly said tech shares had taken a hit as investors weighed what a Democratic-led Congress from January could mean for the AI boom.

The second is that the midterms were a coincidence of timing. The AI anxiety was already weighing on markets on election day, before results were known. Reuters framed it as two worries running in parallel, AI angst and the vote, not one causing the other.

The sequence supports the second reading at least in part. The selloff was under way before the votes were counted. The results may have given it a political explanation and a push. No source reviewed here ties a specific policy proposal to the move, and the regulation fear is an investor expectation, not a legislative fact. Congress cannot pass anything before January.

Safe havens and the backdrop

The moves outside equities were consistent with a risk-off day. Upstox reported COMEX gold up 2.4% and the US Treasury yield falling to 4.91%. Crude traded between $106.20 and $108.70 a barrel, with geopolitical risk in West Asia still in the price.

That last point matters for the cause. A market with oil above $100 and a Treasury yield near 5% has little cushion for stocks priced for rapid earnings growth. Higher rates reduce the present value of profits expected years away, which is how AI valuations are justified.

Some commentary has also raised the prospect of Federal Reserve rate rises and a possible double top in the S&P 500. None of the sources checked supplies index levels, futures pricing or a confirmed chart pattern, so those claims are not carried here as findings.

The rebound is the test

AI stocks recovered part of their losses. The AP report, whose headline said world shares mostly gained after the elections, also noted that US stocks usually climb after midterms regardless of who wins. The size and timing of the AI rebound could not be confirmed from the available material.

The shape of the recovery is the better guide to what happened than the depth of the fall. If AI shares bounced while the rest of the market stayed flat, the selloff was a position unwinding in a crowded trade. If the recovery was broad, it was a general risk reset in which AI happened to be the most exposed corner. The partial rebound leaves both readings open.

The Asian pattern points to the first. The Nikkei and KOSPI collapsed and then recovered much of the loss within a single session, which looks like leveraged positions being cut and then rebuilt. The Chinese indexes' gains suggest the shock did not travel everywhere.

For investors in global index funds, including pension holders, the lesson is that a US-centred AI trade can set the direction in Tokyo, Seoul and the European capitals as well. The common owners of these stocks, and the common chip supply chain, carry the shock across borders.

What to watch

The nearest markers are the next earnings reports from AI hardware suppliers and their customers' spending plans, and any signal from incoming Democratic committee chairs on AI oversight. The next Federal Reserve meeting will show how much the rates argument matters. A second leg down without a new trigger would suggest the concentration risk is structural. A steady recovery would suggest the week was an air pocket.
