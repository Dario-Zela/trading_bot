Tesla ETFs emerge as a side-bet on SpaceX merger speculation

In one sentence
SpaceX's $75bn IPO has traders using Tesla-heavy ETFs as merger proxies, with Wedbush putting deal odds above 80% and prediction markets at 57%.

SpaceX's record-breaking debut on the Nasdaq twelve days ago didn't just add another ticker to track. For a certain class of ETF investor, it turned Tesla into a proxy trade on a merger that hasn't been announced — and may not be for some time.

SpaceX raised roughly $75 billion at $135 a share on June 12, closing its first day at $161.11, up 19% from the IPO price. Its market capitalization exceeded $2 trillion, nudging past Tesla's $1.5 trillion at the time. The listing also triggered a wave of speculation about whether Elon Musk's two largest companies will eventually combine. On the day of the IPO, SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell told CNBC that a merger "might make Elon's life a little easier, actually" — carefully hedged, but noted everywhere.

Musk's biographer Walter Isaacson added, on a separate CNBC appearance, that a combination is "in the cards." Wedbush analyst Dan Ives puts the probability at greater than 80%. On the prediction market Kalshi, the odds of a Tesla-SpaceX deal by May 2027 sit at 57%, up from 52% in early June. SpaceX's own amended S-1, filed before the IPO, quietly added risk-factor language about issuing equity "in connection with future transactions" — a standard disclosure, but one that lit up M&A-watchers.

The ETF angle

For investors who can't or won't buy SPCX outright, Tesla has become the next-best expression of the merger thesis. David Mazza, chief executive of Roundhill Investments, put it plainly: "What is holding Tesla above $400 is an acquisition premium building in."

The vehicle for that bet, increasingly, is ETFs with heavy Tesla exposure. The Roundhill Magnificent Seven ETF (MAGS) holds each of its seven constituents at equal weight — roughly 14.3% in Tesla. The ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK) runs a ~11% Tesla weighting; the ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF (ARKQ) carries just under 10%. Cathie Wood's ARK Space Exploration & Innovation ETF (ARKX) took an unusual step this month, buying 35,766 Tesla shares — marking the first time the fund has held TSLA at all, a move widely read as a merger-positioning bet.

For traders who want direct leverage, the Direxion Daily TSLA Bull 2X Shares (TSLL) offer 200% daily exposure to Tesla, while Direxion launched LOFF — a 2x leveraged SpaceX ETF — immediately after SPCX began trading. The pair now sit at opposite ends of the same speculation.

The underlying logic is straightforward: if a merger happens, Tesla shareholders receive SpaceX equity; if it doesn't, the merger premium bleeds out of Tesla's valuation over time. Tesla is down around 15% year-to-date even with the supposed premium baked in, suggesting the market is pricing the deal at a discount, not a certainty.

Not everyone is buying the narrative. Oppenheimer analysts argued this week that keeping the companies separate better serves Musk's long-term AI ambitions, writing that his vision "is best served by diversified, flexible access to capital." The valuation arithmetic is also awkward: at IPO, SpaceX commanded a higher market cap than Tesla despite generating far less in the way of profits, meaning any all-stock deal would require Tesla shareholders to accept dilution at multiples they may find uncomfortable.

The next concrete catalyst is SpaceX's first quarterly earnings report as a public company, expected in late July. Until then, merger odds on prediction markets and any comment from Musk or Shotwell are likely to move both TSLA and the ETFs that hold it.
