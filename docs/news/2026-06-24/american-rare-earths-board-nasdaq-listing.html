American Rare Earths adds veteran mine builder to board ahead of Nasdaq debut

Mountain Pass, the only operating rare earth mine in the United States, in San Bernardino County, California — a benchmark for what domestic rare earth production looks like at scale. — Ken Lund / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
Ur-Energy CEO Matthew Gili joins American Rare Earths' board as the Wyoming rare earth developer advances its Halleck Creek feasibility study and targets a Nasdaq listing in H2 2026.

American Rare Earths has added Matthew Gili — president and chief executive of Wyoming uranium producer Ur-Energy — to its board as a non-executive director, bolstering the team's operational depth as the company pushes Halleck Creek toward a definitive feasibility study and a planned Nasdaq dual-listing in the second half of this year.

Gili is a professional engineer with more than 25 years in mine development and operations. His career spans Rio Tinto and Barrick, a stint as chief executive of Nevada Copper Corporation, and a run as president and chief operating officer of i-80 Gold Corporation before arriving at Ur-Energy, which operates the Lost Creek in-situ recovery uranium facility in south-central Wyoming. He stepped up to CEO at Ur-Energy in December 2025 as part of an internal succession plan, so he arrives at the American Rare Earths board table while still running an active Wyoming mining operation — an unusual degree of current-practitioner credibility for a non-executive seat.

Why the hire fits

The logic is more specific than it looks. The hydrometallurgical disciplines at the core of ISR uranium operations — acid leaching, solvent extraction, ion exchange — overlap closely with the techniques used to extract and separate rare earth oxides from ore. Gili's team at Lost Creek runs those processes at scale, in Wyoming, under Wyoming regulators. That combination of technical familiarity and state-level permitting experience is precisely what a company still two or three years from first production needs on its Technical Committee, where Gili is expected to contribute directly to the DFS workstream.

Halleck Creek sits on state land in Albany and Platte Counties and holds a JORC-compliant resource of 2.63 billion tonnes grading approximately 3,292 parts per million total rare earth oxide (TREO) — roughly 8.65 million tonnes of contained rare earth oxide across the full deposit, which the company describes as the largest known rare earth resource in the United States by that measure. The Cowboy State Mine sub-deposit, the intended first-production zone, sits at 543 million tonnes at 3,438 ppm TREO. Open-pit extraction is planned, with on-site mineral processing and separation facilities to follow.

The company launched its 2026 drill program on May 13, targeting approximately 3,050 metres across 19 core holes at the Cowboy State Mine. Completion is expected by mid-July, with assay results about six weeks after that — putting a meaningful data release in late summer. The US Export-Import Bank has also issued a non-binding letter of interest for up to $456 million in project financing: not a commitment, but a signal of federal appetite for domestically sourced critical minerals.

Board prep, not a pivot

The appointment is best read as IPO plumbing. American Rare Earths has been assembling the building blocks for a Nasdaq compliance dual-listing — it appointed BDO as auditor earlier this year to satisfy US listing requirements, and has been building out its Wyoming management bench with project-delivery hires since April. The company intends to retain the ASX as its primary listing.

None of that changes the fundamental schedule. First production is not expected before 2029 at the earliest, and the DFS has yet to establish the cost, throughput, and capital parameters that investors will need to underwrite the equity story. The real test comes with the drill assays due in late July: if results push a material portion of the Cowboy State resource from inferred into measured and indicated, the project gains the resource classification that a bankable feasibility study and a credible Nasdaq prospectus both require.
