Trump administration eyes White House aide for top FDA post

The FDA's White Oak Campus in Silver Spring, Maryland, where the commissioner's office is housed — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Heidi Overton, a physician-scientist on the White House Domestic Policy Council, has emerged as a leading pick to lead an FDA that has lacked a permanent commissioner since May.

More than six weeks after the Food and Drug Administration lost its permanent leader, the Trump administration is zeroing in on a candidate from its own house: Heidi Overton, Deputy Assistant to the President for Domestic Policy, has emerged as a leading contender for FDA commissioner, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg Law.

The White House, predictably, swatted the report away. Spokesman Kush Desai offered the now-standard disclaimer: "Unless officially announced by the White House, any reporting about personnel nominations should be considered baseless hearsay." That formulation has become something of a tell in Trump personnel cycles — denial as punctuation, not refutation.

Who is Heidi Overton?

Overton is a physician-scientist with an unusually complete paper trail for a policy aide. She graduated summa cum laude from the University of New Mexico's combined BA/MD programme, completed a general surgery residency at Johns Hopkins, and then earned a doctorate in clinical investigation from Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. She holds board certification in public health and preventive medicine.

Her connection to the FDA world predates this appointment cycle. In 2017, she co-authored a BMJ editorial on opioid overprescribing with none other than Marty Makary — the Johns Hopkins surgeon who became FDA commissioner in early 2025, then resigned under White House pressure in May 2026 after 13 months in the post.¹ That shared intellectual history gives Overton a recognisable policy pedigree, even if it also raises a question: the same administration that forced Makary out is now considering someone who co-authored work with him.

Since the start of Trump's second term, Overton has sat on the White House Domestic Policy Council, where she focuses on health. Before that she served as Chief Policy Officer at the America First Policy Institute, a think tank aligned with the administration.

The path to confirmation is not clear

The agency has been running on an acting basis under Kyle Diamantas, a food-industry lawyer elevated from the deputy commissioner role for food after Makary's exit. Diamantas has won quiet respect from some biotech observers — STAT News called him "no drama" — but a lawyer without a medical background leading an agency that oversees drug approvals has its own risks.

Any permanent nominee faces a Senate confirmation process that has grown complicated. The Senate HELP Committee, which vets FDA picks, is chaired by Louisiana Republican Bill Cassidy, who lost his primary in May in a contest coloured by friction with Trump. It remains unclear how Cassidy will handle an administration nominee clearing his committee. The dynamic has already prompted concern among health policy hands that the seat could stay vacant longer than the administration intends.

There is also the question of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. Bloomberg's reporting noted it is unclear whether the HHS secretary backs Overton — and his imprimatur matters, since FDA sits within HHS.

For the pharmaceutical sector, the prolonged vacancy has translated into uncertainty around drug and device approval timelines. Overton's precise views on accelerated approvals, vaccine policy, and over-the-counter drug expansion have not been publicly tested at scale.

The next visible milestone: a formal White House announcement, or the emergence of a rival name that shifts the calculus. Until then, the agency's permanent leadership remains in limbo.

¹ Makary, M.A., Overton, H.N., Wang, P. "Overprescribing is major contributor to opioid crisis." BMJ 2017;359:j4792.
