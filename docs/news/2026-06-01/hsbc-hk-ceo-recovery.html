HSBC CEO takes personal charge of Hong Kong investment banking recovery

HSBC's headquarters on Hong Kong's Statue Square — a building long synonymous with the bank's dominance in the city's financial markets. — Deror avi / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
HSBC CEO Georges Elhedery is personally pitching Hong Kong clients as the bank has slipped from 5th to 34th in Asia IPO rankings, hiring a dozen+ bankers to reverse a post-restructuring slide.

HSBC Holdings Plc has a simple problem in its own backyard: it used to matter in Hong Kong investment banking, and now it doesn't — or not nearly as much. Chief executive Georges Elhedery is personally trying to fix that.

Elhedery has been pitching directly to key clients in Hong Kong, sending personalised video messages to roughly 400 major customers — buyout firms, hedge funds — across Greater China. It is the kind of outreach that normally flows through coverage bankers, not from the group's top office. That it doesn't this time is itself a data point.

The numbers explain why. HSBC ranked 34th for Asia ex-Japan equity capital markets activity so far in 2026, capturing 0.8% of deal value. A decade ago the bank ranked fifth. Morgan Stanley and China International Capital Corp now sit at the top of the Hong Kong league tables. HSBC was kept off the lead-left role on the A.S. Watson Group's dual listing in London and Hong Kong — a deal expected to exceed $2 billion — which went instead to Goldman Sachs and UBS.

The franchise did not unravel overnight. A sweeping restructuring in early 2025 drained the Hong Kong team: notable departures included a head of US and European commercial banking, a Middle East coverage chief, and a real estate specialist who moved to Standard Chartered. Rebuilding from that base, in a market that is simultaneously booming, made the task harder still.

Hiring and pipeline

Elhedery's response is two-track. HSBC has hired more than a dozen investment bankers for its China business over the past year, drawing from JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Bank of America and Jefferies. Recruits include Karen Chen in consumer banking from JPMorgan, Yuan Shuai in equity capital markets from Goldman, and Randy Wang in technology and media from Jefferies, with further additions still joining through the second half of 2026.

The pipeline has already shifted. HSBC is actively working on around 40 Hong Kong IPO mandates — up from just five across all of 2025 — and holds roughly 70 mandates across Asia in aggregate. The bank has also said it intends to increase investment in Hong Kong and other high-growth Asian markets to $1.8 billion.

Hong Kong's IPO market is providing a useful backdrop: the city is on course to raise more than $43 billion in 2026, driven by technology and biotech listings and Beijing's relaxed rules for high-growth sectors. Whether HSBC harvests a proportionate share — or watches Goldman, Morgan Stanley and CICC continue to dominate — is the test the next 12 months will settle.

The bank's Hong Kong operations generated $9.6 billion in pre-tax profit in 2025, nearly a third of group earnings. That scale makes the investment banking slide a reputational problem as much as a financial one. The pipeline is rebuilt on paper; the league-table recovery will only register when the mandates close.
