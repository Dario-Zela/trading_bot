BT Group's earnings quality improves but the market is not listening

BT Group plc, listed on the London Stock Exchange as BT.A, reported full-year results for the period ended 31 March 2026. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
BT Group met FY2026 guidance on every key metric and guides FCF doubling to £2bn next year — but a £488m impairment and broadband churn pushed the stock 3% lower.

BT Group posted its full-year results on 21 May 2026 and the numbers, read carefully, were better than they looked. The market read them carelessly. By the close of trading that day, shares had slipped toward 222p; by 31 May they were trading around 209p — a roughly 3–4% drift lower from their pre-results level. For a company that met every line of guidance and raised its dividend, that is a curious response.

The headline figures were unambiguous on EBITDA and cash flow. Adjusted group revenue fell 4% year-on-year to £19.65bn — partly the result of equipment-sale mix and completed international divestments — but adjusted EBITDA came in at £8.23bn, up 1% on a like-for-like basis and squarely within the guided range. Normalised free cash flow hit £1.508bn, on target. Profit before tax rose 8% to £1.43bn; profit after tax, up 2%, landed at £1.077bn. The dividend was lifted 2% to 8.32p per share, with management guiding to low-to-mid single-digit annual increases until BT achieves the BBB+ credit metrics it has been targeting.

What the statutory number conceals

The case for better-than-it-looks earnings quality rests on a £488m unusual items charge that sits above the profit line. The bulk of this was a goodwill impairment allocated to the struggling Business division — a non-cash write-down reflecting years of margin pressure in that unit, not a cash outflow. Simply Wall St, in analysis published this weekend, flagged the charge explicitly: unusual items of this type, it noted, are "often not repeated," meaning BT's statutory earnings understate its forward earnings potential. If the impairment does not recur in FY2027 — and impairments by definition do not — the same operational performance produces a materially cleaner profit number.

The operational picture at Openreach reinforces that read. The fibre build passed 23 million premises in the year and added 2.2 million customer connections, reaching a 39% take-up rate. BT's transformation programme, now extended through FY2030, delivered £1.5bn of cumulative savings in two years against a target of £3.7bn gross savings. Consumer reported its first net customer growth in eight years.

None of this moved the stock. The market anchored instead on the negatives: 825,000 Openreach broadband customer losses for the year (and management's guidance for another 800,000 losses in FY2027), a revenue line still declining, and UBS holding a Sell rating as recently as 18 May. Fair concerns, all of them — but they were already in the price.

The FCF trajectory is the real argument

The reason the disconnect matters beyond one quarter is BT's free cash flow guide. Management targets £2bn normalised FCF in FY2027, rising to £3bn by the end of the decade. From £1.508bn, that is a near-doubling in roughly four years — a trajectory that, at BT's current market capitalisation, would push the free cash flow yield into territory that typically draws re-rating. That guide has been on the table for some time, and the FY2026 print, which met the first waypoint, does nothing to undermine it.

The question for the rest of the sector is whether BT is an isolated case or a pattern. Telecom names across Europe have been slow to receive credit for capital-cycle payoffs on fibre build programmes — the CapEx is visible and immediate; the FCF payoff arrives later and requires investors to believe the take-up curve. BT's 39% fibre take-up at Openreach is among the higher rates for an incumbent and suggests the demand side is holding. If the broader market continues to weight near-term revenue softness over structural FCF inflection, quality telecom names may remain cheaper than the medium-term numbers justify.

The next concrete data point is BT's Q1 FY2027 trading update, likely in July, which will give the first read on whether broadband churn is stabilising at the guided level. That number, more than anything else, is what the bear case is watching.
