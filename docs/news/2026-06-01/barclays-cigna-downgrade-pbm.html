Barclays turns cautious on Cigna, flagging risks from pharmacy benefit manager transition

Cigna's Connecticut General Life Insurance Company headquarters in Bloomfield, Connecticut — the company's sprawling modernist campus designed by Gordon Bunshaft and completed in 1957. — Sphilbrick / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Barclays and Deutsche Bank both downgraded Cigna within six days, each flagging its Express Scripts PBM transition as a source of multi-year earnings uncertainty.

On May 26, Barclays moved The Cigna Group (NYSE: CI) from Overweight to Equal Weight, cutting its price target to $304 from $310. In isolation, the call might be filed under routine analyst housekeeping. Placed alongside Deutsche Bank's downgrade six days earlier — Buy to Hold, price target trimmed to $302 from $303, analyst George Hill citing "multi-year uncertainty" — and a theme starts to emerge: Wall Street is increasingly wary of what lies beneath Cigna's otherwise solid headline numbers.

Two banks, one pressure point

The focal anxiety for both firms is Express Scripts, Cigna's pharmacy benefit management arm and the revenue engine of its Evernorth Health Services division. Cigna is midway through a structural reset — moving to a rebate-free pricing model — that is designed to get ahead of a regulatory environment growing hostile to the opaque rebate arrangements that historically drove PBM margins. The direction of travel is defensible; the questions are about execution and timing.

Barclays layered in a second concern: Cigna's limited exposure to government insurance programs — Medicare Advantage, Medicaid — leaves it with fewer levers for earnings upside compared to peers that have built out those segments more aggressively. Add concerns about how quickly elevated investment spending might ease, potential commercial membership attrition, and the possibility of AI-driven cost restructuring across managed care, and the risk-reward calculus around a September investor day starts to look asymmetric.

Neither downgrade came on bad news. Cigna's first-quarter results showed adjusted profit up 16% year-over-year. The company nudged full-year 2026 EPS guidance to at least $30.35 from $30.25 — marginally ahead of the analyst consensus of $30.33. The point being made is not that the business is struggling; it is that the path from here is harder to read than the current numbers suggest.

Not everyone agrees. UBS went the other direction on May 22 — between the two downgrades — raising its price target from $375 to $400 and holding its Buy rating, citing the Q1 performance and growing confidence in margin recovery. The average analyst rating on CI is still Buy, according to FactSet data, with 13 upward earnings revisions for the upcoming period.

There is one contextual factor that tends to get underweighted in analyst notes: in late March, David Cordani — who had run Cigna for more than 15 years — announced his retirement, with CFO Brian Evanko named as his successor. A long-tenured CEO departing mid-restructuring does not necessarily signal trouble, but it concentrates execution risk in a team that has not yet had to answer for the model it is inheriting in front of investors.

The September investor day is where Evanko will need to make the case — laying out the revised PBM economics, updating commercial membership trajectories, and offering a clearer timeline for when the transition costs begin to clear. Until then, the PBM transition remains an open variable sitting inside a stock that the market has already repriced more than once this year. Whether two bank downgrades in six days signals the beginning of a consensus shift or just coincident caution is a question September will help settle.
