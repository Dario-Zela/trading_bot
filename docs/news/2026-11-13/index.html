WORLD · UK JUSTICE · By C. Tanaka

Some childhood offenders may no longer have to reveal convictions to employers

The government plans to reform disclosure rules for childhood convictions, which would mean fewer people with such records having to reveal them to employers.

Under the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974, a conviction for an offence committed under 18 becomes spent in half the time that applies to an adult conviction for the same sentence. In 2019 the Supreme Court, in the P and Gallagher cases, held that blanket, automatic disclosure of old and minor convictions was disproportionate and incompatible with Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

The full article sets out the plan's scope, timetable and the check types it covers, and will show how far it goes beyond the existing spent-time rules.
