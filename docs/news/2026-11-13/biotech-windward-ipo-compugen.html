Biotech deals: Windward prices $175M IPO; Compugen revenue doubles on partner payments

In one sentence
Windward Bio's roughly $175M Nasdaq IPO, built on China-sourced respiratory drugs, and Compugen's doubled revenue both show licensing deals driving biotech finance.

A Swiss-based respiratory biotech built on drugs licensed from Chinese developers was set to list on Nasdaq on Friday, in a deal sized at about $175 million. The same day, a small Israeli drug developer, Compugen, reported that its quarterly revenue had roughly doubled on payments tied to its partners Gilead and AstraZeneca. The two stories are not the same, but both show how much of the sector's money now moves through licensing.

Windward's offering

Windward Bio, based in Basel, set terms on 9 November for an IPO of 10.3 million shares at $16–$18 each (Renaissance Capital). At the $17 midpoint that comes to roughly $175 million. The shares are expected to trade under the ticker WWB. Jefferies, TD Securities, Piper Sandler and UBS are the joint bookrunners.

A final price had not been confirmed at the time of writing. The figures here are therefore the terms as set, not the outcome.

Sanofi has agreed to a separate $50 million private placement of shares and pre-funded warrants, which sits outside the headline figure. Fierce Biotech describes the company as Sanofi-backed and its pipeline as respiratory assets sourced from China. Windward reported $207.3 million in cash and marketable securities at 30 June 2026, no product revenue and a net loss of $68.2 million over the preceding twelve months, according to the same coverage. The specific licensors and drug candidates were not confirmed in the sources reviewed.

The reading is cautious. One listing of this size shows that US public investors will still fund a company whose core assets come from China. It does not show how many would, or at what price. An established pharma partner in the syndicate of backers is also a form of reassurance that a pure-play China-sourced story would lack.

Compugen's doubling

Compugen's third-quarter revenue was $3.8 million, about 102% higher than a year earlier, and its net loss narrowed to $5.8 million from $7.0 million (Panabee). Analysts had expected revenue of about $2.7 million, according to Zacks.

The source of the jump matters more than its size. Reports say the revenue reflects accounting recognition of part of Gilead's upfront payment and an IND milestone, not new cash arriving in the quarter. Compugen has received gross payments of $90 million from Gilead and is eligible for up to $758 million more in milestones. From AstraZeneca it can receive up to $195 million in regulatory and commercial milestones, plus tiered royalties of up to the mid-single digits. Those larger sums depend on clinical success. Third-party summaries differ on the accounting detail, and the company's own release should be treated as the authority.

Management says its cash runway extends into 2029. That is the more durable fact in the report: the revenue line will move as deferred amounts are recognised, but the cash position is what lets a small company wait for trial data.

What links them

Both companies live on assets that someone else paid for, or will pay for, at an earlier stage. Windward's came from Chinese developers. Compugen's come from its own labs, with large pharma funding the next stage. In each case the headline number says little until the terms are read.

What to watch

First, Windward's final pricing and first-day trading, which will show whether the range held. Second, Compugen's MAIA-ovarian trial results, expected by the end of the first quarter of 2027, followed by AstraZeneca's Phase 3 rilvegostomig biliary readout in the second half of 2027.
