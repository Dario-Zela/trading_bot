Siemens posts record orders and accelerating growth in Q4

In one sentence
Siemens' fourth-quarter orders rose 58% to a record €34.8bn, but big rail contracts and data centres flatter the read-through to broader European industrial capex.

Siemens said on 12 November that orders in the July–September quarter, the fourth of its fiscal year, rose 58% to €34.8 billion. That is a record for a single quarter. Revenue grew 11%, so the company booked about €1.47 of new business for every euro it billed. For a group that sells factory software, building systems and trains, that ratio says demand is running well ahead of what it can deliver. Whether it also describes European industry at large is a harder question.

The numbers

For the full fiscal year, orders reached €108.2 billion and revenue €83.4 billion, a book-to-bill of 1.30. Comparable revenue growth was 8%, at the upper end of guidance. In the fourth quarter alone, revenue was €23.7 billion, up 11% (10% comparable). Profit from the industrial business hit a record €3.9 billion, a 16.8% margin, and free cash flow was a record €5.6 billion. For the year, free cash flow was €12.1 billion and net income €9.5 billion. The year-earlier figure was €10.4 billion, but it included a €2.1 billion gain on the sale of Innomotics.

The quarter's headline is flattered by lumpy contracts. According to the company's presentation as reported by Investing.com, Mobility booked a record €10.7 billion, mostly large rolling-stock deals. Those are multi-year orders that say little about the near-term industrial cycle. The release itself says growth was led by Mobility and Smart Infrastructure.

Smart Infrastructure, the unit that sells electrification, grid equipment and building technology, is the more informative line. Investing.com reports its orders at a record €8.7 billion, up 39%, with data-centre contracts a significant driver. That follows a third quarter in which the unit's orders rose about 40% to €8.0 billion. Two consecutive quarters at that level suggest a sustained build-out, not a one-off. It is also an AI-infrastructure story as much as a general capex story. Data centres need switchgear, power distribution and cooling controls, and they are being built on a schedule set by hyperscalers rather than by the European industrial cycle.

Automation: solid, not surging

The read-through to factory investment is more mixed. GuruFocus's summary of the call puts Digital Industries orders up 15%, with automation up 16%. These are good numbers after a long soft patch. But the same coverage notes that automation order growth softened in September, partly because customers had pulled forward purchases ahead of price increases. Management had also flagged in August that Digital Industries orders would be roughly flat on the prior year in the fourth quarter, because of tough comparisons with exceptionally large software bookings. A 15% gain beat that cautious signal, but the cleaner reading is recovery, not acceleration.

Siemens' own guidance is consistent with that reading. For fiscal 2027 it expects Digital Industries comparable revenue growth of 8–12% at a 20–22% margin. Smart Infrastructure is guided higher, at 11–13% growth and a 19.5–20.5% margin. Mobility is guided at 6–8%. At group level, the company expects comparable revenue growth of 9–11% and a book-to-bill above 1. Earnings per share before purchase-price allocation are guided at €10.20–10.80. That compares with a restated €9.27 for fiscal 2026, which excludes Siemens Healthineers.

CEO Roland Busch called fiscal 2026 "an outstanding year for Siemens" and described industrial AI as an exceptional growth opportunity. The company also raised its medium-term comparable growth target to 7–11%, from 6–9%, according to GuruFocus.

What it does and does not show

One company's order book is a weak proxy for a continent. Siemens sells globally, and its infrastructure orders are tilted towards data centres and large public contracts rather than the mid-sized European manufacturers whose capex budgets drive the cycle. The record also arrives with a pull-forward effect, and a base that was depressed a year earlier: group orders in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 were €21.9 billion, down 4%. A 58% rise from a weak base is not the same as a 58% rise in underlying demand.

What the figures do support is narrower but still useful. Grid and building-infrastructure spending is strong and being booked far ahead. Automation demand has turned up from its trough. And a company guiding to a book-to-bill above 1 for the coming year is not signalling that it expects the order wave to break soon.

Siemens has not published the underlying inputs for everything in the coverage. The release provides no segment order figures for Digital Industries or Smart Infrastructure and no group backlog number, so those figures rest on the company's presentation and call as reported by secondary outlets. Third-party summaries also differ on some fiscal 2027 growth ranges, and the release figures are used here.

Next

The dividend proposal of €5.50 per share, up from €5.35, goes to shareholders at the annual meeting. More structurally, Siemens plans to distribute its 34.1% Healthineers stake to shareholders during fiscal 2027, at one Healthineers share for every two Siemens shares. That will remove the unit from the group accounts and complicate year-on-year comparisons. The first quarterly test of the new guidance comes with first-quarter results in early 2027, when the comparison base will be less forgiving and the September automation softness will show whether it was timing or trend.
