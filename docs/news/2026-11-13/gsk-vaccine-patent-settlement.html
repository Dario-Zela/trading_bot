GSK settles key vaccine patent case and wins broad European injunction

In one sentence
GSK is reported to have settled a vaccine patent case and won a broad European injunction, but the counterparty and terms are unnamed and the injunction could not be independently confirmed.

GSK has settled a vaccine patent case and, according to a summary circulated by Simply Wall St, obtained a broad injunction in Europe. That is the substance of the headline, and it is also close to all of it. The summary names neither the counterparty nor the product, and independent searches of court and company announcements did not turn up a European injunction in a GSK vaccine case. The injunction should be read as reported, not confirmed.

What can be verified

The nearest documented event is GSK's settlement with Moderna. Moderna said it resolved GSK's patent litigation over its COVID-19 and RSV shots in the US and Europe on 28 October. The two companies granted each other cross-licences in infectious disease, with no payments due. They also asked a Delaware federal court to dismiss GSK's US infringement claims with prejudice. A settlement with cross-licences is not an injunction, and the sources do not say whether it is the case the summary refers to.

GSK has a record of resolving these disputes. In April 2025 it and Pfizer dismissed the US case over Pfizer's RSV vaccine Abrysvo, which GSK had alleged infringed patents covering its rival Arexvy. No financial terms were disclosed. In that dispute a London court had earlier invalidated related British patents held by GSK, which shows that European courts do not always rule GSK's way. GSK's separate US suit against Pfizer and BioNTech over their COVID-19 vaccine, Comirnaty, is still running.

Why the overhang matters

Patent litigation weighs on a drugmaker's shares in two ways: the possible cost of damages or royalties, and the risk that a product is blocked or its value cut. Each settled case narrows both. The case for relief is plausible, but it cannot be sized from what has been published. There is no revenue exposure, no royalty rate and no damages figure to measure it against. An injunction, if confirmed, would matter more to competitors than to GSK. It would show how willing European courts are to restrain a rival's product on a vaccine patent, a question the London invalidation ruling answered the other way last time.

The Alamar item

The Alamar announcement is separate and smaller. On 3 November, Alamar Biosciences and GSK announced a scientific collaboration to run a proteomic study on Alamar's NULISA platform. It will cover more than 60,000 serum samples across respiratory, liver, kidney, neurological and autoimmune conditions. The aim is to find protein signatures that distinguish biological subtypes of disease and so inform patient selection and trial design. No financial terms were given. It is a research tool deal, not a revenue event, and it has no bearing on the litigation.

What to watch

The first thing to look for is a primary document: a GSK regulatory announcement or court order that names the counterparty, the product and the territories covered. The second is the US COVID-19 case against Pfizer and BioNTech, which remains the largest open item in GSK's vaccine disputes. Until a filing sets out terms, the overhang has been described as removed but not measured.
