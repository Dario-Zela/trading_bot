Unilever faces a valuation test; Starbucks CEO plays down Chipotle merger talk

In one sentence
Unilever's shares are being weighed against its management overhaul, while Starbucks has said it is not buying Chipotle and is focused on its own turnaround.

Two consumer-sector stories sit side by side this week, and neither comes with fresh numbers. Unilever's shares are being assessed after a management shake-up. Starbucks is still fielding questions about a Chipotle deal it ruled out a month ago.

Unilever: a reshuffle and a modest verdict

Unilever has been under new leadership since Fernando Fernandez, previously chief financial officer, succeeded Hein Schumacher as chief executive on 1 March 2025. The shares fell 68p to 4,415p on the day the change was announced, according to contemporaneous reports.

The latest prompt for discussion is a valuation explainer from Simply Wall St, which asks whether the shares are "priced right" after management changes. That is an analysis site, not a company filing, and the piece should be read as commentary. By its account, the chief executive plans to replace around a quarter of the company's top 200 managers. It also rates the stock undervalued on two of six valuation checks, and puts the share-price move over the past twelve months at a decline of 3.0%.

The piece does not identify a new trigger. No guidance change, results date or strategy announcement is cited, and none could be confirmed independently. The substance is a familiar investor question. A management overhaul is meant to lift margins and growth, and the shares have to be judged on what is already priced in. On Simply Wall St's own scorecard, the answer is "partly".

Starbucks: the denial stands

The Chipotle story has a firmer paper trail. The Financial Times reported on 8 October that Starbucks had worked with advisers in recent months on a possible proposal to acquire Chipotle, citing people familiar with the matter. Chipotle shares rose about 6% that day, while Starbucks fell about 3%.

On 12 October a Starbucks spokesperson said the company does not comment on rumours as a matter of policy but wanted to be clear: "we are not buying Chipotle." The company added that it is "laser-focused on executing our 'Back to Starbucks' strategy." The denial came from a spokesperson. This reporting did not find a direct quote from the chief executive, Brian Niccol, nor any reporting after 12 October.

The link was easy to draw. Niccol ran Chipotle for six years before joining Starbucks in 2024. Analysts were sceptical, though. One said that despite the plausibility, a transaction of this size would likely distract from the turnaround. Outlets put Chipotle's value at roughly $41 billion to $50 billion, against about $107 billion for Starbucks. The figures differ by source and should be treated as approximate.

What ties them together

Both stories are about how much weight a share price can bear while management is mid-reset. Unilever is being asked to justify its valuation through execution. Starbucks has told investors that its turnaround is the whole plan, and takes a large deal off the table.

For Unilever, the next test is its next trading update, where any change to guidance or progress on the management overhaul would give the valuation debate something firmer than a scorecard. For Starbucks, the test is its next quarterly results, where progress on the turnaround will show whether the denial was the end of the matter.
