Pochettino emerges as Man Utd target as Carrick's position is examined

In one sentence
BBC Sport reports Mauricio Pochettino is open to becoming Manchester United manager, putting Michael Carrick's position under scrutiny, though the detail on what Carrick was told is unverified.

Manchester United are reported to be looking at Mauricio Pochettino as a possible next manager. That leaves Michael Carrick, who is running the club's football side as head coach, in an awkward spot over how secure his job is.

The source is BBC Sport. The Tribune has not been able to read the BBC piece in full, and what follows rests on that report as relayed by other outlets, plus their own reporting. The one claim that matters most is what Carrick was told about his position. It has not been checked against anything on the record.

What is being reported

According to a summary of the BBC's reporting carried by footballtransfers.com, Pochettino is open to succeeding Carrick. That account says Carrick is set to be relieved of his duties after a poor start to the season. It does not say who told Carrick what, or when. That gap is the weak point in the story.

Other outlets have reported along similar lines since around 10 November. ESPN is reported to have named Pochettino, who currently coaches the United States men's national team, as the leading candidate. Some of those reports put United in the lower reaches of the Premier League table, after a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa. The Tribune has not independently confirmed that league position or that score. They should be read as part of a reported picture, not as settled fact.

The same reports mention other names on United's list: Eddie Howe, Kieran McKenna and Julian Nagelsmann. Being named in a list of that kind is not an approach. Pochettino being "open" to the job does not mean he has been offered it, and the reports do not say he has been.

Why Carrick's position is the real story

A club weighing a successor while the incumbent is still in the dugout is an old pattern in English football. It usually ends in one of two ways. Either the club publicly backs the manager and the speculation fades, or the leaks turn out to have been preparation. Which of those applies here turns on what Carrick was told, and the BBC's wording on that point is what a careful reader would want to see in full.

There is also a practical constraint. Pochettino has a job. A national-team coach would need to be released, or would need to want to leave, and a move would have to be squared with the US federation. Earlier reporting on Pochettino and United has also tangled with other clubs' interest. None of that has been established for this episode.

What to watch

The first marker is a statement from United, or from Carrick himself, on his future. The second is any comment from the US Soccer Federation or Pochettino's camp on talks. Until one of those arrives, the safest reading is a club planning for several outcomes, with a manager whose standing has become a matter of public speculation.
