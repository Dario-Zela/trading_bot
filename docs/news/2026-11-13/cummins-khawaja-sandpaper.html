Cummins calls Khawaja sandpaper claims 'wrong' and 'nonsense' ahead of the Ashes

In one sentence
Australia captain Pat Cummins says Usman Khawaja's claim that the team knew of the 2018 sandpaper plan is '100% wrong' and 'utter nonsense', while Warner backs Khawaja.

Pat Cummins has rejected the central claim of Usman Khawaja's new autobiography: that Australia's players knew about the sandpaper plan before the 2018 Cape Town Test. Speaking to media in Sydney on Friday, the Australia captain called the allegations "100% wrong" and "utter nonsense".

The book, Sabr, is published this week. Khawaja, who retired from Test cricket in January, has said the wider team and staff knew about ball tampering, and that sandpaper had been used in the Test before Cape Town. He also said he had told Cameron Bancroft to be careful with it.

Cummins's response

Cummins's answer turned on a narrow point. If the claim is that the group knew sandpaper was going out on the field, he said, it is wrong. He said he has held that position for eight years and did not know what was happening.

He said he was "genuinely shocked" to read that Khawaja had warned Bancroft, adding that it was not the Khawaja he knew. The tone was more hurt than hostile. "Usman is a friend, but friends aren't casual with each other's lives and reputations," he said, while saying Khawaja was entitled to tell his own story.

Cummins also disputed Khawaja's suggestion that his skin colour and background had kept him from the captaincy. He said he would not take legal action over the book. The pair played 56 Tests together.

Others have weighed in

Cummins is not alone. Mitchell Starc has rejected the claim that the whole team was aware, and Darren Lehmann has pushed back on the "it was all of us" account. David Warner, the other principal figure in the 2018 affair, has backed Khawaja, calling the claims "100% accurate". Cricket Australia says it stands by its original investigation and will consider any relevant information.

No new evidence has been published. What is on the record is a set of conflicting recollections from the people who were there, and Cummins's comments are the most senior denial so far.

What next

The story now runs on the schedule of the cricket calendar. Cummins, Starc and Hazlewood have been rested from this month's white-ball series at home to England, so the first extended questioning of the bowling group may not come until the next Test camp. Cricket Australia's response to the book, and whether it reopens anything, is the thing to watch.
