Kane's place among England's greats, Palmer's commitment and Van Dijk's Liverpool future

In one sentence
England's squad debate turns on Kane's standing among the greats and Palmer's commitment, while Van Dijk's Liverpool contract, running to 2027, remains unresolved.

England's long-running argument about where Harry Kane sits among the country's greats has a new prompt, and so does the question of whether Cole Palmer is fully invested in the national team. Alongside both sits a separate matter on Merseyside: what Virgil van Dijk does next at Liverpool. The three threads share a BBC Sport round-up. On the evidence available, that round-up is commentary and quotation rather than a fresh development.

Kane and the pantheon

The case for Kane is easy to state. He is England's record goalscorer and has long since passed the figures that once defined the position. Wayne Rooney has called him the best goalscorer England has ever had, and that view is widely shared.

The case against placing him at the very top is just as familiar. Critics note that he has no major international trophy, and that the very greatest names in the debate are tied to tournament wins. Any specific tally needs care: published totals for his caps and goals differ between sources and depend on the date they were compiled, so they are not quoted here. The BBC piece is the place to check the current figures.

Palmer and the commitment question

The headline says Palmer is "committed to England", according to Rogers. Neither the speaker's role nor the exact words could be confirmed from available material, so the remark should be read as one voice rather than a statement of fact about Palmer's intentions.

The backdrop is better documented. Palmer scored his first England goal, a penalty, in a 3-0 friendly win over Bosnia-Herzegovina in June 2024. Kane praised the Chelsea player afterwards. How far Palmer's availability is a live issue now, whether through fitness, club demands or selection, is not established by anything found. Absent that detail, the story is a squad-selection conversation rather than a controversy.

Van Dijk's decision

The Liverpool captain's contract is the most concrete of the three threads, though it is also the murkiest. Empire of the Kop reported on 10 November that he has less than eight months left on his current deal, which would take it to the end of the 2026-27 season.

Reports conflict. Some outlets have said he has decided not to renew, with a free departure the consequence. Others have reported that he would extend, and BBC Sport was among those cited as noting a growing expectation that he would commit to the club. Those reports are undated or contradictory, and no official Liverpool statement was found. Until the club or the player speaks, the position is that a decision is pending.

What ties it together

Each thread is a question about who is available and for how long. England's attack leans on a record-holder whose legacy is still being argued over. The club game's contract calendar governs how long a captain stays in one place. Palmer, whatever his actual position, is a reminder that selectors have to plan around players' form and clubs' demands.

The next concrete markers are the England squad announcement for the next international window, which will show whether Palmer is named, and any Liverpool announcement on Van Dijk, with his current deal running out in June 2027.
