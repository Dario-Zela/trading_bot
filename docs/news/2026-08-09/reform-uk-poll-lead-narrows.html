Reform UK's poll lead narrows to within margin of error

Nigel Farage's Reform UK has seen its once-commanding poll lead evaporate over the past three weeks. — Laurie Noble / UK Parliament, Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Reform UK's poll lead has narrowed to within the margin of error three weeks after peaking, though it remains the largest single party at 23-28%.

Three weeks ago, Reform UK's lead over Labour was the defining fact of British politics — wide enough that Westminster had stopped debating whether Nigel Farage could become prime minister and started debating when. That gap has all but closed. The latest YouGov voting intention poll, conducted 2–3 August for The Times and Sky News, puts Reform on 23% and Labour on 22% — a one-point margin that sits inside the poll's margin of error. YouGov's own tracker notes it has now been three weeks since Reform last posted a lead that cleared that statistical bar.

Reform remains, on most counts, the largest single party. Its support across pollsters this year has ranged from the low twenties up to 28% at its spring peak, when Labour was mired in the low twenties under Keir Starmer. PollCheck's seven-poll moving average, updated 6 August, has Reform at 23.6% — but now trailing Labour on 25.4%, a reversal that would have seemed unthinkable in May.

What changed

The hinge point was 20 July, when Andy Burnham succeeded Starmer as Labour leader and prime minister after accepting King Charles's invitation to form a government. The switch delivered an immediate polling bounce: within a week, More in Common had Labour ahead 28% to 24%, and YouGov had the two parties level at 22%. Ipsos found Burnham preferred as prime minister over both Farage and Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch, and by late July, 48% of adults said they wanted a Burnham-led Labour government at the next election against 29% for a Farage-led Reform — a far wider gap than the voting-intention numbers alone suggest, implying Reform's support is broader than it is deep.

Reform's own troubles compounded the squeeze. Farage has spent August contesting a Sunday Times report that he received an undeclared £5m personal gift from cryptocurrency billionaire Christopher Harborne, on top of some £25m in party funding from the same donor — a disclosure that triggered Farage's resignation as MP for Clacton and a subsequent byelection. He has called the reporting an "establishment hit job" and is weighing legal action, while the government moves to tighten donation-disclosure rules via the Representation of the People Bill. Pollster Sir John Curtice and elections expert Lord Hayward have both said the cross-poll trend now points to genuine decline rather than noise, prompting a public dispute between Farage and YouGov over the tracker's reliability.

Why traders are watching

A Reform majority was the scenario UK-focused desks had been pricing tail risk against — sweeping deregulation, a harder line on immigration and trade, and open questions over Bank of England independence. A polling race this close cuts that probability meaningfully, which is one reason gilt yields and sterling positioning have become more sensitive to Burnham's own missteps than to Farage's. The next real test of durability comes with September's party conference season and the following YouGov tracker; a fourth or fifth consecutive poll inside the margin of error would confirm this is a trend rather than a blip.
