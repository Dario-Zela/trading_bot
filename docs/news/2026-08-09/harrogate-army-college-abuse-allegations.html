BEYOND THE TAPE · SAFEGUARDING · By L. Bordeaux · 2026-08-09 · 3 min read

Streeting 'sickened' by sex abuse allegations at Army's Harrogate college

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, who said he was 'sickened' by the allegations and pledged to examine culture change at the college. — David Woolfall / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Four women allege rape and sexual assault as teenage recruits at Army Foundation College Harrogate; Defence Secretary Wes Streeting says he is 'sickened,' as MPs demand an independent inquiry.

Four women have told the BBC they were raped or sexually assaulted as teenage recruits at the Army Foundation College in Harrogate, the sole training establishment for 16- and 17-year-olds joining the British Army — and the account has pulled a response from the very top of the Ministry of Defence.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting called the reports "absolutely sickened," adding that "my heart goes out to this courageous woman who's spoken up, having been so badly failed." Speaking to reporters on 7 August during a visit to the Babcock shipyard in Rosyth, he said the department owed recruits "the very highest standards in uniform" and that he needed to "assure myself that we're doing everything we can to deliver the culture change that's needed."

The women's accounts, spanning roughly five years at the college, describe a pattern rather than a single incident. One, given the pseudonym Tilly, said she was raped at 17 by a fellow soldier after being invited into his room, an assault she said lasted hours. Another, Monica, alleged she was trapped in a room and sexually assaulted. A third, Amy, said harassment was severe enough that she slept across her bedroom door because the lock was broken and she feared men entering. A fourth, Ellie, alleged instructors rated female recruits' appearances aloud and walked into women's dormitories unannounced while cadets were undressed.

The numbers behind the accounts

North Yorkshire Police received 16 reports of sexual offences linked to the college in the year to June, spanning exposure, voyeurism and sexual assault, with three now under active investigation. The MoD's own figures are starker still: 176 complaints of inappropriate sexual behaviour at the college between January 2018 and June 2025, including 32 against permanent staff and 144 against junior soldiers themselves. Between 2021 and 2024 alone, the department logged 122 investigations into sexual offences against under-18s at the college, 80 of them involving sexual assault.

The college is not new to this scrutiny. Instructors there were court-martialled in 2017 and 2018 over alleged abuse of recruits, and a separate MoD-commissioned inquiry into conduct at the site between 2014 and 2018 was later shelved. What's changed is the volume of fresh allegations reaching police in a single year, and a defence secretary willing to put his name to a public apology rather than a written statement.

Harrogate's MP, Tom Gordon, has written to Streeting demanding an independent inquiry into culture and safeguarding at the college, a call echoed by Emma Lewell, a Labour member of the Commons Defence Committee, who has questioned whether the college should keep operating in its current form. About 1,300 recruits pass through the college's Phase 1 training at any one time, some as young as 16 — the youngest cohort the Army takes in at all. Streeting has not yet said whether he will order an independent inquiry or rely on internal MoD review; that decision, and whatever North Yorkshire Police's three live investigations turn up, is the next thing worth watching.
