Rolls-Royce rally extends as JPMorgan lifts target to £18

In one sentence
Rolls-Royce shares are up 11% and JPMorgan has raised its target to £18, but the profit surge is margin-driven in defence and power systems and volume-driven in civil aerospace.

Rolls-Royce shares have climbed 11% since the engine maker's interim results on 30 July, and JPMorgan has added fresh fuel, lifting its price target to £18 from £16.25 and reiterating an 'overweight' rating. With the stock trading around £15.12, that implies roughly 19% further upside — a call the bank is making on the back of what it counts as Rolls-Royce's ninth consecutive beat-and-raise since the 2023 financial year.

The numbers behind the rally are unambiguous. Underlying operating profit rose 46% to £2.5bn in the first half, up from £1.7bn a year earlier, as group revenue climbed 26% to £11.3bn. The operating margin reached 22.5%, up from 19% in the same period of 2025. Management raised full-year guidance across the board: operating profit is now expected to land between £4.7bn and £4.9bn, up from £4.0bn-£4.2bn, with free cash flow guidance lifted to £3.8bn-£4.0bn. Chief executive Tufan Erginbilgic called it "significantly improved operational and financial performance."

Margin gains, not just volume

The more interesting question — and the one JPMorgan's headline number glosses over — is whether this is a cyclical bounce or something more durable. The divisional split suggests both are at work, in different places.

Civil Aerospace, still the group's largest division, looks like a volume story: revenue rose 29% organically to £6.19bn, operating profit rose 31% to £1.57bn, but the margin moved only half a percentage point, to 25.3%. That tracks with a recovery in large-engine flying hours, guided to the lower end of 115%-120% of 2019 levels, and a heavier schedule of shop visits — more planes back in the air, more engines through overhaul, rather than a fundamentally more profitable business per flight hour.

Defence and Power Systems tell a different story. Defence margin jumped to 21.0% from 15.4%, with operating profit up almost 60% to £522m, which the company attributes to stronger aftermarket performance and "continued self-help" — cost and efficiency work rather than a one-off contract. Power Systems margin rose to 20.3% from 15.3%, helped by a 41% jump in power-generation orders as data-centre operators buy generators to backstop AI infrastructure — a demand shift that, if it holds, is closer to structural than seasonal.

That combination — genuine margin expansion in two of three divisions, plus a cyclical volume recovery in the third — is a reasonable case that at least part of the improvement will stick. It is not, on its own, proof that the whole 46% is repeatable.

The valuation catch

The risk JPMorgan's target doesn't resolve is price. At roughly 36-37 times forward 2026 earnings, Rolls-Royce trades well above BAE Systems, which sits closer to 24 times. A stock priced for continued upside surprises has less room to absorb a quarter that merely meets expectations, let alone one where Civil Aerospace's flying-hours recovery plateaus once the post-pandemic shop-visit backlog clears.

The next test comes with Rolls-Royce's Capital Markets Day and Q3 trading update later this year, when investors will be watching whether the Defence and Power Systems margin gains hold without the tailwind of this year's contract catch-ups, and whether AI-driven power-generation demand keeps compounding or was simply an early mover advantage now facing competition.
