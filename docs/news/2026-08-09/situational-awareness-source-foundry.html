Aschenbrenner's fund doubles down with $400m more for chip startup after 78% AUM crash

In one sentence
Weeks after a 78% AUM collapse, Aschenbrenner's fund put a further $400m into stealth chip-tooling startup Source Foundry, taking its stake to $500m.

Three weeks after watching four-fifths of his hedge fund's assets evaporate in a margin call that made trading-desk history, Leopold Aschenbrenner did not retreat to cash. He wrote a $400 million cheque to a chip-tooling startup that is barely a year old, doesn't have a product on the market, and whose website is, by several accounts, a single image.

The move brings Situational Awareness LP's total commitment to Source Foundry to roughly $500 million — $100 million from an earlier round, now topped up by the fresh $400 million — according to a Bloomberg report confirming what had been a closely guarded position. It is, by a wide margin, the largest single bet the fund has placed since its near-collapse in July, and it comes from a firm now managing about $10 billion, down from a peak of $45 billion just five weeks ago.

From 1,000% to a margin call

Situational Awareness was, until late July, one of the best-performing hedge funds in the world. Named after Aschenbrenner's viral 2024 essay series arguing that AGI was arriving faster than most believed, the fund was up 439% net through June 30 on concentrated, heavily leveraged bets across AI infrastructure and semiconductor stocks. The strategy ran roughly four times leverage — for every dollar of investor capital, the fund borrowed three to four more from prime brokers including Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Citigroup and Bank of America, then layered options on top.

That structure worked in reverse with brutal efficiency. When the AI infrastructure trade unwound in July — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 28.6% from its June 22 peak, the Morgan Stanley Momentum TMT Index dropped 53.5% — the fund's underlying positions fell 35% to 47%, wiping out the equity backing the borrowed money. Facing simultaneous margin calls, Situational Awareness sold its entire public-equities book, roughly $16 billion, to Ken Griffin's Citadel on July 30, at what multiple reports describe as close to a 10% discount. The portfolio's mark-to-market loss for the month came to about 67%; measured against total assets under management, the fund's capital base fell from $45 billion to about $10 billion — a 78% collapse that has left Aschenbrenner managing barely a fifth of what he ran five weeks earlier.

What survived the fire sale were the fund's private holdings, chief among them a roughly $5 billion stake in Anthropic acquired in a February 2025 round that valued the AI lab near $60 billion. A spokesperson has denied reports that the fund was shopping that stake, telling CNBC such claims were "not accurate." Bloomberg has since described what remains of Situational Awareness as something close to an Anthropic holding company with a hedge fund attached.

The Source Foundry bet

Against that backdrop, the size of the new Source Foundry commitment stands out. The startup was incorporated in California in July 2025 by two Stanford alumni, Abdulmalik Obaid and Joe Burg, and has since raised capital at a $5 billion valuation with backers including Sequoia Capital. Its pitch is narrow and technically ambitious: rather than fabricate chips itself, or compete head-on with foundries like TSMC, Source Foundry is building semiconductor manufacturing tools aimed at advanced lithography — the etching step widely regarded as the industry's single biggest bottleneck, and the process ASML has dominated for two decades with its extreme ultraviolet (EUV) machines, each costing well over $150 million.

Details beyond that remain scarce; the company operates largely in stealth, and public information about its specific technical approach is limited. What is clear is the scale of conviction Aschenbrenner is putting behind it. Half a billion dollars into a single pre-revenue company, deployed in two tranches straddling the worst month in the fund's history, concentrates an outsized share of Situational Awareness's remaining capital into one illiquid, unlisted bet — precisely the kind of position that cannot be marked down daily, and cannot be sold in a hurry if the thesis is wrong.

Conviction or a bad hand played harder

There are two ways to read the timing. One is that Aschenbrenner's underlying thesis was never about AI stock prices — it was about physical capacity constraints in chip production, and July's selloff was a public-markets panic that has little bearing on whether the world needs more lithography tools by the end of the decade. Under that reading, moving further into private infrastructure, away from leveraged public positions and daily mark-to-market risk, is a rational response to having just been burned by exactly that kind of exposure.

The other reading is less flattering: a fund that lost 78% of its assets in a month, and whose main remaining public story is a stake in Anthropic it insists it isn't selling, has an obvious incentive to point to a single high-conviction private bet as evidence that the thesis is intact and the losses were a market-structure accident rather than a judgment error. Doubling down after a rout is, after all, indistinguishable in the moment from either genuine conviction or the sunk-cost instinct to make one big position work rather than admit the model was too leveraged for the world it was betting on.

Source Foundry itself offers no tiebreaker either way — it is too new, too private, and too undisclosed for outside investors to judge the technology on its merits. What the $400 million does confirm is where the surviving capital in AI infrastructure is willing to go after a shock: not back into liquid semiconductor equities, which remain volatile, but into the unlisted layer beneath them, where valuations are set by the last funding round rather than the next trading session.

The next marker will be whether Source Foundry produces anything resembling a public technical disclosure or a named customer — until then, the market has only Aschenbrenner's cheque size to go on. Situational Awareness's next investor letter, covering August, should also clarify whether the fund's remaining capital base has stabilized near $10 billion or is still being drawn down to fund private commitments like this one.
