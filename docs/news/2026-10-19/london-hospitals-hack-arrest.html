Russian man linked to London hospitals cyberattack arrested

In one sentence
A 28-year-old Russian suspected of belonging to the Qilin ransomware gang is in German custody, but he is wanted over a German company's hack, not the London hospitals attack.

A Russian man suspected of belonging to the Qilin ransomware gang, the group blamed for the 2024 cyberattack on London hospitals, is in German custody. The charge he faces is not for the London attack, though. The warrant against him concerns a separate hack of a German company.

The suspect is 28 and has not been officially named. Some outlets have called him "Vladimir K", but that comes from a single aggregator report. Japanese police arrested him in Osaka in May, according to The Record, which says officials had learned he planned a holiday in Japan and that he was detained at a hotel. Japan's National Police Agency confirmed the arrest and his handover on Thursday 8 October. German authorities issued the warrant.

What he is accused of

SecurityWeek reports that Germany wanted him over the hacking of a logistics company in September 2024. The attackers encrypted the firm's data and demanded more than $160,000 in cryptocurrency. SecurityWeek describes him as "believed to be a core member" of Qilin. No formal charges have been published, and German law enforcement did not respond to The Record's requests for comment.

The reports disagree on when he was sent to Germany. SecurityWeek, citing another outlet, says 2 October. The Record says June. That discrepancy is unresolved.

Nothing in the reporting reviewed here ties him to the London attack. The Record does not mention Synnovis. SecurityWeek mentions it only as background. The one report that links him to the hospital hack, from the NewsBytes aggregator, is not corroborated elsewhere.

The London attack

Synnovis is the pathology provider serving several NHS trusts in south-east London, including King's College Hospital and Guy's and St Thomas'. In June 2024 a ransomware attack on its systems forced hospitals to declare critical incidents, cancel operations and curb blood transfusions. Qilin, a Russia-based group that rents out its ransomware to affiliates, was blamed. It reportedly demanded $50 million.

Qilin works as ransomware-as-a-service. Core developers maintain the software and the leak site, and affiliates carry out individual attacks. An arrest of one member therefore does not by itself show who ran any particular intrusion.

What the arrest does and doesn't show

The arrest is a real result against a group that North Rhine-Westphalia's interior minister, Herbert Reul, says may have attacked nearly 4,000 organisations since 2024, about 150 of them in Germany. Der Spiegel reports that three more suspected members are believed to remain in Russia, out of reach of European police.

It is not yet an accountability step for the Synnovis attack. Only a charge, an indictment or a statement from German or British prosecutors could establish that link, and none has been published. The National Crime Agency, which leads the UK inquiry into Synnovis, has not commented in the reports reviewed. Germany's formal charges and any British statement are the next things to watch.
