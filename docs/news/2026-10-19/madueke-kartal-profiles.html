Madueke shows 'anger and conviction'; Kartal's remarkable comeback

In one sentence
BBC Sport profiles argue Noni Madueke's 'anger and conviction' make him more than a finisher, and trace Sonay Kartal's return from a six-month back injury.

Two profiles on the BBC Sport site this week take the same line: a British athlete whose reputation is being rewritten. In one, Noni Madueke is said to be more than a finisher. In the other, Sonay Kartal is said to be rebuilding after time out of the game.

The BBC analysis of Madueke is built around a phrase, 'anger and conviction'. It argues that those qualities now shape his role in the team, rather than merely decorating his goals. The piece's full text was not available for this article, so its detailed evidence is not reproduced here. The claim is still a notable one for a forward who has largely been defined by what he does in the final third.

The phrase carries some history. In November 2024, as a Chelsea player, Madueke was substituted against Arsenal and went down the tunnel instead of to the bench, and Rio Ferdinand criticised him for it. After joining Arsenal, he said he was 'not really worried' about fan criticism of the move. Mikel Arteta said the backlash gave him 'more conviction' about signing him. Whether the BBC analysis draws on those episodes is not known. They show that anger and conviction have been part of his story before, and that the question is whether they now feed his play.

Kartal's road back

The second profile is of Sonay Kartal, the British tennis player. Her recent history makes the 'comeback' label fair. Reports describe a back injury that kept her out for about six months and cost her three Grand Slam events. Her ranking fell from No. 54 to No. 170 over that spell. She has since reached the semi-finals at Wuhan, her first at WTA 1000 level, beating the world No. 6 Elina Svitolina in the quarter-finals. WTA coverage called it a landmark run. Earlier, she came from 4-1 down against Diane Parry to reach her first Grand Slam fourth round at Wimbledon.

The BBC describes her as Britain's top woman. Other outlets have called her British number two or three at different points, so that label is best treated with caution until checked against the current rankings.

What links them

Neither piece is about a result. Both are about perception: Madueke's role being reassessed while he is playing, and Kartal's career being reassessed after an injury. For Madueke the test is whether the analysis holds across the next run of Arsenal fixtures, when his contribution can be judged against his goals and assists. For Kartal it is whether the Wuhan run turns into a steady climb back up the rankings.
