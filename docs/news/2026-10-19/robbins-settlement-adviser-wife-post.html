Robbins settles with government over sacking; police take no action over adviser's wife's post

In one sentence
The government has settled with Sir Olly Robbins over his sacking, while police have reportedly closed their assessment of a Palestine Action post by the national security adviser's wife.

Two Whitehall rows ended quietly on Friday and over the weekend. The government settled with the senior diplomat it sacked over the vetting of Lord Mandelson. Separately, police closed their look at a social media post by the wife of the prime minister's national security adviser.

A settlement without a public accounting

Sir Olly Robbins, the former permanent under-secretary at the Foreign Office and head of the Diplomatic Service, has reached a settlement with the government over his dismissal. The FDA union, which represented him, announced the deal on 16 October. A government spokesperson confirmed that it had reached a settlement with Sir Oliver following his dismissal.

Robbins was removed over the vetting of Lord Mandelson ahead of his appointment as ambassador to Washington. He then brought a legal challenge. As reported, that challenge argued that the prime minister has no statutory power to dismiss the head of the Diplomatic Service. It also argued that the procedure used was not fair. In a statement Robbins thanked those who had supported him and the FDA for helping to reach the agreement.

The terms have not been published. One outlet's headline describes the sum as six-figure. That figure could not be confirmed in other coverage, so it should be treated with caution.

The question the settlement leaves open is legal as well as financial. A court ruling on whether a prime minister can dismiss the head of the Diplomatic Service would have set a precedent for future cases. A negotiated settlement sets none. The Mandelson vetting questions that led to the sacking will also have to be answered elsewhere, if they are answered at all, because the case will not do it.

The adviser's wife and Palestine Action

The second matter concerns Sarah Helm, a journalist and author married to Jonathan Powell, the national security adviser. Last week she replied on X to a post about the novelist Sally Rooney, with a line that was read as expressing support for Palestine Action. The group was proscribed under terrorism legislation last year, and expressing support for a proscribed organisation can be a criminal offence.

The Metropolitan Police said on 16 October that the post was "being assessed to determine whether any further police investigation may be required". Downing Street distanced itself, saying the views were those of an individual who does not represent the government in any capacity. The Campaign Against Antisemitism urged police to act and asked Powell to denounce the rhetoric publicly if he was to remain in post. The Reform MP Danny Kruger wrote to the prime minister to say he was concerned that Helm appeared to have broken the law.

BBC reporting now says police have concluded there will be no further action. The details of that decision were not available for this article, and it has not been possible to check the reasoning behind it.

What links them

The two stories have little in common on the facts. Both, however, test how far the machinery around the prime minister is accountable for its people. In one case, a dismissed official has been paid to stop pursuing his claim. In the other, a police decision settles the legal question but not the political one. Powell remains in post, and the criticism from opposition figures was about the standards expected of those close to the centre of national security policy. A no-further-action finding does not address that.

What to watch: whether the government or Robbins says anything more about the terms, and whether MPs press for an account of the Mandelson vetting. Whether the police outcome ends the row over Powell will depend on whether opposition MPs keep pressing it.
