Cardinals upset Rams and Eagles lose in NFL week six

In one sentence
Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 on six Chad Ryland field goals, while the Eagles, without Jalen Hurts, lost 25-14 to Carolina for a fourth straight defeat.

Arizona beat the Los Angeles Rams 18-16 at SoFi Stadium on Sunday without scoring a touchdown. Philadelphia, meanwhile, lost 25-14 at home to Carolina. Week six of the NFL season left three teams in the NFC with problems and a good deal of league standing still to sort out.

Six kicks and a clock

The Cardinals entered the late game 1-4 and heavy underdogs, with a spread of about 12.5 points. They left it 2-4, level with the Rams, on the strength of Chad Ryland's right foot. The kicker made all six of his field-goal attempts, the last a 43-yarder as time expired.

It was not a performance built for highlight reels. Arizona went 0-for-5 in the red zone, which is how a team ends up needing six field goals. It still outgained Los Angeles 338 yards to 286, and NFL Research noted that winning on the road without a touchdown is a rare feat.

The Rams had their own share of misfortune. Kicker Blake Grupe left in the first quarter with a knee injury, and Ethan Evans took over the kicking duties. Colby Parkinson's touchdown catch gave Los Angeles a 16-9 lead late on. That left Arizona 47 seconds. Jacoby Brissett found Jalen Brooks for 21 yards, a 15-yard unnecessary-roughness penalty pushed the Cardinals into range, and Ryland did the rest.

The Rams are 0-2 in the NFC West. Sports Illustrated's verdict was blunt, saying the team's two biggest problems are Matthew Stafford and Sean McVay, but that "those two are also the solutions."

Philadelphia's slide

The Eagles' 25-14 defeat by the Panthers was their fourth straight loss, the first such run of Nick Sirianni's tenure, and it left them 2-4. Carolina moved to 3-2. Bryce Young threw for 281 yards and two touchdowns, and Chuba Hubbard ran for 120 yards and a score.

Jalen Hurts is on injured reserve, so Tanner McKee made his first meaningful start. He completed 26 of 45 passes for 215 yards and a touchdown, but was sacked three times. NFL.com's Kevin Patra wrote that starters "can't miss windows as badly as he did Sunday."

What it means

It is early, and week-six upsets are routine. But the NFC picture is untidy. Arizona and the Rams share a 2-4 record, and Philadelphia, a contender in recent years, sits at the same mark with its quarterback sidelined. The next test for each is whether they can string together results. For the Eagles, that depends on how long Hurts is out.
