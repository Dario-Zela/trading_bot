SCIENCE · SPACE · By R. Okonkwo

Mission launches to Mars to collect a sample from its moon

Japan is preparing to send a probe to Phobos, the larger of Mars's two moons, to collect material and bring it back to Earth. The Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft is due to launch on Japan's H3 rocket in the 2026 Mars launch window, with samples expected back around 2031. Phobos is about 22 km across on average. The probe carries a small rover, built with France's CNES and Germany's DLR, to work on the moon's surface.

The mission would be the first to return samples from any Martian moon. Scientists want to know whether Phobos formed from asteroid material captured by Mars or from debris left after a giant impact on the planet.

It is also a rehearsal. Landing, sampling and returning material from Phobos would test the techniques any future return of rock from Mars itself would need. Whether the sampling and return work as planned is the thing to watch.
