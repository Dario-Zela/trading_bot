Meta and Tesla earnings, plus five Dow stocks, report this week

In one sentence
Tesla reports on 21 October and Meta is due to follow, with each report testing whether the share-price weakness is justified by earnings, spending and guidance.

Tesla reports third-quarter results on Wednesday, 21 October, after the US market close, with a webcast at 5:30pm Eastern, according to the filing on the SEC's EDGAR system. Meta is also due to report, and Benzinga's earnings-week preview groups the two among the large companies on this week's calendar. The preview also says five Dow Jones Industrial Average components report this week. The two companies are described as among the Dow's weaker performers, so the question is whether the numbers justify their share-price weakness or only reset expectations.

A date that needs a caveat

Tesla's date is confirmed. Meta's is not. One earnings tracker projects Meta's report for Wednesday, 28 October, which would fall in next week's calendar, and it labels that date unconfirmed. No announcement from Meta was found in time for this article. The Benzinga preview places both companies this week. Until Meta publishes its own date, the claim that the two report together should be treated as provisional.

The names of the other Dow reporters are not established here, and neither are consensus estimates or the year-to-date returns behind the "laggard" label. Those figures are left out rather than guessed.

What each report has to show

The two cases differ in kind.

Tesla is valued far above its earnings. A Motley Fool analysis in July put the stock at more than 200 times consensus forward earnings. At that multiple, the share price depends on growth outside the car business. The company has begun carrying robotaxi riders in Miami. A quarter that shows revenue from autonomy or energy, rather than only vehicle margins, would support the valuation. Weak margins from the core car business would put pressure on it.

Meta has the opposite problem. Its revenue was growing about 33% as of the July analysis, but investors are watching what it spends. In January it guided 2026 capital spending to between $115bn and $135bn, against about $72bn in 2025. The test is whether advertising growth keeps pace with that outlay. A cut or narrowing of the spending range, or a raised revenue outlook, would suggest the market's worry is overdone. A higher spending forecast without faster revenue would confirm it.

What counts as a pass

A share price that has already fallen can move either way on a report, because the result is judged against what investors now expect, not against last year. A beat that leaves guidance unchanged would reset expectations without answering the question of whether the slide was justified. The more informative outcomes are the ones that change the forward numbers: Meta's spending guidance and Tesla's margin and autonomy commentary.

The earnings week follows a day in which chip stocks snapped a long losing streak and added nearly $500bn to the Nasdaq 100. That is covered in Chip stocks snap a Lehman-era losing streak. That rebound shows how heavily the market currently leans on large technology companies' spending plans.

Tesla's results arrive on Wednesday evening. Meta's filing, whether later this week or on the projected 28 October, will be the other data point to watch.
