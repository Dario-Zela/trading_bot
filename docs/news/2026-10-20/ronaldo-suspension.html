Ronaldo given 30-day suspension and £215 fine by Portuguese FA

In one sentence
Ronaldo's 30-day ban and €255 fine from the Portuguese FA is the minimum for walking out of camp and, if backdated as reported, should end before Portugal's November fixtures.

Cristiano Ronaldo has been suspended for 30 days and fined €255 by the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF) for walking out of the national-team camp. The sanction is unlikely to cost Portugal his services in the next window.

The fine is in euros. Early reports gave it as £215, which does not match the FPF's figure. The federation's disciplinary committee found Ronaldo guilty of deliberately breaching Article 160(1) of its disciplinary regulations. The case concerns his departure from camp on 30 September, on the eve of Portugal's Nations League match against Denmark.

The minimum on the menu

FPF rules provide for a suspension of one to six months for leaving the national team, with a fine of €500 to €1,000 mentioned in earlier coverage. Thirty days is therefore the floor of the range. The €255 fine is reported to equal 2.5 accounting units.

The federation said Ronaldo's full admission of the facts, made in a formal statement submitted on Friday, and his clean disciplinary record helped to reduce the punishment. He had already apologised publicly for leaving his teammates and for failing to acknowledge supporters after the Norway game. He said he would remain available for selection after any punishment.

The FPF opened proceedings and provisionally suspended him earlier this month. President Pedro Proença said everyone at the federation wanted Ronaldo back, and that the outcome would "depend solely on the Disciplinary Committee".

Does Portugal miss him?

Reports say the ban is backdated to 1 October, the day of the Denmark match. On that basis he has already served 19 days and the suspension ends on 30 October. Earlier coverage put the best-case end date at the same day, ahead of the November international break, when the Nations League group stage concludes.

That timing means the ban should not touch Portugal's next two matches: Denmark on 14 November and Wales on 17 November. A six-month ban would have kept him out until the June window and raised awkward questions about his place in the 2026 World Cup picture. The minimum sanction removes that risk.

Two caveats apply. The FPF's announcement, as reported, does not spell out which fixtures the suspension covers or exactly how the provisional suspension is credited. Ronaldo also has the right to appeal, though he has said he expected to be punished. Separately, the FPF has been preparing a submission to FIFA over his red card against the Republic of Ireland, so his disciplinary position is not entirely settled.

What to watch

The next check is Portugal's squad announcement for the Denmark and Wales matches. A Ronaldo call-up would confirm the ban has run its course. Anything else would mean the FPF's terms are stricter than reported.
