Woman gives evidence on alleged Russell Brand rape at trial

In one sentence
A third complainant told Southwark Crown Court that Russell Brand raped her in a Soho bar toilet in 2004; he denies all charges and the trial continues.

A woman has told a jury at Southwark Crown Court that Russell Brand raped her in the toilets of a London bar in 2004. She is the third of six complainants to give evidence in the trial, now in its second week. Brand, 51, denies all the allegations.

He is tried on three counts of rape, three of sexual assault and one of indecent assault. The alleged offences span 1999 to 2009 and involve six women. The trial is expected to last around six weeks. The charges and evidence below are allegations, which Brand contests. He has not been convicted of anything.

What the jury heard

The woman, who worked for MTV at the time, said she had wanted to meet Brand because she found him funny. She told jurors that within a minute of their starting to talk at a party in a London bar, he groped her breasts, then pulled her to nearby toilets where he orally raped her. Jurors were shown her police interview, in which she said: "It happened so fast." She said he held her by the head and hair and that she "froze". Afterwards, she said, she felt dazed, numb and ashamed.

She gave her live evidence from behind protective screens. Asked why she waited about two decades to go to the police, she said she was terrified and believed Brand's fame meant it would be "his word against mine".

The defence

Brand's counsel, Ian Winter KC, put it to her that she had been excited to meet him and later regretted what happened, rather than having been raped. He asked why she had not described the incident as rape until after a 2023 documentary and press report about Brand. She replied that she "didn't want people to judge" her and "felt ashamed". She became emotional under cross-examination.

Winter told the court that the toilet incident was "physically impossible and it did not happen". An alternative suggestion put to her was that the pair had gone to the toilets consensually for a "kiss and a cuddle" but stopped because of the state of the room. She rejected that. In his police interview, Brand said he had never engaged in non-consensual sexual activity and that his accusers might have re-imagined events.

Where the trial stands

The first complainant says Brand raped her after they met at a fringe event at the 1999 Labour Party conference. The second says that in 2001, at MTV Europe in London, where she worked, he tried to pull her into the men's toilets. Three further complainants are still to come.

The trial was originally listed for June but is running now, so the "testimony continuing" description holds for 20 October. Reports differ slightly on its length. Some say "around six weeks" and others "at least six weeks".

Brand has not yet given evidence. The jury will hear the remaining complainants, then the defence case, before any verdict. Reporting restrictions apply throughout. The case is a test of whether a famous defendant's trial runs like any other, and that will not be known until the jury returns.
