Nigeria has become the first OPEC member to join the International Energy Agency, breaking decades of cartel solidarity. The membership reflects a strategic realignment, with Lagos prioritizing energy sovereignty and partnerships with Western nations over collective OPEC action.

These investments suggest confidence that Nigeria can emerge as a leading energy hub outside OPEC's traditional framework.

The departure tests OPEC's cohesion in an era when oil prices remain volatile and energy transition pressures mount, with potential implications for cartel discipline and African energy producers' broader pivot away from OPEC solidarity.
