Lululemon shareholders back revised board in settlement with founder Chip Wilson

A Lululemon Athletica storefront on the Upper East Side, Manhattan — the brand's new board faces the task of reversing a 68 percent stock decline since late 2023. — Ajay Suresh / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Lululemon shareholders approved two Chip Wilson-backed board nominees at the June 25 AGM, ending a proxy battle fought with WSJ ads, protest trucks, and months of public acrimony.

Lululemon Athletica shareholders voted Thursday to seat two new directors backed by estranged founder Chip Wilson, closing a governance battle that had spilled well past the boardroom — and briefly onto the streets of Vancouver and Manhattan.

The vote, taken at the company's virtual annual general meeting on June 25, ratified a cooperation agreement struck with Wilson in late May. Under the deal, Laura Gentile, former chief marketing officer of ESPN, and Marc Maurer, former co-chief executive of Swiss running label On, join the company's board immediately after the meeting, expanding it to eleven directors. Lululemon also committed to appointing a third director with product and brand expertise in apparel by October 1, 2026.

Wilson, who founded the yoga-inspired apparel company in Vancouver in 1997 and retains approximately 8.7 percent of its shares, had launched a formal proxy campaign in late December 2025. It followed months of public hostilities: a Wall Street Journal advertisement in October 2025 in which he compared Lululemon's management to a "plane crash" and a "sinking ship," and in spring, trucks bearing critical messages stationed outside the company's Vancouver headquarters and a New York store. He argued the board was "structurally set up to resist change," favouring operators fluent in Wall Street metrics over the product-driven leadership he considered essential to the brand's identity.

What Wilson Actually Won

The settlement delivered Wilson his primary demand: two nominees on the board. That is not purely symbolic. His picks — Gentile, a marketing heavyweight, and Maurer, who helped build On into a credible challenger to Nike at scale — carry the brand and product credentials Wilson had insisted the board lacked. A third seat with apparel expertise, due by October, extends that foothold further.

In exchange, Wilson agreed to cease public attacks through at least the 2028 annual meeting, an effective standstill on the commentary that had begun to cloud the company's narrative with analysts and potential management recruits.

The shareholder vote was not entirely routine. Though the board nominees passed, the advisory vote on executive compensation drew notable resistance: roughly 37 percent of shares were voted against the pay plan — a significant dissent figure even in a contentious year.

Lululemon is operating without a permanent chief executive, an interim arrangement Wilson had specifically criticised, and its stock has lost approximately 68 percent of its value since late 2023, settling near $117 on the day of the meeting. North American comparable sales fell 5 percent last quarter.

What to watch: whether the company names a permanent CEO before Wilson's standstill expires, and who fills the third board seat due by October 1. Those two appointments will clarify whether the settlement marks a genuine strategic recalibration — or a truce that buys enough quiet time to execute a turnaround the market has not yet priced in.
