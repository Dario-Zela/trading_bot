The RealReal vs. RH: two bets on luxury retail in a consumer slowdown

RH's Boston gallery — the kind of monumental retail footprint the company is now replicating in London and Milan. — Restoration Hardware / Wikimedia Commons / CC0
In one sentence
The RealReal's 40M members and 19% Q1 revenue growth face off against RH's European gallery expansion — two luxury models, one deteriorating consumer backdrop.

The luxury goods market has quietly run out of easy fuel. Morgan Stanley forecasts personal luxury goods growth of just 2.5% this year, and Bain & Company notes that roughly 80% of the sector's expansion between 2023 and 2025 came from price increases, not volume. Aspirational buyers are pulling back. The brands built to capture them are reconsidering their playbooks.

The RealReal and RH have arrived at sharply different answers.

The RealReal — the authenticated luxury resale platform now led by co-founder Rati Sahi Levesque — is betting that a slowdown pulls its audience toward it. Membership has crossed 40 million, skewed toward millennials and Gen Z buyers for whom secondhand luxury is a first preference, not a fallback. Some 58% of luxury shoppers now say they favour resale in at least some product categories. First-quarter 2026 gross merchandise volume reached $606 million, up 24% year on year. Total revenue hit $189.7 million, a 19% gain, and the company raised its full-year guidance to $770–$784 million.

The catch is familiar. An accumulated deficit of $1.3 billion and a forward P/E of 187x require the thesis — that scale will eventually convert to cash — to hold longer than investors have often been patient. Adjusted EBITDA guidance for the full year is $59–$67 million: progress, but still a promissory note.

The lifestyle bet

RH is running a different play: bet that the wealthiest consumers barely notice a slowdown, and spend the downcycle expanding the footprint they'll profit from when conditions ease. First-quarter 2026 revenue came in at $800 million, down 1.7% year on year, partly because tariff-related sourcing disruptions deferred roughly $45 million in sales. A quarterly net loss of $13.7 million followed. Management responded by raising the full-year outlook anyway: 4.5–8% revenue growth, with adjusted free cash flow of $300–$400 million.

The expansion plan is deliberately theatrical. CEO Gary Friedman is opening galleries in London's Mayfair and Milan in 2026, the London site designed by Foster + Partners across three Grade II-listed buildings near Savile Row — with a restaurant, wine bar, and Champagne and caviar bar in the basement. Pre-opening costs are expected to clip roughly 270 basis points from adjusted margins. At a forward P/E of around 30x and a price-to-sales ratio below 1x, investors are not yet pricing in the vision.

The test for each company is specific. For The RealReal, the signal arrives in the next two quarters: whether the raised 2026 guidance converts to meaningful cash generation. For RH, it comes when the London and Milan galleries open — and whether a luxury lifestyle concept conceived in Corte Madera can find its footing on a continent that has never lacked its own.
