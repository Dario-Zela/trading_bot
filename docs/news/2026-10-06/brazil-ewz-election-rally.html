Brazil's EWZ jumps 13% as election outlook shifts

In one sentence
Brazil's EWZ ETF rose about 13%, its best day since 2020, after Flávio Bolsonaro led Lula in the first round, setting up a 25 October runoff.

Brazilian equities had their best day in more than six years on Monday after the first round of the presidential election went the opposite way from what many investors expected. The iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ), the main US-listed vehicle for the market, rose about 13%. It was EWZ's biggest one-day gain since March 2020.

What happened

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, a right-wing candidate and the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, finished first on Sunday with just over 47% of the vote. The incumbent, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, took nearly 45%. Neither cleared the threshold for an outright win, so the two meet in a runoff on 25 October.

The first-round result was the surprise. Early on Monday EWZ was up more than 14%, and it ended the session around 13%. In São Paulo the Ibovespa rose roughly 9% to a fresh record high. Other Brazil-focused funds moved in step: the Franklin FTSE Brazil ETF (FLBR) and the VanEck Brazil Small-Cap ETF (BRF) each gained about 14%.

Why investors cheered

The market reads a Bolsonaro presidency as friendlier to investors than another Lula term. The reasoning is mostly about fiscal policy. Reports say his advisers have discussed a fiscal adjustment worth about 1.5% of GDP over 18 months. That is a discussion, not a platform commitment, and the campaign has not been tested on the detail. Still, a credible consolidation plan is what Brazilian assets have lacked, and the price move suggests investors are paying for the chance of one.

The size of the move is also a measure of how much political risk was already in the price. A one-day gain of 13% in a broad country fund means a large block of the market had been priced for a different outcome. Some of Monday's jump is a rebound from that discount rather than new information about earnings. Some well-known macro investors, Stanley Druckenmiller among them, were reported to have held Brazil exposure going into the vote.

The diversification problem

For holders of diversified emerging-market funds, the episode is a reminder that a single country's politics can dominate returns. Brazil is a large weight in most EM benchmarks, so a swing of this size moves the aggregate, and it moves it in a way that has little to do with China, India or Korea. Managers who were underweight Brazil on political grounds will have lagged on the day. Those who chase the rally now are buying after a double-digit gain and before the decisive vote.

That is the main caution. A first-round lead of about two points is not a win, and a runoff is a different contest. Voters who backed other candidates will decide it, and polls did not anticipate Sunday's result. If the runoff narrows or flips, much of Monday's repricing could reverse as quickly as it arrived.

What to watch

The next data points are runoff polling in the coming weeks, any detail the Bolsonaro campaign gives on the fiscal plan, and the 25 October vote itself. The real/dollar exchange rate and foreign flows into Brazilian equities will show whether international money treats the move as durable. Until the runoff, EWZ is likely to trade as a proxy for the polls.
