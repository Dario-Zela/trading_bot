Crypto treasury firms keep buying: Strategy adds 334 BTC, Strive 2,000, Bitmine 15,112 ETH

In one sentence
Strategy, Strive and Bitmine each reported new crypto purchases, but without dates, prices or benchmarks the figures say little about whether corporate buying is accelerating.

Three companies that hold crypto on their balance sheets reported fresh purchases this week, according to Benzinga. Strategy added 334 bitcoin. Strive bought roughly 2,000 bitcoin. Bitmine acquired 15,112 ether. The reports did not give purchase dates or dollar values.

The three firms follow the same playbook. Each holds a cryptocurrency as a treasury asset and adds to the pile on a regular schedule. Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, is the best-known bitcoin holder. Bitmine is an ether-focused counterpart. Strive is another bitcoin accumulator.

What the numbers show

The figures are small next to each company's holdings. Strategy's 334 coins would be a modest top-up for a treasury of its size. Strive's roughly 2,000 is about six times larger, and that gap is the basis for the suggestion that Strategy's pace has slowed. The reports offer nothing more than the two numbers to support that reading.

One week's purchase does not establish a trend. These firms buy in irregular lots, and a small week often follows a large one. Funding also shapes the timing, because many treasury companies pay for coin purchases by selling stock or debt. A smaller buy can reflect how much capital was raised in a given week rather than any change in conviction. Nothing in the available reporting indicates a change of strategy at Strategy, or a competitive contest between it and Strive.

The Bitmine purchase is a different asset and cannot be compared with the bitcoin buys without converting at prices the reports do not supply.

What is missing

The source is a secondary news summary, not the companies' own filings or announcements. Treasury firms typically disclose purchases through regulatory filings and press releases, and those documents carry exact dates, average prices and total holdings. Without them, it is not possible to say whether this week's buys are larger or smaller than recent averages, or whether they are speeding up or slowing down.

The broader signal is real but limited. Continued corporate buying shows that these firms still have access to funding and still want the exposure. It does not show whether demand is growing. The figures needed to answer that are cumulative holdings, the pace of recent purchases and the funding behind them.

What to watch

The next round of company filings should settle the question. If Strategy's weekly additions stay near 334 coins while Strive keeps buying in the thousands, the pace gap is a pattern. If Strategy's next announcement is larger, this week looks like noise. Regulatory developments could also affect how these firms operate; the CFTC has opened its first formal rulemaking on crypto markets.
