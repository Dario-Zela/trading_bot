Saks Global bankruptcy plan wins court approval

The Saks Fifth Avenue flagship on Fifth Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, one of 15 Saks locations the company will retain after exiting bankruptcy. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
A Houston court approved Saks Global's Chapter 11 plan on June 5, clearing the Neiman Marcus and Saks parent to exit bankruptcy around June 22 with 75% less debt and 49 stores.

A federal bankruptcy judge in Houston cleared Saks Global Enterprises LLC on Friday to proceed with what amounts to the largest luxury retail restructuring in recent memory, approving a reorganization plan that slashes the company's debt by nearly 75 percent and transfers ownership to senior lenders.

Judge Alfredo Perez of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas confirmed the plan at a June 5 hearing, saying the company had done an "extraordinary" job stabilizing operations after a difficult start to the case. Saks Global — parent of Saks Fifth Avenue, Neiman Marcus and Bergdorf Goodman — filed for Chapter 11 on January 13, just 13 months after completing its $2.7 billion acquisition of Neiman Marcus Group, a deal that left it carrying $3.4 billion in debt and unable to pay key vendors.

The post-emergence balance sheet looks materially different. Debt falls to approximately $1.2 billion — a $750 million term loan plus a $347 million ABL facility — supported by nearly $700 million in liquidity. The exit financing tranche of $500 million was committed by Pentwater Capital Management and Bracebridge Capital, which had also provided $1 billion in debtor-in-possession financing during the case and are stepping in as new equity owners. Nearly 720 brands that suspended shipments during the restructuring have resumed, and $1.6 billion in retail receipts have been released back into the supply chain.

A smaller company, with long-range targets

The surviving store footprint consists of 49 luxury locations: 33 Neiman Marcus stores, 15 Saks Fifth Avenue outlets and the Bergdorf Goodman flagship. Off-price divisions were wound down entirely; Horchow and 14 standalone Fifth Avenue Club locations closed during the case.

Junior creditors received a $20 million litigation trust — added after court objections — but face minimal recovery on the bulk of their claims. Original equity was extinguished. Management's stated targets run to $9 billion in gross merchandise value and double-digit adjusted EBITDA by fiscal 2030, with 2026 EBITDA of $85 million tracking ahead of plan and 2027 projected at roughly four times that level as cost savings compound. Profitability on a net basis is not projected until fiscal 2029.

CEO Geoffroy van Raemdonck, who took the role at the time of the January filing, framed the outcome as repositioning rather than rescue: a company with "three incredible banners" and a capital structure capable of investing in growth rather than servicing legacy acquisition debt.

For fixed-income holders and retail-sector equity desks, the confirmation order removes the near-term governance overhang. What it does not yet supply is granular visibility into store-level unit economics, the pace at which dropped vendor assortments return to full depth, or how the slimmed-down Saks fares against specialty luxury formats that gained ground during the five-month case. The company expects formal emergence around June 22 — that date, and the first set of post-bankruptcy operating metrics that follow, will be the more telling signal.
