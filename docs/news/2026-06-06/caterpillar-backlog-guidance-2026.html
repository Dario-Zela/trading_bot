Caterpillar hits record backlog, raises 2026 outlook

A Caterpillar 305 CR compact excavator on a job site in Arlington, Massachusetts — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Caterpillar's Q1 revenue rose 22% to $17.4B and its backlog hit a record $63B, prompting the company to raise its 2026 sales growth outlook to low double digits.

Caterpillar entered 2026 with its order book fuller than it has ever been. The Deerfield, Illinois manufacturer reported first-quarter revenues of $17.4 billion — up 22% from the same period a year earlier — and said its backlog had swelled to a record $63 billion, a $28 billion jump that represents 79% growth year over year. Management responded by raising its full-year 2026 sales forecast to low-double-digit growth, lifting guidance that was already pointed upward when the year began.

"Backlog grew to a record level of $63 billion, an increase of $28 billion or 79% compared to the first quarter last year," CEO Joe Creed said on the earnings call late April. "We have a disciplined strategy of scaling capacity in direct alignment with our growing backlog."

Adjusted earnings per share came in at $5.54, up 30% year over year, while gross margin reached 33.1% — the highest reading in eight quarters. Operating income of $3.09 billion was up 17%.

Power leads; construction follows

The clearest story inside the results is power generation. Caterpillar's Power & Energy segment posted $7 billion in revenue, up 22%, with power generation sales specifically surging 48% — driven almost entirely by data center buildout. The company's large reciprocating engine backlog has grown more than 3.5 times since it first announced an expansion plan in January 2024, and management is now targeting nearly 3x the engine capacity it held in 2024, adding roughly 15 gigawatts of annual output when investments between 2027 and 2029 come online.

Construction Industries, the company's largest segment by revenue at $7.2 billion, grew 38%, with dealer inventory builds contributing alongside seven-percent growth in direct sales to end users — the fifth consecutive quarter of user-level demand expansion. Segment profit jumped 50%, even as tariff-related headwinds bit 550 basis points out of the margin.

Resource Industries — mining and quarrying equipment — grew just 4% to $3.8 billion, with margins contracting 700 basis points to 10%. The culprit was tariffs, compounded by product-mix timing. The offsetting signal: order intake in the segment hit its highest level since 2012, driven by copper and gold mining investment.

On tariffs broadly, Caterpillar fared better than it had projected. First-quarter tariff costs landed at roughly $600 million against an $800 million estimate, and the full-year estimate was revised down to $2.2–$2.4 billion from a prior $2.6 billion figure. Incoming CFO Kyle Epley — taking the seat from Andrew Bonfield, who stepped down after this call — noted that excluding tariff drag, the company expects to finish the year in the top half of its adjusted operating profit margin target range.

Caterpillar also returned $5.7 billion to shareholders in the quarter, including a $4.5 billion accelerated share repurchase, a signal that management views the current capital position as comfortable even as it commits to multi-year capacity expansion.

The next set of numbers to watch will be the second-quarter report, where management has guided for roughly $700 million in tariff costs — slightly above the first-quarter pace. Analysts will also be tracking whether the Power & Energy backlog continues to expand or begins converting to revenue faster, a shift that would confirm capacity investments are keeping pace with demand.
