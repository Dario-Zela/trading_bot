BHP halts Escondida, the world's largest copper mine, after worker death

Escondida, in Chile's Atacama Desert, is the world's largest copper mine by production. — Bachelot Pierre J-P / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
BHP halted all operations at Escondida, the world's largest copper mine, after a fatal accident, with no restart date and a labor vote looming days later.

The world's largest copper mine went silent on Wednesday. BHP suspended every operational activity at Escondida, its flagship mine high in Chile's Atacama Desert, after a contractor was killed in an accident involving a front-end loader. The company gave no restart date. For a market already stretched thin on copper supply, the not-knowing is the story.

"Todas las actividades operacionales de la mina se encuentran suspendidas" — all operational activities of the mine are suspended — BHP said in a brief statement, confirming the death of a contract worker and the launch of an internal investigation. Chile's mining regulator, Sernageomin, sent specialists from its Antofagasta regional office to the site the same day.

What happened at Patio 900

The accident occurred in the mine's Patio 900 area, a storage and maintenance zone, where preliminary reports point to the contractor being struck during maintenance work on a front-end loader. The precise mechanism is still under investigation.

It is the second fatality in that same zone within twelve months. In October 2025, a crane operator, Luis David Henríquez Henríquez, died during a loading operation at Patio 900. On that occasion, according to two sources familiar with the matter, mining continued normally while Sernageomin opened its inquiry. This time BHP has gone further, halting the entire operation before any finding has been issued — a marked shift in posture that suggests the company is bracing for closer regulatory scrutiny than last year's incident drew.

That scrutiny carries teeth. Under Chile's mining safety law, Sernageomin does not merely recommend fixes — it can compel corrective action and block a restart outright. BHP cannot simply reopen the pits on its own sign-off; it needs the regulator's clearance, and there is no statutory clock on how long that takes.

A mine that moves markets on its own

Escondida is not just another asset on BHP's books. The Atacama operation, majority-owned by BHP alongside Rio Tinto and two Mitsubishi-linked partners, produces roughly 1.2 to 1.3 million tonnes of copper a year — enough on its own to move global benchmark prices when it so much as hiccups. BHP's guidance for the current fiscal year had already leaned on record throughput at Escondida to keep group copper output near 2 million tonnes.

The timing compounds the pressure. Copper has been on a tear in 2026, touching a record $14,779 a tonne this month, up nearly 48% over the year, as EV production, grid buildout and data-centre demand collide with ore grades that have declined roughly 40% since 1990 and mine-development timelines stretching past 15 years. Exchange inventories have thinned alongside the rally: stockpiles in Shanghai Futures Exchange warehouses sit near their lowest level since January 2024. Into that backdrop, London Metal Exchange copper has been choppy this week — opening near $14,698 a tonne before slipping back to settle around $14,616, down about 1.1% — as traders weigh the halt against broader dollar strength.

The stoppage also lands in the middle of a separate, entirely unrelated flashpoint: Escondida's collective labour contract with more than 1,000 supervisory and staff workers expires on September 30, and union leaders have urged members to reject BHP's final wage offer in a vote scheduled for September 28–30. If workers vote no, Chilean law requires five days of mandatory government mediation, extendable by five more, before any legal strike could begin. The accident and the wage dispute are not connected in cause, but they now sit on the same calendar, doubling the sources of uncertainty hanging over the mine's output in the fourth quarter.

What the market is actually pricing

For traders, the open question isn't whether Escondida restarts — mines almost always do — but when, and at what tempo. A short suspension while Sernageomin verifies fixes is a rounding error against 1.2 million annual tonnes. A prolonged shutdown, layered onto a possible strike vote a week later, is a different calculation entirely, one that copper futures have only begun to reflect.

The next concrete signal will be Sernageomin's inspection findings, which typically determine whether BHP faces a swift, narrow fix or a broader operational review before resuming. After that, the September 28–30 union vote is the second date worth watching — a rejection would add a labour dispute to a safety shutdown at the single largest source of mined copper on Earth.
