Ionis delivers positive late-stage results for two partnered drugs

Roche's headquarters towers in Basel, Switzerland — Roche partners with Ionis on sefaxersen, one of the two drugs behind Tuesday's Phase 3 announcements — HerBauhaus / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Ionis's two Phase 3 wins (Roche's sefaxersen, Otsuka's ulefnersen) drew only a 3% stock bounce after pelacarsen and eplontersen failures had erased 18%.

Ionis Pharmaceuticals told the market on Tuesday and Wednesday that two drugs it developed and handed off to bigger partners had cleared Phase 3 hurdles. The headline framing — "positive late-stage results" — undersold a more specific story: after a brutal stretch in which two other partnered programs failed outright, the antisense-oligonucleotide pioneer got a pair of wins that partly rebuild the case for its partnership model, though how much revenue either drug will actually generate remains contested.

The two readouts are not interchangeable. On September 23, Roche reported that sefaxersen, an RNA-targeted therapy for IgA nephropathy (IgAN) that Ionis discovered, hit its primary endpoint in the Phase 3 IMAgINATION trial — a statistically significant reduction in proteinuria versus placebo at 37 weeks, in a 459-patient study, with no new safety signals. A day earlier, Otsuka announced that ulefnersen, Ionis's antisense drug for FUS-ALS, met its primary endpoint in the Phase 3 FUSION trial (p=0.0005), a joint-rank analysis of survival, time to permanent ventilation and functional decline through roughly 72 weeks.

What Ionis actually stands to collect

The deal economics differ sharply, and neither was mentioned in Tuesday's coverage. Roche paid Ionis $75 million in 2018 for an option on sefaxersen and a further $55 million in 2022 to exercise it, with Ionis still owed regulatory and sales milestones plus tiered royalties on net sales — the structure that actually determines what a Phase 3 win is worth to Ionis shareholders. Otsuka, by contrast, paid just $10 million upfront in November 2024 for global rights to ulefnersen; Ionis's take from a ulefnersen approval, in a genetically defined ALS subtype that affects a few hundred patients in the U.S., will be a rounding error next to sefaxersen regardless of royalty terms, which the companies haven't disclosed.

Sefaxersen also isn't entering an open field. Novartis's Fabhalta already holds FDA approval in IgAN and targets the same complement factor B pathway, giving it a multi-year head start. The two drugs differ mainly on dosing — Fabhalta is twice-daily oral and can raise cholesterol, while sefaxersen is a once-monthly injection that showed no lipid signal in Phase 2 — a convenience argument, not a clearly superior one. Analyst peak-sales estimates for sefaxersen range from roughly €400 million to as much as €1-2 billion, a spread wide enough to say the market genuinely doesn't know how this settles.

Why the stock barely moved

Context matters more than the press releases let on. Ionis shares had already fallen roughly 18% between September 9 and September 22, driven by two separate Phase 3 failures: eplontersen missed its cardiovascular endpoint in July, and Novartis-partnered pelacarsen — a much larger commercial bet in lipoprotein(a)-driven heart disease — missed its primary endpoint on September 4, sending the stock down 13% in a single session. Against that backdrop, Wednesday's shares traded up roughly 3% in premarket dealing, a modest bounce that reflects relief rather than repricing. Leerink Partners raised its price target to $68 on unrelated data for Ionis's Alexander-disease drug zilganersen the same day; JPMorgan had already trimmed its target to $70 after the pelacarsen miss, while H.C. Wainwright held a $100 target throughout.

The two Phase 3 wins do restore some credibility to Ionis's partner-and-license model, which depends on outside companies funding late-stage trials in exchange for royalties. But the muted stock reaction suggests investors are pricing sefaxersen's Novartis competition and ulefnersen's narrow patient pool more than the binary readout itself.

Full sefaxersen data are expected at a medical conference before Roche and Ionis pursue regulatory filings; Otsuka and Ionis said they plan to take the FUSION results to the FDA to discuss an expedited submission path for ulefnersen. Both filings, and any updated royalty guidance from Ionis at its next earnings call, will tell more about what these results are actually worth than Tuesday's announcements did.
