Tech sell-off drags markets as AI bubble worries grow

In one sentence
AI-linked shares fell from Asia to the US, hitting chipmakers hardest, but there is no confirmed Anthropic share move: the company has filed for an IPO and is not shown trading.

A sell-off in artificial-intelligence-linked shares rolled from Asia into Europe and the United States this week. The selling hit chipmakers and AI infrastructure names hardest, and it revived arguments about how much investors should pay for a technology whose spending is running well ahead of its profits. One point needs stating plainly. The reported figures describe listed companies. They do not show any move in shares of Anthropic, and this piece does not claim one (see below).

What moved

The selling began on Sunday night in Asia, according to Computing's account of the session. SoftBank fell 11% on Monday and recovered 7.5% the next morning. SK Hynix and Samsung fell sharply. TSMC lost a little over 1%. In Europe, ASML dropped 6%.

US moves the previous day were concentrated in semiconductors and AI hardware: Nvidia lost 3.3%, AMD 4%, Micron 5% and Intel 6%. Oracle fell 3.6% and Amazon a little over 1%. Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta edged higher, so the damage was uneven rather than a clean sweep of the megacaps.

The source gives no index levels or index percentage changes, and it carries only relative dates. A figure for how far the Nasdaq has fallen from its October record varies by outlet. One report puts the drawdown near 12%, while another dated report in late October had it nearer 6%. Those numbers cannot be reconciled without a dated closing-level report, so none is relied on here.

Why now

The trigger looks less like a single piece of data than a pile-up of doubts. SoftBank has invested or committed around $65bn in OpenAI, and OpenAI has postponed its planned listing this year, with Sam Altman saying the company would "focus on safety". That is a direct hit to the funding chain behind SoftBank's own shares, which helps explain the size of its Monday fall.

Policy added to the unease. Dario Amodei published a proposal for third-party evaluators to check AI labs' safety commitments, and committed Anthropic to it. Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI have each called for the industry to "pace the frontier". President Trump dismissed AI regulation as a "sick conspiracy". Higher oil prices, with the Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed, supplied a separate squeeze on valuations.

Taken together, the evidence points to investors cutting exposure to AI hardware, where earnings are most exposed to a slower build-out, rather than to a repricing of AI software and platforms.

The Anthropic question

The original framing assumed a tradeable Anthropic stock. The record does not support that. Anthropic filed confidentially for a US listing on 1 June, according to reports, and was last privately valued at $965bn after a funding round confirmed on 28 May. Mid-September reports said it had chosen Nasdaq. No source reviewed shows it trading, and the Computing article gives no ticker or share-price move for it. Anthropic enters this story as a company whose safety stance, and whose own pending listing, feed the market's mood, not as a stock that fell.

What to watch

Two things will show whether this is a rotation or something deeper. The first is a dated closing report for the main US indices, which would settle the size of the drawdown. The second is the next steps on the listings now in limbo: OpenAI's delayed offering, and any public filing from Anthropic. A listing that prices well would suggest appetite for AI risk has survived the week. A further delay would suggest the market is waiting for proof of returns on the infrastructure spending first.
