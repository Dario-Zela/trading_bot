Alcaraz wins four titles in 84 days since his return

In one sentence
Alcaraz capped four titles since returning from a wrist injury with a first ATP Finals win over Zverev, though the full list of events needs checking.

Carlos Alcaraz finished the season the way he started it, with a trophy in his hands. On Sunday in Turin he beat world No. 1 Alexander Zverev 6-2, 7-6 to win the ATP Finals for the first time. By the count in the coverage, that was his fourth title since he came back from a wrist injury at the US Open: 84 days, give or take, from the first ball in New York to the last in Italy.

The layoff

The comeback needs context, because the run only looks remarkable against what came before it. Alcaraz won the Australian Open in January to complete his career Grand Slam, and added Doha in February. A right wrist problem, later described as tenosynovitis, then took him out of the French Open, Wimbledon and the North American hard-court swing. He was away for roughly four months.

He returned at the US Open, which he had entered as the defending champion, and reached the quarter-finals before losing. That was the only defeat in the stretch that followed, by the reported tally: 22 wins and one loss, per the Turin coverage.

The four titles

The first trophy came in Tokyo in early October. Alcaraz beat Jiri Lehecka 7-6(4), 6-4 to retain the Japan Open, his first title since Doha in February. He became the first man to win consecutive Japan Open titles since Pete Sampras in 1993 and 1994. "What a journey since I got injured," he said afterwards.

The Rolex Paris Masters followed, with a final win over Ben Shelton on 8 November, according to Tennis Watcher. Reports also credit him with a title in Vienna, though the sourcing there is thinner than for the other three, and the full tournament list is worth checking against the ATP's records before it is treated as settled.

Then came Turin. Alcaraz saved three set points to take the second-set tiebreak. Accounts differ on its score: Al Jazeera and the Japan Times give 9-7, Tennis Watcher says 7-6(7). Forbes put it as his 14th consecutive match win.

How historic is it?

The "historic" label is easier to defend for the Finals win than for the four-in-84-days count. Forbes says Alcaraz is only the fourth man to hold all four Grand Slams and the ATP Finals. Al Jazeera calls him the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam, and Vanguard notes he is the first Spanish champion at the Finals since Alex Corretja in 1998. It is also a title Rafael Nadal never won.

The four-titles figure is a neat statistic rather than a record. Vanguard counts the Finals as his sixth title of 2026, which is a better guide to the season: two before the injury, four after it. Coming back from four months out and then winning at that rate says more about his form than any record-book line.

What next

The season is over, so the next test is the Australian Open in January, where Alcaraz will defend the title and, for the first time, do so as a player who has won every major and the Finals. The wrist, which cost him half a year of tennis, is the variable to watch.
