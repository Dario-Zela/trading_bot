Russell Brand's wife vows to abide by prison rules after video controversy

In one sentence
Russell Brand's wife read his denial of guilt online, prompting a Ministry of Justice inquiry, but a vow to follow prison rules and an age-of-consent review are unconfirmed.

Russell Brand is in prison, his wife has read a message from him in which he denies guilt, and the Ministry of Justice is looking into whether that broke the rules. The headline's claim that Laura Brand has "vowed to abide by prison rules" and the suggestion that ministers are reviewing the age of consent could not be confirmed in the reporting reviewed for this article.

What the reporting shows

Brand, the comedian turned online commentator, is serving a lengthy sentence after being convicted of sexual offences. Coverage of his case refers to a 10-year term and a move to HMP Littlehey, a Category C prison. The detail of the conviction and sentence is taken from secondary and tabloid-leaning outlets and should be checked against court records or a primary outlet.

The present controversy concerns a video. His wife, whose name appears as both Laura Brand and Laura Gallacher in different outlets, read a statement from him on the couple's online show. In it he maintained: "I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for." Some reports, citing The Sun, say he dictated it to her by phone from prison.

The government's response was swift. Ministry of Justice officials opened an urgent inquiry into whether the message breached prison rules. A Justice Secretary described the broadcast as unacceptable, and the Prime Minister's official spokesman called it "clearly appalling". One of his accusers, Mia Latter, called it an "insult" to victims.

The rules at issue

As reported, UK prison rules bar inmates from making public statements that distress victims, and from asking someone else to publish a statement on their behalf. Under the Prison Rules 1999, governors can restrict an inmate's communications with people outside. Reports say Brand could lose phone privileges and even contact with his wife, though no punishment has been announced. One account also says his low-security placement could be at risk.

Laura Brand was reported visiting him in prison within days of the video, which suggests the couple's contact had not been cut off at that point. None of the coverage reviewed quotes her promising to follow prison rules. That framing may come from a later statement not yet found, or it may be a loose paraphrase.

The age-of-consent claim

The triage brief links the episode to ministers reviewing the age of consent. Searches turned up no report of such a review, no named minister or department, and no connection to Brand's case. Until a primary source such as the original Guardian piece is checked, it should be treated as unsupported. If a review exists, it would be a separate policy story and not a consequence of the video.

What to watch

The next marker is the outcome of the Ministry of Justice inquiry: whether the prison service imposes limits on Brand's calls or visits, and whether it addresses the question of statements made on his behalf. Any confirmed government review of consent law would need an official announcement, which has not appeared.
