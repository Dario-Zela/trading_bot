Russia-bound ship towed away after attack in Gulf of Finland

In one sentence
A Russian-flagged cargo ship hit by drones in the Gulf of Finland was towed out of Finnish waters; Finland says Ukraine was probably responsible, a second Baltic strike this month.

A Russian-flagged cargo ship has been hit by drones in the Gulf of Finland and towed out of Finnish waters. Finland's armed forces say Ukraine was probably behind the strike. Kyiv's own account was not available at the time of writing, so attribution rests on Helsinki's assessment.

The vessel, the Sparta II, is a roll-on/roll-off cargo ship built in 2000. It was struck on Saturday, 21 November, west of Gogland Island and off the Finnish town of Loviisa, in international waters. Leningrad governor Alexander Drozdenko said it was bound for Kaliningrad and was hit by two drones about three nautical miles from the Russian border. His counterpart in Kaliningrad, Alexey Besprozvannykh, said supplies to the exclave were not at risk.

A burning ship in a crowded sea

The strike set the ship on fire, and the crew was taken off by a nearby cargo vessel and is reportedly being returned to Russia. Finnish Border Guard vessels and aircraft, two civilian rescue tugs and an Estonian navy patrol ship joined the firefighting effort. At Finland's request, the Russian rescue ship Murman also helped. Finland imposed temporary air and sea traffic restrictions in the eastern gulf.

Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen said the Sparta II would be towed out of Finnish territorial waters on Saturday night. Finland has an obvious interest in getting it out. The gulf is shallow, busy and narrow, and the ship was burning close to the Finnish coast.

Finnish officials said the ship has links to Russia's armed forces, though its cargo is unconfirmed. Border Guard Commander Ilja Iljin said military materiel on board could not be ruled out. That matters for how the strike is read. A ship carrying military supplies is a more conventional target than a commercial vessel, and it is a distinction Kyiv has leaned on in the past.

Prime Minister Petteri Orpo wrote on X that "war is spilling over into our immediate vicinity." Häkkänen also noted a Russian helicopter had violated Finnish airspace by more than four kilometres, possibly in connection with rescue or tracking operations.

Who did it, and what it signals

Finland's Defence Forces said by Saturday afternoon that Ukraine most likely carried out the attack. That is a judgement, not a claim of responsibility, and the ship's cargo and the exact launch point remain unconfirmed.

The incident follows another earlier this month. Finland reported a strike on a Russian warship in the same waters, which Ukraine claimed. Helsinki then raised its concerns with Kyiv about strikes in the area. Ukraine and Russia have attacked each other's shipping throughout the war, but mostly in the Black Sea. Two incidents in a few weeks suggest the Baltic is becoming a second arena, which is the part that worries littoral states.

The risk is partly environmental. Robin Haggblom, a Finnish maritime risk expert, said the absence of ice in the gulf would help any oil-spill response. He also warned of the dangers posed by poorly maintained "shadow fleet" tankers in these waters. The Sparta II is not a tanker, but the point carries: a burning vessel adrift near the Finnish coast is a pollution and navigation hazard for countries that are not party to the war.

What to watch

Three things will shape the story. One is whether Ukraine confirms or disclaims the strike, and how Finland responds if it does. Another is what the Sparta II was carrying, which the Finnish authorities have not yet established. The third is whether Helsinki's earlier representations to Kyiv produce any change, or whether Baltic traffic restrictions of the kind imposed on Saturday become routine.
