Barclays points to double-digit EPS growth in 2027 and £10bn-plus payouts

In one sentence
A MarketBeat claim of double-digit 2027 EPS growth and £10bn-plus payouts at Barclays looks like analyst consensus, not guidance; the bank's own target is £15bn-plus for 2026–28.

Barclays has not, on the evidence available, guided to double-digit earnings-per-share growth in 2027 or to £10bn-plus in shareholder payouts. The claim traces to a MarketBeat item. That aggregator routinely republishes analyst estimates, and 23 November is not a Barclays results date. The figures read as sell-side expectations, not company guidance, and the payout number sits well below what the bank has actually committed to.

What the bank has said

Barclays' current three-year plan, covering 2026 to 2028, targets more than £15bn of distributions, through dividends and buybacks, with return on tangible equity above 12% in 2026 and above 14% in 2028. The £10bn figure belongs to the previous plan, which promised at least £10bn to shareholders across 2024 to 2026. A £10bn-plus headline therefore either recycles that older commitment or understates the present one. In February, Bloomberg reported the bank's pledge as £15bn after a profit beat.

Distributions are running ahead of that pace. A Q2 2026 results transcript put first-half distributions at £2.3bn, up 61% on the first half of 2025.

On earnings, the record is strong but backward-looking. Earnings per share rose 22% in 2025, and management has described itself as on track for a third consecutive year of roughly 20% growth. A search of the bank's published materials found no explicit 2027 EPS growth target.

Where 'double-digit' probably comes from

The more plausible source is analyst consensus. MarketBeat's Barclays coverage in 2026 consists largely of broker estimate changes. Erste Group Bank, for example, put FY2027 EPS for the New York-listed shares at $3.36 in the spring and $3.41 by May, against $2.81 for FY2026. That implies growth of about 21% on the later figures, though the quoted bases mix periods and should be treated loosely. Third-party aggregators show forward EPS growth in the low-teens: Simply Wall St cites about 12.5%, and Stockanalysis.com an average 2027 forecast near 13.7%, from an undated snapshot. All of this is double-digit. None of it is guidance.

MarketBeat's consensus rating on the stock is Hold, with three Buys and four Holds. That is hardly the profile of a bank being re-rated on a company promise.

What would change the picture

If Barclays had formally raised its outlook, it would be a read-across for UK bank valuations and capital policy, and Lloyds, NatWest and HSBC would be the obvious comparisons. No such statement has been found. The next hard data point is the full-year 2026 results, due in February, where the bank will update its distribution run-rate and may say something about 2027. Until then the item is a single-source aggregator claim, and the company's own £15bn-plus target is the figure to use.
