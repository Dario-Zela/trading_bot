Boy, 7, pulled alive from rubble hours after Russian strike

In one sentence
A boy of seven was pulled alive from rubble in Dymer, near Kyiv, about 14 hours after a Russian strike that killed at least six people, with the toll still rising.

A seven-year-old boy was pulled alive from the ruins of a residential building near Kyiv on Sunday, about 14 hours after a Russian strike brought down part of the block, rescuers said. He was reported to be in stable condition and was taken to hospital.

The building is in Dymer, a village roughly 45km (28 miles) north of the Ukrainian capital. Accounts of the attack say it came before dawn on 22 November, at around 05:00 local time, and was carried out with drones. Ukraine's national police and President Volodymyr Zelensky both confirmed the rescue.

A rising toll

The boy's survival sits against a grim and shifting casualty count. Early figures from the Kyiv regional governor, Tymur Tkachenko, put the dead at six, with six injured and others still unaccounted for. Later reports citing his Telegram posts said the toll had risen to ten, and that a 79-year-old woman had also been brought out alive. Other outlets gave a figure of nine. The numbers differ by source and by the hour at which they were taken, and should be treated as provisional until the regional authorities publish a final tally.

Zelensky said more than 130 people were working at the site and that the operation would continue until everyone had been accounted for.

The work of the rescuers

Pulling a survivor out after so long is unusual but not unheard of. Ukraine's State Emergency Service has carried out hundreds of such operations since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022, typically working through collapsed floors by hand and with listening equipment once heavy machinery risks shifting the debris. Survivals after double-digit hours are rare enough to be reported individually, and they tend to be reported for that reason: they are exceptions in a pattern dominated by recovered bodies.

That pattern is the wider context. Strikes on residential buildings, in Kyiv and its surrounding region as well as in cities such as Kharkiv, Odesa and Zaporizhzhia, have produced regular reports of children among the casualties. Moscow says it targets military and energy infrastructure; the toll from apartment blocks says otherwise to those who live in them.

What to watch

The immediate questions are local: whether further survivors are found, and the final death toll from Dymer once the debris is cleared. The boy's condition, described only as stable, has not been detailed. The attack also arrives as diplomatic efforts over the war continue to be debated in Western capitals, a backdrop that gives each such strike political as well as human weight.
