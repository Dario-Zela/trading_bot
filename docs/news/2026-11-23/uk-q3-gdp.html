UK economy grew 0.7% in Q3, its fastest in two years

In one sentence
The ONS put Q3 UK growth at 0.7%, up from 0.5% and the fastest in nearly two years, but the figure was expected and is provisional until revised on 22 December.

The UK economy grew by 0.7% in the three months to September, the Office for National Statistics said on 12 November, the quickest quarterly pace in nearly two years. The figure matches what economists polled by Reuters had expected, so the surprise was in the trend, not the number.

What the ONS reported

The first quarterly estimate put gross domestic product 0.7% higher in July to September than in the previous quarter, up from 0.5% growth in the second quarter. Output was 1.9% higher than a year earlier. The ONS bulletin is the primary source; ITV News, City AM and AJ Bell's market commentary all report the same headline.

Services did most of the work, rising 0.9% on the quarter. Coverage of the release attributes part of the pickup to technology and defence-related spending, with AI-related build-out cited by several outlets. Reports differ on how much business investment contributed, and the ONS tables are the place to settle that. Construction was weaker.

The monthly path was less impressive than the quarterly total. September output rose 0.2%, while August was revised down to no growth, so much of the quarter's strength was built earlier in the period. The quarterly figure is also provisional. Across the series since 2000, first estimates have differed from the final published figure by an average of about 0.27 percentage points in either direction.

A note on sourcing

The growth figure first reached this desk through a newsletter whose link pointed to an unrelated story. That source was not relied on. The figures above come from the ONS and from mainstream outlets that match it.

Why it is a context piece

A single quarter's GDP is a data point, and this one landed 11 days ago and was widely anticipated. It carries weight only through the decisions it feeds. Faster growth, if it holds, narrows the case for the Bank of England to cut Bank Rate quickly. It also affects the Treasury's room for manoeuvre and the pricing of gilts. The Bank has not, on the evidence gathered here, changed course in response to the release, and this piece does not assume it will. Two quarters of acceleration, from 0.5% to 0.7%, are not yet a trend, particularly with August flat.

What to watch

The ONS publishes its fuller quarterly national accounts on 22 December. That release breaks growth down by expenditure and will show whether investment, consumption or government spending drove the quarter. It will also revise the headline. The monthly GDP release for October is the next test of whether September's modest gain was the start of momentum or a pause, and it is the figure the Bank's rate-setters are likely to weigh next.
