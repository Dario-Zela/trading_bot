Poundland's restructuring: store closures and creditor pressure

In one sentence
Poundland, with about 600 stores and 12,000 staff, faces administration on 26 November unless owner Gordon Brothers lands a buyer, with unpaid suppliers and HMRC among creditors.

Poundland, the discount chain with roughly 600 shops across the UK and Ireland, is days from a deadline that could end in administration. Its owner, the US firm Gordon Brothers, has not found a buyer at its asking price. Specialists have reportedly been asked to draw up plans for winding down hundreds of stores if no deal emerges. The protection from creditors that Poundland's directors hold is due to lapse on Wednesday, 26 November.

This is a different story from the one in June 2025, when the chain closed 68 stores through a court-approved restructuring plan. Poundland was then sold for £1 to Gordon Brothers, and the plan deferred repayment of about £276.5m of loans. The restructuring did not resolve the underlying problem, and the creditor question has since become more acute.

What is at stake

The Telegraph, in a report published on 20 November and syndicated by Yahoo Finance, puts the group at about 600 stores and 12,000 employees, with revenue of £1.5bn last year. Other reports have cited around 11,000 jobs. The figures differ, and none has been confirmed by the company. The BBC article this piece was commissioned from could not be read directly, so the numbers here come from secondary coverage.

The pressure comes from creditors who are not banks. Many suppliers are reportedly unpaid and have stopped delivering, leaving shelves thin. Poundland is also said to be unable to meet its tax bills to HMRC. Gordon Brothers has been reported to have held back about £60m of funds. It says the money needed for trading is still being released. Gordon Brothers has also ruled itself out of closing stores itself should the company enter administration, according to the Telegraph. In an insolvency, unpaid suppliers would generally rank behind secured lenders.

Who might buy

Three bidders are reported. None has been confirmed as meeting the reported £30m upfront price Gordon Brothers is said to want.

  • Xyra Group, a little-known investment firm, has not disclosed its backers and has been asked to prove its funding. Its plan leans on e-commerce and click-and-collect, which would need substantial investment. A senior retail executive told the Telegraph they had "never heard of" it.
  • Martin Property Group, an Irish developer, is in talks with Gordon Brothers and has reportedly offered £5m upfront.
  • Zachert Private Equity, a German buyout group, made a late bid on Saturday. It has not completed a British retail deal before, though one source called its interest serious.

Salling Group has reportedly withdrawn. Some rival retailers may bid for individual shops in a break-up, though that is not certain.

What to watch

The gap between a £5m upfront offer and a £30m ask is wide. A further extension of the moratorium is seen as unlikely. One source told the Telegraph the business "is in a worse position now than it was a week ago." Xyra has said it will provide no more information before Tuesday. Wednesday's expiry is therefore the next test. If no deal is agreed, administrators would take over, with Modella Capital and Hilco named as possible firms to oversee a wind-down. The size of any closure list, and how much landlords and suppliers recover, would be known only after that.
