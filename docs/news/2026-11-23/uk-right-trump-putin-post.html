British right distances itself from Trump after Putin 'friend' post

In one sentence
Reform UK's Richard Tice and Conservative Alex Burghart publicly criticised Trump's "My friend" post with Putin, a sign of cooling among his natural UK allies.

Donald Trump has posted a photograph of himself with Vladimir Putin, captioned "My friend". Within hours, senior figures in the two British parties that most warmly welcomed his return to the White House were saying publicly that he had got it wrong.

The image was shared on Truth Social and reported on Sunday 22 November. It was one of a series from Trump's trip to China, where he and Putin met on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on 19 November. Trump also met Xi Jinping on the trip.

The reaction

Richard Tice, deputy leader of Reform UK, answered with a single word: "Horror." He went on: "It's just wrong at every level … And it's a grave mistake." Of the Russian president he added: "He is not our friend, Putin." Another report of the same remarks has him calling Putin "a murderous dictator".

Alex Burghart, deputy to the Conservative leader, Kemi Badenoch, said he was "very sad" about the post. His concern went beyond the picture itself. "I worry that we might be moving into a world where that isn't the case any more," he said, in a reference to the assumption that the United States stands on the side of freedom. He said the Conservatives remained "on the side of freedom" and would meet Nato's defence-spending goals.

From government, the Foreign Office minister Hamish Falconer called Putin "a butcher". That is the expected line from a Labour-led government. The notable voices are those on the right.

A widening gap

Reform and the Conservatives both welcomed Trump's 2024 election win, and Reform's leader, Nigel Farage, has long been counted among his friends. Reports of the reaction say both parties have been putting more distance between themselves and the president. The concerns cited are his temperament and judgement, and his attacks on Iran, which have pushed up global energy prices. Tice said Reform has disagreed with Trump more often of late, including, by his account, Farage.

The pattern is worth reading with some care. Criticism of a Putin photograph costs a British politician very little: Russia's war on Ukraine is a position where public opinion is not divided, and a YouGov poll cited in coverage put favourable views of Trump among Britons at 10%, against 86% unfavourable. A party courting voters has a clear incentive to be heard saying so. What has changed is that the criticism now comes from Trump's natural allies rather than only from his opponents, and that it is aimed at his Russia line specifically rather than at his manner.

Trump, asked whether Putin was a friend of the United States, reportedly said he could not speak to that, and suggested that calling Putin a friend meant the Russian leader did not feel enough pressure.

What to watch

The test is whether the words shift anything in policy. Burghart's Nato pledge and Tice's insistence that Putin is no friend are statements of position, not commitments. The next signals are likely to be how each party handles any US push towards a settlement on Ukraine, and whether Farage himself, the figure with the most to lose from a rupture, joins his deputy in public.
