GSK: lung cancer approval and EMA panel backing for Bexsero booster

In one sentence
The FDA has cleared GSK's second Nuvalent-derived lung cancer drug, while an EMA panel backed a Bexsero booster from age 10 that still needs European Commission sign-off.

GSK has had a good week with regulators on both sides of the Atlantic. In the United States, the FDA has cleared a second lung cancer medicine for the company. In Europe, the European Medicines Agency's human-medicines committee has backed a one-dose booster of the meningitis B vaccine Bexsero for people aged 10 and over. The first is an approval. The second is only a recommendation, and the difference matters.

The lung cancer approval

The drug is Nelkarmi (neladalkib), which the FDA has cleared for adults with previously treated ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer, according to trade-press reports (Fierce Pharma). It is an accelerated approval, and it is narrow. It covers patients who have already had Alecensa (alectinib), or at least two ALK inhibitors plus platinum-based chemotherapy. The decision came ahead of the FDA's original target date of 27 November. This article could not confirm the exact day of approval.

The drug is not GSK's own discovery. It arrived through the company's roughly $10.6bn acquisition of Nuvalent, which also brought Jideytro (zidesamtinib). The FDA approved Jideytro in July for previously treated ROS1-positive lung cancer, about two months before its target date, and it was GSK's first lung cancer medicine (BioSpace). Two approvals in about four months give the deal a quick return on the purchase price.

The market effect is harder to state. The only source in the original triage was a Simply Wall St note, which called the shares reasonably valued rather than cheap. It named no drug and gave no price move, and this article found no independent figure for one. Accelerated approvals also rest on surrogate endpoints, so confirmatory trial data will still have to show benefit. Sales forecasts for such narrow, later-line indications are therefore uncertain.

GSK's other lung asset is still in development. Risvutatug rezetecan, a small-cell lung cancer candidate, has reported positive Phase 3 overall-survival data against standard of care, but it is not approved.

A committee opinion, not a licence

The European item is a different kind of news. The Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) has recommended updating Bexsero's label to allow a single booster dose for people aged 10 and older who were vaccinated at least two years earlier (ChemXplore). The change still needs European Commission approval, which normally follows a CHMP opinion but is not automatic. This article did not find the date of the opinion in an EMA document.

The evidence is the Phase IIIb Men-B-096 study, which enrolled 312 participants in Finland, Italy, Spain and the UK. Of these, 234 had received Bexsero in infancy and 78 had never had a MenB vaccine. The secondary report says the study met its primary endpoint and that safety matched the vaccine's known profile. It also says the data suggest immune memory from infant vaccination can be boosted by a dose given up to 19 years later. These figures come from secondary coverage and should be checked against the EMA's assessment and GSK's release. The report gave no dosing schedule beyond the two-year minimum.

What ties the two together

The approval and the opinion sit at different stages. Nelkarmi is cleared in the US but conditionally, and Bexsero's booster is endorsed in Europe but not yet licensed. Both are incremental additions to existing franchises rather than new revenue lines. Those are the grounds for the description of the shares as fairly valued rather than cheap.

The next dated catalysts are the European Commission's decision on the Bexsero label and any confirmatory data GSK reports for Nelkarmi.
