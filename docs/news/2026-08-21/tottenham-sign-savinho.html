Tottenham agree deal to sign Manchester City's Savinho

Savinho playing for Manchester City against Palermo, August 2025 — SonoGrazy / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Spurs have agreed a deal worth up to £86m for Man City's Savinho, City's biggest-ever sale, racing to register him before Saturday's opener.

Tottenham Hotspur have agreed a deal to sign Savinho from Manchester City, with the Brazilian winger expected in London for a medical before Spurs' Premier League opener against Brentford on Saturday. Fabrizio Romano first reported a verbal agreement worth "over £75m" late this week; subsequent reporting from Yahoo Sports and Brazilian outlet Ge Globo has the package closer to £80m guaranteed plus performance add-ons, taking the total value toward €99m — roughly £86m.

However the final number lands, it will be Manchester City's largest-ever outgoing sale, surpassing the £82m Atlético Madrid paid for Julian Álvarez last year. That City are prepared to cash in on a 22-year-old they signed permanently only in 2024 says as much about squad depth at the Etihad as it does about Tottenham's ambition — Pep Guardiola already has Jeremy Doku, Phil Foden and Rayan Cherki competing for wide berths, with Omar Marmoush also in the building.

Why Savinho, why now

For Roberto De Zerbi, the appeal is tactical as much as reputational. Tottenham spent long stretches of last season without a recognised wide outlet, at one point pushing the full-back Djed Spence into a wing role out of necessity. Savinho — left-footed, happiest cutting in from the right, a proven crosser and dribbler with two full Premier League seasons behind him — fills that gap directly rather than as a projection.

The player's own motivation is playing-time, not pay. Reports from both English and Brazilian outlets describe a squad member who pushed for the move after concluding that regular starts at City were no longer guaranteed. That his release coincides with City's pursuit of Marmoush as a like-for-like creative reinforcement suggests the exit was planned rather than reluctantly conceded.

The clock Spurs are racing

The deal's urgency is administrative rather than financial. The Premier League's registration deadline falls at midday on Friday for a player to be eligible for Saturday's fixtures — Tottenham's medical and paperwork need to clear that window for Savinho to feature against Brentford. Miss it, and summer signing Mathys Tel or academy option Wilson Odobert would likely deputise instead, with Savinho held back for the following weekend's home debut against Newcastle.

It would be Tottenham's seventh signing of the summer, part of a rebuild under De Zerbi that chairman Daniel Levy has so far funded without the usual late-window hesitation. Whether that spending translates into results starts to be tested from Saturday afternoon.
