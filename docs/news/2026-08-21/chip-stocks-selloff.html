Chip stocks selloff continues ahead of Nvidia's earnings next week

NVIDIA's Santa Clara headquarters — the company reports earnings on 26 August, the week's central catalyst for the chip sector. — Coolcaesar / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
In one sentence
Chip stocks fell ~5% — Intel and AMD each down 4% — as Iran-driven oil jitters and AI-spending ROI doubts collide ahead of Nvidia's 26 August earnings.

Semiconductor stocks fell hard this week, with the sector down roughly 5% and heavyweights Intel and AMD each shedding 4% in a single session, as traders positioned ahead of Nvidia's earnings report on 26 August. Broadcom fell even further, down 5% to $361.73, while Nvidia itself — the name everyone is waiting on — was roughly unchanged at $219.54, an unusual divergence that traders read as a sign the selling was tactical rather than a broad loss of conviction in the AI trade.

The proximate trigger looks less like a single event than a convergence of two separate anxieties: renewed uncertainty over Iran, and a slower-building worry about whether the money being poured into AI infrastructure will ever generate a return commensurate with its scale.

The Iran overhang

The geopolitical piece is the more visible of the two. Brent crude pushed above $94 a barrel this week after attacks near the Strait of Hormuz and Washington's decision to tighten sanctions on Iran further, with the White House ruling out near-term talks. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has framed the sanctions campaign as a deliberate alternative to military escalation — but for a technology sector already jumpy about margins and export controls, prolonged uncertainty around the world's most important oil chokepoint adds a layer of macro risk that has nothing to do with silicon.

That tension is compounding a market already on edge over Treasury yields — the 30-year hit a 19-year high near 5.33% earlier in the month — and the chip selloff has continued even on days when yields eased. On the session that saw Intel and AMD each drop 4%, the 10-year yield actually fell five basis points to 4.65%, according to 24/7 Wall St., which undercuts the idea that bond markets alone are driving the move. That points toward the second, more structural story.

The AI spending question

The deeper unease is about capital discipline. JPMorgan estimates AI-related capital expenditure will approach $870 billion in 2026, with hyperscaler debt financing surging to roughly $190 billion as Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta borrow to fund data-centre build-outs at a pace their free cash flow can no longer cover on its own. Goldman Sachs has flagged that hyperscaler leverage ratios have roughly doubled over six months, and Citi has warned some of the largest cloud players could run negative free cash flow between 2027 and 2028 if the buildout continues at its current rate. Oracle's five-year credit default swaps, watched by traders as a proxy for AI debt risk, are trading near multi-year highs.

None of this means the AI infrastructure story is wrong — data centre demand remains real, and Nvidia's own numbers have borne that out for eight consecutive quarters. But the market's patience for capex without near-term payoff appears to be thinning, and semiconductor stocks, as the most direct proxy for that spending, are absorbing the doubt first. Intel is up 162% year-to-date and AMD 126%, gains large enough that a pullback of a few percentage points ahead of a binary earnings event reads as ordinary profit-taking as much as a verdict on the thesis itself.

What Nvidia's report has to answer

All of this sets up Nvidia's 26 August earnings as the sector's referendum. Analysts are looking for roughly $92-95 billion in quarterly revenue, up more than 60% year-on-year, with data-centre sales — driven by Blackwell shipments — expected to account for close to 90% of the total. Prediction markets currently put the odds of a beat above 94%. Nvidia also has a modest tailwind: the Financial Times has reported that China recently allowed limited shipments of Nvidia's H200 chips, with ByteDance and Tencent each receiving around 10,000 units, a small crack in an export relationship that has otherwise been closed for over a year.

Bank of America's Vivek Arya argues Nvidia now trades at a 40-50% discount to AI compute peers on an enterprise-value-to-free-cash-flow basis, calling it underpriced relative to the growth on offer. That view will be tested directly next week. A clean beat with guidance that reassures on demand durability could reverse the week's losses across the sector quickly, given how much of the move looks like positioning rather than conviction. A miss, or even in-line numbers paired with cautious commentary on AI-spending payback, would hand the ROI skeptics their first hard data point in months — and Intel, AMD and Broadcom, none of which have Nvidia's dominant market share to fall back on, would likely take the larger hit.

The next scheduled catalyst is Nvidia's report itself, due after the close on 26 August. Between now and then, oil prices and any further escalation around the Strait of Hormuz remain the wildcard that could move chip stocks independent of anything the earnings call says.
