Google concentrates AI leadership in California as Hassabis steps back to chairman role

Demis Hassabis, who is moving from Google DeepMind CEO to the lab's chairman role. — John Sears / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Google has moved Demis Hassabis upstairs to chairman and handed daily AI control to insider Koray Kavukcuoglu after missed model deadlines and a wave of senior departures.

Demis Hassabis has spent the past two years as the public face of Google's answer to ChatGPT — the Nobel laureate who explained neural networks on stage while his teams built the models underneath. As of this month, that day job belongs to someone else. Hassabis is moving to the chairmanship of Google DeepMind and taking the title of Alphabet chief scientist, while Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind's chief technology officer, steps up to run research and operations day to day.

In one sentence: Google has moved Demis Hassabis upstairs to chairman and handed daily control of its AI push to insider Koray Kavukcuoglu, as missed model deadlines and a wave of departures raise doubts about execution.

Google has framed the switch, announced in a memo from chief executive Sundar Pichai, as a handover Hassabis himself wanted — freeing him up to focus on "the big picture" of long-term research rather than the grind of shipping products. Kavukcuoglu now owns Gemini model development, frontier AI research and the developer teams building on top of it, reporting straight to Pichai. It is a tidy story about specialisation. It is also, on the numbers, a story about a company that has fallen behind schedule at the worst possible time.

A promotion that reads like a demotion

Hassabis co-founded DeepMind in London in 2010 and became, after Google's 2014 acquisition and DeepMind's 2023 merger with Google Brain, the person most identified with the company's AI ambitions. He kept the Isomorphic Labs drug-discovery venture he also runs. What he has given up is the operational seat — the one that decides when a model ships and to whom.

The timing is unflattering. Gemini 3.5 Pro, previewed by Pichai on stage at Google I/O in May, has missed three separate release targets — an implied June window, a June 30 date and a July 17 date — with Bloomberg reporting that Google retrained the model specifically to close a coding-capability gap and got underwhelming results. A rebuilt version has since fallen short of GPT-5.6 on benchmark tests and struggled with reliability. For a company that has told investors it will spend roughly $190–205 billion this year, up from $91.4 billion in 2025, on AI infrastructure, a slipping flagship model is not a footnote.

The exodus that preceded the reshuffle

Hassabis is not the only senior departure. Jeff Dean, Google's chief scientist and a 27-year veteran credited with building much of its AI and search infrastructure, is leaving to launch a startup called Discovery Loop, taking colleagues Sanjay Ghemawat, Quoc Le and Oriol Vinyals with him — Google is reportedly investing in the new venture. Weeks earlier, Gemini co-lead Noam Shazeer, whom Google paid roughly $2.7 billion to bring back from Character.AI in 2024, left for OpenAI. AlphaFold veterans including Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel have also gone, several to Anthropic, within days of the missed Gemini deadlines.

Internal accounts reported by Fortune describe 60-hour weeks, burnout, and staff frustration that Hassabis was often absent during crunch periods — a contrast, employees told the outlet, with Sergey Brin's more hands-on presence elsewhere in Google's AI effort. More than 580 employees also signed a letter opposing a Pentagon contract for military AI use, part of what organisers describe as the first unionisation push at a frontier AI lab. None of this alone explains a leadership change at the top of DeepMind. Together, it describes an organisation that was excelling at research publications and Nobel prizes while losing ground on the product roadmap that actually competes with ChatGPT and Claude.

What Kavukcuoglu inherits

Kavukcuoglu is a DeepMind lifer — he joined as a researcher in 2012 after working under Yann LeCun at NYU, rose to CTO, and was named Alphabet's chief AI architect in mid-2025 before this latest promotion. He is, by design, an execution hire rather than a visionary one: someone who now holds direct responsibility for the Gemini release calendar he previously helped oversee from one level down. Bloomberg's own commentary on the move was blunt, arguing the reshuffle exposes a company that has been "excelling at science and stumbling on execution," and that the new structure only works if it fixes the gap between Google's research output and its ability to ship competitive products on schedule.

There is also a geographic subtext. DeepMind's roots and much of its senior research bench are in London, and Bloomberg has reported that the reshuffle — layered on top of the departures — has undercut the UK's case for remaining a serious hub in the AI race, with decision-making now concentrated more firmly around Kavukcuoglu's Mountain View operation.

The next real test is Gemini 3.5 Pro's actual release, still pending after three missed dates, and whatever comes after it — Google has reportedly already begun training Gemini 4 in parallel. Investors will be watching whether the new structure produces faster ships or simply a faster excuse; Alphabet's next earnings call is the first checkpoint where Kavukcuoglu, not Hassabis, will be the one explaining the roadmap.
