From market stall to high street giant: how Poundland grew

In one sentence
Poundland began as one £1 shop in Burton upon Trent in 1990 and is now in a sale process, with The Range's owner reported among the suitors.

Poundland began in December 1990 as a single shop in a Staffordshire shopping centre. Thirty-six years on it is a chain with a sale process under way, three new independent directors on its board and a rival retailer reportedly circling its stores. The history is a useful way into that story. The idea that built the business, one price for everything, is also the thing that has been hardest to sustain.

The £1 idea

The founders were Dave Dodd and Steve Smith, backed by Keith Smith, who started with about £50,000 and an office in Sedgley. Smith's roots were in the Black Country market-stall trade, around Bilston. Their first shop opened in the Octagon Centre in Burton upon Trent. On its first day it turned over about £13,000 from 624 products.

The pitch was simple and the discipline was harder. Everything cost a pound, so there were no price labels to change and no haggling. Suppliers had to build a product to the price, and shoppers always knew what they would pay. The hundredth store opened in 2003. Smith sold his shares in 2006 for a reported £250m.

Changing owners

The chain then passed through private-equity hands before Poland's Pepco Group bought it in 2016, in a deal reported at about £610m. Pepco's years were marked by an attempt to widen the offer, with a loyalty scheme, home delivery and frozen food. Poundland also moved beyond the single price point, adding multi-price ranges.

In June 2025 Pepco sold the business to the US restructuring specialist Gordon Brothers for a nominal £1. Gordon Brothers promised up to £80m of investment and a court-sanctioned restructuring plan. More than 100 stores have since closed, and the owner scrapped the loyalty scheme, delivery and frozen food. The chain is expected to settle at around 650 to 700 stores, down from roughly 800.

The pricing has been simplified too. Grocery lines now sit at £1, £2 and £3.

A sale, a year on

The current pressure is specific. Gordon Brothers launched a sale process in the autumn, run with Alvarez & Marsal, and is reportedly aiming to finish before Christmas trading. Retail Gazette reported on 15 October that Chris Dawson, whose CDS Superstores owns The Range, had joined the race for some of the stores. Modella Capital has also been linked to the process.

On 16 October Gordon Brothers and Pepco, which keeps a minority stake, appointed Andrew Kirkpatrick, Stephen Welch and Dominic Young as independent directors. They form a majority of the board. Reports say the appointments could pave the way for an administration. Gordon Brothers says it is in active talks with several potential acquirers. One report says the bids are mostly from retail rivals and would buy parts of the business, which could leave the company itself insolvent. No buyer had been confirmed at the time of writing.

The local picture is the same. A Great Barr branch near Birmingham closed on 25 October after lease talks failed.

What to watch

The deadline is the Christmas trading period. A sale of the whole company would keep the brand intact. A sale of stores to rivals such as The Range would split the chain up. A third possibility is that Gordon Brothers keeps the business if no suitable deal emerges. A £1 shop that began in Burton upon Trent is now waiting for a verdict from bidders weighing what its roughly 700 sites are worth.
