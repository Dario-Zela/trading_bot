Verstappen and Lawson given grid penalties after Mexico practice collision

In one sentence
Verstappen and Lawson each drop one grid place and take a licence point after the stewards called their FP2 collision in Mexico City 'unacceptable'.

Max Verstappen and Liam Lawson will each start the Mexico City Grand Prix one place further back than they qualify. The stewards punished them for a collision in Friday's second practice session. It is an unusual sanction, since practice incidents rarely carry grid penalties, but the stewards were clear that this one crossed the line.

What happened

The contact came in FP2. Verstappen, driving for Red Bull, made a move on Lawson into Turn 12. Lawson, in the Racing Bulls car, fought back at the next left-hander. That forced Verstappen over the kerb, and he rejoined alongside Lawson. Debris from the contact brought out a red flag.

The stewards described the conduct of both drivers as "unacceptable". They noted that the team managers' meeting earlier on Friday had included a warning about overtaking in the final sector. Reports say both drivers accepted afterwards that their moves had been unnecessary and clumsy.

The penalty

Each driver receives a one-place grid penalty and one penalty point on their superlicence. The grid drop is the smallest the rulebook can impose, so the practical effect depends on how close the pair qualify to the cars around them. The licence points matter more over time. Twelve within a rolling year means a race ban.

Grid penalties are usually the currency of power-unit and gearbox changes. Stewards have the power to apply them for sporting infringements, though, and a warning that was ignored within hours made this one easy to justify. Punishing a practice collision at all is the unusual part.

Reaction

Verstappen called Lawson's approach "not very smart behaviour", according to coverage of his comments. Racing Bulls team boss Alan Permane described the clash as "completely unnecessary".

The wry subtext is that two drivers fighting hard for position in a session where nothing is at stake is exactly what the Friday warning was meant to prevent. Neither car had a result to protect. Both now carry a penalty point, which will matter if either gets involved in another incident later in the season.

What next

Qualifying on Saturday will show what the penalty costs each driver, and the race follows on Sunday. The figure to watch afterwards is the superlicence tally, because a further sanction for either driver would bring them closer to the twelve-point threshold.
