Celebrity Traitors reaches nail-biting final

In one sentence
The BBC says the Traitors finale drew a large audience and heavy online discussion, but no viewing figure or social-media measure has been published to back it up.

The Traitors, the BBC's parlour game of lies and loyalty, has reached its finale. The corporation says the closing episode drew a large audience and a great deal of chatter online. That is about as far as the available reporting goes. No viewing figure, overnight number or BARB total has been published, and no platform or example has been cited for the social-media reaction. Both claims are, for now, the broadcaster's description of its own success.

How the game works

For anyone who has missed it, the format is a murder-mystery party staged as a competition. A group of contestants gathers in a Scottish castle. A few are secretly chosen as "Traitors" and the rest are "Faithfuls". The Traitors eliminate a player each night. By day everyone argues, accuses and votes to banish a suspect. The Faithfuls win if every Traitor is removed. If any Traitor survives to the end, the Traitors take the prize pot.

The design explains the format's pull. Television reality formats usually reward the loudest personality. This one rewards the most convincing liar, and it gives viewers the same puzzle the players face: who is acting? Finales bring it to a head, because the group is small, the accusations are sharpest and the final choice carries real money.

What can and cannot be said

A big finale for a returning hit is the expected outcome, not a surprise. Strong audiences for the closing episodes of popular series are routine, and a lively social-media reaction comes with the territory for any show built on betrayal and a twist. Without numbers, there is nothing to compare against previous series or against rival Saturday-night and Sunday-night programming.

Three things would turn this into a story rather than a status report:

  • A verified audience figure. Consolidated viewing, which adds catch-up and streaming to the live count, is now the figure that matters most for a show like this. It would show whether the finale beat earlier series.
  • A record, if there is one. The claim would need a named benchmark, such as the biggest audience for a BBC reality format or for the channel in its slot.
  • A measurement angle. Broadcasters and the industry body BARB have spent years trying to capture viewing on phones, tablets and iPlayer, and social reach is harder still. If the BBC cites social engagement as evidence of success, the useful question is how it was counted.

None of these has been established yet.

What to watch

The next real data point is the consolidated ratings release, which should show how many people watched once catch-up viewing is counted. A published figure, plus any BBC statement on iPlayer requests for the series, would show whether the finale's reach matches the description. Until then, the finale is a well-liked piece of television with unverified numbers attached.
