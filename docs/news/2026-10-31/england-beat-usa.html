England beat USA to return to winning ways

In one sentence
England's Red Roses beat the USA 55-12 in New Jersey, ending a two-match losing run against Canada and the Eagles' five-game winning streak.

England's Red Roses ended a short but uncomfortable losing run on Friday, beating the USA 55-12 in New Jersey on the final weekend of the WXV Global Series. It was the first win after two successive defeats by Canada.

The result matters more than a routine win might. England arrived as the world's top-ranked side, and losing twice to Canada had raised awkward questions about that status. The USA, ranked sixth, were on a five-match winning streak, which this defeat ended.

A reply to the Canada losses

England scored nine tries at Sports Illustrated Stadium in Harrison, New Jersey. Claudia Moloney-MacDonald took four of them. Mia Venner added another, Maddie Feaunati scored in the second half, and Bryony Field finished a late maul try. The report available does not name the other scorers.

The USA were not passive. They kept contesting the game, and both of their tries came from their two most-capped players. One was a short-range score from prop Hope Rogers, which left her four points short of the USA's all-time points record.

The scrum was where England's physical edge showed. Hooker Amy Cokayne and the front row won several penalties, and USA prop Mona Lisa Topou was sin-binned in the 67th minute. RugbyPass credited England's patience in attack, along with a physicality that had faded in the Canada games.

Moloney-MacDonald said England's approach was simple: "We attacked it. From minute zero to minute 80." She said the focus afterwards was on learning from the Canada performances.

How much it settles

A 43-point win over the sixth-ranked side is a proper reply, but it is only a reply. BBC Sport described England as having returned to winning ways without entirely removing doubts over their position at the top of the women's game. Those doubts came from the defeats by Canada, and one result against the USA does not settle them. The scoreline shows England can still dominate a strong opponent when their forwards are on top. It does not show they have fixed whatever went wrong in the two losses.

The report does not give the WXV standings or say whether the result changes any qualification picture, so the stakes are best read as reputational.

What comes next

For head coach John Mitchell, the next question is squad shape. He must decide how and when to bring in new players before the Women's Six Nations next April, where England will be expected to answer the Canada defeats over a full tournament.

The USA take a different lesson. Their run through the WXV series, including five straight wins before this loss, sets up an interesting Pacific Four Series next spring.
