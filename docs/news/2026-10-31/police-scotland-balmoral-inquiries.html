Police Scotland make 'active inquiries' into Andrew and Epstein visits to Balmoral

In one sentence
Police Scotland is reported to be making 'active inquiries' into claimed Andrew and Epstein visits to Balmoral, but the BBC-sourced report is unconfirmed and gives no dates.

Police Scotland is reported to be making "active inquiries" into claims that Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor and Jeffrey Epstein visited Balmoral, the royal family's Aberdeenshire estate. The report comes from the BBC. We could not confirm it independently, and the details are thin. It cites no dates for the alleged visits, no statement from the force and no named complainant or referral.

What is known

The account rests on one report. It describes the inquiries as ongoing and says pressure on the royal household is continuing. Police Scotland's wording, "active inquiries", is a standard formula. It usually means officers are assessing information and have not opened a formal criminal investigation. It does not mean a suspect has been identified or a charge is likely.

The Balmoral link has been in the public record for some time. Documents released from the Epstein files include an email dated 16 August 2001 that referred to a "Balmoral Summer Camp for the Royal Family" and asked Ghislaine Maxwell about "inappropriate friends". Coverage has treated it as a document of interest. Our searches found no police action tied to it, and nothing in it establishes that Epstein himself was at the estate.

The wider police picture

Scotland would be a new front. The scrutiny of Andrew has so far centred on England. Thames Valley Police arrested him on suspicion of misconduct in public office. The Metropolitan Police has made "initial inquiries" into his former close protection officers. Earlier reporting also said the Met saw no basis for a criminal investigation into him over Epstein. Those accounts date mostly from early 2026 and may be out of date.

Police Scotland's only publicly documented role we could find is an earlier appeal for anyone with information about Epstein's private jet using Edinburgh Airport. That is a different matter from an inquiry into what happened on a Crown property.

What to watch

The first test is whether Police Scotland confirms the wording and says what prompted it. A statement would show whether this is a fresh referral or a review of material already public. Buckingham Palace has not, on the evidence available, commented on the Scottish inquiries. Any response from the palace, or any request to interview staff at Balmoral, would show whether the household is being treated as a witness or something more. Until then the report is best read as a sign of how far the Epstein questions around Andrew now reach, and not as evidence of wrongdoing at the estate.
