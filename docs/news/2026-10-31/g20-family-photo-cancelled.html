US cancels G20 'family photo' after UK, Australia and others refuse to pose with Russia

In one sentence
The US scrapped the G20 foreign ministers' group photo in Atlanta after Germany and other delegations refused to pose with Russia's Lavrov, exposing a Western split over normalising Moscow.

The United States has scrapped the traditional group photograph at a G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta after several delegations made clear they would not stand beside Russia's Sergey Lavrov. Washington, the host, chose to drop the ceremony rather than force it, or stage it with some chairs empty.

The photo is ceremonial, but its purpose is to project unity among the world's largest economies. Its absence is a small, visible sign of how far the Western allies disagree with the host about how normally Russia should be treated at the table.

What happened in Atlanta

The two-day meeting opened on Thursday 29 October, and Mr Lavrov attended the first plenary session. Reporting of the dispute came on Friday 30 October. The Irish Times reports that the US hosts scrapped the photo after several officials refused to pose with him. Of the countries involved, it names only Germany. Foreign minister Johann Wadephul said: "There is no 'business as usual' with Russia." He added that ministers "must also avoid sending the wrong visual signals."

The triage account for this piece, drawn from a Guardian UK headline, says the UK and Australia were among those refusing. I could not read the Guardian article itself. Reports I did find attribute the decision to European objections and say Australia's foreign minister reportedly backed the move, but none of the accessible accounts confirm the UK's or Australia's stance in their own words. Those two attributions should be read as reported, not verified.

The accounts also differ on who made the call. CNN, citing a US official, says Secretary of State Marco Rubio decided the argument over the photo would become a distraction that overshadowed the gathering. Another US official put it more simply: some delegations would not take part. Mr Lavrov, for his part, shrugged it off. If the photo mattered so much to others, he said, "that says it all."

A pattern, not an accident

This is not the first such episode. At the Bali summit in 2022, the foreign ministers' photo was skipped, and Kyodo reported that the leaders' photo was dropped as well. In Johannesburg in February 2025, South Africa blamed scheduling, though South African media reported that several Western ministers had declined to pose beside Mr Lavrov.

The closest precedent is recent. At a G20 finance meeting in Asheville, North Carolina, at the end of August, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent invited Russian finance minister Anton Siluanov, reportedly without consulting other members. It was his first in-person G20 appearance since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Germany and other allies refused to be photographed with him, and the group picture was taken without him, away from reporters. Germany's Lars Klingbeil called the invitation a "quite troubling" signal.

The common thread is that the United States holds this year's G20 presidency and sets the guest list. European governments cannot veto an invitation, so they have fallen back on the one tool they control: where they stand and who they stand next to.

Why a photograph matters

The dispute sits on top of real policy disagreement. European ministers who favour a hard line are expected to resist American efforts to draw Moscow into negotiations over Ukraine, and to push back on pressure to buy more Russian petroleum products. They are particularly unhappy that President Trump recently said he would waive some tariffs and sanctions as part of a deal letting Russia export diesel. The wars in Ukraine and Iran are expected to dominate the agenda.

The risk for the forum is practical. G20 communiqués need consensus, and a presidency that invites Russia back while allies refuse to be seen with its delegates makes joint language on Ukraine harder to agree. Cancelling the photo avoids an embarrassing image, but it does not resolve the split behind it.

What comes next

The test is the leaders' summit in December at the Trump National Doral Golf Club in Miami. Mr Trump has invited Vladimir Putin; the Kremlin has not said whether he will attend. If he does, the question of the photo, and of the communiqué, will return at a much higher level. Related Western diplomacy on Ukraine is covered in the report on the Patriot sale to Kyiv.
