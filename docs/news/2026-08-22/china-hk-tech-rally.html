China and Hong Kong stocks extend gains as Asian tech rally continues

Exchange Square in Central, Hong Kong, home to the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Hang Seng and CSI 300 extended gains on a tech rebound, but the rally tracked Wall Street's mood swings more than any easing in Treasury yields, which actually rose into Friday.

China's large-cap CSI 300 edged up 0.57% to close near 4,619 on Friday, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 1.2%, or 310 points, to 26,009 — capping a week in which the city's benchmark rose roughly 3% and snapped a two-week losing streak. The Hang Seng Tech Index climbed 1.4% to 4,766. Turnover in Hong Kong ran to HK$257.28 billion, a sign the move was carried on real volume rather than thin holiday trade.

The pattern underneath the headline number is the more interesting part. Both indices were recovering, not simply advancing: the CSI 300 had tumbled 3% on Wednesday in a broad tech selloff, and Hong Kong had been almost flat the same day. What changed was Wall Street. A rout early Wednesday — the Dow was down as much as 700 points at one point after investors judged the US Treasury's newly expanded long-bond buyback plan insufficient to hold yields down — reversed by the close, helped along by a bond rally and a jolt from biotech, where Moderna surged as much as 160% and Merck jumped 12% on cancer-treatment data. That reversal snapped Wall Street's own three-day losing streak and gave Asia something to chase into Thursday: China's vaccine and biotech index shot up 10%, Hong Kong's innovative-drug gauge rose more than 8%, and tech shares clawed back some of Wednesday's losses.

The yield story is messier than it looks

The tidy version of this rally is "stocks up because yields eased." The data only half supports it. The relief in Treasuries that lifted Wednesday's close had faded by Thursday, when the Nasdaq and S&P 500 slipped as the bond rally unwound, and yields resumed climbing into Friday — the 10-year rose another 3 basis points to 4.734%, the 30-year to 5.273%. Yet US equities kept rallying regardless: the Dow gained 517.80 points (0.98%) to 53,277.01 on Friday, the S&P 500 rose 0.43% to 7,674.37, and the Nasdaq added 0.43% to 26,180.45. Asia's tech names moved in step with that risk appetite rather than with the yield curve itself — evidence for the read that regional markets are pricing US policy direction, and shrugging off the day-to-day bond wobbles that would normally accompany it.

Chips found their own reason to rally

Not everything traces back to Washington. South Korea's Kospi rose 0.88% to 6,912.95 on Friday on the back of Samsung Electronics, up 3.87% to 281,500 won, and SK Hynix, up 2.31% to 1.73 million won, both lifted by reports of a shareholder-return package that could reach 110 trillion won. "A large-scale share buyback and cancellation announced by SK Hynix, along with growing expectations for stronger shareholder returns from Samsung Electronics, provided upward momentum for chip stocks and the broader local market," said Lee Kyoung-min at Daishin Securities. Notably, Korean chip stocks rose even as US Treasury yields resumed their climb the same day — a reminder that not every gain in the region is a rates trade.

The cleaner test comes in less than a week. Kevin Warsh delivers his first keynote as Fed chair at Jackson Hole on August 28, nineteen days ahead of the September 16-17 FOMC meeting — the same meeting at which three regional Fed presidents dissented in favour of a hike at the last gathering. If Asia's tech rally really is a leveraged bet on US rate cuts, Warsh's tone next week is where that bet gets marked to market.
