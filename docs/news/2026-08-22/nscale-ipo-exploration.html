AI cloud firm Nscale said to seek up to $3bn in US IPO

A data centre server room, illustrative of the compute infrastructure AI cloud providers like Nscale build and lease out. — SimonWaldherr / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Bloomberg reports British AI data-centre firm Nscale is exploring a US IPO that could raise up to $3bn, with no filing yet confirmed.

Nscale, a London-based builder of AI data centres, is exploring a US initial public offering that could raise as much as $3 billion, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg. The company is working with Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase on the potential listing, which could launch as soon as next month — though the plans remain fluid and Nscale declined to comment.

The timing would cap an extraordinary run for a company that barely existed two years ago. Nscale was incorporated in May 2024 by co-founders Joshua Payne and Nathan Townsend as a spin-off from Arkon Energy, a crypto-mining infrastructure business. It pivoted to renting out data centre capacity and GPU cloud services to AI developers, and the timing has been kind: demand for the computing power behind large language models has outrun supply for most of the past two years.

From crypto rigs to $14.6bn

The growth trajectory shows in the funding rounds. Nscale raised a $1.1 billion Series B in September 2025 from backers including Aker, Nvidia, Dell and Fidelity, then followed in March 2026 with a $2 billion Series C that valued the company at $14.6 billion and brought Nvidia, Nokia and Lenovo onto the cap table. That round also added star power to the board: Sheryl Sandberg, Meta's former chief operating officer, and Nick Clegg, the ex-UK deputy prime minister who spent years running Meta's global policy shop, both joined as directors, alongside former Yahoo president Susan Decker.

Nscale has told prospective investors it holds roughly $51 billion of total contracted revenue ahead of a listing — a figure that, if it holds up to scrutiny in an S-1, would dwarf the company's current scale. Its flagship US project is the Monarch Compute Campus, a 2,250-acre site in Mason County, West Virginia, acquired this year and designed to eventually support more than 8 gigawatts of capacity. In Norway, Nscale is building out sites at Narvik and Glomfjord powered by hydroelectricity. Microsoft is an anchor tenant, having committed to 1.35 gigawatts of capacity and a separate deal for 300,000 Nvidia Blackwell Ultra chips across four locations.

A crowded runway

Nscale's target capacity — from 831 megawatts currently installed to roughly 11 gigawatts — puts a number on the scale of capital an AI infrastructure operator now needs simply to compete. It would follow other so-called neoclouds toward public markets; rival Switch is reportedly preparing its own US IPO at a valuation near $50 billion. For a sector defined until recently by private mega-rounds, that shift toward listed equity is itself a signal of how much capacity buildout still lies ahead, and how much of it public shareholders may soon be asked to fund.

For now, none of this is confirmed. Bloomberg's report is sourced to people familiar with private deliberations, not a filing, and IPO plans at this stage routinely change size, timing or venue before any prospectus appears. Nscale has not filed to go public and has declined to comment on the reporting.

The next concrete marker would be an S-1 filing, which — if the September timeline holds — could arrive within weeks and would give the first audited look at revenue, customer concentration and the debt taken on to fund its build-out, including a $900 million revolving credit facility secured in July from a 12-bank syndicate.
