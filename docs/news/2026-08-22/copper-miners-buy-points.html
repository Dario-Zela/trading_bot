Copper miners near buy points amid supply-demand crunch

The Grasberg mine in Papua, Indonesia, operated by Freeport-McMoRan's local subsidiary — one of the world's largest copper and gold deposits. — Indonesian Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Copper miners including Freeport-McMoRan, Southern Copper and Teck are nearing breakout levels as a widening supply deficit meets AI- and grid-driven demand.

Copper miners are flashing the kind of technical setups chartists watch for — shares pushing toward prior highs on rising volume, several within single-digit percentage points of clearing resistance. The move isn't happening in a vacuum. It's riding a metal that hit its highest price on record this month, propelled by a supply picture that keeps getting tighter just as electrification and AI-driven grid spending push demand the other way.

Copper touched an all-time high of roughly $14,800 a tonne on the London Metal Exchange in mid-August, with COMEX futures briefly trading above $6.70 a pound. The Global X Copper Miners ETF (COPX), a rough proxy for the sector, has more than doubled off its 52-week low near $30.77 to trade around $88, having reclaimed its 50-day moving average on August 4 and flipped from a downtrend to an up one. Resistance sits in the $90-$95 band, with the 52-week high near $99.99 the next real test.

The names near their marks

Among individual miners, Freeport-McMoRan (FCX) — the world's largest publicly traded pure-play copper producer — closed as high as $71.55 in early June and was trading near $69 in mid-August, putting it within roughly 4% of that level. Southern Copper (SCCO), which runs some of the industry's lowest-cost mines across Peru and Mexico, has been setting fresh records outright, closing near $195 in the same stretch on the back of a roughly 17% year-to-date rise in copper prices feeding straight into margins. Teck Resources (TECK), the Canadian miner mid-merger with Anglo American and rated a buy by several sell-side desks with price targets near $76, rounds out the group IBD-style screens have flagged as approaching or breaking through prior bases.

Why the chart and the fundamentals agree

The technical strength lines up with a supply-demand mismatch that has shifted materially over the past year. The International Copper Study Group now projects a refined copper deficit of roughly 150,000 tonnes in 2026, reversing what had been forecast as a 209,000-tonne surplus. J.P. Morgan's estimate is starker still — a 330,000-tonne shortfall, with mine-supply growth crawling at around 1.4% and refined output growth slowing to 0.9%, down from 3.4% in 2025. The bottlenecks are familiar ones for the industry: permitting delays, falling ore grades at aging mines, and disruptions across Chile, Peru and Indonesia, where a thin pipeline of new projects has left little slack to absorb any of it.

Demand is moving in the opposite direction. China's grid operators are mid-way through a roughly $574 billion upgrade program, with grid investment up 13% year-on-year. AI data centres, meanwhile, are proving unusually copper-intensive — around 39 tonnes per megawatt of installed capacity, versus 32-36 tonnes for conventional facilities — just as hyperscalers signal close to $785 billion in combined 2026 capital spending. Longer-dated forecasts see global copper demand climbing roughly 50% by 2030 and total consumption reaching 42 million tonnes by 2040, driven by EVs and grid buildout on top of the AI load.

What the setup is worth

For an algorithmic reading, the interesting part isn't the chart pattern alone — cup-and-handle and base-breakout setups exist on decorative sectors too. It's that the technical strength has an underlying cause a trading model can point to: a metal deficit that multiple independent forecasters (ICSG, J.P. Morgan, S&P Global) now agree is real and widening, feeding into producers whose earnings are directly geared to the spot price. Southern Copper's results already show that pass-through. Whether Freeport and Teck confirm their own breakouts depends less on sentiment than on whether Chile, Peru and Indonesia keep tripping over their own supply chains — the next Chilean and Peruvian production data, due in early September, is the more reliable tell than any single day's candle.
