Man Utd agree £70m fee for Brighton's Baleba

Carlos Baleba, 22, is set to leave Brighton for Manchester United in a deal worth up to £70m. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Manchester United have agreed a £70m deal for Brighton's Carlos Baleba, the latest big-money sale in the Seagulls' well-worn conveyor belt of midfield talent.

Manchester United have agreed a deal worth up to £70m to sign Carlos Baleba from Brighton, one of the largest fees paid by an English club this summer and a reminder that Premier League spending shows no sign of slowing even as clubs across the division tighten belts elsewhere.

The structure is straightforward by the standards of a market that increasingly hides its true price in add-ons: £65m guaranteed, another £5m in performance-related payments, and a sell-on clause that gives Brighton a cut of any future sale. The 22-year-old Cameroon international will sign a five-year contract once his medical is complete, though he will not play for some weeks — Baleba is recovering from an ankle ligament injury picked up in pre-season and is not expected to be fit until around mid-September.

Brighton's conveyor belt

The deal is also the latest instalment in one of English football's more reliable success stories: Brighton's ability to keep selling its best players without appearing to weaken. Baleba himself arrived at the Amex in 2023 for around £23m, signed from Lille as the direct replacement for Moises Caicedo — who left for Chelsea that same year for a then British-record £115m. When Brighton lost Yves Bissouma to Tottenham, they had Caicedo ready. When they lost Caicedo, they had Baleba. The pattern has held long enough that it no longer looks like luck.

For Manchester United, Baleba becomes the third midfielder recruited this summer, following the arrivals of Andrey Santos and Youri Tielemans. Sky Sports's football desk framed the appeal in blunt terms: he offers "specialist defensive power coupled with the athleticism to drive United up the pitch" — the kind of ball-winning profile United have chased, with mixed results, since Paul Pogba's departure. It is worth noting his stock has cooled slightly: a year ago Brighton were said to want more than £100m for him, above Caicedo's fee, before a dip in form during the 2025-26 season brought the price back to earth.

A market that won't cool down

What makes the fee notable beyond United's own rebuild is the context around it. Premier League clubs had already spent roughly £2.14bn this summer with two weeks of the window still to run, putting the division on course to beat — or at least match — last year's record outlay of around £3.1bn. Chelsea lead the individual spending charts, with net outlay estimated between £183m and £242m; Arsenal, Manchester City and promoted Ipswich also feature prominently.

That spending is happening just as the Premier League's financial framework is being rewritten. The current Profit and Sustainability Rules, which cap cumulative losses at £105m over three years, are being replaced from next season by a Squad Cost Ratio system that ties permitted spending on wages, agent fees and transfer amortisation to a fixed share — 85% — of club revenue. The shift explains some of the urgency: clubs have an incentive to complete big-ticket deals under the old regime before the new ratio bites, and to structure fees like Baleba's — modest guaranteed sum, meaningful add-ons, a sell-on clause protecting the selling club — in ways that spread cost and risk under either rulebook.

Brighton, for their part, keep proving that selling well is its own competitive advantage. The next name to watch from the production line is Kaoru Mitoma, whom Al Nassr have already tried and failed to prise away once. Baleba is expected to make his United debut only after the derby with Manchester City on 13 September and the visit of Fulham a week later — by which point the picture of who actually won this transfer window will be considerably clearer.
