Google consolidates AI leadership as Hassabis steps back to chairman role

Demis Hassabis at the 2024 Nobel Prize ceremony in Stockholm, months before Google moved him out of DeepMind's day-to-day leadership. — John Sears / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Google stripped Demis Hassabis of day-to-day control of DeepMind, handing Gemini's delivery to Koray Kavukcuoglu as London's influence shifts to Mountain View.

Google spent the past decade selling Demis Hassabis as the embodiment of its AI ambitions — the DeepMind co-founder and Nobel laureate who beat cancer's protein-folding problem and, along the way, built the lab meant to get the company to artificial general intelligence first. This month, Google moved him out of the job that runs that lab day to day.

Hassabis is now chairman of Google DeepMind and chief scientist of parent company Alphabet, a shift confirmed in early August. Operational control of the roughly 8,000-person division — the Gemini models, the research agenda, the shipping deadlines — passes to Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind's chief technology officer, who becomes senior vice president and reports directly to Alphabet chief executive Sundar Pichai. Hassabis keeps a foot in the building, chairing DeepMind, setting long-range AGI strategy, and continuing to run Alphabet's drug-discovery offshoot Isomorphic Labs. But the executive who built DeepMind into Google's flagship AI lab no longer owns its delivery.

Why now

The reshuffle follows a run of bad news that Google mostly avoided saying out loud. Gemini 3.5 Pro, its next flagship model, has now missed three release windows — June, mid-July, and beyond — while rivals kept shipping. Artificial Analysis, which benchmarks model performance, has Google's strongest publicly available model trailing offerings from Anthropic, OpenAI, several Chinese labs, xAI and Meta on intelligence scores. Engineers who spoke to Fortune pointed to a specific miss: the company underweighted coding capability just as coding became the metric enterprise customers actually care about.

The personnel bleed has been just as pointed. In June, AlphaFold co-inventor John Jumper left for Anthropic, taking colleagues Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel with him. Then, in the same week as the leadership reshuffle, Jeff Dean — a 27-year Google veteran and DeepMind's chief scientist — announced he was leaving alongside Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le to start an independent public-benefit corporation called Discovery Loop, aimed at automating scientific research. Google is, notably, a founding investor and cloud partner in the new venture — an unusually amicable way to lose your chief scientist, and a sign the split was managed rather than acrimonious.

London loses ground

The less-discussed part of the reorganization is geographic. DeepMind was built and run out of London — Hassabis is British, and the lab retained a distinct identity even after its 2023 merger with Google Brain. That identity is now eroding. Kavukcuoglu relocated from London to Mountain View over the past year to take on the sprawling research-and-operations brief he now formally holds, and Sebastian Borgeaud, who leads a key AI coding effort, made the same move to California.

Engineers described the pattern to Forbes as a slow transfer of authority westward, with one calling it the "America-fication" of DeepMind — a shift, they said, from a research culture toward one where, in their words, "it's the loudest person in the room." Others cited asymmetric access to projects (Mountain View could see London's work; the reverse wasn't true) and open competition for computing resources between the two sites. A former researcher summed it up as an environment where "things are quite politicized based on who you report to."

The symbolism cuts both ways. Google is simultaneously investing in London — DeepMind and other Google teams are due to move this year into Platform 37, a new King's Cross building named for AlphaGo's famous "Move 37," designed by Thomas Heatherwick. But real decision-making authority over coding and product timelines is now sitting in California, and the people who used to hold it in London have either relocated or left the company.

The commercialization problem

Strip away the individual moves and the pattern reads clearly: Google is separating scientific ambition from product execution, and betting that clearer lines of accountability fix a speed problem money alone hasn't. Hassabis was, by every account, an outstanding recruiter of research talent and a credible public face for "AI done safely." What DeepMind has struggled to do is ship a frontier model on schedule while OpenAI and Anthropic iterate faster and, increasingly, poach the researchers who'd otherwise close that gap. Putting one person — Kavukcuoglu — in sole charge of Gemini, research and product removes an ambiguity that reportedly cost DeepMind time internally, even if it does nothing by itself to close the technical distance to GPT-5-class or Claude-class models.

For anyone pricing Alphabet's AI position, the read is that the company has diagnosed its problem as organizational rather than purely technical — and organizational fixes are faster to execute than research breakthroughs, but they're also easier to get credit for without actually closing the gap. The next real test isn't a press release; it's whether Gemini 3.5 Pro ships on Kavukcuoglu's watch, and how it scores against Anthropic and OpenAI's models when it does. Alphabet reports third-quarter earnings in late October, by which point the market will want to know whether the new command structure produced a shipped model or just a cleaner org chart.
