BP takes 10% stake in UAE's Bab gas cap project

AD-1, Abu Dhabi's first oil rig, now stands as a monument outside ADNOC's Abu Dhabi headquarters. — Alexandermcnabb / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
BP took a 10% stake in ADNOC's Bab Gas Cap project, its first upstream gas access in Abu Dhabi, alongside TotalEnergies and Asian partners.

BP has signed on for a 10% stake in Abu Dhabi's Bab Gas Cap project, its first-ever piece of upstream gas in the emirate and one more marker in the scramble among Western majors to lock up Middle East reserves before the next decade's demand picture comes into focus.

The concession, finalized with state producer ADNOC in the past week, gives BP a slice of what ADNOC calls the largest gas cap development of its kind anywhere in the world — three reservoirs sitting atop the Bab field, one of Abu Dhabi's oldest and largest onshore oil deposits, expected to yield up to 1.5 billion cubic feet of gas a day alongside associated condensate.

Who else is in the room

ADNOC keeps the controlling hand and operatorship through ADNOC Onshore, holding 60% of the concession. BP and TotalEnergies each take 10%. The remainder is split among three Asian partners: China National Petroleum Corporation's international arm at 8%, Japan's INPEX (through JODCO) at 5%, China's ZhenHua Oil at 4%, and South Korea's GS Energy at 3%. BP will act as asset lead on the project, according to the companies — an operational role distinct from ADNOC's ownership control, giving the UK major a hand in shaping how the reservoirs are developed.

William Lin, BP's executive vice president for gas and low-carbon energy, framed the deal as continuity as much as expansion: "This agreement gives bp access to a material new gas resource and deepens our long-standing partnership with ADNOC," he said. TotalEnergies chief executive Patrick Pouyanné cast the project in similarly familiar terms, calling it consistent with the French major's existing upstream strategy in the region.

The agreement builds on the 40-year renewal ADNOC secured for Abu Dhabi's onshore oil concessions back in 2015, the framework that has let it keep parceling out equity to foreign partners in exchange for capital and technical expertise. Gas from Bab is earmarked largely for domestic use — feedstock for petrochemicals, fertilizer and aluminum plants, and power for desalination — with any surplus feeding ADNOC's expanding LNG export ambitions.

The reserves chase, and the yield

For BP, the timing fits a pattern rather than a pivot. The company confirmed commercial output has started at Azerbaijan's Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli field and said it is preparing to take over operatorship of the offshore Babek gas field there — part of the same push into OPEC-aligned, low-cost gas basins that majors have leaned on since 2022, when the scramble for secure long-term supply replaced just-in-time trading as the industry's watchword.

It is also a business income investors have kept close watch on: BP currently pays an annual dividend near $1.98 a share, a yield hovering around 5.3%, among the higher payouts on the NYSE energy list. A 10% slice of a single Gulf concession will not move that dividend on its own, but it is exactly the kind of low-cost, long-life barrel-and-molecule exposure that lets BP defend the payout while it keeps trimming elsewhere in its portfolio.

What happens next is mostly a matter of engineering and timeline. ADNOC has not published a first-gas date for Bab, and none of the partners has disclosed capital commitments. The next marker worth watching is whether ADNOC folds the new volumes into its Ruwais LNG expansion timetable, which would tell markets how much of Bab's gas ends up on a tanker rather than feeding Abu Dhabi's own industry.
