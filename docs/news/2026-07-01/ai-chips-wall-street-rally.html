AI chips lead Wall Street higher as the Dow eyes fresh records

The Nasdaq MarketSite in Times Square, where chip stocks capped the exchange's strongest quarter since 2020. — ajay_suresh / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Chipmakers pushed the Dow to a record close and capped Wall Street's best quarter since 2020, though quarter-end rebalancing muddies how much reflects real AI demand.

Wall Street closed out the second quarter the way it spent most of it: with chipmakers doing the heavy lifting. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.26% to finish at 52,319.20 on Tuesday, a second consecutive record close, while the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.52% to 26,213.72 and the S&P 500 rose 0.79% to 7,449.36. All three indexes posted their best quarterly performance since the pandemic rebound of 2020.

The rally had a clear address: semiconductors. Nvidia gained 2.6%, AMD 7.7%, Intel 6%, Marvell Technology 7.3% and SanDisk 10.9%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, a broader gauge of chip stocks, finished the quarter up roughly 88% — its strongest three-month stretch on record — as the Nasdaq climbed about 22.5% over the same period.

A rally with two engines

What's less clear is how much of Tuesday's move reflects conviction about artificial intelligence spending and how much reflects the mechanics of the calendar. June 30 marked quarter-end, when pension funds, sovereign wealth vehicles and institutional asset managers rebalance portfolios back toward target allocations — often buying beaten-down assets and trimming winners to keep risk in check. Kenny Polcari of SlateStone Wealth described the pattern as "quarter-end window dressing," telling clients the rebalancing by pension funds, institutional managers and sovereign wealth funds was "just about complete" by Tuesday's close.

That mechanical flow cuts both ways. Chip stocks that had run up so far, so fast, became natural candidates for profit-taking by disciplined allocators — yet Tuesday's session showed the opposite, with the group extending gains rather than giving them back. Kathleen Brooks of XTB framed it as evidence the underlying trade still has legs: "The AI trade is still robust, even if it has splintered in recent months, with the chip makers surging and the hyperscalers struggling." That splintering is itself notable — it suggests investors are increasingly discriminating between companies selling the physical infrastructure of AI computing and the software platforms trying to monetize it, rather than buying the theme indiscriminately.

The scoreboard

The scale of the quarter's gains is difficult to overstate. Several chip names are up well over 100% since January, and equipment makers benefited alongside the chip designers themselves: Applied Materials extended a rally of roughly 60% for June alone, Onto Innovation rose 8.9% on demand for its wafer-inspection systems, and Astera Labs, which makes connectivity chips for AI data centers, added 6.7%. The moves were broad enough that even a defense-drone maker, AeroVironment, rode the AI-infrastructure narrative to a 15.6% jump after posting fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $642 million, up 133% from a year earlier.

Not every corner of the market joined in. Real estate fell 1.2% and healthcare slipped 1%, the kind of defensive underperformance one would expect when capital is rotating toward risk rather than away from it — another data point suggesting Tuesday's move was about more than mechanical rebalancing alone.

What to watch

The test now shifts from index levels to earnings. Second-quarter results begin arriving in earnest over the coming weeks, and chip-sector investors will be looking for confirmation that hyperscaler capital spending on AI infrastructure is holding up, not merely that fund managers needed somewhere to park cash on the last day of June. If bookings and guidance from the likes of Nvidia, AMD and Applied Materials match the price action, the quarter-end rally will look like the start of a trend. If they don't, Tuesday's record close may end up remembered as the moment the calendar did more work than the fundamentals.
