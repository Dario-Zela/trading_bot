Israel to close West Bank crossings on election day as security operation widens

In one sentence
Israel will seal all West Bank land crossings and bar Palestinian workers on 27 October, election day, as a security operation around Hebron widens after two soldiers were killed.

Israel's military says it will close every land crossing into the West Bank on Tuesday, 27 October, the day the country votes in a general election. The announcement, issued on the army's Telegram channel on Sunday, lands in the middle of an expanding security operation around Hebron. It puts a national ballot and a military campaign on the same calendar.

What has been announced

According to the Israel Defense Forces' statement, as reported by AFP, Israel's political leadership approved "the closure of all land crossings throughout Election Day (Tuesday), October 27, 2026". A second measure bars Palestinian workers from entering or leaving the West Bank across the whole territory, including the Jordan Valley and its industrial zones.

The published accounts do not say whether there will be exceptions. Medical cases and humanitarian travel are normally handled case by case during Israeli closures, but nothing seen so far confirms that arrangement here. It is also unclear when the closure begins and ends, and whether it extends beyond polling day. Those details matter, because a closure of a single day and one that rolls into the vote count are different things for the tens of thousands of Palestinians who cross daily for work.

General closures of this kind are not new. Israel has sealed the West Bank around Jewish holidays and at times of heightened threat for decades. Applying one to a national election day is less routine, and it is the timing that has drawn attention.

The operation behind it

The closure follows a deadly incident near Hebron. Reports say gunmen shot dead two Israeli soldiers at a checkpoint on Friday and wounded others. AFP photographs from 23 October show Israeli forces beside the bodies of two armed Palestinian men after an exchange of fire at Hebron's northern checkpoint, though the agency's text does not explicitly tie that exchange to the soldiers' deaths. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is reported to have vowed that Israel would exact a heavy price.

Aggregated coverage of the Hebron operation says it has displaced around 2,000 residents. It also attributes to Defence Minister Israel Katz a statement that they will not be allowed back until Israel has finished dealing with Hamas in the city. These figures and that quotation come from secondary reports. They have not been confirmed against an official transcript or independent count, and should be read with that caution.

Why the timing matters

This is Israel's first general election since the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023. The 25th Knesset dissolved on 17 July, and the vote is the first held on its scheduled date since 1988. Mr Netanyahu leads a caretaker government, facing voters while his corruption trial continues. Security has dominated the campaign.

There are two ways to read the closure. The security reading is straightforward: after a fatal attack, with polling stations and crowds across Israel, the army wants the border sealed against any attempt at a follow-up strike. Closures on sensitive dates are a standard tool, and the Hebron attack gives the army a concrete reason.

The political reading is that a government facing an election is being seen to act forcefully after soldiers were killed. Nothing in the available reporting establishes motive, and officials have framed the step as a security measure. What can be said is that the two threads are now tied together: the operation widening in Hebron and a ballot that will be held under a territory-wide lockdown.

For Palestinians the effect is practical. West Bank residents do not vote in Israeli elections, apart from settlers, who are Israeli citizens. A worker barred from a job for a day loses a day's wages, and a patient or student may lose more. Rights groups have long argued that blanket closures amount to collective restriction. Israel says they are a proportionate response to threats.

What to watch

Three things will show the real scale. The first is whether the army publishes exceptions for medical and humanitarian travel before Tuesday. The second is whether the closure lifts as planned once polls shut, or is extended while results are counted. The third is whether the Hebron operation widens to other cities. Polling day itself is Tuesday, and the coalition arithmetic that follows will decide who is responsible for whatever comes next in the West Bank.
