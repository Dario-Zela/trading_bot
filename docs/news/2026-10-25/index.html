UK BUDGET · By M. Halloway

Gilt yields squeeze Healey's headroom days before a Budget built around housing and devolution

With 30-year gilts near 5.9% and the 10-year at an 18-year high, outside estimates put Healey's headroom at roughly £11-14bn, down from the OBR's £23.6bn, before the 28 October Budget.

Rising long-dated gilt yields are eating into the Chancellor's room for manoeuvre ahead of a Budget built around housing and devolution. Yields on 30-year gilts are reported at around 5.9%, and the 10-year yield is at its highest since 2008. The Office for Budget Responsibility put fiscal headroom at £23.6bn in March 2026. An outside estimate suggests it has since shrunk by £10-12bn, though that figure is not an OBR publication.

Headroom is the margin against the fiscal rules that sets how much tax cutting or extra spending the Chancellor can afford. Higher borrowing costs also raise debt-interest bills, so market pricing now constrains the Budget before any policy is announced. The bond market, rather than the Treasury's own forecasts, is setting the terms the Budget has to meet.

The key question is whether the OBR will publish a fresh forecast alongside the Budget, since that would reset the headroom figure. Until then, the £10-12bn estimate is the only measure of how far the margin has narrowed.
