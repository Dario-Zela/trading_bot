Met says it shared allegations with Meghan but not with other forces

In one sentence
The Met says it gave a 2019 letter of allegations about Thomas Markle to Meghan's side but not to other forces, and is reviewing its handling, including a possible watchdog referral.

The Metropolitan Police has said it passed a letter containing allegations about Meghan's father, Thomas Markle, to the Duchess of Sussex's side, and that it did not pass them to any other law enforcement body. That is the one part of this family dispute that concerns how the police handled information rather than who said what to whom.

In a statement reported on Saturday and Sunday, the force said it received a letter in January 2019 containing "a number of claims about alleged offending outside the United Kingdom". It was referred to the Met's Protection Command "to raise concerns in relation to the security of the Duchess of Sussex", and to the office of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex. It was not shared with other law enforcement agencies. The reports do not say who sent the letter.

The Met is now "reviewing the handling of this matter". That includes whether it should refer itself to the police watchdog.

A discrepancy of months

The dates do not quite match. Meghan said on Friday that she was told of "disturbing allegations" about her father in 2018, and that this led her to keep her children away from him. The Met puts the letter's arrival in early 2019. A representative for the Duchess has said any discrepancy of a few months was down to the passage of time.

The allegations, and the denial

The claims in circulation concern Mr Markle's former home in the Philippines. International media have reported that child sexual abuse material was found there, and UK reports say Philippine authorities are investigating. Mr Markle has denied possessing the images. He told the Daily Mail the accusations were "sick lies". These are allegations only. The reports seen for this article mention no charges or findings. The FBI said it was aware of the allegations and would provide support to the Philippine authorities as appropriate.

The Met's account leaves open what happened to the information after it left the Protection Command. A force can reasonably treat a letter as a security matter for a protected person. It is a different thing to pass on claims of offending abroad to the bodies that might investigate them. The Met's own statement draws that line, saying the letter went to the Sussexes' office and not to other agencies. The reports give no detail on what policy applied in 2019, or on whether anyone considered wider sharing.

What to watch

The decision on whether the Met refers itself to the police watchdog is the next marker. A referral would bring an outside look at how the letter was handled. The Philippine inquiry will be the test of the underlying claims, and nothing so far suggests it has produced a finding.
