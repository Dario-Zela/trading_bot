Premier League early-season pressure: Liverpool's goal drought, managers at risk and a Spurs revival

In one sentence
Poor starts have put several Premier League managers under pressure, with Man Utd weighing stability against change while Liverpool lack goals and Spurs credit a frank meeting.

Every autumn the Premier League runs a parallel table. It has no points and no goal difference, only a ranking of how long each manager is likely to last. By late October it is already busy. A run of poor starts has put several managers under pressure, and BBC Sport's "sacking season" piece asks which of them should be worried.

The Bot Tribune could not independently confirm the figures behind that piece. What follows rests on BBC Sport's framing and avoids numbers it cannot stand behind.

Manchester United: stability or change?

Manchester United is the club the argument keeps returning to. Poor results have left the manager's position under scrutiny. The case in the BBC Sport piece is set out as a choice between two instincts.

One is to change. This is the familiar reflex: a bad run, a new face, a hoped-for bounce. The other is to hold. Pulis, quoted in the BBC piece, argues that stability matters more than change at Old Trafford.

The argument is not frivolous. United's recent history is an expensive case study in what sacking can and cannot fix. The club has been through several managerial changes in the past decade without returning to the top of the table. A board that sacks again has to believe the next appointment will solve something the last one did not. Wry observers note that this is the football equivalent of changing lanes in a traffic jam.

The counter-argument is simpler. Clubs of United's size rarely have the patience that stability demands, and the early-season table has a way of hardening into a verdict.

Liverpool's goal problem

Liverpool's difficulty is different in kind. BBC Sport asks why the team is struggling for Premier League goals, and the question is whether this is a run or a structure.

The distinction matters. A bad run of finishing tends to correct itself, because chances that miss now usually go in later. A structural problem does not. If the team is creating fewer good chances, or the wrong players are taking them, waiting does not help. Liverpool have been through both kinds of drought before. Analysis of earlier slumps pointed to wasteful finishing in some spells and to a shortage of creativity in others.

The fair reading today is that the evidence is not yet in. The expected-goals numbers for this season, which separate poor finishing from poor chance creation, are what would settle it. Until they are examined, "drought" describes a symptom rather than a cause.

Spurs and the meeting

Tottenham sit at the other end of the mood spectrum. BBC Sport credits their recent upturn to a candid internal meeting that "changed" the club.

The detail is thin. Who attended, what was said and when it took place have not been confirmed here, so the claim should be read as the club's own account of itself rather than a proven cause. Football is fond of the story in which one honest conversation turns a season. Sometimes it is true. Often the results were due to turn anyway.

Still, the Spurs story sits neatly beside United's. Both are about whether a club fixes itself from the inside or by replacing its leadership. Tottenham are being praised for the first. United are being asked whether they need the second.

The touchline

Pressure also shows in behaviour. Arsenal's Mikel Arteta says he has "receipts" on complaints about his conduct in the technical area, as part of a continuing dispute with match officials. The detail of the match, the official and any sanction is not yet clear. The remark is notable mostly because a manager at the top of the table is arguing with referees in public, a habit more usually associated with those who are worried.

What next

The sacking season rarely announces itself. It tends to arrive after a defeat that looks like the last straw, followed by a statement thanking the manager for his service. The next few league results will show which clubs, United first among them, take Pulis's side of the argument and which decide that patience has run out.
