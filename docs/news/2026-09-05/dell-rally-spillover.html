Dell's post-earnings rally spills into other hardware names

Dell's corporate campus in Round Rock, Texas. — Jjpwiki / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Dell's 58%-revenue, $95bn-AI-backlog earnings beat sent HPE, Super Micro and other hardware names toward technical buy points, per IBD.

Dell Technologies delivered one of the cleanest earnings beats of the AI-infrastructure boom on September 2, and the market has spent the days since deciding which other hardware makers deserve the same treatment.

Dell's fiscal second-quarter numbers were not close. Adjusted earnings came in at $7.04 a share against a Street estimate of roughly $4.90-4.92, and revenue reached $46.97 billion versus $44.92 billion expected — a 58% year-over-year jump. The Infrastructure Solutions Group, which houses Dell's AI server business, posted $31.78 billion in revenue, up 89%, with AI-optimized servers alone contributing $16.4 billion. The company booked $60.9 billion in new AI orders during the quarter and now sits on a $95 billion backlog of signed business not yet shipped. Management raised its full-year revenue guidance to a $192 billion midpoint, from $167 billion, and lifted adjusted EPS guidance to $25.50 from $17.90. The stock rose nearly 16% the next session to a fresh 52-week high, breaking out of a three-month consolidation range, and is now up roughly 270-300% year to date against an 11% gain for the S&P 500.

Why one company's print moves a sector

Investor's Business Daily's read on the move is less about Dell in isolation and more about what it validates: that hardware vendors selling profitable, revenue-generating AI infrastructure are a distinct trade from the unprofitable AI software and model-layer names that dominate headlines. Dell's guidance raise came with actual margin expansion, not just topline growth, and IBD's technical screens flagged a cluster of peers moving in sympathy — Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Super Micro Computer, NetApp, Lenovo, Seagate Technology, Western Digital and Sandisk among them. Several of those names combine the momentum IBD's algorithms look for with valuations that haven't yet caught up to Dell's, which trades at a forward price-to-earnings ratio about 54% above its five-year average.

The reaction across those peers has been uneven, which is itself informative. HPE and Super Micro both moved up on Dell's print — HPE gained roughly 4-9% in the sessions around the release, Super Micro's monthly gain reached as high as 29% — but Super Micro remains down about 27% over the trailing year, meaning its bounce reads as a relief rally against a depressed base rather than a fresh breakout in IBD's stricter sense of a stock clearing a defined buy point on rising volume. Dell itself offers two: a breakout entry above $500, and a lower-cost pullback entry near $467, per IBD's published levels, with modeled upside of 10-18% and 18-26% respectively.

What the rotation is actually pricing

The distinction IBD is drawing — profitable hardware sellers versus cash-burning AI vendors — matters for capital allocation because it's a bet on where in the AI supply chain the durable margin sits. Dell's backlog figure, in particular, is doing a lot of work: $95 billion in signed, unrecognized orders is a forward revenue claim that doesn't depend on future AI product demand materializing, since the orders already exist. That's a different risk profile than a company trading on model performance or subscriber growth, and it's the kind of distinction algorithmic screens can price mechanically once the print lands — relative strength, volume confirmation and distance from a 52-week high all update within a session.

Whether the sympathy move persists past this news cycle depends on whether the peer names post their own confirming numbers. HPE reports fiscal fourth-quarter results in early December; Western Digital and Seagate both have quarters due in October. Until then, the sector-wide breakout thesis rests on one company's print and IBD's screens — a single-source signal that traders treating this as more than a short-term rotation should be aware they're extrapolating from.
