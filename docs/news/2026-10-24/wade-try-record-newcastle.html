Wade equals Premiership try record as Newcastle beat Sale

Christian Wade playing for England at Twickenham in 2012. — David Iliff / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Christian Wade scored his 101st Premiership try to equal Chris Ashton's record as Newcastle beat Sale 17-3 for their first win of the season.

Christian Wade has drawn level with Chris Ashton as the highest try-scorer in Premiership history, and he did it on a night when little else in the game was worth remembering. Newcastle Red Bulls beat Sale Sharks 17-3 at Kingston Park in blustery conditions. Wade's 101st Premiership try, in the 49th minute, equalled the mark Ashton set in April 2023.

A record in the corner

Sale led 3-0 at half-time through a George Ford penalty. The first half was mostly kicking, penalties and defence, and the wind did not help either side. Newcastle chose to take the corner rather than kick for goal after the break, and that decision set up the afternoon.

Wade's try, nine minutes into the second half, came in the right corner. Josh Hodge converted. It moved Newcastle ahead 7-3 and made the winger, who is 35, joint holder of a record many assumed would last far longer.

George McGuigan added a try from a maul, and Hodge converted that too and kicked a penalty. Newcastle won without Sale scoring again.

Ashton, who was working as a pundit for TNT Sports, said of the winger: "He has been on the wing all night waiting for his chance, as he always has done." Newcastle head coach Dan McFarland put it more drily: Wade "knows how to find the try-line as all good wingers do."

The long way round

Wade reached 100 Premiership tries with a hat-trick in a 34-28 defeat at Exeter about two weeks earlier, which left him one short of the record. The Sale game was the first chance to level it, and he took it.

His route to this point has been unusual. He left rugby union for a spell in American football with the Buffalo Bills, then spent time in rugby league with Wigan, before returning to union with Newcastle. He has said the NFL showed him how hard it is to hold a place.

He has been candid that the record is not the only thing on his mind. His contract expires at the end of the season, and he has said he would like to stay in the North East. "Obviously, I would love to stay on, but I'm not sure what the plans are going forward," he said this week. Many people, he added, assume he came back to break the record, but he says he is focused on making the most of his gifts.

The result matters too, a little

The win was Newcastle's first of the Premiership season, and only their fifth across four Premiership seasons. Sale had not lost either of their previous two league games. Newcastle have long struggled, so for the club the points mattered as much as the milestone.

What comes next

Wade's next chance to take the record outright comes next Saturday, when Newcastle travel to Saracens. Whether he is selected for that match is the open question. If he scores, the record is his alone.
