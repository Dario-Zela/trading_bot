Shein to pay $100m to settle FTC probe

In one sentence
Shein reportedly agreed to pay $100m to settle an FTC probe into delivery delays and customer complaints, though formal terms and the legal basis have not been published.

Shein has agreed to pay $100m to settle a US Federal Trade Commission investigation into delivery delays and other customer complaints, according to a person with direct knowledge of the deal who spoke to Reuters on Friday. Shein has called it a "constructive outcome". The FTC declined to comment, and no formal announcement or settlement terms have been published.

What the probe covered

The reported subject is consumer protection, not trade or national security. FTC records reviewed by Reuters show more than 12,000 complaints about Shein from January 2020 to June 2026, tied to more than $11m in purchases. Some customers said packages marked as delivered never arrived and that customer service did not fix the problem. Reuters could not independently verify the complaints or whether they were resolved.

The reports do not say which law was invoked. They also do not say whether the money takes the form of a civil penalty, redress to consumers or both, or whether a consent order will impose conduct requirements on top of it. Those details decide how much the deal costs Shein beyond the headline figure.

Size against precedent

The FTC has pursued shipping-promise cases before, but at far smaller scale. Fashion Nova paid $9.3m in 2020 over missed shipping promises and gift cards offered in place of refunds. Crocs-owned Hey Dude paid $1.95m in 2023 for failing to tell customers about shipping delays and for suppressing negative reviews. At $100m, the Shein figure is roughly ten times the larger of those.

Against Shein's own earnings the payment is large but manageable. Reports put it at about 44% of second-quarter profit, which implies quarterly profit of roughly $227m, a figure the reports do not state directly. It is a heavy quarter's charge, not a threat to the business.

A market that was braced for it

Shein is founded in China and headquartered in Singapore. It warned in its July filings for a Hong Kong IPO that the US investigation could bring significant fines, so the outcome is a disclosed risk rather than a surprise. The shares have fallen 22% from their September 1 listing price, with investors focused on the effect of European Union fees on e-commerce packages. Jefferies analysts say app downloads across ten markets fell 42% in September from a year earlier.

Regulatory pressure elsewhere has not eased. The European Commission is still investigating illegal products on Shein's marketplace, and France's data protection authority fined the company €150m ($168m) last year over its use of cookies.

What to watch

The first test is the paperwork. Shein has said more information will follow soon, and an FTC filing or announcement would show the legal basis, the split between penalty and refunds, and any compliance obligations. Investors will then weigh whether the $100m closes the US matter or only the delivery complaints. The next data point is Shein's quarterly results, where the charge should appear and the download slide can be checked against management's account.
