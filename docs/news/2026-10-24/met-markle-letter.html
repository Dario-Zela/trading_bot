Met investigates letter making claims about Meghan's father

In one sentence
Police are looking into an unverified letter making serious claims about Thomas Markle, which the Duchess of Sussex called deeply distressing; the claims are unproven.

The Metropolitan Police are looking into a letter that makes serious claims about Thomas Markle, the father of the Duchess of Sussex. The letter is unverified. The Duchess has publicly described it as deeply distressing, according to reports from BBC UK and the Guardian.

The reports say the letter contains allegations involving child sexual abuse images. They are only allegations, made in a document whose origin and authenticity have not been established. Nothing in the reporting so far shows that they have any basis, and this article does not treat them as fact.

What is known

Three points are on the record. The Met is examining the letter. The letter makes claims about Mr Markle. And Meghan has responded publicly, calling it deeply distressing.

Much is still unknown. The reports do not make clear who wrote the letter, who received it, how it reached the police, or what stage the Met's work has reached. A police inquiry into a document is not a finding about the person named in it, and the force has not said that any offence has been committed. Mr Markle is a private individual, and the reputational cost of a claim like this falls on him whether or not it is ever substantiated.

The family backdrop

The Markle family's dealings with the press have been fraught for years. The relationship between Meghan and her father has been the subject of tabloid coverage, public statements and litigation. That history is why this story will draw attention, and it is also a reason for caution. Where a private individual is named in an unproven allegation of this gravity, repeating the detail adds nothing to public understanding and risks real harm.

What to watch

The next development is likely to come from the Met, which may say whether the letter is being treated as a criminal matter, whether it has been authenticated, or whether the inquiry has closed. Any statement from Mr Markle or his representatives would also matter. Until the police say more, the one firm fact is that an inquiry exists. The claims themselves remain unverified.
