Reform UK braces for standards verdict on Farage's undeclared £5m gift

In one sentence
The parliamentary standards commissioner is due to rule on Nigel Farage's undeclared £5m gift, a test of Commons disclosure rules and of Reform UK's clean-politics pitch.

The parliamentary commissioner for standards is investigating Nigel Farage over a £5m gift he did not put on the Commons register. The inquiry has run since May. A ruling is reportedly close, though no ruling date has been verified and no finding has been published. The case has a wider interest because Reform UK, which Farage leads, tops national opinion polls on a pitch of cleaning up Westminster.

The gift and the rules

The money came from Christopher Harborne, a Thailand-based crypto investor and Reform UK donor. It reportedly arrived in 2024, before Farage had decided to stand in Clacton. He changed his mind and entered the race in June 2024, weeks after receiving it. Farage has said the payment was for personal security, to cover private protection for the rest of his life. He has called it a personal, unconditional gift, and says it was unconnected to his political activity.

The inquiry was opened on 13 May by the commissioner, Daniel Greenberg, after the Conservative Party wrote to the watchdog. It is being conducted under rule 5 of the House of Commons code of conduct, which covers failure to register an interest.

The rules in force at the time required new MPs to register gifts worth more than £300 received in the previous 12 months. There was an exception where the gift "could not be reasonably thought by others" to relate to the member's political activities. The case turns on that exception: whether a reasonable observer would link a £5m gift from a party donor to the political career of the man who leads the party. Farage's account is that they would not.

A defiant defence

Farage has not played the matter down in private. In June, ITV reported him saying "no one cares" about the gift. He also said he had not "spent a penny" of it, and told GMB it was unconditional and could be spent on anything. That last point is awkward for the security explanation, because a gift described as free of any conditions sits uneasily with a claim that it was earmarked for one purpose. Reform UK says his office is in contact with the commissioner and that no rules were broken. Farage has also said the watchdog may take a different view from him.

The figures and timing come from press reporting, chiefly the Guardian, and have not been independently verified here.

What a finding could mean

The commissioner investigates and reports. The Commons standards committee then decides on any sanction. The most serious outcome would be a suspension. A suspension long enough could open a recall petition in Clacton and, if enough constituents signed, a by-election. Lesser outcomes include a requirement to apologise or repay.

The commissioner's own judgment is also open to a milder reading. The registration rules were written for a Commons in which gifts to individual MPs were small, and their exception clause is a matter of judgment. A finding that the gift should have been registered but that the breach was inadvertent is a plausible middle course. This is an inference from the rule's wording, not from any reported leak. The Electoral Commission is separately reported to be considering a request to look at the matter, which would involve a different set of rules on donations.

The wider test

The broader question is political. Reform's poll lead rests partly on a claim to be an outsider that plays by plainer rules. A finding against its leader would test whether voters who back that claim punish the lapse or discount the process. A clean outcome would also settle little: the case would still show how much of the disclosure regime depends on how an individual member reads an exception clause.

The immediate catalyst is the publication of the commissioner's findings, and then any referral to the standards committee. No date has been confirmed. Until it is, the sanction question is open.
