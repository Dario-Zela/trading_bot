Trump praises Burnham as 'getting it done' after Jackdaw decision

In one sentence
Trump praised PM Andy Burnham as 'getting it done' after the government approved the Jackdaw North Sea gas field, a decision that puts Labour's energy stance under fresh scrutiny.

Donald Trump has praised Andy Burnham, now Britain's prime minister, for approving the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea. He spoke after a phone call with Burnham on Friday. The remark is more than a passing compliment. It concerns a project that tests Labour's energy policy, and it comes with fuel prices high and the Iran conflict in the background.

What Jackdaw is

Jackdaw is a gas field about 150 miles east of Aberdeen. Reports say it could supply around 6% of UK gas production, enough to heat about 1.4 million homes. Those figures come from press accounts. The operator was not named in the coverage reviewed.

The Conservatives first approved drilling in 2022. Climate campaigners challenged that consent in court, and it was ruled unlawful, so the project had to be decided again. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh granted approval on Wednesday, under Burnham's government. Accounts differ on the exact date and on whether the decision is better credited to Burnham or to his energy secretary.

Labour's 2024 manifesto pledged to stop new North Sea drilling licences. That pledge did not cover projects already approved, which is the position Jackdaw falls into. Environmental groups still strongly oppose the decision.

What Trump said

Trump spoke to reporters as he left the White House for a campaign rally in Florida. "Andy is getting it done, and I guess it's against the liberal principle, but he knows what's right," he said. He added that he had pressed the UK on the issue for two years, and that the project would make Britain "a very wealthy country." He described Keir Starmer, Burnham's predecessor, as "a very nice man, but he never got it done."

He also appeared to mix up oil and gas. He spoke of the UK "opening up North Sea oil", but Jackdaw is a gas field. On Truth Social he called the approval "great news for the wealth and prosperity of the United Kingdom." He said the call "mostly discussed Aberdeen and the North Sea." Ukraine and Iran were touched on only lightly. It was reportedly the first conversation between the two men since they met at the UN in September.

Downing Street said the leaders agreed Jackdaw would boost the economy and support British jobs. It did not echo Trump's framing of the decision as a break with liberal principle. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch had accused the prime minister of "dithering" over the field.

Why it matters

Trump's praise is awkward for a Labour leader as well as flattering. Burnham is on the party's left, and an endorsement that casts the decision as defying liberal principle gives critics a line to use. Campaigners argue the field would make "no meaningful difference" to energy security.

The timing is also political. Fuel prices are at their highest since December 2022, and energy bills are expected to rise this winter amid the Iran conflict. Ministers can point to domestic gas as a response.

The larger test is Rosebank, an oil and gas field described as the UK's last known major untapped oil reserve. No decision has been made. Fahnbulleh has called it a "different proposition" from Jackdaw. Whether the government treats Jackdaw as a one-off or a precedent will show if Trump's compliment was well placed.
