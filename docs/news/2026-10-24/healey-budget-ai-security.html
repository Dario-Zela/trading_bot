Healey pledges to use Budget to harness AI for national security

In one sentence
Ahead of Wednesday's Budget, John Healey has trailed over £600m of AI spending framed around security, mostly cyber and biosecurity, with little yet on military AI.

Britain's government is trailing a package of artificial-intelligence spending, framed as a matter of national security, ahead of a Budget due on Wednesday. John Healey is the minister fronting it. Early coverage described him as a defence secretary making a bare pledge. Reports of the announcement itself show more detail than that, and they raise a question about his job title.

What has been announced

Healey said the Budget would be used to harness AI to bolster Britain's security, according to the Guardian's report of 24 October. I could read that report only through syndicated copies. City AM, reporting the same weekend announcement, puts the package at more than £600m. It quotes Healey calling for a "distinctively British approach to AI", one "that doesn't leave workers behind and defends our national interest".

The security items, as City AM lists them, are:

  • Biosecurity: £165m for a National AI Biosecurity Initiative. That includes £100m for a new laboratory, plus up to £200m in model credits from OpenAI and Anthropic.
  • AI Security Institute: £120m.
  • Cyber defence: £60m for a National Cyber Shield that uses AI agents.
  • Critical infrastructure: £70m for a centre that identifies vulnerabilities in critical national infrastructure.
  • Training: a new cyber security training centre in Manchester.

The rest of the £600m covers economic and industrial items. These include £60m added to the Sovereign AI Fund, £23m over three years for an AI Economics Institute and £10m a year to study AI's effect on jobs.

The totals are not uniform across outlets. One report puts the biosecurity effort at up to £300m. The difference appears to come from counting company-supplied compute and credits alongside public money. The £100m laboratory is covered in more detail in our separate report.

A question of title

The brief for this piece called Healey the defence secretary. The City AM report does not give his title. It describes him announcing Treasury funding and refers to "the Chancellor" separately. Some lower-quality aggregator results also say he left the Defence Secretary's post in June over the defence investment plan. I could not confirm that against a reliable source, and I could not confirm his current office either. Readers should treat his title as unsettled until an official source settles it.

What is missing

The package is detailed on the AI side and thin on the defence side. Nothing I found ties the money to military procurement, such as AI in targeting, logistics or autonomous systems. The announced items are mainly civil-protection and scientific bodies: cyber, biosecurity, vulnerability testing and the AI Security Institute. If the Budget is meant to shape defence AI as well, that has yet to be set out.

The politics around it are also unresolved. Kemi Badenoch has called on Healey to find the money to reach 3% of GDP on defence in the Budget, calling it a test of national security. A £600m AI package is small beside that. The government's stated goal of 3.5% of GDP by 2035 would involve sums many times larger.

What to watch

The Budget speech and the Treasury's accompanying documents are the next test. They should show whether the figures above are new money or re-announced commitments. They should also show whether any of it reaches the Ministry of Defence's equipment budget. I could not independently verify the Budget date, which City AM gives only as "Wednesday", presumably 28 October, so that should be confirmed against the Treasury's own notice.
