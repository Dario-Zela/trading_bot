Carrick won't rule out dropping Fernandes

In one sentence
Michael Carrick, speaking after seven games without a win, would not rule out dropping captain Bruno Fernandes for Sunday's match against Bournemouth.

Michael Carrick has declined to say whether Bruno Fernandes will keep his place in the Manchester United team. The head coach was asked about it on Friday, before Sunday's Premier League game against Bournemouth at Old Trafford. He would not rule out leaving the captain out.

The answer matters because of what came before it. Carrick opened his pre-match media briefing at Carrington with an address of about five and a half minutes, rather than taking questions first. It was emotional by the standards of the genre. United have gone seven games without a win in all competitions. The low point was a 4-1 Champions League defeat by Como on Wednesday.

The address

Carrick used the time to set out what he thinks the club stands for. He did not dress up the Como result.

"Wednesday was a bad night, and it hurt me to see us play like that. It was unacceptable."

He then moved to the standard he wants applied in the dressing room:

"Entitlement cannot be at this football club."

He spoke of humility and effort as the club's identity. The BBC's Simon Stone reported the address, and its tone was of a manager who knows his position is under scrutiny.

The Fernandes question

The backdrop was a messy week. Defender Lisandro Martinez criticised the club's hierarchy. Fernandes publicly criticised the team's tactics after the defeat in Italy. Both are senior figures, and neither comment was likely to be welcomed by a coach in Carrick's position.

Asked directly about dropping his captain, Carrick gave nothing away. His answer, as reported, was about the group rather than the individual.

"The most important thing for me is the team, the squad and dynamics in the squad."

He added: "I'll always do what is best for the football club."

Neither sentence is a threat, and neither is a reassurance. That is the point. A manager who wanted to calm things would have said the captain was not in doubt. A manager who wanted to hold the line would say what Carrick said.

What it signals

It would be a stretch to read this as a decision. Carrick has not said Fernandes is out, and refusing to name a team two days early is routine. The evidence for a message sits in the pairing: an address about entitlement, followed by a refusal to protect the player who had just criticised the tactics.

Taken together, they suggest Carrick wants it understood that no one is undroppable. That is a modest signal about squad discipline, and it is addressed mainly to a dressing room that has now gone seven games without winning.

Fernandes was the standout figure of United's last campaign, so the cost of leaving him out is real. The test is the teamsheet for Bournemouth on Sunday. Whether the captain starts will tell the dressing room, and the supporters, how much weight Friday's words carry.
