Leclerc fastest in US Grand Prix practice; Canada beat England twice

In one sentence
Charles Leclerc led both US GP practice sessions ahead of title-chasing Kimi Antonelli, while Canada beat England's Red Roses 31-22 for a second straight win.

Ferrari finished Friday at the Circuit of the Americas as the only team with an answer for the Mercedes that dominates this season. Charles Leclerc topped both practice sessions for the United States Grand Prix. In the second, he was 0.042 seconds clear of Kimi Antonelli, the Mercedes driver who stands closest to the world title.

Friday in Austin

Leclerc was fastest in the opening session, narrowly ahead of Max Verstappen. He then found almost eight tenths in the second, setting a 1m 35.291s. Behind him the order was Antonelli (+0.042), Verstappen (+0.189) and Leclerc's Ferrari team-mate Lewis Hamilton (+0.616). George Russell, in the other Mercedes, was fifth at +0.744.

Antonelli's gap is smaller than it looks. His seat came loose early in the session, and he spent about 10 minutes in the garage while it was repaired. Russell was also looked at by the stewards for forcing Leclerc off the track. They found "nothing dangerous" and took no further action.

The McLaren pair were well back. Lando Norris was seventh, 1.082s off the pace, and Oscar Piastri eighth. Neither managed a clean lap on the soft tyres.

Leclerc himself was cautious. "The balance of the car was good," he said, but "we shouldn't carry too high expectations going into tomorrow." Practice runs are flattering guides, because teams run different fuel loads and engine modes. Ferrari have also gone eight races without a win, so a Friday lead is something to note rather than build on.

The stakes are on Antonelli's side. Sky Sports reports that he would clinch his first world championship with a win on Sunday, which would make him the sport's youngest champion. Qualifying is at 10pm UK time on Saturday and the race at 8pm on Sunday. Those sessions will say whether Ferrari's pace holds up.

Canada do it twice

In women's rugby, England's Red Roses lost to Canada for the second time in a week. Canada won 31-22 in Ottawa on Friday, a week after a 24-21 win in Toronto. The Toronto result ended England's 41-match unbeaten run, which had lasted four years.

The two games were part of a three-Test WXV Global Series. A 26-26 draw in Exeter in September came first, so Canada went unbeaten across the three matches. Sophie de Goede, the Canada captain, scored a hat-trick in Ottawa. England's tries came from Maddie Feaunati early on and Emma Sing late. Reports say it is the first time since 2016 that England have lost back-to-back Tests.

England captain Meg Jones insisted the side were "not broken" after the defeat. Her words carry weight because England arrived as the top-ranked team. The Exeter draw, then the Toronto defeat and now Ottawa, suggest Canada have closed the gap rather than caught England on a bad day.

The two stories have little else in common, but both leave an unbeaten reputation under pressure. Ferrari have to turn a quick Friday into a result on Sunday. England have to show that the Red Roses are still the team to beat.
