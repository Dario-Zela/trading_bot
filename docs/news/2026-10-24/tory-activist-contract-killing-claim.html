Tory activist in contract-killing claim previously worked at party HQ

In one sentence
A Conservative member suspended after an Al Jazeera film showed him discussing contract killings reportedly once worked at party headquarters; he denies seriousness and no charge has been reported.

A Conservative Party member who stood as a candidate in 2023 has been suspended after an undercover film showed him discussing what it would cost to have people attacked or killed. According to the BBC's report, he previously worked at Conservative headquarters. That raises the question of how close the alleged broker was to the party machine.

The man is Jack Ross, chief executive of Turning Point UK, an independent offshoot of the American group Turning Point USA. He features in "Contract Killers and the Far Right", an Al Jazeera investigation. The programme grew out of a reporter joining Turning Point UK in 2021.

What the film alleges

Al Jazeera says Ross, speaking to a reporter working undercover, described how much it would cost to hire people to carry out violence. Fees would be set by the severity of the attack, and Ross is quoted as saying: "They can shoot someone. They could kill someone."

The broadcaster also says he offered to broker the services of a far-right activist from Glasgow, named as Nesbitt, who is alleged to have paramilitary links. Ross is said to have introduced the reporter to him.

These are allegations made in a documentary. Ross has rejected its findings and, according to Al Jazeera, said his words to the reporter were not serious. No charge has been reported.

The party's response

The Conservatives acted within days. A spokesperson said Ross's membership had been "suspended with immediate effect pending an investigation". The party added that it could not comment further while that investigation continued. Police Scotland has said it is aware of the documentary and will consider and assess it for possible criminal offences. That is a long way from an investigation into a specific offence.

The headquarters question

The claim that Ross once worked at Conservative headquarters comes from the BBC's account. The Al Jazeera material available to this newspaper does not mention it, and the details are unclear: the role, its dates and its seniority. Those details matter. A junior staffer who left years ago is a different matter from someone with continuing access to senior figures. Al Jazeera does say Turning Point UK has close ties to several senior Conservative figures, and to leaders of Reform UK. That makes the organisation's reach wider than any single job on a CV.

The political exposure is less about guilt than about oversight. Parties give candidacies and staff roles to people they can vet only so far. What the Conservatives will have to answer is what was known about Ross, and when, and whether his Turning Point role was ever a concern while he held party roles. The suspension suggests the party thinks the footage, if genuine, is serious enough to act on before any finding.

What to watch

Two things will decide whether this stays a one-week story. The first is whether Police Scotland's assessment leads to a formal inquiry into Ross or Nesbitt. The second is the outcome of the party's own investigation, including whether it publishes anything about Ross's past work at headquarters. Ross's account of what he meant, and whether the unedited footage supports it, will also bear on both.
