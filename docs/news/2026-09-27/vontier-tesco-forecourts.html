Vontier wins Tesco forecourt technology rollout across 700-plus UK sites

A Tesco petrol station forecourt in the UK, part of the estate now being upgraded with Vontier's payment and fuelling technology. — Rodhullandemu / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
Vontier will fit payment and pump technology across 700-plus Tesco UK forecourts by mid-2027, a rare large, multi-year contract in a fragmented market.

Tesco has picked Vontier, the US-listed industrial technology group, to overhaul the payment and fuelling systems across more than 700 of its UK petrol stations — one of the largest forecourt technology contracts awarded in Britain this year.

The deal, announced on 18 September, will see Tesco integrate two Vontier businesses into its forecourt estate: Gilbarco Veeder-Root's DOMS PSS 5000 forecourt controller, which manages pump operations and compliance data, and Invenco's outdoor payment terminals, which handle transactions at the pump itself. Financial terms were not disclosed, and neither company gave a total contract value.

What's actually being installed

The core of the upgrade is plumbing, not storefront. The DOMS controller sits between the pumps and Tesco's point-of-sale software, feeding real-time data on fuel levels, transaction status and regulatory compliance back to head office — the kind of visibility that lets a retailer spot a faulty pump or a compliance gap without a site visit. Invenco's terminals then let customers pay by card at the pump or inside the kiosk, with the two systems designed to talk to each other rather than run as separate bolt-ons, which has often been the case at older UK forecourts.

"By leveraging the combined strengths of our market leading businesses, Vontier is proud to deliver integrated solutions designed to best serve the unique needs of our customers," said Andy Bennett, Vontier's group president of convenience retail, in the companies' joint statement.

The rollout covers Tesco's roughly 720 UK forecourts and is expected to run through to mid-2027, according to trade coverage of the announcement — a multi-year implementation rather than a single cutover, which is typical for estate-wide forecourt work given the need to keep pumps operating during installation.

Why a fuel retailer bothers with backend tech

Forecourt technology is an unglamorous but sticky category: once a retailer's pumps, payment rails and point-of-sale are wired to a single vendor's controller, switching costs are high and service contracts tend to run for years. That is precisely the economics Vontier is chasing. The Tesco award slots a large, recurring UK customer into a business line — its Gilbarco Veeder-Root and Invenco units — built on exactly this kind of infrastructure lock-in, at a moment when fuel retailers are under pressure to modernise ageing pump hardware while squeezing more margin out of convenience and non-fuel sales.

For Tesco, the upgrade addresses a practical problem: much of its forecourt hardware predates the current wave of contactless and app-based payment expectations, and disconnected systems make compliance reporting and pump diagnostics slower than they need to be. Tying point-of-sale to pump-level data in real time is as much an operations play as a customer-experience one.

The market reaction

Vontier shares, which trade on the NYSE under VNT, ticked up on the announcement, though the stock remains down roughly 27% over the past year and has traded well below its 52-week high of $48.20 — closer to $31 at last check, against a market capitalisation near $4.3 billion. Some sell-side commentary has flagged the shares as undervalued relative to fair-value estimates near $37, citing Vontier's capital-allocation discipline inherited from former parents Danaher and Fortive. The Tesco contract alone will not move that valuation gap on its own, but it is a concrete data point for a stock that has otherwise struggled against a narrative of sluggish core growth and long-term pressure from electric-vehicle adoption on fuel retailing.

What to watch

The practical test comes at rollout, not announcement: watch for how quickly Vontier and Tesco move through the 720-site estate, and whether Vontier discloses contract economics or scale details in its next quarterly results. A clean, on-schedule deployment through 2027 would support the case that legacy forecourt infrastructure remains a durable, if unglamorous, revenue line even as the industry debates the pace of the shift away from liquid fuel.
