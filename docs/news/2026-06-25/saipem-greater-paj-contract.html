Saipem wins Greater PAJ offshore contract, signalling upstream confidence despite softer oil

Saipem's Castorone pipelaying vessel in the North Sea — one of two heavy construction ships earmarked for the Greater PAJ offshore Angola installation campaign. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Azule Energy (Eni-BP) sanctioned the $5.1B Greater PAJ deepwater project off Angola and awarded Saipem a $1B installation contract, signalling long-cycle confidence despite Brent near $73.

Azule Energy, the Angolan venture that combines Eni and bp's in-country assets, handed Italian contractor Saipem a $1 billion subsea installation contract on June 22 for the Greater PAJ field development — a project committing $5.1 billion to ultra-deepwater Angola at a moment when Brent crude is trading near multi-year lows.

The project spans two adjacent offshore concessions — Blocks 31 and 31/21 — roughly 200 kilometres from the Angolan coast, making it the country's first integrated cross-block development. Five fields will feed a single new-build FPSO rated at 95,000 barrels per day and 70 million standard cubic feet of gas per day. The partnership behind it — Azule as operator, alongside Sonangol E&P and Equinor — holds an estimated 252 million barrels of recoverable oil. First oil is targeted for the first half of 2029.

Saipem's scope covers the engineering, fabrication and installation of roughly 180 kilometres of rigid deepwater pipelines, plus 38 kilometres of flexible flowlines and jumpers and 54 kilometres of umbilicals, all at water depths reaching 2,000 metres. Fabrication runs through the company's Ambriz yard in Angola; its heavy pipelay vessels FDS and Castorone handle offshore installation. The contract duration of approximately 40 months positions Saipem to deliver ahead of first oil.

TechnipFMC also secured a contract — described as "significant," implying a value between $75 million and $250 million — to design and manufacture flexible flowlines and risers for the same development. The combined contractor commitments disclosed so far exceed $1.25 billion, sitting within a $5.1 billion total project budget that was formally sanctioned at FID.

A $5.1 billion commitment at $73 oil

The timing deserves scrutiny. Brent crude fell roughly 24 percent across June, sliding from above $100 earlier in the month to around $73 on June 24 — driven by easing tensions over Hormuz supply disruption and evolving OPEC+ dynamics. That level is uncomfortably close to the breakeven thresholds often cited for new ultra-deepwater developments.

Azule and its partners sanctioned the project regardless. Long-cycle deepwater economics are not set against spot prices but against decade-long curves, and operators willing to commit at $73 are either pricing in mean reversion or have structured the project — through Angola's cost-recovery fiscal terms, hedging, or offtake agreements — to function at current levels. Angola's production-sharing framework, which allows contractors to recover capital costs before profit splits kick in, generally tolerates low headline prices better than licence structures elsewhere.

Saipem's own numbers reinforce the sense of a sector that is building rather than contracting. The company entered Q2 with a backlog of €29.6 billion, reported a 24 percent year-on-year rise in EBITDA to €434 million in Q1, and posted free cash flow of €199 million in the quarter — balance-sheet conditions that allow the company to absorb a 40-month capital-intensive commitment without liquidity strain.

The real test arrives if Brent sustains below $70 — a level it briefly approached this week before recovering — at which point project economics for the next wave of sanctions become considerably more fraught. Saipem's Q2 order intake, due in late July, will indicate whether Greater PAJ reflects broad sector momentum or an isolated decision by two oil majors with long Angolan histories. The H1 2029 first-oil target is also a hard milestone to monitor: slippage would delay Azule's revenue recognition and test partner appetite for additional capex draws.
