Novo Nordisk navigates GLP-1 competition and US pricing pressure as analysts trim outlook

Novo Nordisk's US headquarters in Plainsboro, New Jersey — the American commercial operation sits at the centre of the company's pricing and competitive battles. — Jonathan Schilling / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Analysts hold Novo Nordisk at Hold as Eli Lilly takes 60% US GLP-1 share and a White House pricing deal cuts Wegovy to as low as $199/month, with full-year sales seen declining up to 12%.

Novo Nordisk built Europe's most valuable pharmaceutical franchise on the back of semaglutide — the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy — but 2026 is testing that position from two directions simultaneously: Eli Lilly's tirzepatide is steadily eroding Novo's US market share, while a voluntary pricing deal with the White House has materially cut what Americans pay for GLP-1 therapies. Analysts at Zacks, reviewing the stock this month, maintain a Hold rating, with consensus pointing to a year of declining sales and earnings.

A Tightening Grip From Lilly

In the US obesity and diabetes GLP-1 market — the most commercially decisive for both companies — Eli Lilly now holds roughly 60% of prescriptions, against Novo's approximately 39%, according to market-share data compiled by analysts tracking injectable and oral GLP-1 volumes.¹ That gap has widened over the past year. The clinical rationale is straightforward: tirzepatide delivered roughly 27% mean body-weight reduction in pivotal trials against approximately 15% for semaglutide. In the first quarter of 2026, Lilly's Mounjaro and Zepbound together generated $12.82 billion in quarterly sales, roughly matching the pace of Novo's expected full-year revenue.

Novo is not standing still. The company launched the first FDA-approved oral GLP-1 obesity therapy — Wegovy as a once-daily pill — in US pharmacies in January 2026, priced from $149 per month at the starter dose. Analysts note the product's efficacy is comparable to the injectable (16.6% mean weight loss in the OASIS 4 trial), but a fasting requirement before dosing introduces a compliance friction that Lilly's rival oral entry, Foundayo, does not share. Smaller biotechs including Viking Therapeutics and Structure Therapeutics are advancing next-generation candidates, suggesting the competitive overhang will not lighten soon.

The MFN Pricing Lever

Layered on top is the pricing policy dimension. Under a voluntary most-favoured-nation agreement signed with the Trump administration in November 2025, Wegovy's monthly list price fell from $1,349 to approximately $350 through the TrumpRx.gov direct-to-consumer portal, and to $245 for Medicare and Medicaid patients — the first time federal programmes have covered Wegovy for obesity. An April 2026 executive order tightened the arrangement further, linking tariff relief to manufacturers' sustained participation in MFN pricing.

Novo Nordisk has characterised the combined pricing impact as a "low single-digit" drag on global sales growth, arguing that lower out-of-pocket costs should unlock proportional prescription volume. Whether that volume bet holds is the central uncertainty for the year.

What Analysts Make of It

Zacks rates NVO a #3 Hold, with a 2026 consensus earnings-per-share estimate of $3.46 — implying a year-on-year decline of roughly 12.6% — and consensus revenue of approximately $45 billion, off about 3.6%. Novo's own full-year guidance calls for adjusted sales and operating profit to decline 4–12% at constant exchange rates. The stock trades at around 13x forward earnings, below the pharmaceutical sector's approximately 17x average and sharply below its own five-year historical mean near 29x — signalling how far investor expectations have been reset.

Not all broker views are negative: Nordea upgraded NVO to Buy on 19 June; Berenberg lifted its Copenhagen-listed target to DKK 325 from DKK 300 on 17 June, citing the oral Wegovy ramp and potential pediatric GLP-1 approvals expected in the second half. The 12-month consensus price target across 23 brokerages averages $65.56, within a range of $40 to $175 — a spread that reflects genuine uncertainty about which scenario prevails.

The next hard data will come with Novo Nordisk's Q2 2026 earnings in August. Prescription volume trends in the Medicare segment and the early adoption trajectory of oral Wegovy will be the figures to watch against that 4–12% guidance range.
