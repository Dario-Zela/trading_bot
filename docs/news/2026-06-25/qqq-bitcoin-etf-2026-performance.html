QQQ widens 2026 lead over Bitcoin ETFs as AI equity wins the institutional allocation battle

The Nasdaq MarketSite in Times Square, New York — the LED tower whose index has outrun Bitcoin ETFs by nearly 46 percentage points in 2026. — Larry D. Moore / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
QQQ is up ~17% YTD while Bitcoin spot ETFs have shed ~29%, a 46-point gap that marks the sharpest institutional divergence yet between AI equity and digital assets.

Invesco's QQQ, which tracks the Nasdaq-100, has widened its lead over US-listed Bitcoin spot ETFs to roughly 46 percentage points in 2026 — an outperformance gap that is starting to look less like a bad quarter for crypto and more like a structural reallocation.

In one sentence: QQQ is up about 17% year-to-date while IBIT has shed nearly 29%, a 46-point gap that marks the sharpest institutional divergence between AI equity and digital assets since Bitcoin ETFs launched.

Through June 18, QQQ's price return stood at +16.74%; total return with reinvested distributions is closer to 19%. Bitcoin, which opened 2026 near $88,400, has retreated to around $62,700 — a decline of roughly 29%. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), the category leader by assets, tracks that slide almost exactly, with a year-to-date NAV return of approximately -28.8%.

The timing matters. Bitcoin spot ETFs launched on January 11, 2024, making 2026 only their third calendar year of institutional availability. The early pitch was straightforward: a regulated, exchange-traded wrapper would give endowments, pension funds, and private wealth desks a clean on-ramp to digital-asset diversification. At peak, US Bitcoin ETPs held more than 1.25 million BTC. But year-to-date AUM across all global crypto exchange-traded products has contracted by roughly 15%, to about $140 billion — a figure that 21Shares, at the start of 2026, expected would hit $400 billion and eventually surpass QQQ's own asset base, now approaching $500 billion.

The outflow record

The headline is two consecutive record-breaking streaks. Between May 15 and June 3, US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled $4.33 billion across 13 consecutive trading days — their longest outflow run since launch. A separate weekly record of $3.4 billion in net redemptions followed in early June. In the first quarter alone, institutional holders cut their exposure by 17%, from roughly 313,000 BTC to 261,000 BTC. BlackRock's IBIT absorbed approximately $3.3 billion of the redemptions during the extended streak; Fidelity's FBTC shed around $457 million.

On the other side of the ledger, QQQ is doing what it does in AI bull markets. Nvidia, representing about 9% of the fund's holdings, has been the primary catalyst; Micron and Broadcom have also contributed. Of the 46 Nasdaq-100 companies that reported Q1 2026 earnings, 36 beat analyst estimates. The Magnificent Seven are collectively budgeted to spend $527 billion on AI and data-centre infrastructure this year — the capex cycle feeding the revenue line that underpins the index's premium.

The two narratives are not strictly zero-sum: institutions can hold AI equity and digital assets simultaneously. But the flows suggest a reordering of priorities. Where Bitcoin once offered the appeal of a low-correlation diversifier in institutional-grade wrapper form, 2026's macro backdrop — a May CPI print that reawakened inflation anxiety — has made Bitcoin act more like a leveraged risk-on trade than a hedge, and without the earnings visibility that Nasdaq-100 components provide.

The next clear read on whether AI equity can sustain current valuations comes tonight, when Micron reports quarterly earnings — effectively a live stress-test of the AI capex thesis that has carried QQQ through the year. For Bitcoin's side of the ledger, a sustained hold above $65,000 would be the first signal that institutional redemptions have run their course.
