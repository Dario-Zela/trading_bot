SK Hynix plans US listing, bringing Micron's HBM rival to American exchanges

The Nasdaq MarketSite display in Times Square, New York — the exchange where SK Hynix plans to list its ADRs as soon as July 10. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
SK Hynix plans a record $29B Nasdaq ADR listing to fund HBM capacity expansion, giving US investors direct exposure to Micron's dominant rival in AI memory.

South Korea's SK Hynix filed this week to list American depositary receipts on the Nasdaq, targeting a deal worth roughly $29 billion — which, if it closes at that size, would be the largest ADR offering ever recorded, topping Alibaba's $21.8 billion US debut in 2014 and Saudi Aramco's $25.6 billion IPO in 2019.

The company set a tentative first trading date of July 10, though that date remains subject to regulatory clearance. Each ADR corresponds to one-tenth of a common share, with 17.79 million new shares being issued against a filing amount of 45.45 trillion won. Underwriters include Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, BofA Securities, and Citigroup.

Capital for a supply-constrained market

All proceeds are directed at capacity expansion, not shareholder returns or debt reduction. The build-out spans a new fabrication campus in Yongin, South Korea — the so-called Yongin Cluster, slated to begin coming online in 2027 — an advanced packaging facility in Cheongju, and orders for extreme ultraviolet lithography equipment from ASML. A $4 billion packaging plant in Indiana, SK Hynix's first US manufacturing footprint, is already under construction.

The timing is deliberate. High-bandwidth memory — the stacked, vertically-integrated DRAM that sits alongside the GPU die in AI accelerators — is sold out for 2026, with shortages widely expected to extend into 2027. SK Hynix commands roughly 58 to 62 percent of the global HBM market by revenue, making it the dominant supplier to Nvidia across its Hopper and Blackwell processor families. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has publicly called SK Hynix "Nvidia's largest memory partner," noting the two companies transact "billions of dollars" annually.

Samsung and Micron are the only other suppliers at scale; Micron holds a single-digit revenue share in HBM, though it has been closing the gap on HBM3E qualification and is racing to compete on the next-generation HBM4 standard. SK Hynix's ADR filing acknowledges the competitive dynamic directly: the company stated it expects the Nasdaq listing to give it "the opportunity to be valued in line with U.S. peers" — a pointed reference to the persistent valuation gap between Korean-listed chipmakers and their American counterparts.

The financials support that case. SK Hynix reported 2025 revenue of 97 trillion won ($63.1 billion), up 47 percent year-on-year, and net profit of 42.9 trillion won ($27.9 billion), roughly double the prior year. The stock has gained several hundred percent over the past two years on the KOSPI, and on June 23 briefly overtook Samsung as South Korea's most valuable listed company before giving back the lead in a broader tech pullback.

For US institutional investors, the listing creates a direct vehicle to hold the supply-side bet on AI infrastructure expansion — something previously available only through ADR-thin access or Korea-specialist funds. Whether that demand translates into a tighter multiple, the kind the company is seeking, will become clearer once the book is run ahead of the July 10 target date.

Micron reports quarterly earnings tonight, offering the first read on HBM demand and pricing as the SK Hynix deal roadshow gets underway.
