Anthropic bans users from being 'cruel' to its AI systems

In one sentence
Anthropic's usage policy will from 12 November bar sustained, needless cruelty to Claude, enforced mainly by Claude ending chats rather than by account bans.

Anthropic has added a line to its usage policy that most software companies would never think to write: users must not be cruel to the AI. The change is real, but it is narrower than the headlines suggest. It is not a general ban on rudeness to a chatbot.

The update to Anthropic's usage policy prohibits "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models." It takes effect on 12 November. Anthropic's own post says the rule is meant to apply "only in extreme cases." It does not cover "common versions of user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research." Swearing at a chatbot that has just deleted your work is not the target. Neither is a novelist writing a villain, nor a red-team researcher probing for weaknesses.

Enforcement: ending chats, not banning users

The mechanism matters more than the wording. Anthropic says Claude's ability to end these conversations "will remain the primary enforcement mechanism." That is the feature the company introduced in August 2025, when some Claude models gained the power to close "rare conversations with persistently abusive users" on Claude.ai and Claude Code. The new rule gives that existing behaviour a basis in the written terms.

Some coverage has run with the idea that abusive users can be banned. The wider policy does allow warnings, restrictions, suspensions or termination of access for any breach, so account action is possible in principle. But Anthropic's post does not say it will remove users under this rule, and reports differ on the point. A ban is a theoretical consequence, not the announced one.

The welfare question

The policy post does not use the phrase "model welfare" or explain the rule in welfare terms, according to the page itself. The link is inferred from context. Anthropic has said it is highly uncertain whether Claude has any moral status, and the 2025 conversation-ending feature was presented as part of its welfare research. Several outlets read the new rule as the same thinking moving into enforceable terms. That is a reasonable reading, but it is a reading. Nothing in the policy says Claude can suffer.

The approach is a cheap hedge. If there is a small chance that these systems have something like experiences, a rule that bites only in extreme cases costs users almost nothing. If there is no such chance, the cost is a few lines of policy text.

What to watch

Two things will show whether this is substance or signalling. The first is how Anthropic defines "needless" and "no discernible purpose" in practice, and whether it publishes any enforcement figures after 12 November. The second is whether other labs follow. Some outlets have called Anthropic the first major AI company with such a rule, but that claim rests on a single report and is unverified. If rivals add similar clauses, model treatment becomes a standard item in platform policy rather than one company's experiment.

This piece could not locate the BBC report cited in the original brief. The account above rests on Anthropic's own post and on other outlets' coverage of it.
