Antonelli on the brink of the F1 title as Hamilton apologises to Ferrari

In one sentence
Antonelli leads the 2026 F1 standings by a reported 102 points with six rounds left, while Hamilton, third, keeps apologising to Ferrari over the radio.

Kimi Antonelli is the clear favourite for the 2026 Formula One drivers' title. He is not yet champion, and the arithmetic does not yet let anyone say he is on the brink. The figures that can be checked tell a plain story: a 19-year-old in a Mercedes with a very large cushion. The more awkward story sits a little further down the order, where Lewis Hamilton keeps finding reasons to say sorry to Ferrari.

Editor's note: the standings below come from aggregator pages that disagree with each other. The wording and date of Hamilton's latest apology could not be confirmed against a primary source such as BBC Sport. Treat both as provisional.

The standings

After the Singapore Grand Prix, round 17 of 23, Antonelli led the championship on 340 points, according to ESPN and F1 Fansite. That put him 102 clear of his Mercedes team-mate George Russell. Hamilton was third on 229. Other tables show lower totals for Antonelli, but they appear to date from earlier in the season. Motorsport.com's round-13 and round-14 tables, for instance, have him on 267 and then 292.

Six rounds remain. A win is worth 25 points, so a driver can take at most 150 from six Grands Prix, plus whatever the sprint weekends add. Russell could in theory still close a 102-point gap. A title decided in Antonelli's favour inside the next few rounds is plausible, but this reading of the table does not make it certain. Whoever finally confirms it should check the sprint calendar, because that decides how soon the maths allows it.

The seat itself is the neat part of the story. Antonelli took the Mercedes drive Hamilton left after 11 seasons to join Ferrari for 2025. A title for the man who replaced a seven-time champion would be a tidy piece of narrative, whatever Mercedes make of it.

Hamilton's apologies

Hamilton's Ferrari tenure has produced a recurring habit: apologising over the radio. Reports have him saying sorry to the team after a disappointing Japanese Grand Prix. Earlier reports have him apologising after being passed by Max Verstappen in Hungary, saying he simply did not see the Red Bull coming. Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur has conceded that the team made mistakes of its own in Hungary.

An apology over team radio is not a rupture. Drivers do it constantly, and it can signal good manners as readily as strain. What gives it weight here is the context. Hamilton is third in the standings and nowhere near the leader, and his team-mate Charles Leclerc is fourth, on 191 points in one earlier snapshot of the table. A seven-time champion who is saying sorry to his team is a driver still working out what the car and the garage will give him. The exact wording of the latest apology has not been confirmed, so it would be wrong to read a message into it.

What to watch

Two things will settle the picture. The first is the next round on the calendar. Antonelli needs to keep outscoring Russell by a margin that shrinks the remaining points available to rivals. The second is whether Ferrari can turn Hamilton's season from a catalogue of near-misses into a podium. If Antonelli clinches with rounds to spare, the closing races will matter mainly for the fight behind him, and the Hamilton–Leclerc order at Ferrari is part of it.
