Eurozone CPI due this week as the ECB weighs energy-driven inflation

In one sentence
Eurozone September inflation was flashed at 3.8% on energy at 18.8%; this week's final print matters only if core, at 2.5%, shifts the ECB's 29 October decision.

The euro-area inflation figure due this week is unlikely to surprise anyone. The surprise already arrived on 2 October, when Eurostat's flash estimate put September inflation at 3.8%, up from 3.2% in August and the highest reading since September 2023, according to Business Today. The release now due is, on the usual calendar, the final confirmation of that number. The exact date and any consensus forecast could not be confirmed against the Eurostat calendar. Revisions to flash HICP figures are normally a tenth of a point or nothing.

Energy is doing the work

The flash breakdown shows where the pressure sits. Energy inflation was put at 18.8% a year, against 14.3% in August. Core inflation, which strips out energy and food, was 2.5%, up from 2.4%. The gap between headline and core is the whole argument: most of the move comes from fuel, not from broad price-setting in the economy.

The national figures were uneven. Italy was at 4.1% and Spain at 5.0%.

The background is oil above $100 a barrel since late August, after attacks by the US and Iran on military, shipping and energy assets in the Middle East. This week's other Markets coverage tracks the same price: Brent near $100 after a second Houthi strike on Riyadh airport.

What the ECB has already said

The central bank has moved ahead of the data. On 10 September it raised its deposit rate by 25 basis points to 2.50%, the second quarter-point increase of the tightening cycle that began in June. Christine Lagarde described the decision as unanimous and a "no-brainer". She named energy prices as the main threat to price stability and warned that the longer they stay high, the greater the spillover into core inflation.

She also declined to commit to a path: "We have not debated at all any kind of future path." Markets read that as a hold on guidance, not on policy. After the meeting, investors priced more than three moves over the following 12 months, up from two to three beforehand.

What would change the picture

The next policy meeting is on 29 October. Forecasters are split. Some expect a 25bp increase then and another in December, taking the deposit rate to 3.00%. Others call October "live" but think the ECB may wait until December. Sources cited by Reuters said further tightening was likely and could come as soon as 29 October.

That makes the arithmetic of this week's print simple. A final figure that matches 3.8% with core at 2.5% leaves expectations where they are. A downward revision to core would help the patient camp. A move up would help the October camp, because it would suggest energy costs are spreading into other prices, which is the channel Lagarde has said she is watching.

The release is a calendar item rather than a turning point. The signals that matter more sit further out: the oil price into late October, and the core reading in the October flash estimate, which is due just after the ECB meets. Rate-setters will decide on 29 October without it.

The same pressure is visible elsewhere. Markets are pricing a Bank of England hike on 5 November, and US CPI sits at the centre of a thin week for Fed expectations.
