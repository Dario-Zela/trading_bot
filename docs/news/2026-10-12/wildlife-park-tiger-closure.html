Wildlife park stays closed after fatal tiger attack

In one sentence
A man died after entering the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park near Doncaster; the park remained closed on Sunday as police and the park establish how he got in.

A man has died after entering the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park, near Doncaster, and the park stayed shut on Sunday while police and the park's own staff work out how it happened.

The alarm was raised at about 1:35pm on Saturday, according to police, after reports that a man had got into the enclosure. Officers and medical staff went to the park. He had been attacked by one of the tigers and died at the scene. Police said the animal was not injured, stayed inside its enclosure, and that there was no wider risk to the public. The park was evacuated, and officers were still at the site on Saturday night.

What is known

The man has not been publicly named. Police said his family had been told and were being supported by specialist officers. How he got into the enclosure was not immediately clear.

Police also said they were aware of footage on social media that appeared to show him inside the enclosure shortly before the attack. They urged people not to share what they called a "distressing video".

The tiger involved is Altai, an Amur tiger. The park said he is "safe and well" and will not be euthanised.

In a statement on Sunday, the park expressed sympathy to the man's family and said it would remain closed while staff "deeply affected by events" received specialist support. It said it was working closely with the authorities to establish exactly what happened.

The park

Yorkshire Wildlife Park opened in 2009. Its Land of the Tigers attraction, a two-acre (0.4-hectare) reserve for endangered Amur, or Siberian, tigers, followed in 2011. Visitors watch from a 300-metre raised walkway.

What is not yet known

The question of licensing and safety, which prompted the closure coverage, is where the available reporting thins out. The accounts reviewed for this article describe the park working with the authorities but do not set out a formal inquiry, name a regulator or say what a review of the park's licence would cover. The central factual question is simpler and still open: how a member of the public came to be inside a tiger enclosure that is overlooked by a raised walkway.

UK zoos operate under a licensing regime run by local councils, with inspections. After past fatal incidents at British zoos, closures have typically lasted until a council inspection was complete. Hamerton Zoo Park in Cambridgeshire, for example, reopened after a licence and public-safety inspection by its district council. Nothing yet indicates how long Yorkshire's closure will last.

What to watch

The next markers are the police's account of how the man entered the enclosure, his formal identification, and any statement from the local authority on whether the park's licence conditions are being reviewed. The park has not said when it will reopen.
