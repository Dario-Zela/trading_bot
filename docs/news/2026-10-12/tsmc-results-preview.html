TSMC results due this week as a read-through for the AI-chip trade

In one sentence
TSMC's third-quarter results on 15 October will test whether AI-chip demand is still accelerating, with guidance, capex and margins likely to move Nvidia and ASML.

TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, reports third-quarter results on Thursday, and investors are treating the numbers as a test of whether spending on AI chips is still accelerating. Little in the print itself is likely to be a surprise. The market is waiting for what the company says about the next few quarters.

Why the foundry matters

TSMC manufactures the accelerators Nvidia designs, so its order book is an early indicator of how many AI chips will reach customers. It also depends on ASML, the sole supplier of the extreme-ultraviolet lithography machines needed for the most advanced chips it makes. A move in TSMC's outlook therefore tends to carry through to both companies, and to the wider semiconductor trade.

TSMC's investor site lists the earnings call for Thursday 15 October at 2pm Taiwan time, which is 2am Eastern. The company's quiet period runs from 5 to 14 October. Third-party calendars also place ASML's results on 15 October; that date should be checked against ASML's own calendar.

What is already known

The headline number is largely in the public domain. TSMC publishes monthly sales, and its September figure, released on 8 October, was NT$511.86 billion. That was 0.6% below August but 54.6% above a year earlier. Reports based on the monthly data put third-quarter sales at about NT$1.49 trillion, or roughly $46.7 billion. That compares with LSEG consensus of NT$1.46 trillion, and with TSMC's own guidance of $44.6 billion to $45.8 billion.

A revenue beat is thus mostly anticipated. The monthly release carries no profit or margin data, so Thursday brings the first full read on gross margin. TSMC guided to 65%–67% for the quarter, and one report puts consensus earnings per share near $4.39. Consensus figures vary by source and date, and an earlier July estimate predates the guidance increase.

The company has said 2026 revenue in dollar terms should grow by slightly more than 40%. It has also raised planned capital expenditure to between $60 billion and $64 billion. Reports say its 5/4nm and 3nm capacity is fully booked.

What would move the trade

With the quarter largely known, three items are likely to matter more:

  • Fourth-quarter guidance. A range implying continued sequential growth would support the argument that AI demand has not peaked. Flat guidance after a record quarter would invite the opposite reading.
  • Capital spending. A further increase from the $60–64 billion range would be good news for ASML, whose equipment orders follow foundry investment. A trim would hurt it first.
  • Margins. TSMC's pricing power on advanced nodes is part of the bull case for the whole supply chain. A margin miss, even with strong revenue, would raise questions about costs and mix.

The asymmetry is plain enough. Sales running well ahead of consensus have set a high bar, and a guidance range merely in line could register as a disappointment even though the underlying business is growing by more than half year on year.

What comes next

The sequence is TSMC first, then ASML on the same day if the calendar listings hold, then Nvidia, whose next quarterly results arrive later in the autumn. Each will be read against the others. A cautious TSMC on Thursday would put the AI-chip trade on the defensive before the companies that depend on its output have spoken.
