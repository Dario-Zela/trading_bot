Markets price about 90% chance of a Bank of England hike to 4.0% on 5 November

In one sentence
After a 6-3 hold at 3.75% in September, Bailey and three deputies have signalled openness to a hike, and markets give about 90% odds of a 25bp rise to 4.0% on 5 November.

The Bank of England's rate-setters held Bank Rate at 3.75% last month, but the vote was 6-3, and the three who lost wanted a rise. Since then the Governor and three deputy governors have said publicly that they could join them. Money markets now put the chance of a quarter-point increase to 4.0% at the 5 November meeting at around 90%. That is a market-implied probability, not a decision, and the figures here come mainly from one trade-press account, Pound Sterling Live. The Bank's own minutes and speeches are the primary record.

A hold that did not look like one

The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) left rates unchanged on 17 September for the sixth meeting running. Professional Pensions reports that Megan Greene, Catherine Mann and Huw Pill voted to raise to 4.0%. Pound Sterling Live says the same three also voted for a rise in June and July. The dissent is therefore a standing position, not a one-off.

What has changed is the centre of the committee. Pound Sterling Live reports that deputy governors Sarah Breeden, Clare Lombardelli and Dave Ramsden said in September they could back a hike. Governor Andrew Bailey has also moved. In a speech in Istanbul on 8 October, he said a "look-through" approach to energy-driven inflation "only works if inflation expectations stay anchored to target". Pound Sterling Live reads that as a signal that the Bank's tolerance for letting energy prices pass through is running out.

Lombardelli is described as the swing vote. In Warsaw on 24 September she said policy was "increasingly likely to need to tighten if elevated energy prices persist". That is conditional language, not a commitment. The committee has nine members, so five votes are needed. Greene, Pill and Mann are three. Bailey and the three deputies would take a hike past the line, if they all moved.

What the market is pricing

The backdrop is not subtle:

  • Brent crude is near $103 a barrel (see Houthi strikes on Riyadh).
  • The 10-year gilt yield reached 5.44% on Wednesday, a 19-year high.
  • The Bank expects inflation to rise above 4% in the coming months. It has been above the 2% target in all but three months of the past five years.
  • Markets price roughly four hikes over the next two years, so a November move would be the start of a path, not a one-off.

Sterling has been firm rather than frantic. GBP/USD is at about 1.3244 and GBP/EUR at 1.1788, close to its one-year high. Resistance for the dollar pair sits at 1.3274, then the 30 September peak of 1.3311.

The forecasters quoted are not unanimous on the path. Andrew Goodwin of Oxford Economics says a November hike "looks ever more certain". Berenberg expects one 25bp rise and then a return to loosening in the fourth quarter of 2027. Jeremy Stretch of CIBC thinks the market is "too deterministic". A 90% price leaves little room for a surprise. If the committee holds, sterling and gilts would probably move sharply the other way.

Why it matters

The interpretive claim here is that the easing cycle has stalled. The evidence is the vote count: a 6-3 hold with the three dissenters leaning hawkish, followed by public conditional support from the Governor and three deputies. The committee that once split between holding and cutting is now split between holding and tightening. That is a different debate from the one households and firms were planning around.

The channels are conventional. Higher Bank Rate feeds into fixed-rate mortgage pricing and corporate borrowing costs, and with gilt yields already at multi-decade highs, lenders have less room to absorb it. The Treasury also has less room. The Institute for Fiscal Studies has already warned that the Budget lacks a plan for £35bn of pressures (see the IFS on fiscal headroom). A tighter Bank Rate raises the cost of that gap.

The UK is also not alone in facing a hawkish-leaning central bank. Traders are watching the same energy-inflation dynamic in the US and the euro area.

What to watch

The next fixed point is the Budget on 28 October, which will show how much fiscal tightening the Treasury is prepared to add, and how gilts react. Then comes the MPC decision on 5 November, published with the vote split. The number to read is not just whether the Bank hikes, but how many members vote for it. A 9-0 or 8-1 vote would suggest the committee sees more than a single move. A narrow 5-4 would suggest the market had run ahead of the Bank.
