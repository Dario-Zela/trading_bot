Burnham to release details of his Manchester City meetings

In one sentence
Prime Minister Andy Burnham is set to publish details of his Manchester City meetings after Lib Dem demands, but what the release will cover is not yet known.

Andy Burnham, the prime minister, is set to publish details of his meetings with Manchester City, according to the BBC. The move follows ten days of pressure over his remarks on the club's owners after a Premier League commission ruled against the club.

The BBC report is the only source for the announcement. As briefed, it gives no date for publication, no description of the documents and no quote. What will be released, and how far back it will reach, is not yet known.

How the demand arose

Burnham took office on 20 July 2026, succeeding Keir Starmer, and spent years before that as Mayor of Greater Manchester, a post he first won in 2017. In that role he dealt regularly with the club's owners, who have been part of the city's regeneration story.

The pressure began after an independent Premier League commission found City guilty of serious financial breaches. The club has appealed. Burnham said he would be concerned if the owners were forced to sell, and described them as a key partner in building modern Manchester, according to reports.

On 2 October the Liberal Democrats, through their culture, media and sport spokesperson Anna Sabine, wrote to him asking for disclosure. Reuters reported that the letter sought details of meetings with City Football Group, Abu Dhabi United Group or representatives of the UAE government. The Guardian reported that it reached back to his time as mayor and also covered any hospitality received.

What the stakes are

The significance depends on what the records show. Routine contact between a regional mayor and a major local employer and investor would be unremarkable. Contact that touched on the disciplinary case, or on the owners' position, would be harder to defend, particularly now that Burnham is prime minister and his earlier remarks were made in public.

The Lib Dem request is wider than ministerial practice usually requires. Ministers' meetings are published in periodic transparency returns. Records from Burnham's mayoral years, and any hospitality, sit under a different regime, and it is not clear which of those he intends to cover.

The case is also a test of how far a government will go in leaving a sport's own disciplinary process to run its course. The City ruling is under appeal, and a prime minister who has publicly voiced concern about the outcome has invited questions about whether his position could be seen as influencing it. Publishing the records would let others check that.

What to watch

Three things will settle how much the release matters: its date, its time span, and whether it includes hospitality and contact with Abu Dhabi officials rather than only the club. The Lib Dems have asked for all three. Whether they judge the release sufficient will show quickly. The outcome of City's appeal will determine how much the disclosure matters politically.
