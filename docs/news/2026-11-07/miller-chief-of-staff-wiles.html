Trump names Stephen Miller chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles from 9 November

In one sentence
An unconfirmed Truth Social post, relayed by Benzinga, says Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as chief of staff on 9 November; Trump has recently denied such reports.

A report that Donald Trump has named Stephen Miller his chief of staff, to replace Susie Wiles from 9 November, has not been confirmed. The claim comes from a Truth Social post as relayed by the financial site Benzinga. Checks for a wire report, a White House statement or the post itself turned up nothing. What follows is what the claim would mean if it holds, and what is known about the two people involved.

What is claimed, and what is not established

As relayed, the announcement says Miller takes over on 9 November, days after the midterm elections. Wiles would move to the Trump political operation. No independent outlet has been found carrying the post or a White House confirmation, and the Truth Social text could not be inspected directly.

The claim also sits against recent denials. Earlier reports, some from anonymous or partisan-leaning sources, said Trump had been sounding out allies on whether to replace Wiles. Miller was named on one reported shortlist, alongside Steve Witkoff and Bill Pulte. Trump dismissed a Politico report on the subject as fake news. He called Wiles "my loyal and longest-serving Chief of Staff" and said she "has my COMPLETE confidence." A reversal days after the midterms is possible, but it would need to be shown, not inferred from a secondary write-up.

Two careers, one West Wing

Wiles ran Trump's 2024 campaign and was named chief of staff on 7 November 2024, the first woman to hold the job. She is known for avoiding the public eye and for managing a staff and a president who both resist management.

Miller is already inside the building. He serves as deputy chief of staff for policy and as homeland security adviser, and he reports to Wiles. He has long been the administration's most prominent advocate of hardline immigration policy. He was an early, loyal Trump aide, and his influence over the border and deportation agenda is well documented.

Why the post matters

The chief of staff controls who reaches the president, what paper he sees and how the staff is run. A move from Wiles to Miller would therefore change process as well as personnel. Policy that now runs through a coordinator could run through its most committed advocate. Moving Wiles to the political operation would also fit the shift after the midterms toward 2028. Our colleagues' analysis of the vote, After the midterms: four takeaways for 2028, covers that terrain.

That reading is conditional. Moving a campaign architect into a political role is a natural step after an election, but it is also what a graceful exit is often called. Without the original post or any White House statement, the stated reasons cannot be tested.

What to watch

The test is simple. A White House statement, an official announcement or a wire report would settle it, and Trump's own account should carry the post itself. If 9 November passes with Wiles still in her office and no correction, the Benzinga account will have been wrong or premature. Until one of those happens, the report should be treated as a claim, not a change of government.
