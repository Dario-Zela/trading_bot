Married at First Sight UK axed

In one sentence
Married at First Sight UK has been axed, and its cast reacted with relief, tears and sadness, the BBC reports, after months of controversy over contributor welfare.

Married at First Sight UK, the Channel 4 reality series in which strangers marry on first meeting, has been axed. According to the BBC, cast members have responded with a mix of relief, tears and sadness.

The reaction fits the show's recent history. In May, the BBC's Panorama aired The Dark Side of Married at First Sight, in which three former brides said they had been abused during filming. Channel 4 then removed earlier series from its streaming service and said an external review had been launched. It said the wellbeing of contributors was of paramount importance. Former production staff separately told the press that some producers were preoccupied with whether contestants were being intimate. As of May, reports on whether the programme would continue were conflicting, and an executive denied it had been cancelled at that point.

A format that ran its course

The premise is simple. Specialists pair single people who have never met, and the couples wed on camera and then live together for weeks. The show worked as a social experiment and as Sunday-night viewing, and it made minor celebrities of its participants.

For some of those participants, the end of the show brings relief that it is over. Others are sad to lose the platform, or the friendships made in the strange hothouse of filming. A single cancellation can prompt both responses, as the BBC's account of the cast's reaction suggests.

What the cancellation changes

Little beyond the programme itself. It matters to viewers who followed the couples, and to the cast and production staff whose work ends with it. The wider reality-TV business is unlikely to feel it, though it does show how far a duty-of-care row can reach once a broadcaster pulls a show's back catalogue.

I could not independently confirm the individual cast reactions beyond the BBC's summary, so none are quoted here.

What to watch

The external review Channel 4 commissioned is the next thing to look for. Whether its findings are published, and what they say about the care of contributors, will say more about the show's legacy than the cancellation announcement does.
