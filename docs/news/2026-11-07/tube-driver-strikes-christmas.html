Tube driver strikes planned in run-up to Christmas

In one sentence
The RMT has called 24-hour Tube driver strikes for 26 November, 17 December and 19 December, over a four-day-week and rostering dispute, while Aslef has announced no action.

London Underground drivers belonging to the RMT union are planning a series of 24-hour strikes, with three walkouts falling in the weeks before Christmas. The dispute is over how drivers' rosters are reorganised. It threatens the capital's commuting and its busiest shopping weeks.

The dates

The RMT has announced eight walkouts, each starting at midday and lasting 24 hours. Three fall before the holiday: Thursday 26 November, Thursday 17 December and Saturday 19 December. The others are in the new year: 4 and 6 January, then 17, 23 and 25 February. The 19 December date is the last Saturday before Christmas week, a peak day for West End and high-street trade.

During major walkouts much of the Tube network can be affected. The Elizabeth line, the Overground and the Docklands Light Railway are expected to keep running, so some passengers will have alternatives. Those routes will probably be crowded on strike days.

What the dispute is about

The row centres on a "voluntary" four-day working week for drivers and a related Trains Modernisation Framework. The RMT says the package would make drivers give up existing protections, whether they work four days or five. It also says the package would mean more driving time, earlier starts and later finishes, and a risk of drivers being moved between depots. The union argues it could open the way to hundreds of job cuts.

The union says it backs a genuine four-day week, just not one it believes is being used to cut jobs or worsen conditions.

"There is nothing voluntary about a scheme that forces drivers to give up existing protections," said Eddie Dempsey, the RMT's general secretary. He said London Underground "must withdraw these damaging proposals".

The dispute is not united on the drivers' side. Aslef, the other union representing Tube drivers, has announced no strike action. Its London Underground organiser, Finn Brennan, has said management agreed that anyone who wants to keep working a five-day week will be able to. He described the RMT action as "a strike designed to stop people having the choice of a shorter working week".

That split matters for how much disruption the strikes cause. The RMT represents a substantial share of drivers but not all of them. How many Tube trains run on strike days will depend on how many drivers walk out and on how many Aslef members keep working. Neither the union nor management has published figures on this.

What is not yet clear

The sources reviewed did not set out the details of the RMT ballot, such as turnout or the margin of the vote. Nor did they carry a full response from Transport for London. Reports indicate the dispute could still be settled before the first strike. Strikes of this kind have been called off at the last minute before, usually after a revised offer.

What to watch

The first test is 26 November. By then, either London Underground will have put a revised package to the RMT, or the union will have shown how much of the network it can stop. Any talks in the intervening weeks will show whether management is prepared to change the framework's terms on protections and depot moves. Failing that, the 17 and 19 December dates would fall squarely in the final fortnight of Christmas trading.
