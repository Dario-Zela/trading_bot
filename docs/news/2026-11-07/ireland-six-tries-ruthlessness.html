Ireland run in six tries but questions remain over ruthlessness

In one sentence
A six-try autumn win for Ireland has prompted debate over their finishing, which would matter in the Six Nations only if the pattern of missed chances repeats.

Ireland scored six tries in their latest autumn international and still left the debate about their finishing unresolved. A big winning margin has prompted a familiar question: whether a side that creates this many chances takes enough of them.

The score and opponent are not given in the source material for this piece and could not be independently confirmed, so this report does not state them. Ireland's autumn schedule lists Argentina at the Aviva Stadium on 6 November, but no match report was available to check against. BBC Sport's coverage describes the discussion about ruthlessness. It does not name a critic or cite a statistic, and none is invented here.

The argument

The finishing debate is a standard one for a team that wins comfortably. Six tries is a healthy return by any measure. The criticism is about the chances that did not become points: a pass dropped at the line, a final ball forced, a visiting defence let off when the game was still live. Without the match data, it is not possible to say how many such moments there were or how they compare with recent Ireland performances.

The distinction matters. A side can score six and still be wasteful if it dominated territory and possession for many more. It can also look wasteful in victory simply because expectations are high. Ireland have spent several years being judged against a standard of near-clinical efficiency, and a performance that falls a little short of it draws comment even when the scoreboard is generous.

Why it is a small story, for now

On its own, this is a mild talking point. A winning margin that attracts questions rather than praise is not a crisis, and the autumn series exists partly to let a squad work through exactly these imperfections before the tournament that counts.

It becomes a larger story only if it recurs. Six Nations matches are tighter. They are played against sides who defend well and give away little, and a missed chance there can decide a result rather than merely trim a margin. If the same pattern shows up in February and March, it will bear on selection, particularly in the backs and at half-back, where decisions in the final third are made.

What to watch

The remaining autumn fixtures are the next test. Ireland's coaches will look at how many clear chances become tries against stronger opposition, and whether the side's decision-making in the final third improves. The Six Nations squad announcement will show whether any of this has changed their thinking.
