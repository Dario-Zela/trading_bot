Gladiator II stuntman sues over injuries from on-set explosion

In one sentence
A British actor working as an extra on Gladiator II is suing Paramount's August Street Films in the High Court over burns from a 2023 gas explosion in Morocco; liability is admitted.

A British actor who was working as an extra on Gladiator II is suing the production company over an explosion on the film's Morocco set in 2023. The claim, which appears in a report from the BBC, is for burns and lasting scarring. The defendant has admitted liability for the accident but not the injuries as alleged.

The claimant is Edward Sarsfield, 47, from Liverpool. Triage described him as a stuntman. The report as it reached us describes a British stuntman and actor who was working as an extra and played a dead soldier. His High Court claim in London names August Street Films, a Paramount subsidiary, as defendant. He is asking for £200,000, a figure the company has not agreed. We could not reach the BBC original or the court filing, so the account below relies on a syndicated copy of the report.

What the claim says

The scene was a burning ship. Sarsfield was lying on it, a few feet from a gas line that fed gas-powered flame bars. The claim says that, "without warning, there was a gas explosion that caught the Claimant and blew him forwards." It puts the flames at about 20ft and says his costume caught fire. He "panicked and ran" before colleagues threw water on him, and he "remained in excruciating pain."

He was flown by helicopter to a hospital in Marrakesh and repatriated to the UK four days later. The claim alleges:

  • flash burns to about 15% of his body, including his face, arms and hands;
  • injuries to both knees and his right wrist;
  • permanent, hypersensitive scarring that means he must avoid the sun;
  • reduced mobility from "considerable physical de-conditioning" and psychological symptoms.

The studio's position

August Street Films admits liability for the accident. Its defence adds that "no admissions are made as to the alleged injuries sustained by the Claimant." That is a common split in personal-injury cases. It narrows the dispute to how badly he was hurt, and so to how much he is owed.

Paramount said at the time that the incident happened during a planned stunt sequence. It said "several crew members experienced non-life-threatening injuries" and that on-site safety and medical teams responded quickly. It also said it has "strict health and safety procedures in place on all our productions." Reports in June 2023 put the number hurt at six, with burns the common injury. Early talk of a ruptured gas pipe was unconfirmed then. Sarsfield, the company and Paramount declined to comment further on the claim.

What it does and doesn't show

This is one individual's claim, and the report points to no wider pattern. It describes no safety failure beyond the explosion itself, and it names no other claimants. An admission of liability suggests the studio does not expect to contest that something went wrong. It does not establish why. The pleadings as reported say nothing on whether the gas rig was inspected, whether performers near it were briefed, or whether the lack of warning broke any rule. Those questions would only come up if the case ran to trial, and an admitted-liability claim often does not.

The case still touches a familiar tension in large productions. Extras and background performers are placed close to effects rigs for the sake of a convincing frame, often with less briefing than principal stunt performers get. Filming abroad also complicates emergency care. Here, the nearest treatment the claim describes was a hospital in Marrakesh.

The next marker is procedural. The court will have to settle the extent of the injuries and the damages, either by agreement or by a ruling. Any settlement may not be made public.
