Saputo's third-quarter results draw a higher RBC price target

In one sentence
RBC reportedly kept Outperform and raised its Saputo target after a second-quarter (not third) report with adjusted EBITDA up 13.8% and margin at 10.3%; target figures are unconfirmed.

Saputo's latest quarterly report was a good one, and RBC Capital has reportedly responded by keeping its Outperform rating and raising its price target. The label attached to the quarter needs correcting first. Saputo's fiscal year ends on March 31, so the three months to September 30, 2026 are its second quarter of fiscal 2027, not its third. The company's own release is titled "Second Quarter of Fiscal 2027 Ended September 30, 2026". The third quarter ends in December and is normally reported in February.

What the quarter showed

The numbers, released after the close on November 5, are better than routine. All figures are in Canadian dollars and cover continuing operations.

  • Revenue rose 3.6% to $4.256 billion, from $4.108 billion a year earlier.
  • Adjusted EBITDA rose 13.8% to $438 million, from $385 million.
  • The adjusted EBITDA margin widened to 10.3% from 9.4%.
  • Adjusted earnings per share were $0.55 diluted, against $0.43.

Revenue grew by a few percent and profit by roughly four times that. That points to margin and mix, not volume.

Where the margin came from

The US sector did most of the work. Its adjusted EBITDA rose 22.8% to $205 million on revenue that slipped 0.6% to $2.140 billion, lifting its margin to 9.6% from 7.8%. Saputo's outlook text describes US ingredients as keeping "positive momentum", and the company has pointed to high-protein dairy ingredients as a driver.

International revenue rose 20.4% to $701 million and adjusted EBITDA rose 38.5% to $54 million. The Canadian sector was flat at $179 million of adjusted EBITDA, and its margin eased to 12.7% from 13.0%.

The outlook carries a caution. Saputo expects US cheese markets to face more supply as milk production rises. Cheese prices matter more to its US earnings than any single quarter's ingredient mix.

Portfolio pruning

The headline net earnings figure is muddied by disposals. Saputo has agreed to sell its UK dairy division to Lactalis for about $1.661 billion (£880 million). It sold 80% of its Argentine dairy business in June for about $710 million. It also has a deal to sell its Australian joint-venture stake to Danone Asia for about $254 million, expected to close in the third quarter. Net earnings from continuing operations were $213 million, up from $171 million. Including discontinued operations, quarterly net earnings were $259 million, up 40%. For the six months, the discontinued businesses produced a $130 million loss, which left total net earnings at $262 million against $350 million a year earlier.

The company repurchased 12.5 million shares for about $516 million in the first half and declared a quarterly dividend of $0.21 a share, payable December 11. It intends to renew its buyback programme, which expires November 18.

The RBC call

The reported RBC move is the least certain part of the story. The source material says only that RBC kept an Outperform rating and raised its target. The new and previous target figures could not be confirmed against a dated RBC note or a primary source, and none is given here. RBC's last confirmed target changes were earlier in the year, including a rise from C$47 to C$50 in February. A target change of this kind reflects one analyst's view of one stock. The earnings release is the more informative document.

The quarter does include something new. A 0.9-point margin gain, concentrated in US ingredients, is the sort of result that prompts target revisions. The open question is whether it lasts.

What to watch

The next test is the third-quarter report, expected in early February. It will show whether US ingredient margins hold as cheese-market supply grows. The Australian sale is scheduled to close in that quarter, and the UK sale is expected to complete within fiscal 2027.
