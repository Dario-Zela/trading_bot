Nationwide launches savings account paying 5.3%

In one sentence
Nationwide's 5.30% Member Exclusive Bond tops three-year fixed-rate tables, but it is a 30-month, £10,000-capped product for existing members only, so it is a loyalty offer, not a market shift.

Nationwide Building Society has put a 5.30% rate on a new savings product, but the account is narrower than the headline suggests. It is a 30-month fixed-rate bond open only to existing members, with a £10,000 cap on what each saver can put in. The society launched it on 3 November as the Member Exclusive Bond.

What is on offer

The bond pays 5.30% AER, gross and fixed. On the full £10,000, Nationwide says a saver would earn £1,381.92 in interest over the 30 months, assuming the money goes in on day one. The money must be paid in within 14 days of opening the account. Savers can open it in branch, online or through the app, and each saver is limited to one account.

Eligibility is the main restriction. The bond is for Nationwide members, including Virgin Money customers who are also Nationwide members. Moneyfacts Compare reports that applicants must have held a current account, savings account or mortgage with Nationwide or Virgin Money since at least 2 November 2026. Nationwide's announcement does not set out early-withdrawal terms. It says only that the society can withdraw the offer at any time.

Is it market leading?

On the evidence available, yes, within limits. Moneyfacts says the 5.30% is the highest rate among three-year fixed bonds and is joint-top of the best-buy tables for two-year bonds, alongside RCI Bank UK. The 30-month term sits between those two categories, so the comparison is imperfect.

The lead is also not absolute. Afin Bank's five-year account is reported to pay 5.40% AER, though that means a longer lock-in. The claim holds for this term and not for every savings product.

A loyalty product, not a market shift

The cap and the eligibility rule limit how much this can do to the wider market. The £10,000 ceiling means the most the bond can attract from any one saver is modest. Restricting it to existing customers mainly protects the society's own deposits rather than pulling money from rivals. Nationwide's own framing points the same way. It cites a Censuswide survey of 2,000 UK adults, carried out 9–12 October, in which 85% said existing customers should be rewarded at least as much as new ones. Some 59% believed the best rates go only to new customers.

"We don't think being a loyal customer should mean watching the best deals go to someone else," said Richard Stocker, Nationwide's head of savings.

The survey was commissioned for the launch, so it shows the pitch Nationwide wants to make more than independent evidence of saver behaviour. Nothing in the material reviewed shows deposits moving between providers or rivals changing their rates in response. A single product with a ceiling of this size is more likely to follow the market than to move it.

What to watch

Two things would turn this from a launch into a signal. One is whether competitors with larger or open-access bond ranges match 5.30% over comparable terms. The other is the next set of Bank of England and Moneyfacts data on average fixed-bond rates. For savers, the practical points are the lock-in, the lack of published early-access terms and the tax treatment of the interest above the personal savings allowance. Savers with larger balances may find an ISA wrapper worth considering.
