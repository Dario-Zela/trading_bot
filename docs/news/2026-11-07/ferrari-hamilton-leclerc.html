Ferrari's Hamilton–Leclerc tension tops F1 run-in storylines

In one sentence
Friction between Ferrari's Hamilton and Leclerc has run through 2026, and the Brazil, Las Vegas and Qatar races give it three more chances to turn into a decision.

Two drivers, one red car, and a championship run-in that nobody at Maranello wants to be defined by an argument. Ferrari's pairing of Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc has produced friction for much of the 2026 season, and with three races listed on the calendar from this weekend, it is the subplot most likely to follow the team to the finish.

No single new incident prompts this note. The BBC Sport piece that flagged the storyline attaches no fresh quote or result to it, and the case for it rests on a season's worth of accumulated evidence rather than one afternoon's.

A season of small fractures

Hamilton joined Ferrari for the 2025 season, and the arrangement was always going to test the team's talent for managing two big egos in one garage. Leclerc is the home favourite who has been with the team for years. Hamilton is the seven-time champion hired to finish a job the team has chased since 2007.

Reporting this season has pointed to several flashpoints. Motorsport and fan-site coverage describes the pair making contact at Turn 2 on the opening lap of the Italian Grand Prix at Monza. Leclerc has since said he sometimes went too far in their on-track battles. Team radio has been another recurring source of friction. After Monaco, Hamilton said communication "wasn't that clear" in a race that again strained his relationship with his race engineer, Riccardo Adami. Several outlets have also reported on wider tension at the top of the team, including comments from chairman John Elkann and Hamilton's response to them. Those reports vary in quality, and the detail is not independently confirmed here.

Treat the more breathless of those accounts with care. Ferrari has consistently played down the radio exchanges, and both drivers have publicly insisted there are no bad feelings. A fair reading is that the rivalry is real but has been narrated more loudly than it has been lived.

What the run-in offers

The calendar supplies the stage. Brazil is on 8 November, Las Vegas on 22 November and Qatar on 29 November. Each gives Ferrari the chance to repeat a familiar test: what happens when the cars run nose to tail and the pit wall has to choose.

That choice is the real story. Team orders are the usual currency of these situations, and Ferrari has a long and not always happy history of using them. A swap request that one driver resists would turn a simmering theme into a dated, quotable event. Until then, the tension is context rather than news.

A second marker is the standings between the two. Reports have suggested the gap between them has narrowed through the second half of the season, though the exact margin should be checked against the official classification before anyone leans on it.

What to watch

The next data point is Brazil this weekend. Watch the first-lap sequence, any radio exchange about position, and whether Ferrari holds its drivers to a strict running order or lets them race. If the team declines to intervene and the pair touch again, the subplot becomes the plot.
