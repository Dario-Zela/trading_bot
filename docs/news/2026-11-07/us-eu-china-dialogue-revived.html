US–EU channel on China talks revived after stalling since Trump's return

In one sentence
The SCMP reports, citing unnamed sources, that the US and EU will resume their China dialogue in Brussels in early December; agenda and outcomes are unconfirmed.

A channel through which Washington and Brussels compare notes on China is reported to be reopening, after going quiet for most of the time Donald Trump has been back in the White House. The South China Morning Post, which broke the story, says the two sides plan to resume their dialogue on China in Brussels in the first week of December. The report rests on unnamed sources. The White House, the State Department and the European External Action Service have not confirmed it publicly, and no second outlet has independently verified the detail.

What is being reported

According to the SCMP, the approach came from the American side. The paper reports that the US delegation would be led by Christopher Landau, the deputy secretary of state, and that an agenda has not been settled. Earlier rounds ranged across China's support for Russia's war effort, technology controls, Taiwan and human rights. Whether the December session will cover the same ground, or concentrate on trade and export controls, is not yet known.

The SCMP also frames the meeting as one part of a busy end to the year in great-power diplomacy. It says a further meeting between Kaja Kallas, the EU's foreign-policy chief, and China's foreign minister, Wang Yi, is under discussion, though no date has been given.

The reporting is not entirely consistent about how long the gap has been. One account describes a stall dating from Mr Trump's return in January 2025; another speaks of a break of two years. Both cannot be exact, and the discrepancy is a reason for caution about the precise history.

Why the channel matters

The dialogue has a short and uneven history. It was proposed in 2020, in Mr Trump's first term, and went nowhere, partly because Europeans doubted that Washington's approach was coordinated with theirs. Under Joe Biden it was revived in 2021 and became a regular forum, with senior officials from both sides meeting to compare assessments of Beijing.

Since 2025 the two sides have had more reasons to talk past each other than to each other. Tariff disputes between the US and the EU have soured relations, and Brussels has run a separate track with Beijing. In October the EU trade commissioner, Maroš Šefčovič, held talks in Beijing with China's commerce minister, Wang Wentao. Analysts described the aim as stabilisation rather than breakthrough. The most concrete item was a preliminary understanding on curbing exports of Chinese hybrid vehicles to the EU.

That context shapes how the revived channel should be read. If Washington wants European help on chip export controls or on the terms of its own trade understanding with China, it needs Brussels at the table before the EU settles its own arrangements. The timing, soon after the EU-China talks, is consistent with that motive, though the sources do not say so.

What would make it substantive

A single meeting in Brussels does not amount to a shared policy. The dialogue could be a procedural exercise, with officials trading assessments and issuing a short statement. It would become more significant if it produced coordination on specific measures: aligned controls on advanced semiconductors, common positions on Chinese overcapacity, or a joint approach to critical minerals. Exporters and chipmakers on both sides would feel the effect of such steps. A general communiqué would change little.

The pieces elsewhere in this edition show how closely the strands are linked. Washington is pressing Beijing to meet its Busan trade commitments even as American companies book large Chinese orders, and Europe is watching to see whether a US-China settlement leaves it exposed.

What to watch

The first marker is whether either government confirms the December date and names its delegation. The second is the published readout, if there is one: a list of topics would indicate a procedural exchange, while named measures would indicate alignment. Until then the report is best read as a sign that the channel is being reopened, not as evidence of what it will achieve.
