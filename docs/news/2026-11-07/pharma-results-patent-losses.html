J&J and Merck results show newer drugs offsetting patent losses

In one sentence
J&J's drug unit grew about 8% despite a 54% Stelara slump and Merck beat estimates, but a $550m Merck pull-forward and the looming Keytruda cliff leave the offset thesis unproven.

Two of the world's largest drugmakers reported third-quarter results within a week of each other, and both show newer products covering for older ones that are losing protection. Neither quarter settles whether that holds once Merck's Keytruda loses exclusivity, which is expected from 2028. The reported figures do show the offset working at J&J today.

J&J: the drag is large, and it is being covered

Johnson & Johnson's Innovative Medicine unit, the pharmaceutical arm, had sales of about $16.8bn in the quarter. That is up 7.7% as reported and 7.9% operationally. The biosimilar erosion of Stelara, its psoriasis and Crohn's drug, took roughly 6.9 percentage points off that operational growth, according to the company's 10-Q. Stelara revenue fell 54.4% to $716m, by one account of the filing. Growth of 7.9% after that drag implies the rest of the portfolio grew at a low-teens rate.

The replacements are visible. Tremfya, an immunology drug, was reported up 68%. Carvykti, a cell therapy for myeloma, rose 35.5% to $710m. Oncology grew 16.4%. Group sales were $25.4bn, up 5.9%, and guidance was raised. The weak spot was MedTech, which grew 2.5%. Reports differ on whether total sales beat or missed estimates, and Zacks says they fell slightly short. GAAP net earnings dropped about 96% to roughly $210m, which reflects a proposed talc litigation resolution and deal-related costs. These figures come from secondary summaries of the filing and should be checked against it.

Merck: strong quarter, with a pull-forward

Merck reported on 29 October. Revenue was $18.1bn against a consensus of about $17.3bn, and the company raised the low end of its full-year guidance to $66.8bn from $66.3bn. The high end stays at $67.3bn.

Keytruda brought in $8.6bn, up 6% from $8.15bn, including $830m from the subcutaneous Keytruda QLEX. Gardasil sales rose about 14% to just under $2bn, helped by early US government purchasing after weak demand in China. Winrevair, a pulmonary hypertension drug, nearly doubled to $694m, ahead of the roughly $645m analysts expected.

About $550m of the quarter's growth came from customers buying earlier than planned. That figure is roughly 3% of revenue, and it is likely to make the next quarter look softer.

What the evidence supports

The Zacks item that prompted this piece says Merck shares show "sustained strength" but gives no price move or time window, so that claim cannot be tested here. What can be tested is the revenue arithmetic, and it is less symmetrical than the headline suggests.

J&J is absorbing a loss that has already happened. Stelara is down by more than half, and the unit still grew nearly 8%. That is evidence the offset is real, though one company over one quarter is a small sample.

Merck is in a different position. Keytruda is still growing, and it supplies close to half of group revenue. At $8.6bn for the quarter, it is on a run rate above $34bn a year. Winrevair at $694m a quarter, and a Gardasil business that is volatile, are small against that. Keytruda QLEX is also a lifecycle measure rather than a new franchise, since it moves existing patients onto a formulation that carries later protection. Whether the newer products can fill a Keytruda-sized gap is therefore still an open question.

What to watch

Merck's fourth-quarter report, likely in early 2027, will show how much of the $550m pull-forward reverses. Its Keytruda QLEX uptake will indicate how much of the franchise is shifting to the subcutaneous version. For J&J, the next marker is the first-quarter 2027 Stelara decline set against Tremfya and Carvykti growth. Together those figures will show whether the quarter's offset is a pattern or a one-off.
