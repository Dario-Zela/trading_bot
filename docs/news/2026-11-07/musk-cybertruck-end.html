Musk appears to signal the end of the road for the Cybertruck

In one sentence
A BBC Business report says Musk's comments suggest Tesla may wind down the Cybertruck, but there is no company confirmation, quote or sales data to show it.

Elon Musk has made remarks that suggest Tesla may be winding down the Cybertruck, according to a BBC Business report. As of 7 November 2026, Tesla has not announced an end to production, and the exact wording of Musk's comments has not been independently confirmed. Until a company statement or regulatory filing says otherwise, this is a signal and not a decision.

What is known

Tesla unveiled the Cybertruck in November 2019, with its stainless-steel body and angular lines. Customer deliveries began in late November 2023, years after the first promised dates. The report rests on a single source, and it describes Musk's remarks as pointing towards a wind-down. It does not give a quote that settles the matter. Musk is known for offhand remarks on social media and in interviews, and these sometimes describe intentions that never become policy, or that arrive in a different form.

The reporting available also gives no sales, production or financial figures for the Cybertruck. Without them, the claim that the vehicle has failed commercially cannot be tested. Tesla does not break out Cybertruck volumes in its quarterly results, which makes outside estimates a matter of registration data and third-party tracking.

Why the remark carries weight

The Cybertruck was always an awkward product. Musk himself said in 2023 that Tesla had "dug our own grave" with it, describing the production challenges of a design that was hard to manufacture. It is also Tesla's most polarising vehicle: admired by some buyers, mocked by others, and unlike anything else in the company's range.

If Tesla were quietly ending it, the implication would be plain enough. The company's highest-profile niche bet did not earn its keep, and Tesla is willing to cut projects that fail to sell, however much of Musk's personal brand is invested in them. That would matter to investors judging how disciplined capital allocation is at the company. It would matter to the wider EV sector too, where electric pickups have generally sold more slowly than the first forecasts suggested.

There is a counter-reading. A company can slow, pause or restructure a model without ending it. A "wind-down" could mean a trimmed line-up, reduced shifts or a successor design. Each of those is a long way from end-of-life.

What to watch

The test is a Tesla statement, a filing, or a change to the company's website and order pages. Tesla's next quarterly results and deliveries report would also show whether the Cybertruck line is mentioned, reduced or dropped. Until one of those appears, the remark is best read as a hint about Musk's thinking, not a product decision.
