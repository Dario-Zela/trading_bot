Rolls-Royce North America chief: Spectre is 'a Rolls-Royce first, EV second'

In one sentence
Rolls-Royce's North America chief pitches the all-electric Spectre as a Rolls-Royce first and an EV second, echoing the line used at its 2022 launch, with no new sales data.

Rolls-Royce is selling its first electric car as a Rolls-Royce that happens to be electric. That is the message from the company's North America chief in a Yahoo Finance video interview, summarised under the line that the Spectre is "a Rolls-Royce first, EV second". The wording comes from the interview's billing. A full transcript was not available, so the line is not quoted at length here.

The interview carries no new orders, delivery figures or policy. Its value is as a statement of positioning.

An old line, repeated

The phrasing is not new. When the Spectre was unveiled in October 2022, Torsten Müller-Ötvös, then chief executive of Rolls-Royce Motor Cars, was quoted by Euronews as calling it a Rolls-Royce first and an electric Rolls-Royce second. The North American pitch therefore echoes the head office script from the car's launch.

The approach differs from the one many mass-market makers take. Their electric models are often sold as proof of a technological turn, with range and charging speed as the headline numbers. Rolls-Royce is treating electrification as a way of keeping what the brand already offers: silence, effortless torque, a ride described as floating. An electric drivetrain suits those traits. The Spectre rides on the same aluminium "Architecture of Luxury" platform as the Phantom and Cullinan. Its two electric motors, from parent BMW Group's eDrive family, produce about 430 kW (577 hp), according to Wikipedia's summary of the car.

Why the framing suits the buyer

Ultra-luxury customers are a narrow group. They typically own several cars and buy for commissioning, personalisation and status rather than for running costs or emissions. Rolls-Royce's pitch rests on bespoke work, a point made in a Business Chief profile of the company's leadership. For that clientele, a car marketed as an EV first would invite comparison with a Tesla or a Porsche Taycan, at a price of roughly $413,000 for the Spectre, per ABC News. Marketing it as a Rolls-Royce first avoids that comparison.

The commercial picture since launch has been less clear. Early order books were strong: in 2022 Martin Fritsches, then head of the US business, said hard orders already exceeded the totals for the Dawn or Cullinan. Bloomberg-sourced reports later said Spectre deliveries fell sharply in 2025 after a strong first full year, and that current chief executive Chris Brownridge has said the company will keep building what customers want. Those figures are reported second-hand and are not confirmed here. They would explain why the message stresses the badge over the powertrain.

What to watch

Rolls-Royce Motor Cars is wholly owned by BMW Group, so its sales appear only as part of the group's reporting. The next test is whether Spectre volumes steady or recover, and whether the company keeps to an electric-only future for its range or adds other powertrains. Rolls-Royce publishes full-year deliveries each January. BMW's quarterly results show whether the brand's ultra-luxury EV positioning is holding up commercially.
