FDA accepts Gilead's once-weekly HIV prevention pill Yeztugo for review

Gilead Sciences headquarters in Foster City, California, where the Yeztugo franchise — injectable and now oral — is managed. — Suiren2022 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
FDA accepted Gilead's application for once-weekly oral Yeztugo as HIV PrEP, with a decision expected February 2, 2027 — potentially the first long-acting oral PrEP option.

The FDA confirmed on June 15 that it had accepted Gilead Sciences' application to review once-weekly oral Yeztugo for HIV prevention — a routine regulatory intake step that sets a decision clock running but guarantees nothing about the outcome.

The drug, lenacapavir 300 mg taken weekly, would if approved become the first long-acting oral option for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), the preventive regimen used by people at significant risk of contracting HIV. An injectable form of the same molecule — also marketed as Yeztugo, administered twice yearly — already carries FDA approval for PrEP, granted in June 2025.¹ The oral tablets have also been used as a loading dose within that approved injectable regimen. What Gilead is now seeking is regulatory clearance for the weekly oral-only formulation as a standalone prevention strategy. The FDA has set a PDUFA action date of February 2, 2027.

The adherence case

Gilead's argument for an oral formulation centres on a well-documented problem in HIV prevention: daily pill regimens are effective, but only when people actually take them. Truvada and Descovy — the two dominant oral PrEP options — are highly protective under strict adherence, a condition that is, in practice, difficult to sustain across a lifetime of daily dosing. A weekly pill does not dissolve that challenge entirely, but it reduces friction considerably. Dr. Dietmar Berger, Gilead's Chief Medical Officer, framed the logic plainly: "HIV prevention is not one-size-fits-all."

The clinical foundation supporting the application is substantial, if already well-publicised. The PURPOSE 1 trial found 100% efficacy for injectable lenacapavir in cisgender women in Uganda and South Africa.² PURPOSE 2, enrolling cisgender men and gender-diverse individuals across seven countries, found a 96% reduction in HIV incidence.³ Science magazine named lenacapavir its Breakthrough of the Year for 2024. Gilead is leaning on that same dataset — and on lenacapavir's established safety profile — to support the oral extension. The compound is a capsid inhibitor, a mechanism with no known cross-resistance to existing antiretroviral classes, which adds a layer of pharmacological durability that is relevant if resistance becomes a longer-term concern.

A crowded market

Gilead is not navigating an open field. ViiV Healthcare's Apretude (cabotegravir), a bi-monthly injectable, was approved in 2021 and has demonstrated better than 99% real-world effectiveness in studies of nearly 1,300 patients.⁴ GSK, ViiV's majority owner, is planning an every-four-month formulation for 2026 and a twice-yearly version entering trials in 2027 — directly shadowing Yeztugo's own long-acting cadence. The PrEP market is broadening, but each new entrant must carve out patients who either cannot tolerate injections, prefer an oral route, or fall through the gaps of existing options.

For investors, the filing acceptance is procedural. Gilead has already raised its 2026 Yeztugo sales guidance to $1 billion, reflecting the injectable's early commercial momentum. An oral approval in February 2027 would open a new delivery channel, though the extent to which it expands the PrEP-using population versus cannibalising the injectable franchise remains to be seen. The company has also disclosed no major HIV patent expirations before 2036, giving it runway to build the Yeztugo portfolio without generic pressure in the near term. The next meaningful signal is the FDA's decision itself — acceptance merely confirms the paperwork was in order.
