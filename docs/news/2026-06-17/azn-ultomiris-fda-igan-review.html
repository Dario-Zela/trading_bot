FDA grants priority review to AstraZeneca's Ultomiris for kidney disease indication

AstraZeneca's global headquarters on Cambridge Biomedical Campus, which houses the Alexion rare-disease unit behind the Ultomiris franchise. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
FDA's priority review for Ultomiris in IgA nephropathy — backed by a 43.4% proteinuria reduction — sets a Q4 2026 decision date for AstraZeneca's fifth indication.

The US Food and Drug Administration has accepted AstraZeneca's application to expand the use of Ultomiris into IgA nephropathy, a chronic inflammatory kidney disease, and has granted priority review status. A regulatory decision is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026 — roughly four months sooner than a standard review timeline would allow.

IgA nephropathy, or IgAN, develops when abnormal IgA antibodies form immune complexes that lodge in the kidney's filtration apparatus, setting off complement-driven inflammation and, over time, progressive loss of kidney function. More than 217,000 Americans carry a diagnosis; among those with persistently elevated protein in their urine, roughly half will reach end-stage kidney disease within a decade.¹ That trajectory has drawn a wave of therapies in recent years — sparsentan, iptacopan, atrasentan, and sibeprenlimab have all cleared US regulatory review since 2023 — making IgAN one of the more competitive niches in rare-disease nephrology.

What the I CAN Trial Found

The filing rests on a pre-specified interim analysis of the I CAN phase 3 trial (ALXN1210-IgAN-320), a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in adults with IgAN at risk of progression. At week 34, patients on Ultomiris — chemically ravulizumab-cwvz — showed a 46.6% reduction in 24-hour urine protein-creatinine ratio from baseline, against 5.6% in the placebo arm, yielding a placebo-adjusted treatment effect of 43.4% (95% CI: 33.5%, 51.8%; p<0.0001).² Proteinuria fell as early as week 10, indicating a rapid onset. The safety profile was consistent with the drug's known record across its existing indications, with no new signals identified. The interim data were presented at the 2026 European Renal Association Congress before the regulatory submission was filed.

A note of interpretive caution is warranted: the 34-week proteinuria readout is a surrogate endpoint, not a direct measure of kidney survival. The I CAN trial's primary eGFR endpoint — tracking actual kidney function decline — will be assessed at week 106. Regulatory precedent in IgAN does support accelerated pathways based on proteinuria, and the effect size here is material, but the longer-term functional data will be essential for characterising the drug's full benefit-risk profile.³

Ultomiris is already approved in four complement-mediated conditions: paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria, atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome, generalised myasthenia gravis, and neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder. An IgAN approval would constitute its fifth indication and, specifically, would make it the first C5 complement inhibitor cleared for the disease. The existing complement-pathway rival in IgAN — Novartis's iptacopan (Fabhalta), approved August 2024 — inhibits factor B, a node upstream of C5, so the two agents are mechanistically distinct rather than directly interchangeable.

For AstraZeneca shareholders, the significance is incremental rather than transformative. The IgAN patient population is considerably smaller than those in the drug's established hematology and neurology indications, and the treatment landscape is already populated. What the priority review does is reduce timing risk: the Q4 2026 PDUFA date is the next concrete milestone, and a clean approval would underpin the expanded-label assumptions that sit inside longer-range revenue models for the Alexion franchise.
