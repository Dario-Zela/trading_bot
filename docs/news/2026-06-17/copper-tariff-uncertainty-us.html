Copper miners face policy wildcard ahead of US tariff ruling

Bingham Canyon open-pit copper mine, Utah — one of the largest copper operations in North America — NASA / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
A June 30 Commerce Dept report on refined copper duties is the next hard catalyst for HudBay and peers navigating an unresolved Section 232 wildcard.

Investors in copper mining stocks are pricing in a policy question nobody can yet answer. The U.S. Department of Commerce has until June 30, 2026, to deliver an updated report on domestic copper markets — a formal precondition for the President to decide whether to extend Section 232 tariffs to refined copper. Until that date passes, the sector is navigating a genuine asymmetry: fundamentals support the bull case, but the tariff outcome could shift domestic price premiums, supply dynamics, and margin profiles in ways that analysts are still stress-testing.

HudBay Minerals (NYSE: HBM), one of the names flagged by analysts covering undervalued copper exposures, trades at a forward P/E of 15.20x. Analyst consensus targets implied upside of roughly 12.90% from recent levels, though more current aggregations — stockanalysis.com showed 20 buy ratings and no sells as of mid-June — put the average 12-month target at $33.60 against a recent price near $29.10, implying a wider gap of around 15%. The spread in estimates reflects, in part, how differently analysts are modelling the tariff scenario.

What is already in force, and what is still pending

The current tariff architecture imposes a 50% duty on semi-finished copper products — pipes, rods, wires, sheets, tubes — and 25% on copper-intensive derivatives, both effective from August 2025. Refined copper, which accounts for roughly 40–50% of U.S. supply, has been held back from that framework pending the Commerce review. A June 1 presidential proclamation (Proclamation 11032) further adjusted the broader Section 232 metals structure, effective June 8, but left the refined-copper question explicitly open.

Jefferies analyst Fahad Tariq, whose recent $41 price target for HBM stands as an outlier among a consensus clustered around $29–$32.50, models a phased tariff path: 15% on refined copper beginning January 2027, rising to 30% by January 2028. That structure, if enacted, would lift domestic price premiums and tighten supply well before new domestic refining capacity could offset it — a timeline measured in years, not quarters.

For HudBay specifically, the Copper World project in Arizona — fully permitted since January 2025 — offers a potential hedge against import-tariff headwinds that would disadvantage overseas supply. The project is targeting 85,000 tonnes of copper annually over a 20-year mine life, and a sanctioning decision was expected by early 2026 pending feasibility work. The company announced a share repurchase programme in late May, authorising buybacks of up to 5% of outstanding shares (roughly 19.9 million) through May 2027; management stated explicitly that it "believes the current share price does not fully reflect its underlying value."

For peers with heavier import exposure, the calculus runs in the opposite direction. Freeport-McMoRan, whose U.S. operations carry cost structures roughly triple its international assets, faces a more complicated read: a domestic price premium would help margins on U.S. output but could complicate integration across its broader production base.

The June 30 Commerce Department deadline is the next hard date. If the Secretary's report supports action, a formal presidential determination on refined copper duties could follow quickly. If the report recommends delay, the uncertainty extends — and with it, the difficulty of putting a confident multiple on any mining name with North American copper exposure.
