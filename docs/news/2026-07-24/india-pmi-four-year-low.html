India's private-sector growth slumps to four-year low

The Reserve Bank of India's headquarters tower in Mumbai; the central bank has already cut its growth forecast for the year. — Pinakpani / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
India's composite PMI slumped to its weakest reading in over four years, with both manufacturing and services losing momentum as global demand softens.

India's private sector lost more momentum than at any point in over four years last month, according to the latest HSBC-sponsored S&P Global purchasing managers' survey, adding a fresh data point to a growth story that has been cooling steadily since the spring.

The composite PMI, which blends manufacturing and services activity, slipped to its weakest reading since 2021, extending a decline that has run almost uninterrupted since India's post-conflict rebound faded earlier this year. Manufacturing and services both lost pace: factory output growth eased as new export orders expanded at close to their slowest rate in nearly two years, while services firms — the larger share of India's economy — reported softer domestic demand and increased hesitancy among corporate clients.

The reading still sits above the 50 mark that separates expansion from contraction — India's private sector is growing, just at its most sluggish clip since the pandemic-recovery years. But the direction of travel is what has drawn attention. Composite output had been running near 59 as recently as May; four months of consecutive slowing has taken roughly six points off that pace.

A cooling economy meets a harder world

The timing compounds the concern. Global demand has been softening on multiple fronts at once — oil above $100 a barrel amid the widening Middle East conflict, and Washington's reimposition of tariffs on 60 countries as forced-labour exemptions lapsed, both of which show up in Indian exporters' order books. Input cost inflation, which had eased earlier in the summer, remains elevated enough that firms are reporting tighter margins even as new business slows.

India's central bank has already recalibrated for a weaker year. At its June meeting, the Reserve Bank of India held its repo rate at 5.25% for a third straight sitting, trimmed its GDP growth forecast for the fiscal year to 6.6% from 6.9%, and lifted its inflation projection by 50 basis points to 5.1% — a combination that has revived talk, still mostly dismissed by economists, of mild stagflationary risk. The rupee has held in a range near 94–96 to the dollar, a level analysts attribute more to the narrowing gap with US rates than to any acute India-specific stress.

For positioning purposes, the question is whether this is a cyclical air pocket — driven by an external shock that eventually passes — or the first hard evidence that India's above-trend growth rate is reverting toward something more ordinary. One soft PMI print rarely settles that argument. A second consecutive four-year-low reading would.

The RBI's next policy decision, due in early August, will show whether the central bank reads this slowdown as reason to resume cutting rates. August's PMI release, in early September, will show whether July was a trough or the start of a longer slide.
