SAP shares rally despite Q2 earnings miss

In one sentence
SAP missed Q2 earnings estimates but shares rose after a 27% jump in cloud backlog and a reaffirmed 2027 growth acceleration call.

SAP missed Wall Street's earnings targets for the second quarter, and its shares went up anyway — a reminder that in this earnings season, the number that moves a stock isn't always the one at the top of the press release.

The German software giant reported adjusted earnings per share of $1.85, short of the $2.01 analysts had penciled in, a miss of roughly 8%. Revenue came in essentially flat against forecasts at $11.48 billion versus an expected $11.49 billion — technically a miss, though not one that would normally trouble a room full of portfolio managers. Shares rose about 2% in the session following the July 23 release, clawing back a sliver of a stock that had been sliding into the print: SAP had shed nearly half its value from 2025 highs and sat near a 52-week low heading into earnings, as investors fretted over whether its cloud business was monetizing artificial intelligence fast enough to justify its valuation.

Where the beat actually was

The headline numbers missed, but the metrics that matter most to SAP's bull case did not. Cloud revenue rose 22% to €6.3 billion, and current cloud backlog — a proxy for contracted future business — jumped 27% to €22.9 billion, reversing two straight quarters of deceleration that had unnerved the market. Cloud ERP Suite revenue, the core of SAP's shift away from on-premise software licensing, climbed 25% to €5.5 billion. Total revenue rose 9% to €9.9 billion.

CFO Dominik Asam described the quarter as "a little bit of a special quarter," pointing to concentrated R&D spending and marketing costs tied to SAP's Sapphire customer conference as one-off drags on the bottom line. CEO Christian Klein struck a more forward-looking note, telling analysts SAP is "more and more confident" in hitting its full-year targets.

The guidance that mattered

SAP trimmed its 2026 non-IFRS operating profit outlook to €11.8–12.2 billion at constant currencies, down from €11.9–12.3 billion previously — a cut driven not by softening demand but by dilution from two July acquisitions, data-platform maker Dremio and AI startup Prior Labs, expected to cost more than €100 million combined. Management chose to absorb that dilution rather than offset it by trimming investment elsewhere, a signal it reads as confidence rather than caution.

The company held its cloud revenue guidance at €25.8–26.2 billion for the year and reiterated free cash flow of roughly €10 billion. More significant for the stock reaction: SAP said it expects total revenue growth in 2026 to hold near 2025's 10.6% pace before accelerating in 2027, as the cloud backlog converts into recognized revenue and one-time headwinds from professional-services contracts fade.

Why the market let the miss slide

The pattern is a familiar one in growth-stock earnings: a backward-looking miss gets waved off when the forward-looking story holds up. SAP's bet is that investors are less interested in whether it beat a consensus EPS number built around one-off cost timing, and more interested in whether the backlog — effectively contracted revenue not yet booked — keeps compounding. A 27% jump in that figure, after two quarters of softening, gave management something concrete to point to when arguing that 2027 acceleration is a plan rather than a hope.

That argument will next be tested at Q3 results, expected in October, when investors will want to see the backlog growth translate into cloud revenue that's actually accelerating — not merely a stock that's stopped falling.
