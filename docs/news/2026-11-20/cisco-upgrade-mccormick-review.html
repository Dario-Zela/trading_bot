HSBC upgrades Cisco on AI networking; McCormick's UK deal faces scrutiny

In one sentence
HSBC's bullish Cisco call and Barclays' Synopsys initiation both cite AI demand, but they are routine broker moves, and key details and the McCormick scrutiny remain unverified.

Two pieces of sell-side research are circulating among investors holding AI-linked technology stocks: a bullish call on Cisco from HSBC and an initiation of Synopsys at Overweight from Barclays. Both lean on the same argument, that spending on AI infrastructure is feeding demand for networking hardware and for the software used to design chips. Neither is a turn in the sector. They are routine broker calls, and the details available are thinner than the headlines suggest.

Cisco: the case for AI networking

HSBC has turned positive on Cisco, according to Barchart's account of the note, and sees roughly 40% upside, citing demand for AI networking. The bank's stance is not new. HSBC upgraded Cisco to Buy from Hold in mid-May, when analyst Stephen Bersey raised the price target to $137 from $77. The bank pointed to Cisco's AI infrastructure momentum and better earnings visibility, and applied a higher target multiple of 29 times earnings, up from 17.5. The trigger then was Cisco's fiscal third-quarter report, which lifted its fiscal 2026 AI infrastructure orders target to about $9bn from $5bn.

The 40% upside figure comes from the triage summary, and the price target, date and any quote from the latest HSBC note could not be confirmed independently. Whether the current note restates the May upgrade or revises it is not established here. The 40% figure also cannot be reconciled with the roughly 19% upside implied by the May target at the time. Either the share price has fallen since, or the target has changed, and the note itself would say which.

Synopsys: Barclays initiates

Barclays has started coverage of Synopsys at Overweight, according to Investing.com, on AI-driven demand for electronic design automation (EDA) tools, the software chipmakers use to lay out and verify their designs. A search for the note found no price target, analyst name or date, and no independent record of the initiation. The detail is therefore attributed to the source reports rather than confirmed.

Synopsys is already well covered. Piper Sandler, Berenberg, HSBC, Goldman Sachs, Loop Capital and others have all initiated at Buy or Overweight in recent years, so a further positive initiation adds one voice to a crowded, mostly bullish chorus.

McCormick and a thin competition story

A third thread concerns McCormick, the US spice group, whose UK acquisition is under competition scrutiny, according to Simply Wall St. The source gives no target, deal value, regulator, case reference or timetable, and it is a stock-valuation article rather than a report from the authorities or the company. Its one concrete judgment is that McCormick's shares look reasonably valued, which is a generic view and not news about the deal. Until the UK competition authority or the company publishes something, the scrutiny itself is unverified.

What would make it a pattern

Two AI-linked calls in one session do not show that brokers are rotating into networking and chip-design software. That would need a count: how many rating changes and target increases across Cisco, Arista, Synopsys and Cadence, and in which direction, over a period of weeks. This session's evidence does not supply one. Broker moves typically shift a single stock for a day.

The next hard data point for Cisco is its fiscal second-quarter report, due in February, which will show whether AI infrastructure orders are tracking the $9bn target. For McCormick, the first useful document would be any published notice from the UK competition authority.
