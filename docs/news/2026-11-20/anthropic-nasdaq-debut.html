Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq; reported $81bn raise is unverified against pre-pricing terms

In one sentence
Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 and raised about $81.3bn; the earlier ~$61bn was a pre-pricing proceeds estimate, and the record claim depends on SpaceX's overallotment.

Anthropic's shares began trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT. The headline figure attached to the deal, $81bn raised, checks out against the pricing coverage. It is proceeds, not valuation. The roughly $61bn reported before pricing was also proceeds, an estimate rather than a rival number. The gap between the two is mostly arithmetic.

The numbers, reconciled

The BBC reported proceeds of $81bn. CNBC, reporting on Wednesday's pricing, puts them at $81.3bn: about 980 million shares sold at $83 each. (980m × $83 is $81.3bn.) NPR also reported record-setting demand. One secondary source cites 956 million shares, so the exact count may be revised. Neither the final prospectus nor the pricing release was available to this desk, so the share count remains reported rather than filed.

The pre-pricing terms were an offering of 825 million shares at $70 to $78. At the midpoint of $74, that is about $61bn (825m × $74 = $61.05bn), which matches the roughly $61bn figure circulating before the deal. The range itself spanned about $57.8bn to $64.4bn.

The $20bn difference has two sources:

  • Price. The deal priced at $83, $5 above the top of the range.
  • Size. The company upsized the offering by roughly 155 million shares, about 19% more than planned.

The $81bn figure is therefore not a conflation of proceeds with valuation. Valuation is a separate and much larger number: at $83 the fully diluted market capitalisation was about $1.5tn, according to CNBC.

A record, with a caveat

CNBC describes the offering as the largest on record, ahead of SpaceX's June listing. That claim needs qualifying. SpaceX raised $75bn initially and $85.7bn once underwriters exercised their overallotment option, the so-called greenshoe. On the headline base deal Anthropic leads. Against SpaceX's final total it does not, unless Anthropic's underwriters also exercise an option. CNBC does not mention one for Anthropic. The 'record' label depends on which SpaceX number is used.

CNBC also reports that Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD agreed to invest $26bn collectively at the IPO price. If that is right, roughly a third of the proceeds came from strategic investors, some of them suppliers or partners of the company. That is a different kind of demand from a book filled entirely by index funds and long-only managers.

First-day trading

Shares opened at $111.50, 34% above the offer price. They then eased and closed at $97.17, up 17%. One report put the opening market capitalisation above $2tn, though that figure is not confirmed against exchange data.

A 17% close is a conventional, if large, first-day pop. It also shows the deal was priced below what the market would pay that day. Having raised the price once, the company still left about $14 a share on the table by the close, around $13.7bn across 980 million shares. Whether that counts as a cost depends on whether the pricing was meant to maximise proceeds or to seed an orderly aftermarket.

What it tests

The listing is a test of whether public investors will price a frontier AI lab at scale. A company valued near $1.5tn is being asked to justify that figure on cash flows that remain well below it. The offering also arrives as the Federal Reserve has flagged roughly $3tn of AI-linked exposure and the risk of contagion (see related coverage). A record raise with a first-day gain is the sort of evidence that supports the view that the sector is well funded. It is also the sort that worries supervisors.

The co-founders' letter framed the listing as inclusion: 'Everyone who owns our stock, whether an institution or an individual, becomes part of that effort.'

Three data points will matter in the coming weeks:

  • Final filings. The prospectus and closing release will fix the share count, any greenshoe and the exact proceeds.
  • Trading. The first weeks will show whether ANT holds above the $83 offer price once the opening momentum fades.
  • Rivals. Other labs considering a listing will read the $83 price, and the discount to the first-day close, as a guide to what a deal of this size can command.
