Russell fastest in Las Vegas practice after rain

In one sentence
Mercedes' George Russell topped practice for the Las Vegas Grand Prix after rain disrupted the start, though practice times carry no points and set no grid positions.

George Russell was quickest in practice for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, topping the timing sheets for Mercedes after a session programme disrupted by weather at the start. Rain on the Strip is not what the calendar's most theatrical night race is supposed to deliver, and it handed the teams an awkward first day.

A damp start to a dry-run weekend

The weather hit the opening of the practice programme, according to BBC Sport's report. The detail that matters to the teams is less the rain itself than what it takes away. Friday practice is where engineers work through tyre compounds, ride heights and race-pace runs. A wet or damp track shortens the useful window and muddies whatever data does get collected.

The Las Vegas street circuit makes that harder than usual. It is a low-grip, long-straight layout laid over public roads, and track temperatures drop sharply after dark. The surface tends to evolve quickly as rubber goes down, so a lap time set early in a session can be a poor guide to one set an hour later. Russell's top spot is a snapshot of one set of conditions, not a ranking of the cars.

This report does not establish the length of the delay, the gap between Russell and the next driver, or which session produced his best time. Those numbers should be confirmed against the official timing before they are relied on.

Why Russell's name is worth noting

Russell has history here. He won the 2024 Las Vegas Grand Prix from pole position, in a Mercedes one-two with Lewis Hamilton, on a weekend when Max Verstappen finished fifth and clinched his fourth world title. That result came on a circuit where Mercedes had struggled to get its tyres into the right window in earlier practice, which is a fair reminder of how unreliable Friday order can be.

The point cuts both ways. A fast Friday does not guarantee a strong Saturday, and a poor one does not rule it out. Teams run different fuel loads and engine modes in practice, and on a track where tyre warm-up is the central problem, a driver can look quick or slow for reasons that have little to do with the car's underlying pace.

What it does and does not change

Nothing about the grid or the standings has been decided. Practice carries no points and sets no starting positions. Qualifying does that, and the race follows it. Whether Mercedes' pace carries over will depend on the temperature, on how much running the other teams managed in the wet and dry spells, and on whether the weather returns.

The forecast, and the track's behaviour once the full field has had a proper dry run on it, are the things to watch before qualifying. If the rain stays away, Saturday's running will give a far clearer reading of where Russell and Mercedes actually stand than a first day cut short.
