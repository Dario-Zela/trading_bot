ROHINGYA GENOCIDE RULING · By C. Tanaka

World court rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The ICJ ruled 9-6 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017, a binding judgment with no appeal but no obvious enforcement route.

The International Court of Justice has found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, a mostly Muslim minority. The Gambia brought the case on behalf of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation on 11 November 2019, ending more than six years of litigation.

The court had acted early. In January 2020 it unanimously ordered Myanmar to prevent genocide against the Rohingya and to preserve evidence. Those were provisional measures. This judgment is the binding finding on whether the army's campaign met the legal definition of genocide, and it is the court's clearest ruling on a state's responsibility for mass atrocities since Bosnia v Serbia in 2007.

UN estimates put the number of Rohingya who fled to Bangladesh after the army's 2017 'clearance operations' at roughly 700,000 or more. The ruling bears directly on their repatriation and on accountability for the military leadership. Watch Myanmar's response, and how prosecutors, aid donors and courts in other cases use the finding.
