GTA 6 launch posts record first-period sales, estimated above $2.3bn

In one sentence
Third-party estimates put GTA VI's first-day sales above $2bn, double GTA V's, but Take-Two has published no figure and its shares fell 3.7% on launch day.

Take-Two Interactive's shares fell on the day its biggest game ever went on sale. Grand Theft Auto VI, from the publisher's Rockstar studio, launched worldwide on 19 November. Third-party estimates put its first-day sales above $2bn, more than double what its predecessor took on day one. Take-Two has published no figure of its own. The headline number is therefore an analyst's estimate, not a company disclosure, and the stock's reaction suggests investors had already priced much of it in.

What the estimates say

The $2.3bn figure traces to Sensor Tower, the analytics firm. Its running estimate on Thursday evening put units sold at about 24.6 million, equivalent to roughly $2.36bn in revenue for Take-Two. An earlier Sensor Tower reading had launch-day sales at 17.07 million units, worth about $1.65bn, so the estimate rose through the day as data came in. It is a model built on platform and storefront signals, and it may be revised.

Reuters, ahead of the close, described the game as on course to bring in nearly $2bn in first-day sales. It set that against the roughly $800m worth of copies GTA V sold on its first day in 2013. If the estimates hold, the "record" claim is a safe one by that comparison, though the comparison is between an estimate and a figure from a different era of pricing and distribution.

The figure also needs separating from an earlier number that circulates alongside it. Take-Two reported $1.39bn in net bookings for its April–June quarter. That was companywide, driven mainly by its NBA 2K and GTA franchises, and was not GTA VI revenue. CEO Strauss Zelnick said then that pre-orders had started exceptionally well, but he gave no figures.

The share price

Take-Two stock closed at $208.65 on launch day, down 3.7%, after touching $225.69 in the opening minutes of trading. The day before, it had risen more than 6% to $216.61 as the first reviews arrived. The game's review score was reported at 99.

The pattern fits a familiar one. Investors bought the run-up and took profits once the event arrived, and the wider market was also weak that day. That explanation comes from market commentary, not from any Take-Two statement, and a one-day move in either direction says little about what the game earns over a quarter.

Guidance and the read-across

Take-Two kept its full-year net bookings forecast at $8.0bn to $8.2bn. For the December quarter, which contains the launch, it has guided to $3.35bn to $3.5bn. Rough arithmetic shows why the estimates matter. If first-day sales really are around $2.3bn and consumers keep buying through the holiday weeks, the quarter's own guidance looks conservative. Note, though, that bookings are not the same as the retail sales figures analysts quote, and Take-Two reports on its own definitions. Third-party estimates are also usually calculated at retail prices before platform fees, so the revenue reaching Take-Two will be lower than the headline.

For the rest of the industry, the question is concentration. A single title absorbing this much consumer spending in one window raises the cost for rivals of releasing anywhere nearby, and publishers will have planned their calendars with GTA VI's date in mind. Newzoo's pre-launch forecasts of $3.3bn to $5.2bn by the end of launch week show how wide the range of expectations was. A first day near $2.3bn sits inside it, at the lower end.

What to watch

The hard data comes with Take-Two's next earnings report, which will cover the December quarter and show whether the company raises its full-year guidance. Before then, Take-Two may issue its own milestone announcement, as publishers often do after a launch. Sensor Tower's weekly estimates will show whether sales hold up after the first-day rush, and the stock's reaction to either will indicate how much of the opening result was already in the price.
