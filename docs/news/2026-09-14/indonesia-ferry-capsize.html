At least six dead, 130 missing after Indonesian ferry capsizes in Java Sea storm

A roll-on/roll-off passenger ferry at a port on the Surabaya–Madura route, the same waters the Virgo Transport 8 departed from. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
The Virgo Transport 8, a 39-year-old ferry carrying 243 people, overturned in a Java Sea storm; six are confirmed dead and 130 remain missing as a 600-strong search races the weather.

The ferry Virgo Transport 8 left Surabaya on Saturday morning carrying 243 people — 213 passengers and 30 crew — bound for Banjarmasin, a port city on the southern coast of Indonesian Borneo. It was a routine crossing of the Java Sea, the kind made thousands of times a year by vessels linking Indonesia's more than 17,000 islands. By Sunday morning it had capsized, and more than half the people aboard were unaccounted for.

Indonesia's search and rescue agency, Basarnas, said the captain radioed shortly before 2am local time on Sunday to report the ship was listing badly in rough weather. Waves in the area had built to roughly 2.5 metres. Communication was lost minutes later, and the ferry's operator, PT Virgo Karya Shipping, filed a missing-vessel report just after 4am. Rescue crews found the hull overturned but still afloat roughly 80 nautical miles off Banjarmasin, in open water far from any coastline.

A crossing that went wrong at 2am

As of the latest Basarnas count, six people are confirmed dead, 107 have been rescued, and 130 remain missing. Three commercial vessels that happened to be in the area — the tugs TCP and Mauhau 9, and the cargo ship MV Haida — pulled dozens of survivors from the water before official rescue ships arrived, an improvised response that likely saved lives given how far offshore the ferry went down. Basarnas has since deployed around 600 personnel and a dozen ships, along with a helicopter flown out of Juanda Naval Air Base near Surabaya, to search a stretch of sea still churned by the same weather system that sank the vessel.

"It is extremely risky for vessels. Even our Basarnas vessel, which is 40 metres long, is at great risk," I Putu Sudayana, head of the agency's Banjarmasin office, told reporters, describing conditions that have slowed the search for survivors still adrift or trapped in air pockets within the hull. Indonesia's transport ministry has said it will investigate whether the sinking was caused by "natural factors, technical factors or human factors" — a formulation that leaves open both the storm and the ship itself.

A 39-year-old ferry, two months into a new life

The ship itself gives the technical-factors question some weight. Built in 1987 at the Shin Kurushima Onishi shipyard in Imabari, Japan, the vessel spent nearly four decades as the Ferry Kurushima, running the Kokura–Matsuyama route in Japan's Seto Inland Sea until June 2025. It was then sold to PT Virgo Karya Shipping, renamed, and put into service on the considerably rougher, more exposed Surabaya–Banjarmasin crossing in July 2026 — barely two months before it went down.

That trajectory — a vessel retired from a calmer domestic market abroad, resold and redeployed on a more demanding route in a country with looser oversight — is a familiar one in Indonesian shipping. The average age of vessels in the country's ferry fleet is around 28 years, and used ships from Japan and elsewhere routinely enter service after their operators can no longer justify running them at home. There is no evidence yet that the Virgo Transport 8's age directly caused Sunday's capsizing; the KNKT, Indonesia's National Transportation Safety Committee, has not linked the incident to any specific mechanical failure, and rough weather alone can overwhelm a well-maintained ship of this size. But the timing of the ship's redeployment adds an obvious line of inquiry for investigators once the search for survivors concludes.

A regulator that warned four days earlier

What makes the sinking harder to file away as simple bad luck is that the KNKT had just finished reviewing exactly this kind of risk. On September 9 — four days before the Virgo Transport 8 lost contact — the committee published findings from a review of 46 ro-ro ferry accidents in Indonesian waters between 2003 and August 2026, including 19 fires since 2014. It flagged a familiar list of failures: shippers routinely not declaring hazardous cargo, vehicles and freight loaded past manifest limits, and inconsistent inspection before departure. The committee called for tighter cargo controls, more accurate passenger counts and stronger pre-departure checks.

Those findings concerned fire and cargo risk specifically, not weather-related capsizing, and officials have not tied them to this incident. But they describe a regulatory environment that has repeatedly identified its own weaknesses without closing them. Indonesia's ferry disasters have a long, grim lineage: around 400 people drowned when the Senopati Nusantara sank in the Java Sea in December 2006, 50 died in a fire aboard the Levina I two months later, and at least 30 were killed when the Acita III capsized off Sulawesi in 2007. Each produced an investigation and a set of recommendations. The pattern since has been one of disaster, review, and partial follow-through, constrained by the sheer scale of policing a coastline that runs to tens of thousands of kilometres and an inter-island travel market that depends on keeping fares — and therefore vessel costs — low.

What comes next

The search will likely narrow within days: rescuers are racing both the odds of finding survivors in open water and a weather window that, if it worsens again, could force operations to pause. Indonesia's transport ministry has committed to a formal review of the incident, and the KNKT's involvement means any findings on cargo loading, structural condition or the ship's fitness for its new route will surface eventually, as they did after Senopati Nusantara and Levina I. The immediate test is whether this time the response outlasts the news cycle — whether the ministry acts on its own regulator's September 9 warnings before the next ferry, possibly just as old, casts off from Surabaya.
