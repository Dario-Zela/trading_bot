Barclays initiates SK Hynix at Overweight, sees shares doubling on AI memory demand

An SK Hynix DRAM chip. Barclays says the company's high-bandwidth memory leadership positions it to capture outsized pricing power as AI-driven demand outstrips supply. — Raimond Spekking / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
In one sentence
Barclays initiated SK Hynix at 'Overweight' with a $330 target — implying the stock could nearly double — citing an AI-driven memory shortage through 2027.

Barclays initiated coverage of SK Hynix on Tuesday with an 'Overweight' rating and a $330 price target on the company's newly listed Nasdaq shares — a call that implies the stock could nearly double from where it closed the previous session. The ADRs, trading under the ticker SKHY, surged more than 22% on the news, one of the largest single-day moves for any large-cap chipmaker this year.

The timing is notable. SK Hynix listed its American depositary receipts on Nasdaq only four days earlier, on July 10, in a $26.5 billion offering — the largest U.S. share sale ever completed by a foreign company, eclipsing Alibaba's $25 billion listing in 2014. It was also the first ADR listing by a South Korean company in more than two decades. Barclays' bullish initiation, arriving so soon after debut, is as much a bet on the AI memory cycle as it is a vote of confidence in the listing itself.

The case for doubling

Barclays analyst Simon Coles built the call around a widening gap between memory supply and demand. He expects DRAM bit demand to grow 35% in 2027, against supply growth of only around 20% — a shortfall he argues will keep pushing prices higher, particularly for high-bandwidth memory, the stacked DRAM chips that sit alongside GPUs in AI servers and have become one of the tightest bottlenecks in the AI buildout. SK Hynix has led that market since it began shipping HBM to Nvidia in volume, and Coles expects it to hold that position.

"The biggest delta to Bloomberg consensus is materially higher 2027 revenues driven by HBM pricing uplift and SKHY's strong position," Barclays wrote in the note, according to reporting on its research.

The firm also pointed to balance-sheet strength: it estimates SK Hynix's cash pile could exceed 40% of its market capitalisation by the end of 2027, leaving room for substantial share buybacks on top of the earnings case.

A supply squeeze with years left to run

Barclays' thesis lines up with warnings memory makers themselves have issued. Samsung and SK Hynix have both flagged that AI-driven shortages could persist through 2027 and possibly beyond, with data centres now absorbing an estimated 70% of global memory output. New capacity from Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron's Idaho fab is not expected to ramp until the second half of 2027 at the earliest, and current expansion plans are running below the roughly 12% annual output growth analysts say is needed to close the gap. Every wafer diverted to HBM is one not making conventional DDR5 for PCs and phones, tightening that market too.

The next test for the thesis comes with SK Hynix's second-quarter earnings, where investors will look for confirmation that HBM pricing and volume commitments are tracking toward the 2027 numbers Barclays is underwriting.
