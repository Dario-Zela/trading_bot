Macy's taps former Kering Beauté Americas chief to run Bluemercury

A Bluemercury storefront on Manhattan's Upper East Side. — Ajay Suresh / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Macy's hired former Kering Beauté Americas CEO Alex Choueiri to run Bluemercury, betting on luxury beauty as its growth engine.

Macy's Inc. has hired a Kering executive to run its luxury beauty chain, the latest sign that the department-store operator is leaning harder on prestige beauty as one of the few parts of its business that is reliably growing.

The company said Alexandre "Alex" Choueiri will become chief executive of Bluemercury on August 3, arriving from Kering Beauté, the newly formed fragrance and beauty arm of the French luxury group, where he served as CEO and president of the Americas. Before Kering, Choueiri spent more than two decades at L'Oréal, including a stint as global president of Ralph Lauren Fragrances, and started his career in strategy consulting at Bain & Company in France and Australia. He will report to Olivier Bron, chief executive of Bloomingdale's, under whose umbrella Bluemercury now sits.

"Alex is a proven global leader with deep beauty expertise and a record of leading complex global organizations," Bron said in a statement announcing the hire.

A vacancy left by a Bath & Body Works departure

The role had sat effectively open since late September 2025, when Maly Bernstein — Bluemercury's CEO since 2021 — left to become chief commercial officer at Bath & Body Works. Choueiri's arrival closes a ten-month gap in permanent leadership at a chain that, unlike much of Macy's portfolio, has kept posting growth through the interim.

Bluemercury has strung together roughly nineteen consecutive quarters of positive comparable sales, including a 6.4% gain reported this year, and Macy's has been opening rather than shuttering its doors: the company has said it will add at least 30 new Bluemercury locations and remodel about 30 more over the next three years, chasing what it estimates is a $100 billion prestige beauty market. Founded in 1999 in Georgetown as an online cosmetics seller by Marla Malcolm Beck and Barry Beck, Bluemercury grew into a chain of curated boutiques carrying brands such as Diptyque, Oribe and SkinCeuticals before Macy's bought it for $210 million in 2015.

Why a beauty executive, not a retailer

Choueiri's résumé is heavier on brand-building and fragrance launches — he worked on Bottega Veneta and Balenciaga scent rollouts at Kering and on strengthening Creed's position in the category — than on the operational side of running stores. That is arguably the point. Under CEO Tony Spring's "Bold New Chapter" turnaround, Macy's has been narrowing its focus to roughly 350 core namesake stores while explicitly trying to grow Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury faster than the flagship banner, on the view that luxury and prestige beauty carry better margins and structural growth than mid-market department stores. Bloomingdale's posted 9% comparable growth last quarter, its best in more than three years; Bluemercury has been the more consistent performer of the two.

Spring has resisted calls to spin off the two brands, arguing shared warehousing, finance and buying create synergies that outweigh the market's apparent unwillingness to credit their value inside Macy's stock. Recruiting a two-decade beauty-industry veteran to run Bluemercury, rather than promoting from within Macy's retail ranks, suggests the company wants Bluemercury run more like the luxury houses it competes with than like a department-store subsidiary.

"I am honored to join Bluemercury at such an exciting time in its evolution," Choueiri said. Macy's next earnings report will be the first test of whether that evolution shows up in the numbers, and whether the hire has any measurable effect on Bluemercury's growth streak — or, more broadly, on how the market prices Macy's luxury assets against its struggling core.
