PVH names Sephora executive Alexis Rollier as CFO

Calvin Klein's former Madison Avenue flagship — one of the two brands Alexis Rollier will now help steer financially. — David Long / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
PVH has hired Sephora's COO/CFO Alexis Rollier as its new finance chief, betting an LVMH retail playbook can accelerate its Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger turnaround.

PVH Corp. has hired a finance chief from the world of lipstick and loyalty points rather than denim and knitwear. The company said Alexis Rollier, currently global chief operating officer and chief financial officer of Sephora, will become PVH's next CFO, joining in early September and reporting to chief executive Stefan Larsson.

Rollier fills a seat that has sat empty since Zac Coughlin, CFO since 2022, left at the end of 2025 for Sirius XM. In the interim, PVH — parent of Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger — has run its finance function without a permanent head at exactly the moment Larsson is trying to prove his turnaround plan for the two brands is working.

A finance chief from the LVMH stable

Rollier has spent more than 30 years in global finance, operations and retail, the last eight of them at Sephora, where he has held the combined COO/CFO role since 2018. Before that he was global CFO at Guerlain, another house under the LVMH umbrella. His time on both sides of the Atlantic, plus stints touching the Middle East and Latin America, is the detail PVH's statement leaned on hardest — a pointed contrast with a company that has spent recent years wrestling with how to grow outside its core U.S. and European wholesale base.

"Alexis is a unique global finance leader, who brings highly relevant expertise across multibrand and omnichannel retail, deep financial and operational experience, and a strong track record of driving disciplined growth with profit expansion," Larsson said in the announcement. Rollier, for his part, called Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger "two of the most iconic brands in the world, with powerful consumer relevance," and said he was "energized" to join at what he termed an important stage in the company's journey.

Why the pedigree matters

Sephora is not a struggling business. Under Rollier's operational watch it has been LVMH's fastest-growing division, built on a membership program, an aggressive store rollout and a DTC and omnichannel model that other retailers have spent years trying to copy. Hiring the person who ran its finances is PVH's clearest signal yet that Larsson wants Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger managed with the discipline of a premium specialty retailer rather than a legacy wholesale apparel group — the thesis behind what the company calls its "PVH+" plan to lift both brands toward their "full potential."

That plan has shown some traction: PVH beat guidance in its first-quarter 2026 results, with Larsson pointing to direct-to-consumer growth in categories like Calvin Klein underwear and denim and Tommy Hilfiger outerwear. But the company has also flagged that the Middle East conflict is weighing on EMEA consumer spending, a headwind that a finance chief steeped in Sephora's cross-border retail model may be tasked with managing as much as any glamorous repositioning.

What to watch

Rollier does not start until September, leaving PVH to report second-quarter results without a permanent CFO in place. His first real test will be PVH's next earnings call, where analysts are likely to press on whether his hire foreshadows more than a change in personnel — deeper European investment, sharper margin targets, or a harder pivot toward the higher-margin DTC categories Larsson has been touting all year. For a stock that trades on the credibility of the PVH+ turnaround, the market's read on Rollier will hinge less on his résumé than on what he does with it.
