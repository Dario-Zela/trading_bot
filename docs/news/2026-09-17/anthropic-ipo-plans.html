Anthropic reportedly plans IPO as early as September after overtaking OpenAI on revenue

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, whose company is reportedly preparing a September IPO filing. — Kimberly White/Getty Images for TechCrunch, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Reports say Anthropic may IPO as early as September after passing OpenAI on revenue — but the figures come from private trackers, not audited accounts.

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot and the Claude Code programming tool, is reportedly gearing up for an initial public offering as early as September — a filing that, if it lands on schedule, would arrive alongside claims that the company has overtaken rival OpenAI on revenue. Both halves of that sentence deserve scrutiny before anyone treats them as settled fact.

The IPO detail is the more concrete of the two. Anthropic confidentially filed a draft S-1 with the SEC on June 1, and reporting since — including from Dealroom, TIME and CNBC — points to a prospectus becoming public in late September with a roadshow to follow in October. Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan are named as lead underwriters. None of that is nailed down: confidential filings can slip, and "as early as September" is a window, not a date on a calendar.

The revenue claim, and why it's slippery

The more interesting problem is the revenue comparison. Anthropic is privately held, as is OpenAI, and neither publishes audited financial statements. What circulates instead are "annualised revenue run rates" — a single month's revenue multiplied by twelve — sourced from investor decks, leaked figures and trackers like Dealroom, which compiles private-company data from a mix of self-reported and third-party inputs rather than filed accounts.

By those measures, Anthropic's run rate has climbed sharply this year: from roughly $1 billion in annualised Claude Code revenue by last November to figures north of $47 billion by May and above $65 billion cited more recently, driven in large part by enterprise coding contracts with customers such as Netflix, Spotify and Salesforce. OpenAI's run rate is put at around $40 billion. On the numbers as reported, Anthropic has indeed pulled ahead — a reversal from a year ago, when OpenAI's consumer subscription base gave it a clear lead.

But Axios's own accounting of the gap, published September 3, found that bookkeeping differences only explain part of the story. Anthropic counts the full value of Claude sales made through cloud partners as revenue, then books the partners' cut as an expense — treating itself as the "principal" in the transaction. OpenAI reports only its net share of sales run through partners like Microsoft. Switching Anthropic to net accounting would trim its figure by an estimated 6 to 10 percent — nowhere near enough to close a gap reported at $25 billion or more. Something beyond methodology is driving the divergence, and without audited numbers from either company, outside observers are left comparing two black boxes.

Why the opacity is the story

That matters for how markets are pricing both firms. Anthropic's Series H in May reportedly valued it at $965 billion; some investors are now floating a $2 trillion IPO valuation, which would eclipse SpaceX's $1.77 trillion mark as the largest listing in history. Those figures are being built on revenue claims that cannot currently be independently verified — not because anyone has been caught fabricating numbers, but because private companies are under no obligation to disclose the kind of granular, audited detail public markets take for granted. Forbes and other outlets have noted that investors are actively pressing both labs on how they count revenue, precisely because the accounting doesn't yet add up cleanly.

None of this means the underlying growth is fake. Claude Code's rise from a niche developer tool to a multi-billion-dollar product in little more than a year is real and well documented through customer counts and third-party usage data. But "real growth" and "a $2 trillion valuation is justified" are different claims, and the gap between them is exactly the kind of thing an S-1 is supposed to close.

What to watch

The actual filing, once public, will force Anthropic to disclose audited financials for the first time — a moment that will either validate the run-rate figures circulating now or expose how much daylight sits between marketing numbers and GAAP revenue. Until then, an Anthropic IPO remains a plausible September event, not a confirmed one, and the revenue "overtaking" is a claim built on trackers and leaks rather than filed accounts. Traders pricing in a listing on the back of these reports are, for now, pricing in a rumour with a plausible timeline attached.
