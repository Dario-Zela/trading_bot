AstraZeneca shares rise as JPMorgan defends stock despite trial setbacks

AstraZeneca's headquarters and R&D centre at Cambridge Biomedical Campus. — D Wells / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
J.P. Morgan and UBS both defended AstraZeneca after trial failures and merger talk, but their bull case leans on 2027 data that hasn't arrived yet.

AstraZeneca shares edged up 2.2% in London trading after J.P. Morgan reiterated its Overweight rating and lifted its price target to 16,000p from 14,000p, adding the drugmaker to its European Analyst Focus List — a vote of confidence that sits awkwardly next to two clinical trial failures and merger speculation that has dogged the stock since midsummer.

The stock has fallen roughly 15% since early July, when back-to-back Phase III setbacks knocked out two of the pipeline's higher-profile bets. Camizestrant, AstraZeneca's oral breast cancer therapy, missed its primary endpoint in the SERENA-4 trial. Days later, Wainua — a gene-silencing drug developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals — failed to beat placebo at reducing cardiovascular deaths in patients with a heart condition called ATTR-CM, wiping out one of the few genuine surprises in an otherwise well-telegraphed pipeline. The stock dropped 9% on the Wainua news alone.

In one sentence: J.P. Morgan and UBS both defend AstraZeneca after trial flops and merger talk, but their support rests on 2027 data readouts that haven't happened yet.

Two banks, one bullish read

J.P. Morgan analyst Richard Vosser built his case around six Phase III readouts due in 2027 that the bank estimates carry a combined $10 billion in risk-adjusted peak sales. The list includes laroprovstat, an oral PCSK9 inhibitor for cholesterol that J.P. Morgan models at $3.2 billion in peak sales against a Street consensus of $2.4 billion, partly on the argument that it won't require the fasting regimen rivals like Merck's Lipfendra do. Also on the list: a zibotentan-Farxiga combination for kidney disease that the bank rates an 80% probability of success, a balcinerenone-Farxiga heart-failure combination rated 50-50, the breast cancer drug Datroway, an endometrial cancer candidate called puxi-sam, and saruparib, a prostate cancer drug the bank thinks de-risks $3 billion in sales particularly among the roughly 30% of trial patients with HRR mutations.

UBS reached a similar conclusion by a different route. Analyst Colin White cut his price target to 15,200p from 17,600p — still implying more than 25% upside from the 11,925p close on 15 September — while keeping a Buy rating. UBS argues the recent slide has "gone too far," and that AstraZeneca can still hit its long-standing target of $80 billion in revenue by 2030 and grow through a patent cliff in the early 2030s. The bank separately flagged four drugs with 2027 readouts it believes each carry more than $5 billion in sales potential, and lifted its peak-sales forecast for a lung disease drug to $5.7 billion after strong data at a September medical conference.

The merger noise

Layered on top of the trial setbacks was a Financial Times report, published 2 August, that AstraZeneca and Bristol Myers Squibb had held preliminary talks about a merger that would value the combined company at roughly $400 billion. Analysts reacted with what CNBC characterized as bewilderment — Jefferies noted AstraZeneca was a company that didn't need "financial engineering," while others pointed to the antitrust exposure and business overlap a deal that size would invite. UBS was more pointed, questioning whether merger synergies realized around 2030 would do enough to offset AstraZeneca's own patent expiries later in the decade. Both companies subsequently denied the talks had taken place.

What the disagreement actually signals

What's notable is not that two banks like the stock — most sell-side coverage on AstraZeneca remains Buy or Overweight — but that neither is defending the current pipeline as much as pricing in data that doesn't exist yet. J.P. Morgan's and UBS's targets both lean on 2027 trial readouts to justify today's valuation gap; strip those out and the bull case for the next twelve months is thinner. That's a reasonable bet on a company with AstraZeneca's trial base rate, but it also means the next confirmation or disconfirmation of the thesis is more than a year away, leaving the stock to trade on sentiment and merger rumor in the interim. The nearest concrete catalyst is the laroprovstat readout expected in the first quarter of 2027; until then, expect the stock to keep reacting to headlines rather than data.
