UK to ban porn showing choking or incest from early November

In one sentence
A ban on realistic pornography depicting strangulation or certain incest scenarios takes effect in England and Wales on 6 November 2026, with enforcement against offshore sites the open question.

From 6 November 2026, it will be a criminal offence in England and Wales to possess or publish pornography that realistically depicts strangulation or suffocation, or certain kinds of incest. The government has now fixed the date for rules that Parliament passed earlier this year. The harder question is whether a law written around what an image shows can be enforced against sites that sit outside UK reach.

What the law covers

The offences come from the Crime and Policing Act 2026. According to the government's guidance, they apply to pornographic images that explicitly and realistically depict the listed categories. Those are:

  • strangulation or suffocation;
  • sex between close relatives, such as father and daughter or brother and sister, plus some step and foster relationships where one person is, or appears to be, under 18;
  • adults role-playing as children under 16.

The test is realism. The material has to show people who would appear, to a reasonable person, to be real. The government factsheet says that includes realistic AI-generated images.

Penalties differ by offence. Possession carries up to two years' imprisonment and publication up to five. One law firm advises that anyone holding such material should delete it before the start date. Someone who receives an image they did not ask for must delete it within a reasonable time.

Why now

The choking measure was added to the bill in November 2025, after an independent review led by Baroness Bertin. It found that depictions of choking were common on mainstream porn sites. Ministers presented the change as a way to protect women and girls from sexual violence.

Critics, including some civil-liberties writers, say the law reaches into consensual adult behaviour. They note that many adults engage in breath play, and that the offence turns on the content of an image rather than on any proven harm. That is the precedent that matters here. Existing UK law on extreme pornography already bans some depictions. This extends it to categories defined by what is shown.

The enforcement problem

A criminal offence reaches people in the UK. Much of the content in question is hosted abroad. The government's factsheet says the new offences will be listed as priority offences under the Online Safety Act 2023. Other coverage says this means platforms must proactively detect and remove the material or face action from the regulator, Ofcom. I could not confirm the final Act's wording on platform duties, so that point should be checked against the text.

Ofcom's powers against overseas sites are the practical lever. It can fine non-compliant services and seek court orders to restrict access. Whether it uses them against offshore adult sites that ignore UK rules is untested for this category. Ordinary users are the easier target. Possession is an offence even for people who never visit a UK-hosted site.

What to watch

The first signals will come before and just after 6 November. Mainstream platforms will show whether they filter UK traffic or remove the content globally. Ofcom's first enforcement notices will show whether the regulator treats these offences as a priority. The first prosecutions for possession will show how courts apply the "appears real" test, particularly to AI-generated images.
