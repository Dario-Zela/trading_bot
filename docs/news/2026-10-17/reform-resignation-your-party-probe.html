Reform councillor quits over racist remarks; Met looks into Your Party founding row

In one sentence
Reform UK councillors have quit the party after being filmed making racist remarks, raising vetting questions, while the Met is reported to be examining the row over Your Party's founding.

Reform UK is again explaining how it checks the people it puts into local government. Councillors have resigned from the party after being filmed making racist remarks, and the pattern is now the story as much as any one individual.

The latest departures follow an undercover investigation by ITV News, published on 14 October, which filmed several Reform councillors and candidates. In Solihull, Alan Feeney was recorded calling Asian homeowners "parasites". He said he had "mutually agreed" to leave Reform for "personal reasons" and would sit as an independent. Dean Miller, a Bromley councillor, was filmed making racist comments, including calling for King Charles to be beheaded over a claim that the King is a Muslim. He has also resigned from the party. He told ITV News he was heavily medicated for a chronic pain condition at the time. Chris Whelan has gone too, citing personal and health reasons. ITV also reported misogynistic and racist comments from Jamie Campbell. This article could not establish his current status.

Both Feeney and Miller are sitting councillors who left the party rather than their seats. Resigning from Reform is not the same as resigning from the council, so the two men remain in office.

The party's response

Reform says those filmed are under investigation and have either resigned or been suspended. Deputy leader Richard Tice went further, accusing one of those recorded of being a "Tory plant" who knew they were being filmed and who was trying to make the party "look bad". He did not name the person, though his description appeared to point to Feeney. The claim is unproven, and it does not answer what was said on camera.

Opponents moved quickly. Labour's Bridget Phillipson said Nigel Farage needed to explain why this keeps happening in his party. Liberal Democrat leader Ed Davey called the footage "chilling".

A pattern, not an incident

The "keeps happening" charge rests on precedent. In August 2025, Robert Bloom resigned as a Reform councillor in North Northamptonshire after allegations that he racially abused a Black neighbouring family, triggering a by-election. That was a complaint made to the council. This month's cases were caught on camera in an undercover operation.

The common thread is scale. Reform took control of a number of English councils in 2025 and has recruited heavily since. A party that grows that fast depends on vetting to filter out people who hold views it says it rejects, and a sting suggests the filter has holes. Reform will argue, as Tice has, that it acts once problems surface. Its critics will say that is the point: the checks happen after the footage, not before the nomination.

Elsewhere: the Met and Your Party

Separately, the Guardian reports that the Metropolitan Police are investigating a row over the founding of Your Party, the left-wing group associated with Jeremy Corbyn and Zarah Sultana. This article could not independently confirm the police inquiry, or what exactly is being investigated. The party's origins have been disputed since September 2025, when Sultana launched a membership portal that Corbyn and other founders said was not legitimate. Party sources later alleged that more than £800,000 in membership fees had gone to an account held by MOU Operations and that three founders had failed to hand over funds and data. Those founders denied it, and the party reported itself to the data protection regulator. If the police are now involved, an internal quarrel over money and membership data has become a matter for officers.

The two stories are not equivalent. Both, though, concern how new or fast-growing parties handle basic governance. For Reform, the next test is whether its investigations produce published outcomes, and whether any further footage emerges. For Your Party, it is whether the Met confirms what it is examining.
