England's 41-match unbeaten run ends against Canada

In one sentence
Canada beat England 24-21 in Toronto with a 77th-minute try, ending the Red Roses' unbeaten run, reported as 41 Tests, which had survived a 26-26 draw with Canada in September.

England's women lost a Test match for the first time in almost four years on Friday, beaten 24-21 by Canada at BMO Field in Toronto. The defeat ended an unbeaten run that most outlets put at 41 matches. Canada had already come close to ending it a month earlier.

The game was played in the WXV Global Series. England led 14-5 at half-time after tries from Maddie Feaunati (32 minutes) and Zoe Harrison (37). Canada scored four tries in all, through Savannah Bauder, Alysha Corrigan, Daleaka Menin (63) and the replacement Shoshanah Semanutafa, whose score in the 77th minute was the winner. Sarah Bern also scored for England, after Corrigan's try. The crowd of 18,621 was reported as a North American record for a women's rugby match.

A streak with two numbers

The length of the run depends on who is counting. TSN and Yahoo Sports said 41 matches. RugbyPass headlined it as 40. The difference comes from September, when Canada drew 26-26 with England at Exeter's Sandy Park on 19 September. That ended England's record winning streak at 39, but the unbeaten run carried on. England beat New Zealand 26-19 a week later, which Reuters said took the unbeaten run to 41 Tests.

The Toronto match is the first time a side has beaten England since New Zealand won the 2022 World Cup final in November that year.

Reaction

Canada's captain, Sophie de Goede, said the comeback was "kind of the DNA of our team and our group." England's captain, Meg Jones, was matter-of-fact: "It's just the nature of the beast, and we go again." Head coach John Mitchell said he was disappointed with the result but that there were "some very good things" in England's performance.

Canada have now drawn with and beaten England within five weeks, so the result is hard to dismiss as a one-off.

What next

The two sides play again on 23 October in Ottawa, as part of the Rugby Rematch Tour. England will want to show that Toronto was a single bad night. Canada will be looking to win two in a row against the top-ranked side.
