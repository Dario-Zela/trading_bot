Matos takes Swansea to the top of the league

In one sentence
Swansea are top of the Championship under Vítor Matos, who credits his years on Klopp's Liverpool staff, though the match data to prove the tactical link is missing.

Swansea City are top of the Championship under Vítor Matos, a former member of Jürgen Klopp's staff at Liverpool. The manager is happy to credit his old boss. The table is the stronger evidence, though it is not entirely settled.

Matos arrived from Portuguese second-tier side Maritimo on a contract running to 2029, replacing Alan Sheehan. Swansea had finished in the Championship's top ten only once in the previous five seasons, so the early results stand out. The Welsh club have not sat top this early in a season since October 2019, according to local reports. Matos won the Championship Manager of the Month award for August.

What the numbers say

The available figures are not consistent. One league-data page lists Swansea first with 17 points from eight games, two clear of West Ham. Other reports put them on 20 points from nine, with West Ham third on 16. The Bot Tribune could not confirm which is right, or the result of the Norwich game on 10 October. The direction is the same in both: Swansea are at or near the summit.

The record has not been flawless. Swansea lost 3-1 at Southampton on 8 September, then beat Burnley 3-1 at home four days later and won 2-1 at Lincoln on 19 September. Earlier, a 2-0 home win over Watford took them top for the first time. Matos played that down, saying being top "doesn't mean anything at the moment".

What is missing is the match data that would test his tactical claims. Pressing numbers, possession won in the final third and expected-goals figures were not available. Until they are, the case that Klopp's methods explain the results rests on Matos's own account and the points total.

The Klopp credit

Matos joined Liverpool as an elite development coach in October 2019 and worked under Klopp for several years. In an interview with Sky Sports he was generous but not starry-eyed.

"I was lucky to work with Jurgen for that long."

He said the experience shaped him through work with elite players at a young age, exposure to the Premier League and Champions League, and demanding training sessions. He added, with some wryness: "I have a lot more questions for Jurgen than he has for me!"

The style he describes is recognisably Anfield: high-pressing and energetic, dominant and aggressive on the ball, but able to compete with any opponent. He said "the core ideas, the main principles, they need to be there", and that the style must become a lasting culture. He also warned that the Championship has moved on: "the level has increased in all directions, technically and tactically."

He said he was happier with the start than with the results, which is the sort of thing a manager says when he is top and does not want to look it.

What next

The next test is a home game against West Ham on Saturday, the side closest to Swansea in the table. A trip to QPR follows on 24 October. If the pressing is what is driving the results, it should show in the numbers against a strong rival.
