STANDING WATCH · RUSSIA SANCTIONS · By C. Tanaka · 2026-10-17 · 3 min read

Russia sanctions: Trump waives more measures as a new law takes effect; Macron criticises the diesel deal

In one sentence
France has criticised a US-Russia diesel arrangement, and Moscow's Dmitriev says the US Treasury asked RDIF to help, a claim no primary source has yet confirmed.

Washington is loosening its grip on Russia sanctions at the moment a new US law takes effect. Its closest European partner is now publicly questioning a diesel arrangement with Moscow. The facts are thin and mostly come from interested parties, so the story should be read as a set of claims rather than a settled account.

The diesel claim

The sharpest thread is the diesel arrangement. According to Benzinga, France's president, Emmanuel Macron, criticised the US-Russia deal, citing doubts and defiance. The same report quotes Kirill Dmitriev, who heads Russia's Direct Investment Fund (RDIF), saying that the US Treasury asked RDIF to help facilitate it.

If that account is accurate, it matters. RDIF is Russia's sovereign wealth fund, and Washington has treated it as a sanctions target. A request from the Treasury to such a body to help move Russian fuel would sit awkwardly beside the stated purpose of the sanctions regime. It would also explain why a close ally felt moved to object in public.

The account has clear limits. It comes from one aggregator, and the central claim comes from Dmitriev, who has an evident interest in presenting Moscow as a sought-after commercial partner. The report carries no Treasury statement, and it gives no volumes, terms or dates. Until the Treasury confirms or denies its role, the Treasury request is Dmitriev's assertion.

Waivers and a new law

The BBC reports that the US has waived "several" Russia sanctions as a new US law comes into force. The summary available does not name the statute, give its effective date, or list which measures were waived and for whom. That gap decides how much the story is worth. A waiver is usually a temporary, reversible instrument, and it can be a narrow licence for a single transaction. It can also be the first step in a broader retreat.

The timing invites a link to the diesel arrangement, but nothing in the reporting establishes one. The waivers and the diesel deal may be related, or they may simply have landed in the same week.

A wider commercial offer

Dmitriev has also said that Russia and the US are discussing joint data centres, some of them nuclear-powered, along with Arctic and LNG projects. This too is reported by Benzinga alone, and it is described only as discussion. There is no agreement, no named signatory, no funding and no timeline, and the report records no US response.

The pattern is still worth noting. Dmitriev has a history of putting commercial prospects to Washington, and the strategic sectors he names are ones where sanctions bite hardest. Offering them can be read as a bargaining position as much as a plan. That reading is an interpretation, not something the sources establish.

What would settle it

Three things would turn the claims into a story. The first is a Treasury or White House statement on whether the department asked RDIF for help. The second is the text of the waivers, showing which measures they cover and how long they last. The third is a fuller account of Macron's objection, including whether Paris has raised it with Washington directly. Until then, the French criticism is the only element of this cluster that is on the record, and the claim it responds to is unconfirmed.
