Former Sale Sharks lock Andrews dies at team hotel in La Rochelle

Hyron Andrews, who died aged 31 in France. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Hyron Andrews, 31, who played 45 times for Sale Sharks, died at his current club the Johannesburg Lions' hotel near La Rochelle before their Champions Cup opener; the cause is under investigation.

Hyron Andrews, the South African lock who spent two seasons at Sale Sharks, has died at the age of 31. He was found in his team's hotel on the Île de Ré, off La Rochelle, on Friday, the day before the Johannesburg Lions' European Champions Cup opener against La Rochelle.

The hotel was the Lions' own. Andrews had left Sale for South Africa in the summer and was a current Lions player, so this is not a Sale club story. Sale are mentioned because that is where British supporters knew him.

What is known

The cause is not settled, and accounts differ. SA Rugby described the death as an accident and said he "died after a fall". Local reports say the public prosecutor in La Rochelle is investigating and that Andrews may have suffered a heart attack in the shower. Wikipedia's entry, which was not independently confirmed here, records a cardiorespiratory arrest. These accounts do not agree, and none has been confirmed by the investigation. Until the inquiry reports, none should be treated as established.

Lions chief executive Rudolf Straeuli said: "We are heartbroken by the passing of Hyron." SA Rugby president Mark Alexander called it "terrible and distressing news". Andrews leaves his wife, Maighlin, and their two young sons.

A career spent in the second row

Born in Paarl in 1995, Andrews came through the Blue Bulls youth ranks and the Sharks academy in Durban. He made 77 appearances for the Sharks between 2016 and 2024, and played for South Africa at Schools and Under-20 level. Wikipedia lists no senior Springbok caps.

He arrived at Sale in February 2024 on a short-term deal, as cover for England lock Jonny Hill, and was kept on for the 2024–25 season. He made 45 appearances for the club in all. The Lions signed him this year, and he played three times for them.

What happens next

The Lions will play La Rochelle in his honour, with kick-off at 17:30 BST. A minute's silence will be held at every fixture in the Champions Cup's opening round.

The welfare question in the headline depends on the prosecutor's findings. If they show the death was linked to the trip, the story changes from an obituary to a review of how clubs look after players on away tours. If they show a medical event or a domestic accident, it does not. The prosecutor's findings are the next thing to watch for.
