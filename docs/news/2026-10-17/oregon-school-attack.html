Oregon teacher fought school attacker before armed father shot suspect

In one sentence
A teacher tackled a 16-year-old gunman at a Bend, Oregon, primary school before a passing father with a legal pistol shot him dead; four people were killed.

A 16-year-old with a shotgun opened fire inside a special-needs classroom at Silver Rail Elementary in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday. Four people died before the gunman was himself shot dead by the father of two pupils, who had a legally carried pistol in his car. A teacher who threw her phone at the attacker, tackled him and tried to wrestle the gun away is credited by police with limiting the toll.

The shooting happened at lunchtime on 15 October. Police named the dead as Meghan Lambert, a 25-year-old teacher, and a five-year-old, Rhoam Summer. Two other children, aged eight and 11, also died; their families have not agreed to be named. A 54-year-old teacher and a seven-year-old pupil were wounded. The child is in a serious but stable condition, and the teacher remains in hospital.

The attack

Police identified the suspect as Griffin J. John II, a former pupil at the school who had special educational needs. Reports say he threw his shotgun over the fence, climbed after it, and fired on a passing truck whose driver had confronted him. He then shot through locked glass doors into the classroom, firing as many as 15 rounds. He was carrying about 44 more.

The teacher who fought him was not hurt and has not been named. Police Chief Mike Krantz said she "almost certainly helped prevent more bloodshed" and "was willing to stand and fight for her children, that's true heroism."

Kaylub Giamanco, a 36-year-old carpenter, was driving past and saw the gunman firing in the car park. He went in with a .40-calibre pistol and fired at the suspect "multiple times", killing him. Police arrived within three minutes of the first call, and the shooter was already dead. Officials have not confirmed Giamanco's name; his family gave it to US media, and he has asked for privacy.

Accounts differ in detail. Some outlets describe the teacher as having immobilised the gunman before the fatal shots, and the order of events inside the room has not been set out officially.

Reaction and open questions

Chief Krantz said the community owes those who intervened "a debt of gratitude". Oregon's governor, Tina Kotek, acknowledged the bystander's actions but said such people carry "a heavy emotional burden". President Donald Trump called him a "great hero".

Police have searched the suspect's home. They have not established a motive or how a 16-year-old obtained the shotgun. "Everyone wants to know why, including us," Krantz said.

The wider argument

The case arrives in a country where school shootings are counted in the hundreds. The K-12 School Shooting Database records 149 incidents involving a firearm on school grounds so far this year, with 118 people wounded or killed. The Gun Violence Archive has logged 371 mass shootings since January, three of them at public schools, Bend among them.

Advocates of arming staff or encouraging parents to carry will point to a single armed civilian ending the attack within minutes. Critics will note that the man was there by chance and that four people were already dead. The teacher's unarmed intervention also shows how much hung on seconds. The tests ahead are the police account of the timeline and the source of the weapon, which will shape whether the case becomes evidence for either side.
