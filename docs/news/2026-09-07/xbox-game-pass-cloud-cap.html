Xbox to cap Game Pass cloud gaming at 15 hours a month

An Xbox Series controller in Carbon Black — the default pad used to play games streamed through Xbox Cloud Gaming. — uKER / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Xbox will cap Game Pass cloud streaming at 15/10/5 hours a month by tier from November, with extra hours for sale — hitting about 4% of subscribers.

Xbox is putting a meter on a feature it spent six years calling unlimited. Starting in November, Microsoft will cap how many hours Game Pass subscribers can stream games through Xbox Cloud Gaming each month — 15 hours for Ultimate subscribers paying $22.99 a month, 10 hours for Premium at $14.99, and five hours for the entry-level Essential tier. Run out, and the option will be to buy more.

Microsoft says the change will touch about 4% of its Game Pass subscriber base — the heaviest streamers, in other words, not the casual ones. Pricing for extra hours hasn't been set yet; Xbox says it will publish details "before the changes take effect." In a related move, people who don't subscribe to Game Pass at all will, for the first time, be able to pay to stream individual games they already own, without a subscription.

Six years of "unlimited"

Cloud streaming launched inside Xbox Game Pass Ultimate on September 15, 2020, under the xCloud name, covering 22 countries and more than 150 games — always pitched as a bundled, uncapped perk. That framing was partly competitive: Google's rival service, Stadia, charged separately and shut down in 2023, while Microsoft kept folding cloud access into a flat subscription fee. By 2022 Microsoft counted more than 10 million cloud players; the service didn't formally exit beta until October 2025. Earlier this year Microsoft even added a free, ad-supported cloud tier, capped at five hours a month from the start — a sign the meter was already being tested at the edges before it reached paying subscribers.

Microsoft's stated reason is straightforward: running remote servers that render and stream games in real time gets more expensive the more people use it, and heavier users cost disproportionately more than the average subscriber pays in. "The cost of providing cloud gaming grows as more people use it and play for longer," Xbox said in its announcement. "Moving to monthly limits allows us to keep offering the service while continuing to invest in its reliability and performance."

A familiar pattern

The move fits a pattern common once a subscription product moves from growth to maturity: features marketed as unlimited during the land-grab phase get metered once the user base stabilizes and the cost of serving the heaviest users becomes visible on a balance sheet. Nvidia took a similar step with its GeForce Now cloud service, capping monthly play at 100 hours for all users in 2025. Xbox's version is far tighter — 15 hours is roughly 30 minutes a day — which is exactly why Microsoft is framing it as a change that hits only a small, unrepresentative slice of subscribers rather than the norm.

What happens next depends on a number Microsoft hasn't released: the price per extra hour. A cheap top-up preserves the goodwill of heavy users at modest cost; an expensive one signals Microsoft views cloud streaming less as a subscriber perk and more as a distinct product it can price on its own terms. That number is due before November.
