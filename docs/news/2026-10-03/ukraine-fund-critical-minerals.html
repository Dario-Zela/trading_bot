US-Ukrainian reconstruction fund seals first critical minerals deal and four other projects

In one sentence
The US-Ukrainian reconstruction fund sealed its first critical minerals deal, about $30m with BGV Group, within roughly $70m of new projects.

The joint US-Ukrainian reconstruction investment fund has moved from planning to deal-making. On Friday it announced its first critical minerals transaction, together with four other projects. The fund was created under the April 2025 minerals pact between Washington and Kyiv.

The minerals deal is small by the standards of the sector. It puts roughly $30m into a joint investment platform with BGV Group, a firm founded by Hennadii Butkevych, the billionaire co-owner of Ukraine's largest supermarket chain, ATB. The platform will initially concentrate on early-stage mining projects across Ukraine. Its targets are rare earths, uranium, beryllium and zirconium deposits.

Conor Coleman, head of investments at the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC), said the fund's projects now total around $70m. That means the minerals platform accounts for a little under half of the committed capital, and the other four projects share the remainder.

What the money is for

The fund is organised around five strategic sectors: minerals, defence, energy and infrastructure. Two of the other projects announced on Friday are aimed at Ukraine's power and heating networks, after an increase in Russian attacks on key infrastructure ahead of winter. The details of the remaining projects were not set out in the early reports.

So the first batch is not purely extractive. Roughly half the money is going to grid and heating resilience, which is the most immediate humanitarian need in the country. The minerals platform is still the headline, and it is the piece that matters most to the strategic case for the fund.

Reading the signal

The argument that supply-chain strategy is now setting the agenda rests on two points. First, minerals went first in the announcement. Second, the arrangement itself was sold in Washington as a way to secure access to materials used in defence manufacturing and in green-energy equipment. Rare earths and zirconium feed into magnets, alloys and nuclear applications; uranium and beryllium have similar defence and energy uses.

The caveats are material. The platform is aimed at early-stage exploration, so it is a bet on what may be found and not a supply contract. Ukraine's most promising deposits are also not all in territory Kyiv controls, and production, if it comes, is years away. At $30m the commitment is closer to a pilot than to a challenge to China's dominance of processing.

The size of the sums is the measure to keep in view. Ukraine's reconstruction needs are counted in hundreds of billions of dollars, and $70m across five projects is a rounding error against that. The fund's value is as a template and a signal, and not as a source of volume.

What to watch

The next data points are the follow-on investments. Investors tracking supply-chain resilience will want to see whether later deals move from exploration towards processing and offtake, and whether defence and energy projects keep their share of capital as the fund grows. Winter will test the power and heating projects first, since they are meant to reduce the damage from the next round of strikes on infrastructure.
