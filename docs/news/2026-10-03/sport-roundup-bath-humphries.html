Bath beat Exeter in 11-try thriller; Humphries and Northern Ireland shine

In one sentence
Bath avenged an earlier defeat by Exeter in an 11-try Premiership match, while Northern Ireland advanced in the Nations League and Luke Humphries reached the Grand Prix semi-finals.

Bath have settled a score with Exeter. In a Premiership rugby match that produced 11 tries between the two sides, they beat the club that had beaten them earlier, and did it in a game where the defences on both sides spent much of the afternoon looking for each other.

The bare facts, as BBC Sport reports them, are these: Bath won, the sides crossed the line 11 times between them, and the result avenges an earlier defeat by Exeter. Fuller detail was not available at the time of writing, so this piece does not try to supply a scoreline, scorers or post-match quotes it cannot stand behind.

A game of open lines

Eleven tries is a lot for any top-flight fixture. A tally like that usually says something about both defences, not only the winners' attack. Exeter have long been associated with a disciplined, structured game, so a contest that opens up this far is notable, and it fits the volatility the Premiership has shown this season, when a side can look dominant one week and porous the next.

That volatility is the main point of interest here. Revenge fixtures carry a neat narrative, but the number of tries suggests a match decided by who attacked more cleanly in the gaps, not one won by squeezing the opposition. For Bath, a win of this kind is welcome but not necessarily a template: sides that score freely can also concede freely, and a coach would want to see the same result produced with a tighter defence.

Elsewhere: Northern Ireland and Humphries

Two other results from the same sporting day were reported by BBC Sport. Northern Ireland advanced in the UEFA Nations League, and Luke Humphries reached the semi-finals of the PDC Grand Prix darts event. Neither shares anything with the rugby beyond the date, and the details of each were not available to this piece. Humphries is a former world champion, so a semi-final place is in keeping with his standing. For Northern Ireland, progress in a competition with promotion and relegation at stake is worth more than the headline suggests.

What to watch

The next question for Bath is whether they can repeat the result without the open-field exchanges that produced it. Their next Premiership fixture will show whether this was a one-off or the start of a pattern. Humphries' semi-final is the next scheduled step for the darts, and Northern Ireland's next Nations League fixture will show how much the latest result changes their position.
