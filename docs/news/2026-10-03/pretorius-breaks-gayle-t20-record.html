Pretorius breaks Gayle's T20 record score

In one sentence
Lhuan-dre Pretorius, 20, hit an unbeaten 188 off 79 balls for the Titans, passing Chris Gayle's 175* from 2013 as the highest individual score in men's T20 cricket.

A record that had looked like a permanent fixture of cricket's record books for 13 years went on Friday, and it went in a domestic game in Bloemfontein rather than on a franchise-league stage. Lhuan-dre Pretorius, a 20-year-old South African opener, made an unbeaten 188 off 79 balls for the Titans against the Knights in the CSA T20 Challenge. That passed the 175 not out that Chris Gayle hit for Royal Challengers Bangalore against Pune Warriors India in the 2013 Indian Premier League.

Pretorius finished 12 short of what would have been the first double-century in men's T20 cricket. His innings included 15 fours and 13 sixes, and his strike rate was 237.97.

The innings

The pace of it explains why the record went. Pretorius reached 50 in 24 balls and 100 in 40. His 150 came in 58 balls, so the second hundred took only 18 deliveries more than the first. The last 38 runs took 21 balls. That is the sign of a batter who stayed ahead of the required rate from the first over and did not settle for a lower one once he was set.

Titans finished on 267 for 3, the second-highest total in the competition's history. Pretorius faced 79 balls, which equals the longest T20 innings by balls faced. Aaron Finch and Marcus Stoinis also reached that figure. Few T20 innings last that long, and almost none are this fast. The record needed an opener who could bat deep into the innings at that tempo, and 79 balls is about two-thirds of a 120-ball innings.

He arrived in form. In his first match of the 2026-27 season he had come off scores of 94 and 101 in recent tri-series matches against Zimbabwe and Namibia. The innings did not come from nowhere.

What Gayle's 175 meant

Gayle's 175* was the standard for so long partly because of how it was made. It took 66 balls and included 17 sixes, a count Pretorius did not match. It came against an attack that was one of the weaker ones in that IPL. For a decade the number sat just beyond what other batters reached, though several came near it.

The case that technique, equipment and conditioning have caught up with Gayle is plausible, but this innings alone does not prove it. It is a single match, played in a domestic competition that does not have the standing of an IPL final. One fact does support it: Pretorius had already made a Test century on debut, 153 against Zimbabwe in June 2025 at the age of 19. That makes him a batter with a full range of strokes who is also able to bat quickly, rather than a specialist hitter whose method works in only one format.

Gayle's hold on the record also carried a hidden cost to the sport. A 175 was a figure that analysts and bookmakers treated as an outer limit when pricing individual scores. That limit is now higher, and the 188 is a new data point for anyone modelling the top end of T20 batting.

What comes next

The near-term question is whether the mark is a one-off or the first of several. Pretorius has the strongest claim on a double-century, given how little he left on the table at 188. The Titans' remaining fixtures in the CSA T20 Challenge are the next place to look. After that, the franchise leagues will show whether he can reproduce the form against bowlers who have a plan for him. Odds and fantasy-cricket pricing will need to be revised for individual scoring, but the main audience for the record remains cricket followers, and the game will be watching whether Pretorius can follow it up.
