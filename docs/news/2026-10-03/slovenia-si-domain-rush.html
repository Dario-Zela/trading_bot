Trump's AI rebrand drives 'unprecedented' demand for Slovenian website names

In one sentence
Trump's order to rename AI as 'super intelligence' (SI) has set off a surge in registrations of Slovenia's .si web domains, though buyers' motives remain unclear.

Slovenia's internet registry is having the busiest fortnight of its existence, and the cause is a coincidence of spelling. Donald Trump has decided that "artificial intelligence" sounds fake, and that the technology should be called "super intelligence" instead, abbreviated SI. Slovenia's country-code domain is .si.

The speech and the order

Speaking at the UN General Assembly, Trump said the word "artificial" undersold the thing, and that SI "sounded much better". He said the US would use "the much more accurate term 'super intelligence'" in its official documents. He then signed an executive order telling federal agencies and departments to replace "AI" with "SI" and "Super Intelligence" in correspondence, reports and digital platforms, according to reports from Foreign Policy and Euronews.

The domain industry noticed at once. For most of the past year Slovenia's registry handled roughly a thousand new .si registrations a month. After the speech, the portal reportedly took more than 6,000 in two days.

The numbers

The figures vary between outlets, so they are best read as an order of magnitude. Klara Herman, a spokeswoman for Register.si, said 44,000 .si addresses were registered in September, against fewer than 2,000 in August. One day, 30 September, produced about 11,000 new registrations. Since the speech, more .si than .ai domains have reportedly been registered each day.

That last comparison is a small joke with a real edge. The .ai suffix belongs to Anguilla, a Caribbean territory, and it has been a lucrative asset for the island as AI start-ups have bought up names. Slovenia has now had a brief taste of the same windfall.

What it does and doesn't tell us

The sources reviewed do not say who is buying. Nothing found separates speculators from Trump supporters, American start-ups hedging on the new vocabulary, or ordinary Slovenian demand. Domain speculation is cheap and fast, and a rush like this is the textbook signature of people buying names they expect to sell on. Whether SI ever becomes standard usage outside Washington is a different matter. Most of the industry still says AI, and an executive order binds only federal agencies.

There is also no trading angle. The registry is a small, nationally run operation, and a registration spike there says little about the technology sector, which is currently worried about valuations rather than vocabulary. The episode shows how a naming quirk can briefly move a micro-market.

What to watch

The test is renewals. Speculative registrations are typically held for a year, so the real data point is how many of the September names are still registered when the first renewal bills arrive in autumn 2027, and whether federal documents actually switch to SI before then.
