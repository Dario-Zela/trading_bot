BEYOND THE TAPE · FOOTBALL · By L. Bordeaux · 2026-11-05 · 3 min read

World-record 51 red cards shown in Brazilian youth match

In one sentence
A Minas Gerais Under-20 semi-final between Uberlândia and Mamoré reportedly ended with 51 dismissals, but the record claim and several details remain unconfirmed.

A youth football match in Brazil has ended with 51 red cards, a total reported as a world record. The game was the first leg of a Minas Gerais Under-20 Championship semi-final between Uberlândia and Mamoré. The BBC is credited in some coverage; the reports reviewed here rest mainly on other outlets, and the BBC piece itself could not be read.

What is reported

Accounts agree on the broad outline. A mass brawl broke out and, by the tally in circulation, 43 players and eight members of staff were sent off, which makes 51 dismissals. Reports say the trigger came after a third goal for Mamoré, when a player celebrated with a taunt toward the opposing bench. Uberlândia's goalkeeping coach is said to have come onto the pitch to confront him, and the confrontation spread to both sets of players.

The aftermath is reported in some detail. Police were called, and a police report is said to have recorded 14 broken doors, damaged showers and smashed windows. One account says Uberlândia then conceded the second leg because Mamoré had only seven players left available. That detail rests on thin sourcing and has not been confirmed.

The coverage also disagrees on the particulars. The minute the brawl began is given differently, and accounts vary on how many cards were shown during normal play. Some reports suggest many of the dismissals were recorded afterwards on the match report, not brandished on the pitch. The spelling of the home side also varies between Mamoré and Mamore. No date for the fixture was established.

Is it a record?

The figure that is usually cited as the benchmark is 36, from a fifth-division match in Argentina in 2011, when every player involved in the game was sent off. Brazil's own senior record also looks modest by comparison: 23 dismissals in the Cruzeiro–Atlético Mineiro state final in March this year. Fifty-one would clear both comfortably.

The record claim is hedged in the coverage itself. Bein Sports said the match "may have broken" a Guinness World Record, and nothing found here shows that Guinness has ruled. There is also a definitional wrinkle: counting staff alongside players makes the total harder to compare with earlier marks, which are usually counted in players.

What it says about the level

The number is the least interesting part. A match in which 51 people are dismissed is, by definition, one where nobody in authority regained control, not the coaches, not the officials and not the people responsible for the touchline. That is a different thing from a spontaneous scuffle among players. Here, on the reported account, an adult member of staff entering the pitch was the spark, and staff made up a sizeable share of those sent off.

Youth competitions sit in an awkward place. They are run by state federations with small officiating crews, and they rarely have the security or video backup of senior fixtures. Nothing in the reports shows a wider pattern of indiscipline in Brazilian youth football, and one match cannot establish one.

What to watch

The next data points are institutional. The Minas Gerais football federation will decide what sanctions to hand to the clubs, players and staff involved, and whether the semi-final stands as played. Whether Guinness is asked to adjudicate, and on what counting basis, will settle the record question. Until then, the claim is best treated as reported and unratified.
