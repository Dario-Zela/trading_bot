LIV Golf to wind down after backers withdraw support

In one sentence
LIV Golf is reportedly to be wound down after its proposed BC Partners rescue collapsed, though the details come from reprints of a BBC report and no official statement was found.

The breakaway LIV Golf tour is set to be wound down after the investor lined up to rescue it pulled out, according to a BBC Sport report that other outlets have repeated. If confirmed, it ends the best-funded challenge the PGA Tour has faced. The tour was launched in 2022 on Saudi money and promised to remake the sport's economics.

No official LIV statement confirming a shutdown had surfaced at the time of writing. Much of the detail below comes from reprints and aggregators of the BBC report, not from the BBC's own page, which could not be accessed. The figures and dates are reported, not verified.

How the money ran out

The first withdrawal came from the original paymaster. The Saudi Public Investment Fund (PIF) said in late April that it would stop funding LIV after the 2026 season, having put in roughly $5bn over five years. It said the long-term commitment LIV required no longer fitted the "current phase" of its investment strategy. Bryson DeChambeau said afterwards he had not seen it coming.

LIV's response was to install an independent board, led by the investment bankers Gene Davis and Jon Zinman, and to look for new money. Reports then pointed to a restructured "LIV 2.0" backed by the private equity firm BC Partners, in a deal reported at about $300m. That is a fraction of what the league had been spending.

The latest reports say that deal has now collapsed. The reason given is that too few players committed to the new league. One aggregator reports that BC set 25 October as the deadline for a "requisite number of players" to sign, and that the PIF set 30 October to decide whether LIV continued or was wound down. Neither date has been officially confirmed. Another report says about 30 players had signed up, which sits awkwardly with the claim that numbers fell short. Whatever the true count, the investor evidently judged it insufficient.

A league short of its stars

The player exodus helps explain the shortfall. Reports name Jon Rahm, Ian Poulter, Tyrrell Hatton and Graeme McDowell among those who left. LIV's final event is reported to have been staged in Indianapolis in August.

The European tour added pressure. It announced that players who signed up to LIV for next season would not be allowed to be members in 2027. That turned a contract decision into a choice about whether a golfer could keep playing in Europe, and it cut against LIV's pitch of guaranteed money without conditions.

Some outlets also report that LIV sought bankruptcy protection in September. That claim appears in aggregator coverage only and should be treated with caution until it is confirmed.

What it means for golf

If LIV does close, three questions follow. The first is where its players go. Those whose contracts run beyond the shutdown would need settlements, and the tour's own talks about readmission, the subject of the PGA Tour's public wrangling since 2022, would reopen. The PGA Tour and DP World Tour hold the leverage and have shown little appetite for easy terms.

The second is the PGA Tour's own position. Its counter-offensive, with bigger purses and equity for top players, was built partly to see off LIV. Without a rival bidding for talent, the case for that spending gets harder to make to sponsors.

The third is what becomes of the Saudi interest in golf. The PIF has stepped back from LIV, but it has not left the sport entirely, and its relationship with the tours is unresolved.

The next catalyst is the announcement reported for Thursday. If LIV or the PIF confirms a wind-down, the details to watch are player buyouts, the fate of the team franchises, and whether the PGA Tour offers any route back for those who left.
