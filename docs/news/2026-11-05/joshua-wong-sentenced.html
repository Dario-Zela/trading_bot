Joshua Wong jailed for 10 years in Hong Kong

In one sentence
A Hong Kong court jailed Joshua Wong for 10 years after he pleaded guilty to conspiring to collude with foreign forces, among the heaviest terms under the national security law.

A Hong Kong court on Thursday sentenced Joshua Wong, the student leader who became the international face of the 2014 Umbrella Movement, to 10 years in prison. Wong pleaded guilty to conspiracy to collude with foreign forces, according to reports from Bloomberg and wire services. The case centred on calls for foreign sanctions against China and Hong Kong. The sentence reaches a man who was a teenager when he first led protests, and it suggests the city's national security regime now covers the generation that built the opposition as well as those still organising it.

The sentence

The term was handed down by the High Court. Bloomberg reports that it is among the heaviest imposed under the Beijing-imposed national security law. Only the 20-year sentence given to the former media owner Jimmy Lai is longer. It matches the 10 years given to Benny Tai, the former law professor who was a founder of the 2014 civic-disobedience campaign.

The conviction is Wong's second under the national security law. In 2024 he was sentenced to four years and eight months in the case over an unofficial 2020 opposition primary. Reports do not make clear how the new term will run alongside that one, and the full reasoning of the court has yet to be widely published. Those details will determine how long he actually spends in custody.

Human Rights Watch condemned the ruling. "The harsh sentence on baseless charges against Joshua Wong is cruel and unjust," said Elaine Pearson, the group's Asia director, urging his release. Beijing and the Hong Kong authorities see Wong differently. They cast him as an anti-China figure and treat appeals to foreign governments as a threat to national security.

A career in the dock

Wong co-founded the student group Scholarism in 2011, at the age of 14, and led a campaign against a mandatory "national education" curriculum. In 2014 he was one of the best-known figures of the Umbrella Movement, the 79-day occupation of central Hong Kong against Beijing's restrictions on how the city's leader could be chosen. The protests won no concessions on the franchise.

He has been in and out of custody since. He was jailed over his part in the 2014 protests, and again in 2021 over an unauthorised assembly marking the Tiananmen Square crackdown. His party, Demosisto, was disbanded in 2020 as the national security law took effect. He has been in prison, on and off, for much of his adult life.

The pattern matters more than any single case. Early prosecutions dealt with specific protests and assemblies. The national security cases deal with advocacy itself, such as lobbying foreign legislatures, organising a primary or running an opposition party. Those offences carry much heavier penalties, and the Wong and Tai sentences show that past activism does not fall outside the law's reach.

What to watch

The first question is legal. Wong may appeal, and the sentencing judgment will show how the court weighed his guilty plea, which usually earns a reduction, against the gravity it placed on calls for sanctions. A 10-year term after a plea implies the court treated the underlying conduct as very serious.

The second is diplomatic. Governments in Washington, London and elsewhere have previously criticised such sentences and in some cases imposed targeted measures on officials. Sanctions advocacy is the very conduct for which Wong was convicted, so any new foreign response will test how Hong Kong reacts. Whether other veterans of the 2014 movement face comparable terms will show whether this sentence marks a new standard or an outlier.
