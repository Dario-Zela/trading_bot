Apple unsure when it can supply more foldable iPhones before Christmas

In one sentence
Apple says it cannot yet say when it can supply more foldable iPhones before Christmas, making the first foldable a test of whether it can build the phone at mass-market volume.

Apple has told customers it does not know when it can supply more of its new foldable iPhone before Christmas. That leaves the first foldable handset short of stock going into the busiest shopping weeks of the year. The constraint applies to the foldable model, not the wider iPhone range.

The account rests on one outlet, BBC Technology. The report as relayed carries no unit numbers, no production figures and no quoted Apple statement. Apple has not, in anything this reporter could find, given a date or a volume. Everything below that is a figure comes from analysts and secondary reports, and is labelled as such.

What is known, and what is not

The shortage was widely predicted. Before launch, analyst Ming-Chi Kuo said limited supply combined with strong demand could mean sell-outs and long waits. Morgan Stanley's Erik Woodring was reported to expect Apple to make 7 to 8 million foldable units in the second half of 2026, with demand possibly exceeding that. The same report put potential sales at close to $14bn in the December quarter. These are forecasts, not Apple's numbers.

The launch itself appears to have followed that script. A BeInCrypto report, a single secondary source, said pre-orders sold out within minutes on 16 October. It said the $1,999 device no longer shipped before Christmas, and that Apple's US store listed delivery in late January. It also suggested some stock or pickup slots might still turn up in shops. Treat those details as unconfirmed until Apple or the BBC piece supplies them.

Pre-launch supply-chain reports pointed to early output of only a few hundred units a day, though that figure is unverified. Other reports said Apple had raised its first order for foldable OLED panels from about 15 million to as many as 20 million. The sources differ on how long tightness will last: some say the end of 2026, others well into 2027.

Why a foldable is a harder build

A conventional iPhone uses a glass slab and a mature, high-yield assembly line. A foldable needs a flexible OLED panel that survives repeated bending without a visible crease, plus a hinge built to tight tolerances. Each extra step is another place for units to fail inspection. Apple's quality bar is high, which in practice means more rejected panels and a slower ramp. That is a plausible reading of the reports of very low early output, but it is inference, not something Apple has said.

The comparison that matters is with Apple's own record. The company routinely makes tens of millions of phones a quarter. Missing demand on a model expected to sell in the single-digit millions says less about demand than about how hard the part is to make.

What it means for Apple and suppliers

For Apple, a holiday quarter with the headline product on back order means revenue slips into the new year rather than vanishing, provided buyers wait. For suppliers, especially the foldable display makers named in earlier reports, including Samsung Display, the question is whether panel yields improve fast enough to justify the larger orders.

The next hard data should come from Apple's results for the quarter ending in December, expected in late January, where management may say how much demand it could not meet. Until then, a delivery date from Apple or figures from the full BBC report would turn the estimates into something firmer.
