Analysts turn cautious on NuScale and Adobe ahead of earnings

In one sentence
Barclays turned bearish on NuScale as it reports today and Barron's flags another Adobe downgrade before earnings; the results, not the calls, will show whether anything is being repriced.

Two analyst calls have landed on the same morning, both ahead of results. Barclays has turned bearish on NuScale Power, the small modular reactor developer, which reports earnings today. Separately, Adobe has drawn what Barron's calls "another downgrade" before its own next report. Neither call is large. Together they show how sell-side caution tends to arrive just before a print.

NuScale: a downgrade into the number

NuScale (NYSE: SMR) is trading at a 52-week low, according to a TIKR-sourced report. The source gives no price level and no percentage move, so the scale of the decline is not established here. It also does not give Barclays' rating label, price target or the date of the call.

The timing matters more than the call itself. NuScale reports on Thursday 5 November 2026, so the bearish note comes within hours of the results. A single-broker downgrade on results day is a routine move. Analysts reposition before an event, and the stock often reflects that before the numbers appear. One broker's change of view says little about whether the wider case for small modular reactors has weakened.

That case rests on demand and financing. Developers in the sector are judged less on current revenue than on whether customers sign binding orders and whether the capital to build the first units is lined up. The results and any guidance are where that would show. A print that confirms the low, or breaks it in either direction, would say more about the market's view of the sector than Barclays' note does.

Adobe: a second downgrade, thinly sourced

Barron's reports that Adobe faces another downgrade ahead of earnings. The word "another" implies at least one earlier cut, but the source material does not identify the analyst, the firm, the price target or the date of either call. Those details need to come from the original Barron's article before anything firmer can be said.

The framing also suggests caution is building across software stocks before results. No sector data or peer names support that reading here, so it stays an inference. One downgrade of one software company does not establish a de-rating of the group. It would take several brokers moving on several names, or a visible shift in valuation multiples, to show that.

What links the two

The link is the calendar, not the industry. A nuclear developer and a software group have little in common beyond the analyst note that precedes an earnings date. In both cases the call is a signal about positioning, and the results decide whether it was right.

For NuScale, the thing to watch is today's report: whether the numbers and guidance show orders and funding moving forward, and whether the shares hold or break the 52-week low afterwards. For Adobe, the test is its next report, and whether other brokers follow the downgrades with cuts of their own.
