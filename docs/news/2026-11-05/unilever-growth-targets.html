Unilever targets 4-6% sales growth and higher margins after foods separation

In one sentence
Unilever has reportedly set post-foods-separation targets of 4-6% sales growth and higher margins, plus €35m for Indian R&D centres; the figures are unverified against primary sources.

Unilever has told investors what it expects to look like once its foods business is gone: a faster-growing, higher-margin company built around beauty, personal care and home products. The FTSE 100 group has set medium-term targets of 4-6% sales growth, with margins to improve, and says it will invest €35m in research and development centres in India, according to reports from MarketBeat and WWD.

The figures come from those secondary reports. Unilever's own release and results materials have not been checked, so the numbers below should be read as reported rather than confirmed.

What was set out

The growth range is the headline. A 4-6% band is a mid-single-digit ambition, close to the language Unilever has used before. In 2024 the group said that, after separating its ice cream business, it aimed for "mid single digit underlying sales growth, supported by underlying volume growth of at least 2%". The new range reads as a continuation of that stance, applied to a smaller and simpler company, not as a step up.

The margin commitment is vaguer. "Higher margins" gives investors a direction but no number in the reports seen so far. Removing a lower-margin foods division mechanically lifts the group's blended margin, so some of the improvement may arrive without any operating gain. The test will be whether the margin rises by more than the separation itself explains.

That is why this is a reset rather than a turnaround. Targets set alongside a portfolio change are routine: they define the new company and give analysts a yardstick. They are judged on delivery, quarter by quarter, and a range as wide as two percentage points leaves room to miss the midpoint and still claim to be on track. Underlying volume, which strips out price increases, is the cleaner measure of whether shoppers are buying more.

The India commitment

The €35m for Indian R&D centres is the one new, concrete commitment in the reports. It is small against a group of Unilever's size, but it is specific, and it fits a pattern. Management has called India a key long-term growth engine, and the country accounts for roughly 14% of group revenue, against about 21% for the United States. Putting research capacity there suggests the company wants products designed for, and in, a market where it expects volume growth to outpace Europe's.

The spend is modest enough that it should not move earnings. Its value is as a signal of where management intends the 4-6% to come from.

What to watch

The next data point is Unilever's next set of quarterly results, where the first post-separation underlying sales and volume figures will show how far the group sits from the new range. Confirmation of the margin target, and of the €35m figure, in Unilever's primary materials is the other thing outstanding.
