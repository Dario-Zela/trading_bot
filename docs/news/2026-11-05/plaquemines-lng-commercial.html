Venture Global's Plaquemines LNG begins commercial operations

In one sentence
Venture Global declared Plaquemines LNG Phase 1 commercial on 31 October, moving output from spot sales to long-term buyers; the plant has exported since December 2024.

The "first cargo" framing on Plaquemines is a year out of date. Venture Global's Louisiana export terminal shipped its first LNG in December 2024 and has exported commissioning cargoes ever since. The new development is contractual: the company declared commercial operations for Phase 1 on 31 October 2026, just over 53 months after its May 2022 final investment decision. That declaration moves the plant's output from the spot market to long-term buyers.

What changed

Until now Plaquemines operated under commissioning status. In that period Venture Global sold cargoes into the spot market rather than delivering them to its long-term customers. A commercial-operations declaration ends that phase. The long-term sales-and-purchase agreements start, and the buyers receive their contracted volumes.

Phase 1 customers include Shell, Poland's Orlen, China's Sinopec and CNOOC Gas & Power, and France's EDF. Shell's first cargo under its long-term agreement sailed at the turn of the month. Reports differ on whether it left on 31 October or 1 November.

The numbers

Phase 1 has a nameplate capacity of about 13.3 million tonnes a year, according to reports of the declaration. Phase 2 is expected to begin supplying long-term customers from mid-2027. The two phases together are put at 20 million tonnes a year, which is the figure triage used for the plant as a whole. Some outlets cite a 28 million tonne maximum liquefaction capacity once Phase 2 is complete. The gap between 20 and 28 is probably the difference between contract nameplate and peak physical output, though the sources reviewed do not say so. Venture Global is also pursuing an expansion, with a final investment decision targeted for the first half of 2027 and first LNG in 2029, subject to regulatory approvals.

The supply effect is smaller than the headline implies. The plant has been producing for almost two years, so the barrels are already in the market. What changes is who gets them. Volumes that went to spot buyers now go to contract holders. Spot availability of US LNG therefore tightens at the margin, while Henry Hub-linked feedgas demand stays high, because the plant needs gas whoever buys the cargo.

The question behind the declaration

The timing of a commercial-operations date has been contested before. Buyers at Venture Global's earlier Calcasieu Pass plant pursued arbitration over the delay in declaring it, while the company sold LNG at higher spot prices. In October 2026 a tribunal ruled that Venture Global breached its agreement with Portugal's Galp. The company has previously prevailed in disputes with Shell and Repsol.

Plaquemines buyers have little to dispute on timing here. The plant was declared roughly 22 months after first export, and the contractual start now matches the milestone the contracts anticipated. The commercial question has moved on to delivery. Whether the plant sustains its 13.3 million tonne rating, and how quickly Phase 2 ramps up, will determine how much of a supply addition this is.

What to watch

The next data points are monthly export volumes from Plaquemines, which will show whether loadings run at nameplate once contract cargoes dominate. After that comes the Phase 2 start-up in mid-2027, and the expansion decision due in the first half of that year.
