Zverev annoyed by reaction to becoming world number one

In one sentence
Newly crowned world number one Alexander Zverev has brushed off criticism of his rise, dismissing Boris Becker's opinion, though the BBC's exact report could not be checked.

Alexander Zverev has reached the top of men's tennis, and he is not enjoying the reception. The German, who became world number one this week, has bristled at criticism of his rise, and one critic in particular.

Zverev clinched the top spot on 29 October with a win over Tallon Griekspoor in Vienna, and the ATP rankings dated 2 November made it official. He replaces Jannik Sinner, who had held the position since April and has not played since winning Wimbledon in July because of a right knee injury. TennisNerd puts Zverev on 9,550 points to Sinner's 9,450, a margin of 100.

At 29, Zverev is the second-oldest man to reach number one for the first time, behind John Newcombe, and the 30th since the rankings began in 1973. He spent more than four years stuck at number two.

The edge to the achievement

That long wait explains some of the sensitivity. Through 2025 Zverev was needled as the "worst world number two ever", a jibe he said he found funny. He also argued that the media enjoys putting players down. When Sinner's absence opened the door, a section of the tennis public argued that the ranking was a product of rival injuries rather than dominance. Zverev's own line, offered in Rome last year, was that "the rankings don't lie."

The critic he has singled out is Boris Becker. According to Punto de Break, which reported the exchange on 26 October, Zverev was asked about criticism from the press, analysts and the public. He answered that "there is only one person who has persistently criticized me lately," and added: "I'm not too interested in his opinion." The report identifies that person as Becker.

Becker's record on Zverev is mixed. He has questioned the role of Zverev's father as head coach and said the player had "not evolved much". He has also, more recently, defended Zverev against those who called his 2026 success undeserved, and said he would rightly become number one. Zverev's jab came regardless.

What is and isn't known

The BBC Sport item that prompted this report was not available to check. The reaction quoted above was made before the ranking became official, so it may not be the comment the BBC reported. No direct quote tied to the final days of the climb has been confirmed, and the identity of any critic other than Becker is not established.

The ranking itself is solid. Several outlets, including Punto de Break, TennisNerd and wire reports carried by regional papers, agree on the date and the order.

What next

Number one is easier to reach than to keep. Sinner's absence is the obvious variable: the Italian's return from injury, and how quickly he can win back points, will decide whether Zverev's tenure is a long one or a brief one. The next measure comes in the final weeks of the season, when Zverev has to show that a ranking earned in Sinner's absence can be defended against him.
