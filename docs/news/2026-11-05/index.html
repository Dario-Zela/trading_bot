BANK RATE · By M. Halloway

Bank of England expected to raise rates for the first time in over three years as sterling hits a ten-year high

The Bank of England is expected to lift Bank Rate to 4.00% today, its first rise since August 2023, with the vote split and guidance showing how far the MPC now prioritises inflation.

The Bank of England is widely expected to raise Bank Rate at its next decision, its first increase since August 2023 and a reversal of the easing cycle it began in August 2024. Markets and economists are pricing the move as sterling sits at a ten-year high. The decision is made by the nine-member Monetary Policy Committee, and the vote split, published with each decision, will show how far the committee has moved toward treating inflation, rather than weak growth, as the bigger risk.

The last rise took Bank Rate to 5.25%, closing a run of 14 consecutive increases that began in December 2021. A hike would pass straight through to tracker and variable mortgage rates, unsecured loan costs and savings rates.

The vote and the forward guidance that accompanies it are the details to watch. A unanimous or near-unanimous move would point to a settled view on the direction of policy. A narrow split would suggest the committee is still divided on whether the easing cycle is over.
