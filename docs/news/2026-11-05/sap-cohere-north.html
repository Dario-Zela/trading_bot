SAP makes Cohere North generally available on its Business AI platform

In one sentence
SAP has made Cohere's North agent platform generally available on its Business AI platform, though the single source gives no date, pricing or customer figures.

SAP has made Cohere North, an AI agent platform built by the Toronto-based company Cohere, generally available on its Business AI platform. That means SAP's customers can now buy and deploy a partner's agent software inside SAP's own environment. They no longer need to run it as a separate product alongside SAP.

The only source for the latest step is a single brief item from the financial-data site fiscal.ai. It gives no date, price, customer count or executive quote. A search found no dated SAP or Cohere announcement confirming the general-availability milestone on SAP's platform, so those details remain unconfirmed.

What North is

North is Cohere's workplace platform. It combines the company's language models with agents that can search a company's documents and carry out tasks. Cohere says it is built for deployment inside a customer's own infrastructure, which suits regulated industries and governments. The company moved North to general availability on its own account in August 2025, according to SiliconANGLE and Constellation Research.

The SAP version is a separate step. North is offered through SAP's platform, not sold as an SAP product, so the model, agent tooling and security work stay with Cohere.

A partnership with a track record

The two companies have been moving in this direction for some time. In 2025 they announced that Cohere's models would be offered through the generative AI hub in SAP AI Core, alongside other SAP and third-party models. A later expansion, reported by Techzine, tied North to SAP's Business Technology Platform and its EU AI Cloud. The pitch there was sovereignty: European customers with data-residency rules want AI that runs on infrastructure they can place under their own jurisdiction.

This helps explain why SAP picked Cohere. Cohere sells itself as an enterprise-first, privacy-minded alternative to the consumer-facing model labs, and SAP's customers are large organisations that worry about where their data goes.

Why it matters, and how much

This is a product-availability update, not a market-moving event. Its significance is in the pattern. Incumbent enterprise software vendors are filling out their AI offerings with partner models, not relying only on models they build themselves. SAP's rivals are assembling similar catalogues. For buyers, the practical question is whether an agent platform inside the SAP stack connects more easily to finance, procurement and HR data than a standalone tool would. That is the reason to choose the bundled route.

The open questions are the ones the source leaves blank: pricing, which SAP modules North connects to out of the box, and how many customers are live. SAP's and Cohere's own announcements are the places to check for those, and the first customer deployments would show whether general availability turns into real usage.
