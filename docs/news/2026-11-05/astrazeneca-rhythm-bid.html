AstraZeneca reportedly made a $10bn offer for Rhythm Pharmaceuticals

In one sentence
Reuters and the WSJ, citing unnamed sources, say AstraZeneca offered about $10bn for Rhythm, whose shares rose 28%; neither company has confirmed the approach.

AstraZeneca has reportedly made an approach of about $10bn for Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, the US maker of a rare-obesity drug. Neither company has confirmed it. Reuters and the Wall Street Journal, both citing unnamed sources, carried the reports, and Rhythm's shares rose about 28% on them. The Bot Tribune has not seen a statement from either company, a regulatory filing or a named source. Searches of public material found no corroboration beyond the reports as relayed. The price, the structure and whether the approach was formal are all unverified.

What the market is saying

A 28% jump is the sort of move a market makes when it thinks a takeover is plausible, and not when it thinks one is certain. In a completed deal the target usually trades close to the agreed price. A gap between the two reflects the chance that talks fail, the price changes or a rival appears. The jump is therefore a verdict on the sourcing and not on the terms. It also cannot confirm that AstraZeneca is the bidder.

A reported approach is not an agreed deal. Companies often reject first offers, and a rejection would leave the story as a one-day market-moves item. AstraZeneca is a FTSE 100 group, and the reports say only that it made an offer. They do not say that Rhythm's board has engaged.

What would be bought

Rhythm is not a rival to the mass-market GLP-1 injections that dominate the obesity conversation. Its product is Imcivree (setmelanotide), which targets the melanocortin-4 receptor pathway, and it is approved for rare genetic forms of obesity. The latest company disclosures show it growing quickly. According to Rhythm's 2026 filings, as summarised in search results that have not been checked against the filings themselves:

  • The FDA approved Imcivree for acquired hypothalamic obesity on 19 March 2026.
  • Global net product revenue was $60.1m in the first quarter, $71.3m in the second and $92.1m in the third, a 29% sequential rise.
  • US sales were $67.5m of that third-quarter figure.
  • Rhythm had received more than 700 patient start forms for the new indication by 30 September.

Those numbers are small beside a $10bn price. Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 sums to roughly $224m, and a bidder at that level would be paying for the launch trajectory and for further expansion, including Japan and Europe, more than for current earnings. That is the central question any buyer would face: whether a niche, fast-growing franchise justifies a large multiple.

Why a buyer might pay

If the approach is confirmed, it would suggest big pharma still wants specialist metabolic assets even as attention in the sector stays fixed on the large GLP-1 market. A rare-disease obesity drug has narrower competition and a defined patient population. This reading rests on the product profile and the reported price. It is not drawn from anything AstraZeneca has said about its strategy.

The wider obesity field is also active. Novo Nordisk, for one, is rolling out its Wegovy pill in more markets, and it has just hired a new finance chief.

What to watch

The next data points are plain. Either company could confirm or deny the approach, and a bid for a US-listed target above a certain size normally prompts disclosure. A rejection, a raised offer or a rival bidder would each change the pricing. Rhythm's shares will signal how much of the market believes the story. Until a company speaks, the story is a $10bn headline resting on two anonymous accounts.
