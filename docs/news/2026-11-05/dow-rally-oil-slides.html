Dow posts its biggest post-midterm rally in 44 years as oil falls 7%; FTSE 100 gains 1%

In one sentence
The Dow rose about 2.9% and oil fell roughly 7% after Democrats won Congress, though the S&P 500's smaller gain and unresolved Iran talks suggest the rally is narrower than it looks.

US stocks rose sharply on Wednesday after Democrats won control of Congress, and crude oil fell by about 7%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained roughly 2.9% on Benzinga's count, closing near 53,300. Other outlets put the gain slightly higher, at about 3.03% or 1,569 points, and the difference looks like a matter of timing and rounding. Either way it was the Dow's best day since April 2025. London joined in more modestly, with the FTSE 100 up about 1%.

The headline comparison, that this is the biggest post-midterm rally in 44 years, could not be confirmed. None of the market reports reviewed made that claim, and it is best treated as unverified until checked against historical index data.

Oil does the heavy lifting

The more informative number is the move in crude. West Texas Intermediate fell about 7.2% to roughly $89 a barrel, and Brent fell about 8.5% to roughly $95. Traders tied the drop to hopes that the result could push Washington towards ending the Iran war, which has kept energy prices elevated for months.

The oil move had started before the vote. On Monday the Dow added about 500 points and crude slid on hopes of a US-Iran deal, with reports citing talks between Iranian and Qatari officials. By Wednesday, then, part of the oil decline was already a bet on diplomacy, and the election may have added to it rather than started it.

That matters for how the equity gain is read. A fall in crude of this size lowers fuel costs for airlines, retailers and consumers, and it eases inflation expectations, which in turn bears on the rate outlook. The Consumer Discretionary sector fund (XLY) led the S&P 500 with a gain of about 4.3%, which fits that reading better than it fits a pure "gridlock is good" story.

A narrower rally than it looks

The breadth of the move was thinner than the Dow's gain suggests. The S&P 500 rose about 1.6% to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite about 1.4% to 25,736.51, roughly half the Dow's percentage move. The Dow is a price-weighted average of 30 stocks, so a handful of large rises can lift it well beyond the broader market. The gap between the indices is the clearest evidence for the commentators' view that the rally is narrower than the headline figure implies. An advance-decline count would settle it, and none was available.

The political reading is the conventional one. Analysts said a divided Washington would limit the chances of large new tax and spending packages. The Democrats still lack a filibuster-proof Senate majority, and President Trump retains his veto. Markets have often favoured that kind of stalemate.

There were losers. Trump Media & Technology Group fell almost 25%, and shares in Tesla and SpaceX fell about 3.3% and 3.8%.

London follows, with less drama

The FTSE 100 rose about 1%, with banks and miners reported as the leading sectors. The closing level, the exact percentage and the size of individual stock moves could not be confirmed, and no source cited a specific mechanism. Plausible channels include a weaker dollar, which tends to help dollar-priced metals and miners, and lower oil. Neither was shown to be the driver, so the attribution to the US result remains an inference. A one-day gain of this size is within normal range for the index.

What to watch

The unresolved variable is Iran. If talks produce a durable deal, the oil decline could extend and ease the inflation pressure that has weighed on rate expectations. If they stall, the premium that left crude on Wednesday could return quickly, and the Dow's gain with it. The next data points are the oil price itself and the Bank of England's rate decision, where a hike is widely expected.
