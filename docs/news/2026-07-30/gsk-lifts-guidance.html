GSK lifts full-year guidance after strong Q2 sales and pipeline progress

GSK's global headquarters in Brentford, west London, seen from Boston Manor Park — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
GSK's Q2 core EPS of 50.5p beat forecasts and the group lifted sales and profit guidance, even as EPS guidance softened on Nuvalent-deal costs.

GSK shares climbed as much as 4% on Tuesday after the drugmaker posted second-quarter core earnings per share of 50.5p, comfortably ahead of the 47.1p analysts had pencilled in, and lifted the top end of its sales and profit expectations for the year. Group sales rose 5% at constant exchange rates to £8.4bn, with core operating profit up 7% to £2.8bn — a 33.3% margin — driven by Specialty Medicines, up 14%, and a vaccines unit, up 8%, that benefited from expanded use of the respiratory jab Arexvy and unplanned demand tied to meningitis outbreaks.

The guidance picture is more textured than a straight upgrade. GSK now expects full-year turnover growth and core operating profit growth to land in the upper half of its existing 3-5% and 7-9% ranges respectively — effectively a raise, without moving the ranges themselves. Core EPS growth, by contrast, is now guided to the lower half of its own 7-9% range, a function of roughly £160m in extra interest expense tied to the $7.1bn acquisition of Nuvalent, the lung-cancer biotech GSK bought earlier this year, plus a higher expected tax rate. Investors appear to have read through that detail: the operating numbers, not the financing drag, set the tone.

Pipeline, not cost-cutting, is doing the work

The more consequential news sat underneath the headline print. GSK said it now expects more than 20 Phase III trial starts in 2026, roughly double its original plan, spanning seven key assets across 18 indications in oncology, respiratory disease, hepatology and vaccines — part of what chief executive Luke Miels called a step-up in R&D investment. Alongside it, the company launched a three-year cost programme targeting £1.9bn in annual savings by 2029, at an implementation cost of £2.4bn, with most of the freed-up cash earmarked for late-stage development rather than the balance sheet.

That combination — cutting cost in one part of the business to fund a faster clinical pipeline in another — is the clearest evidence yet that the R&D-led turnaround GSK has pursued since splitting off its consumer health arm in 2022 is starting to show up in the numbers rather than just the roadmap. It is also the first set of quarterly results delivered under Miels, who took over from Emma Walmsley on 1 January after nine years in which she rebuilt the group's specialty medicines and vaccines franchises largely from scratch. Walmsley remains at GSK in an advisory capacity until her notice period ends on 30 September.

Analysts at Hargreaves Lansdown noted the stock has already re-rated sharply over the past two years and now trades broadly in line with consensus forecasts, meaning further upside likely depends on GSK continuing to beat rather than merely meet expectations. The next test comes with Q3 results and early readouts from the accelerated trial slate, alongside how quickly the Nuvalent integration costs fade from the EPS line.
