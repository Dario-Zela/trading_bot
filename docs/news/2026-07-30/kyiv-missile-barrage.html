Russia hits Kyiv with fresh missile barrage hours after Zelensky's warning

A Patriot interceptor launches during a live-fire exercise — the system at the centre of Ukraine's air-defense requests to Washington. — Bernd vdB / U.S. Army, via Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Russia hit Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities with a deadly missile barrage hours after Zelensky warned of an imminent assault, a day after asking Trump for more air defenses.

Kyiv had barely absorbed the news from Washington before the sirens sounded again. Hours after President Volodymyr Zelensky warned that Russia was massing for a fresh assault, Moscow sent ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and drones across Ukraine overnight — the first major test of whatever goodwill was banked during his White House meeting with Donald Trump a day earlier.

Zelensky had told his air force staff hours before the strike that a "massive attack" on all regions was likely that night, saying "there is a high probability that the strike will be carried out tonight." Russia's defence ministry described the barrage as a strike by "long-range precision weapons from land, air, and sea," paired with attack drones aimed at what it called military sites. At least 13 people were killed across the country, according to regional officials: one in Kyiv, six in Dnipropetrovsk and Poltava regions, and six more — including two girls aged five and twelve — when a missile hit a family home in Kryvyi Rih, Zelensky's hometown. Fifteen people were wounded in Lviv, near the Polish border, where two apartment blocks were damaged. Poland scrambled fighter jets and activated early-warning aircraft as a precaution.

A request tested within a day

The timing sharpened the stakes of Tuesday's Oval Office meeting, where Zelensky and Trump spent roughly an hour discussing what Zelensky called "licenses for Patriot interceptor production and several other ideas that could help," alongside efforts to revive diplomacy with Moscow. Trump had already announced at a NATO summit in Ankara that Washington would let Ukraine manufacture Patriot interceptors under license — a deal Kyiv has welcomed but repeatedly cautioned is a medium-term fix, not a response to Thursday-night barrages. Zelensky has separately pressed for an emergency "winter package" of ready-made interceptors, telling Axios that stockpiles have been nearly exhausted and that the shortage is why recent Russian strikes have gotten through.

The gap between promise and delivery

The pattern is now familiar enough to have its own shape: a diplomatic gesture from Washington, followed within a news cycle by Russian escalation that argues for more urgency than the gesture supplied. It happened after the Ankara summit, and it happened again this week. Ukrainian officials have not suggested Thursday's barrage was retaliation for the Trump meeting specifically — Russia has launched near-nightly strikes on Kyiv for months — but the sequencing keeps landing the same way, underscoring how far a manufacturing license is from an interceptor on a launcher.

What happens next depends less on rhetoric than logistics: how quickly the licensed Patriot production can be stood up, and whether Washington agrees to backfill Ukraine's depleted interceptor stocks in the meantime. Zelensky's teams and Trump's officials are due to work out those details in the coming weeks, with winter — and the likelihood of intensified strikes on energy infrastructure — the deadline that matters most.
