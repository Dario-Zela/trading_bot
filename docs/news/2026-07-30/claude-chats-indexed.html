Some users' Claude AI conversations found searchable on Google

The Claude AI symbol, Anthropic's chatbot at the centre of the indexing episode. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Reddit users found 200-plus shared Claude conversations indexed on Google over the weekend, exposing medical and personal data before Anthropic scrubbed the listings.

Type a question into Claude, hit share, and — until this week — there was a decent chance a stranger could find your conversation by typing a few words into Google. That is what a batch of Reddit users discovered over the weekend of 25–26 July, running site-specific searches against Anthropic's chatbot and turning up, by the BBC's count, more than 200 shared Claude conversations sitting in Google's public index across at least 25 pages of results.

What they found was not anonymous small talk. Reporters and researchers who followed the trail turned up a patient's medical report, clinical trial data with real names attached, a document listing the phone numbers of primary-school-aged children, internal company files marked for staff eyes only, and staff performance reviews. Resumés, API keys and financial details were also reported among the exposed links, according to Fortune and TechCrunch.

In one sentence: Reddit users found 200-plus shared Claude conversations sitting in Google's search index over the weekend, exposing everything from medical reports to children's phone numbers before Anthropic scrubbed the listings.

A share link is a publish button

Claude conversations are private by default. But the "share" button generates a public URL — the same mechanism that lets someone forward a useful chat to a colleague, or embed a Claude-built spreadsheet in a blog post. The trouble is that a public URL is, definitionally, a public webpage, and public webpages are exactly what search engines are built to find.

Anthropic's account is that this only happens if a link gets posted somewhere a crawler can see it — a forum thread, a tweet, a public repo — and that "these shareable links are not guessable or discoverable unless people choose to share them themselves." That is true as far as it goes. It just skates past the part where the company's own crawl directives made the problem worse than it needed to be.

Where the technical fix broke down

Anthropic's /share/ pages were set to "disallow" in Claude's robots.txt file, and the pages themselves carried an X-Robots-Tag: none header — the standard signal telling a search engine not to index a page. Belt and braces, in theory. In practice, the two instructions worked against each other: a robots.txt disallow tells a crawler not to open a page, which means it never gets to read the noindex header sitting inside. If another site links to that URL, Google can still list it, because listing a URL only requires knowing it exists, not reading its contents — a distinction the SEO trade press has needled Anthropic over.

A Google spokesperson told the BBC the company simply enforces whatever instructions site owners give it: "We give site owners clear controls to decide whether pages can be crawled or indexed, and we always respect those directives." The indexed pages had stopped appearing in test searches by Monday afternoon, though neither Anthropic nor Google detailed exactly how the cleanup was done.

Not Anthropic's first rodeo, or the industry's

OpenAI ran into an almost identical problem a year earlier. In July 2025, ChatGPT briefly offered users a "make this chat discoverable" checkbox when generating a share link — an explicit opt-in, unlike Claude's more passive exposure — and thousands of conversations turned up in Google search anyway, including resumés and emotionally sensitive personal disclosures. OpenAI killed the feature within days and began scrubbing the index.

The pattern across both incidents is the same: consumer chat products treat "shareable" as a convenience feature, not a publishing decision with search-engine consequences, and the safety net (noindex tags, opt-in boxes) has twice failed to catch what it was built for.

Why it matters for market-watchers

Traders and analysts have been quick to fold AI chatbots into research workflows — thesis-building, scenario modelling, drafting notes — often on the assumption that a conversation stays between them and the machine. This episode is a reminder that the assumption doesn't hold once a share link is generated, and that consumer-grade defaults were never built with the kind of information-sensitivity institutional users take for granted. The direct exposure here looks limited: nothing in the indexed batch has been reported as trading-strategy material, and the fix mainly matters to anyone who actively used Claude's share feature rather than to algorithmic operations generally. But the mechanics are the lesson — a share button and a broken noindex tag are all it takes.

Watch for whether Anthropic publishes a fuller post-mortem, and whether Google or other search engines start treating AI-chat share pages as a distinct category requiring stricter default handling, the way image search and PDF indexing eventually got their own carve-outs.
