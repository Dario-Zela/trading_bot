Amazon Q2 preview: AWS growth in focus as ecommerce momentum questioned again

Amazon's Seattle headquarters campus, home to the Spheres, ahead of the company's second-quarter earnings report — SounderBruce / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Amazon reports Q2 results Wednesday with AWS growth expected near 32% eclipsing steady but unspectacular retail gains, while a possible capex guidance raise looms as the bigger risk to the stock.

Amazon reports second-quarter results Wednesday after the closing bell, and for the second straight quarter Wall Street has largely stopped pretending the headline number is the one that matters. The question analysts keep returning to isn't whether Amazon beats — it has topped revenue estimates for seven consecutive quarters — but whether AWS can keep accelerating fast enough to make the retail business look beside the point.

Consensus puts revenue at roughly $196 billion, against Amazon's own guidance range of $194 billion to $199 billion and up from $167.7 billion a year earlier. Earnings per share are expected near $1.82, versus $1.68 in the second quarter of 2025. Those are respectable numbers. They are not the ones moving the stock.

That distinction belongs to AWS. The cloud unit posted $37.6 billion in revenue last quarter, up 28% year-over-year — its fastest growth in 15 quarters — with $14.2 billion of segment operating income. The Zacks consensus for Q2 sits at $40.6 billion, implying acceleration to roughly 31.6% growth; Bank of America has gone further, lifting its estimate to 33%, pointing to Anthropic and OpenAI workloads running through Amazon's Bedrock platform as a source of demand that didn't exist at this scale a year ago. By contrast, North America retail revenue is expected to grow around 12%, in line with last quarter's $104.1 billion print, and international growth is pegged near 19% — solid, but not the number analysts are building models around.

The capex question hangs over the print

The complication is spending. Amazon has guided to roughly $200 billion of capital expenditure for 2026, up from $131 billion last year, almost all of it tied to AI infrastructure. Chief executive Andy Jassy has defended the outlay bluntly: "We're not investing approximately $200 billion in capex in 2026 on a hunch." He has also leaned on Amazon's custom chip programme, arguing in-house silicon will "save us tens of billions of dollars of capex each year and provide several hundred basis points of operating margin advantage" — an argument aimed squarely at investors nervous about how long the spending cycle runs.

That nervousness has a recent precedent. Alphabet raised its own 2026 capex guidance last week, from $180–190 billion to $195–205 billion, and its stock fell on the news. Cowen analyst Derrick Wood has said plainly that if Jassy raises Amazon's guidance again on the call, "it's probably going to lead to selling pressure in the stock" — the same dynamic that hit Alphabet, applied to the company whose cloud business is supposed to be the AI trade's clearest winner.

Amazon shares have traded in a wide $196–$278.56 range over the past year and sat near $226 heading into the report. Analyst targets are split on the outcome: BMO Capital raised its target to $360 and Wells Fargo to $322, while UBS trimmed its target to $305 even while keeping a Buy rating, citing confidence in AWS alongside improving e-commerce margins.

The print itself lands after markets close. The two figures worth watching are AWS's growth rate against that 31–33% band, and whatever capex number Jassy puts on the call — the second may do more to move the stock than the first.
