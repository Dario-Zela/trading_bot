Commonwealth Games open in Glasgow with 74 nations competing

The OVO Hydro in Glasgow, where the Commonwealth Games opening ceremony was staged entirely indoors for the first time in the event's history. — Fraser MacDonald / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
Glasgow reopened as Commonwealth Games host on a quarter of its 2014 budget, after Australia's Victoria abandoned the role in 2023.

Glasgow did it again on Thursday night, if rather more modestly than last time. King Charles III declared the 2026 Commonwealth Games open at the OVO Hydro, twelve years after the city's last turn as host, with athletes from 74 nations and territories filing into an indoor arena for a ceremony that began — Doctor Who fans will not be surprised — with Sir Chris Hoy piloting the King and Queen across Scotland in a TARDIS, taking in Loch Ness and Balmoral before touching down beside the Clyde.

Gabon and Togo marched at a Commonwealth Games for the first time. Every delegation carried its own decorated baton, set down in a garden at the centre of the arena as it entered — a nod to the relay tradition, condensed for a hall rather than a stadium bowl. Roughly 3,000 athletes will compete across ten sports over eleven days: athletics, swimming, gymnastics, track cycling, netball, weightlifting, boxing, judo, bowls and 3x3 basketball, held at four venues within a few miles of each other — Scotstoun Stadium, Tollcross International Swimming Centre, the Scottish Event Campus and the Hydro itself. Around 500,000 tickets have gone on sale, with the cheapest seats starting at £12.

A Games built to a much smaller budget

The scale is deliberately modest, and the reason is worth dwelling on. Glasgow was not the first choice for 2026 — Victoria, in Australia, was awarded the Games in 2022 before its state government walked away in 2023, unwilling to meet a bill that had climbed toward A$6 billion. Glasgow's organisers, working from a venue list already built for 2014, agreed to stage a leaner version instead: ten sports rather than eighteen, four venues rather than a sprawling regional programme, and a total cost of around £150 million — roughly a quarter of what the city spent hosting in 2014, when the bill ran to £543 million. Most of that has come from the Commonwealth Games Federation and commercial revenue rather than Scottish taxpayers, organisers say, with Victoria's compensation payments to Glasgow, reported at close to £150 million, helping cover the gap left by its withdrawal.

Whether a stripped-back model becomes the template for future Games, rather than a one-off improvisation, is the question hanging over the closing ceremony on 2 August. Competition proper begins Friday with swimming heats at Tollcross and the first athletics sessions at Scotstoun.
