FTSE earnings season opens with Lloyds, Standard Chartered, BAE and more

The London Stock Exchange building in Paternoster Square, where LSEG reports half-year results on Thursday. — London Stock Exchange / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
UK earnings season crowds into one week — Barclays, Standard Chartered, Lloyds, BAE, LSEG, Drax, NatWest and Taylor Wimpey all report by Friday.

The FTSE 100's results calendar tips over into its busiest stretch of the summer this week, with five of the index's biggest banks and a handful of blue-chip industrials due to report inside four trading days. Barclays opens the account on Tuesday, followed by Standard Chartered on Wednesday, then a crowded Thursday that brings Lloyds Banking Group, BAE Systems, London Stock Exchange Group and Drax Group within hours of one another. NatWest and the housebuilder Taylor Wimpey close the week on Friday, alongside International Consolidated Airlines Group. Rentokil Initial is also due to report its interim numbers around the same time.

None of the figures exist yet — that is the point of a reporting week. What matters is what analysts are pricing in before the numbers land, because the gap between forecast and outcome is usually where the share-price moves are.

What the banks are expected to show

Consensus estimates gathered ahead of the results point to a stronger half than last year for Britain's biggest lenders. Barclays is forecast to post pre-tax profit of roughly £5.9 billion for the first six months of 2026, up from £5.2 billion a year earlier; Lloyds is expected around £4.1 billion, against £3.5 billion in the same period of 2025. The tailwind is familiar: interest rates that were expected to keep falling have instead stayed elevated, after the Israel–Iran conflict revived inflation fears and pushed the Bank of England into a more cautious stance — the same dynamic behind gilt yields sticking above 5% and the Fed's own hawkish tilt into its July decision.

The question is whether that tailwind survives contact with the loan book. KPMG's Steve Payne has flagged "at least some marginal, maybe slightly bigger than marginal" increases in bad-loan provisions as higher fuel and food costs squeeze household finances — a knock-on from oil prices that touched $100 a barrel after the Houthi strike on Aramco infrastructure. Interactive Investor's Richard Hunter puts it more bluntly: impairment charges, not headline profit, will set the tone for how investors read the results. A bank that beats on revenue but guides provisions higher is a different story than one beating on both.

Beyond the banks

The non-bank names in Thursday's cluster carry their own, separate questions. BAE Systems goes into its results with a record order backlog — north of £75 billion on its latest trading update — built on NATO rearmament spending, so the number to watch is conversion: how much of that backlog is turning into billed revenue rather than sitting on the books. LSEG's half-year update will test whether data-and-analytics subscription growth is offsetting slower trading volumes. Drax, meanwhile, reports into a UK power market still adjusting to the biomass giant's post-subsidy economics.

Strip out the noise and the week is really one story told five ways: whether elevated rates are still a net positive for corporate Britain, or whether the costs they impose — on borrowers, on energy bills, on shipping and insurance through the Gulf — are starting to bite harder than the benefits they deliver to lenders' margins. The first hard evidence arrives Tuesday with Barclays; Lloyds, BAE, LSEG and Drax follow together on Thursday.
