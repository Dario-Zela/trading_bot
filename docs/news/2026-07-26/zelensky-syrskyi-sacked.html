Zelensky sacks military chief Syrskyi after minister shake-up backlash

Oleksandr Syrskyi, pictured in 2024, was dismissed as Ukraine's commander-in-chief after days of street protests. — Press Service of the President of Ukraine / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Zelensky sacked commander-in-chief Syrskyi after protests over the earlier firing of defence minister Fedorov, naming 43-year-old Mykhailo Drapatyi to replace him.

Ukraine's president has now sacked two of his most senior officials in the space of a week, the second dismissal a direct consequence of public fury over the first. On July 21, Volodymyr Zelensky removed General Oleksandr Syrskyi as commander-in-chief of the armed forces, six days after firing his popular defence minister, Mykhailo Fedorov, triggered the largest street protests Ukraine has seen since the full-scale invasion began.

Syrskyi's replacement is Mykhailo Drapatyi, 43, previously commander of Ukraine's Joint Forces and, before that, the youngest officer ever to lead its Ground Forces. "There is a difficult task ahead, so we are not making loud promises," Drapatyi wrote in a Facebook post announcing his appointment. "We are working with responsibility for our decisions and for the people who hold the front line."

A minister's exit sparks the street

Zelensky fired Fedorov on July 15 as part of a wider cabinet reshuffle under the incoming Koretskyi government. Fedorov, a tech-focused reformer credited with expanding Ukraine's drone programme and jamming Russian access to Starlink terminals, had spent months in open conflict with Syrskyi's military establishment, which he accused of dragging its feet on procurement reform and resisting tech-driven changes to how the army fights. His removal was meant to quiet that fight. Instead, it set off six days of demonstrations in Kyiv and other cities, with crowds demanding Syrskyi's own removal rather than Fedorov's.

The general goes, again

Zelensky bowed to the pressure. Announcing Syrskyi's dismissal, he thanked the general for "Ukraine's results in the defense of Kyiv, the Kharkiv counteroffensive, and the Kursk operation," adding that he had already discussed a future service arrangement with him and the chief of the General Staff, Andrii Hnatov. It is the second time in under two and a half years that Zelensky has removed his top general under pressure: Syrskyi himself took the job in February 2024 after Zelensky dismissed the widely admired Valerii Zaluzhnyi. Where that move drew criticism for defying public opinion, this one is a direct response to it — Syrskyi had been faulted by critics for a centralised, Soviet-style command style at odds with the faster, tech-enabled war Fedorov was pushing.

The reshuffle is not fully resolved. Zelensky has since offered Fedorov a new post overseeing military innovation as a deputy prime minister, but Fedorov has said he will accept nothing short of his old ministry — leaving the government without the settlement it needs to move past the episode.

What it signals

Back-to-back removals of a defence minister and a commander-in-chief, forced by street pressure rather than battlefield failure, point to a level of domestic strain inside Ukraine's wartime leadership that had not been visible before. For allies weighing continued military and financial support, and for markets pricing war-risk premiums into energy and European equities, the question now is whether Drapatyi's appointment settles the argument over strategy or simply moves it to a younger general with less time to prove himself. His handling of the front through the autumn, and whether Fedorov can be brought back into government in some form, will be the first tests.
