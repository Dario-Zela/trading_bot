UK retail sales jump 1% in June, confounding recession fears

Oxford Street, London's principal shopping thoroughfare, where warm weather and promotions helped drive June's retail rebound. — Ysangkok / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
UK retail sales rose 1.0% in June against a forecast 0.3% fall, with online's share hitting a five-year high and the FTSE 100 rising on the news.

Retail sales in Britain rose 1.0% in June, the Office for National Statistics said, confounding a City consensus that had braced for a 0.3% decline. It is a swing of 1.3 percentage points from what forecasters expected, and it follows a 1.2% rise in May — meaning consumer spending has now beaten expectations for two consecutive months even as households continue to complain, in surveys at least, that money is tight.

The ONS attributed the bulk of the gain to warm weather and heavy promotional activity during what was the second-warmest June on record. Clothing stores had their best month since September 2025, with sales up 1.9%, while electric fans and paddling pools moved briskly as temperatures climbed. Year-on-year, sales volumes were up 4.2%, nearly double the 2.3% pace economists had pencilled in. Over the second quarter as a whole, retail volumes rose 0.6% against the first quarter — a modest but unambiguous improvement on a retail sector that spent much of 2025 flat on its back.

Online's five-year high

The more structurally interesting number sat inside the headline figure. Online sales climbed to 29.4% of total retail spending, the highest proportion since April 2021, when pandemic lockdowns had forced shopping onto the internet almost by decree. This time there was no lockdown forcing the shift — non-store retail value was up 14.4% year-on-year, more than three times the pace of the market as a whole, suggesting the move online has outlasted its pandemic origins and settled into something closer to a permanent reallocation of retail spend.

Helen Dickinson, chief executive of the British Retail Consortium, whose own retail monitor tracks a narrower measure of member sales, put it plainly: "Retail sales maintained momentum despite June's heatwave. While in-store sales were stifled by soaring temperatures, the proportion of sales online was the highest of 2026, bolstered by well-timed promotions." The BRC's figures, gathered separately from the ONS's official survey, showed total sales up a more modest 1.9% year-on-year — a reminder that the two measures capture different slices of the market, even when they point the same direction.

The data landed well with equity investors. The FTSE 100 added roughly 0.3% on the day, changing hands near 10,736, with the retail beat helping offset a jumpier backdrop of Middle Eastern oil-price volatility and simmering trade tensions. Andreas Lipkow, an independent market analyst, noted that "both the latest GfK Consumer Confidence Index and retail sales figures came in ahead of expectations," reinforcing a view that UK household spending — long assumed to be the weak link in growth forecasts — may be holding up rather better than the recession calls implied.

Sterling firmed too, with the pound pushing back above $1.33 as traders trimmed bets on near-term Bank of England rate cuts. That reaction matters more than the day's index move: two Monetary Policy Committee members, including chief economist Huw Pill, voted to raise rates to 4% at June's meeting rather than hold at 3.75%, arguing that services inflation near 3.7% risks becoming entrenched. A retail sector running hotter than forecast, layered on top of an oil-price shock from the Iran conflict, hands the committee's hawks more ammunition ahead of the Bank's next decision on 30 July — three days before the Federal Reserve's own hawkish-leaning meeting.

What happens next depends on whether June's strength was weather and promotions doing one-off work, or a genuine change in household behaviour. The Bank will have August's employment and inflation readings before it needs to decide.
