Cracker Barrel CEO steps down a year after rebrand backlash

A Cracker Barrel Old Country Store location in Bloomsburg, Pennsylvania. — Deansfa / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Cracker Barrel CEO Julie Masino exits 10 August, replaced by ex-Bloomin' Brands chief David Deno, a year after a $100m logo backlash.

Cracker Barrel's board has removed the chief executive who presided over its worst branding crisis in decades, ending Julie Masino's three-year tenure and installing a veteran restaurant-and-retail operator in her place.

The company said Monday that Masino will step down as CEO effective 10 August, staying on in an advisory capacity through 9 October to help hand over the business. David Deno, who ran Bloomin' Brands — the parent of Outback Steakhouse — from 2019 to 2024, will take over immediately. Deno also joins the board; Masino leaves hers.

"Following a robust and thoughtful search process, we are pleased to welcome David as Cracker Barrel's next CEO," said Carl Berquist, the board's independent chairman, who also thanked Masino for her "leadership and commitment" and her "partnership to ensure a smooth leadership transition." Neither the company's statement nor its regulatory filing said whether the exit was Masino's choice or the board's.

A logo that cost $100 million in a week

The context is hard to separate from the outcome. Last August, Cracker Barrel unveiled a stripped-down wordmark logo, dropping "Uncle Herschel" — the illustration of founder Dan Evins' relative sitting in a wicker chair against a barrel — that had anchored the brand since the 1970s. Masino later said the goal was a cleaner mark that would read better on highway billboards. Customers, and eventually Donald Trump, read it differently: as the chain abandoning the nostalgic, small-town identity its diners came for.

The backlash was fast and expensive. Within a week, roughly $100 million had come off the company's market value, and on 26 August 2025 Cracker Barrel reversed course, restoring the original mark. Two months later it cut ties with Prophet, the branding firm it had hired only seven months earlier to lead the redesign. In a November podcast appearance, Masino described the episode bluntly: "I feel like I've been fired by America."

Board pressure was already building

Masino's exit also lands against a longer-running fight over the company's governance. Sardar Biglari, the activist investor behind Biglari Holdings, has pushed for board influence at Cracker Barrel for years — this is his sixth run at a seat — and two directors with ties to his campaigns, Jody Bilney and Michael Goodwin, now sit on the board. Whether that pressure factored into the timing of Masino's departure is not something the company has addressed publicly, but a CEO change coming almost exactly a year after a rebrand that erased nine figures of value, delivered by a board that has spent years fending off an activist, reads less like routine succession than a board signaling it wants different judgment at the top.

Shares fell as much as 5% Monday on the news before paring losses to close down about 3% — a reaction that suggests investors see uncertainty in the change, not relief.

The new chief's brief

Deno brings more than four decades in restaurants and retail, including stints as chief financial officer at Bloomin' Brands, president of Best Buy's Asia business, and fifteen years at Yum! Brands and Pizza Hut. He currently sits on the boards of Krispy Kreme and Panera Brands. "Cracker Barrel is a truly iconic American brand, defined by its unique combination of warm country hospitality, timeless appeal and deep connection with guests across generations," Deno said in the announcement. "I am honored to lead the Cracker Barrel team and look forward to unlocking the full potential of this remarkable brand."

That language — hospitality, timeless appeal, heritage — reads as a deliberate contrast to the modernizing pitch that got Masino into trouble. Cracker Barrel has not disclosed same-store sales or traffic trends tied specifically to the rebrand fallout, so the scale of any lasting customer damage remains the open question for Deno to answer when the company next reports results. Watch for commentary on comparable sales and remodel plans on that call, and for whether Biglari uses the leadership vacuum to press his board case again before Cracker Barrel's next annual meeting.
