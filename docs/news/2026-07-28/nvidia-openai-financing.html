Nvidia in talks to guarantee $250bn OpenAI data-center financing

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, whose company is negotiating to guarantee financing for OpenAI's $500bn-plus Ohio data center project. — Government Open Data License – India / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Nvidia is negotiating to guarantee $250bn of financing for OpenAI's Ohio data center and fund up to $350bn of chip purchases for it.

Nvidia is in advanced talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion of financing for a single data center project being built for OpenAI in rural Ohio — and, separately, to help finance up to $350 billion of the chip purchases that will fill it. Put together, the two arrangements would see the world's most valuable company underwriting both the building that houses its customer's computers and the silicon running inside them.

The reporting, first from the Wall Street Journal and matched by Bloomberg on 26 July, describes a company moving well beyond its traditional role as a component supplier. Nvidia, market capitalisation around $5.1 trillion, is becoming something closer to a merchant bank for the AI buildout — one whose main customer is also, in effect, its own revenue stream.

The Ohio project

The facility at the centre of the talks is a 10-gigawatt data center campus that SoftBank's energy arm, SB Energy, is developing at the former Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant near Piketon, Ohio — a Cold War-era uranium enrichment site the federal government handed over for redevelopment. Site work broke ground in March 2026, backed by a public-private partnership involving the Department of Energy, the Department of Commerce and the Ohio utility AEP. The first phase, about 800 megawatts, is budgeted at $30–40 billion and due to come online in 2028; the full 10-gigawatt build, including a $33 billion gas-fired power plant to feed it, has been priced by SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son at more than $500 billion once the chips are counted.

What OpenAI needs from Nvidia is straightforward, if unusual: a credit rating it doesn't have. OpenAI is not investment-grade, and lenders have been unwilling to finance construction debt of this size against the company's own balance sheet. Nvidia's guarantee would let SoftBank and its lenders price the debt against Nvidia's creditworthiness instead — if OpenAI cannot make its lease payments, Nvidia covers the shortfall, OpenAI keeps use of the building, and Nvidia absorbs the loss. That guarantee, under discussion, covers the lease and construction financing but explicitly excludes the chips that will sit inside the data halls once it's built.

A second, larger number

That's where the separate $350 billion figure comes in. Nvidia is discussing financing OpenAI's purchases of the GPUs and networking gear that will actually occupy the Ohio campus — hardware Nvidia itself sells. Jim Chanos, the short-seller who built his reputation on Enron, framed the arithmetic bluntly on social media: Nvidia would be providing financing guarantees for roughly two-thirds of the cost of the chips it is selling into the project. Michael Burry, another prominent short-seller, was more caustic: "Around and around we go," he wrote, characterising it as Nvidia guaranteeing $200 billion-plus of its own customer's spending on Nvidia chips.

The terms in both talks remain preliminary and could still change or fall apart, according to the Journal's sourcing, and Nvidia has pushed back hard on the broader framing. In a seven-page memo to Wall Street analysts, the company said it does not engage in "vendor financing" — the practice, associated with telecom-bust casualties like Lucent, of lending to customers so they can afford your product. Nvidia noted that its customers typically settle invoices within 53 days and argued its business is "economically sound" with reporting that is "complete and transparent." Whether a $250 billion loan guarantee plus a $350 billion chip-financing facility constitutes something narrower than vendor financing is, for critics, a matter of definition rather than substance.

From chipmaker to financier

This is not Nvidia's first foray into funding its own demand. In September 2025 the company committed up to $100 billion to OpenAI progressively as gigawatts of Nvidia systems are deployed — one leg of a roughly $1 trillion web of interlocking deals across OpenAI, Oracle, SoftBank and others that has already drawn scrutiny for circularity. The Ohio guarantees would dwarf that commitment and extend the pattern from equity investment into direct credit backing, a materially different risk. An equity stake can go to zero; a financing guarantee is a liability that comes due.

The strategic logic is nonetheless coherent from Nvidia's side. By underwriting the capital structure of the largest dedicated AI campus under construction, Nvidia ensures the site is built, built quickly and built with Nvidia hardware rather than a rival's — AMD or in-house silicon from Google or Amazon. It also means that non-aligned developers competing for the same construction financing, land, power contracts and engineering talent are competing against a project effectively backstopped by a $5 trillion balance sheet. That is a formidable moat, and one regulators focused on AI market concentration are likely to notice, even if no formal review has been announced.

The risk sits on the other side of the ledger. Nvidia's quarterly revenue is large but not infinite next to $600 billion of combined exposure, and the arrangement ties the company's credit profile to the fortunes of a single customer that does not generate positive free cash flow today. If AI infrastructure demand or OpenAI's own revenue growth disappoints, Nvidia would not just lose a customer — it would be on the hook for the debt.

What comes next

Nothing here is signed. The Journal's sourcing describes both the $250 billion guarantee and the $350 billion chip-financing facility as live negotiations, not concluded deals, and either could be resized or abandoned before terms are finalised. Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter earnings in late August, where analysts are expected to press management directly on the structure of both facilities following the pushback memo. Watch also for SoftBank's own disclosures on the Ohio project's financing stack, and for whether other hyperscalers — Microsoft, Google, Amazon — respond with financing guarantees of their own rather than cede ground on the infrastructure land grab.
