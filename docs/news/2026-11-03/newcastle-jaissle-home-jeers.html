Jaissle faces growing home problems at Newcastle as jeers return

In one sentence
Newcastle's Matthias Jaissle has met boos at St James' Park after back-to-back defeats to Coventry and Aston Villa left the side 11th, with the crowd's patience already thinning.

Matthias Jaissle has been at Newcastle for little more than two months, and the St James' Park crowd has already started to let him know what it thinks. The jeers that followed the 1-0 defeat by Aston Villa in mid-October were the first sign that the honeymoon for the club's new head coach is over. BBC Sport reports they have returned as home form has soured.

The caveat is that the evidence available is thin. The record below runs to mid-October. A fixture list shows Newcastle hosting Everton on 2 November, but no result for that game could be confirmed, so the picture since the Villa loss is incomplete.

A good start, then two defeats

It began well enough. Jaissle's first home game, against Liverpool on 23 August, finished 2-2 after Dominik Szoboszlai scored a penalty in the ninth minute of stoppage time. Jaissle called the award "soft" and said it had cost him a winning debut. A 2-0 win over Tottenham followed, then a 1-1 draw with Bournemouth in which his side came from behind.

Then came two defeats in five days. Coventry City won 4-1 on 12 October. Five days later Villa won 1-0 at St James' Park, a first home loss of the season and the first of Jaissle's tenure. By one account, Villa should have led by more at half-time. Newcastle stayed 11th, and the boos followed the final whistle.

One listing puts the club on 8 points from seven games, 13 behind leaders Manchester City. That source could not be cross-checked, and a second table gave inconsistent figures, so the numbers are best treated as approximate.

Why the home crowd matters here

St James' Park has long been the club's main asset. Newcastle finished 12th last season, and 32 of their 49 points came at home. A team that cannot hold that ground is not going to climb the table. Supporters also know what a lively home crowd can deliver, which is part of why the patience runs short when it does not.

Reports on the Villa defeat questioned the 4-2-3-1 system Jaissle has favoured, and said patience was running thin. It is a familiar early-tenure complaint: a new coach's structure is judged against the results before the squad has fully adapted to it. Jaissle's reaction after the Villa game was conciliatory rather than defensive. He said he understood the supporters' frustration and that the players shared it.

The jeers have another edge. Newcastle fans have had a busy few months of emotion, not least the hostile reception for Alexander Isak on his return with Liverpool. Booing a departed striker is one thing. Booing your own side is a different, more worrying signal.

What comes next

No one is calling for Jaissle's job on this evidence, and two defeats do not make a crisis. The real test is the next run of home fixtures. Another dropped home match would turn the jeers from a mood into a pattern, and would give the board a decision to weigh. A win would quieten St James' Park quickly. It usually does.
