Apple's first foldable iPhone sells out, with no date for restocking

In one sentence
Apple's first foldable iPhone, the $1,999 iPhone Duo, sold out within minutes and Apple gives no restock date; reporting points to hinge and panel constraints more than a demand surprise.

Apple's first foldable iPhone has sold out, and the company has not said when more will be available. The iPhone Duo went on pre-order on 16 October at $1,999. Launch stock was gone within minutes. Delivery estimates for every colour and storage option moved to January almost immediately. Apple's US store was showing 25 January about half an hour after orders opened, according to reports.

The BBC reported that Apple cannot give a date for resupply. That leaves two possible explanations. Either buyers wanted more phones than Apple planned for, or Apple could not make as many as it hoped. The reporting so far points mostly to the second.

What happened at launch

The Duo was unveiled in September. It has a 5.4-inch outer screen that opens into a 7.6-inch inner display. Pre-orders opened at 8am New York time on Friday 16 October, and the phone went on sale on 23 October. Storage runs from 256GB to 2TB, and the top model costs $3,199, according to AppleInsider as cited by The Next Web.

Bloomberg reported that stock sold out at Apple's own stores almost as soon as pre-orders began. Apple normally holds some phones back for walk-in customers on launch day. This time it said only that it would have "limited" numbers in stores. Reports from launch day described queues turned away before the doors opened, and Apple reportedly told stores to warn walk-in customers off.

Apple has called demand "outstanding" but has not said how many phones it had to sell. It has also not said when supply will recover. The January dates are Apple's own delivery estimates for online orders, not a restocking commitment. They may also apply mainly to the US. I found no Apple statement promising a single shipping date across every market.

Demand or supply?

A sell-out on its own cannot tell the two explanations apart. Several things in the pre-launch reporting favour a supply problem.

  • The hinge. Analyst Jeff Pu cut his 2026 Duo shipment forecast from 7 million to 6 million in September. He blamed hinge constraints, not weak demand. Counterpoint Research, per Bloomberg, also expects about 6 million units this year.
  • The panel. Korea JoongAng Daily reports that Samsung Display is the sole supplier of foldable panels for the Duo. Chinese outlet Jiemian News, citing supply-chain sources, reported low yields, delayed deliveries and design changes at Samsung Display. It also reported that assembly yield at Foxconn sat just above 60%, partly because the panel delays left less time to tune final assembly. Apple has not commented on any of this.
  • Apple's own foreknowledge. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported that Apple knew supply would be severely constrained and opened pre-orders in more than 70 countries anyway. Analyst Ming-Chi Kuo had said in July that the phone could sell out immediately and leave long waits.

The unit numbers come largely from one analyst and anonymous supply-chain sources, so they are estimates. For scale, Pu's note put iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max shipments at about 12 times his Duo estimate. A phone planned at 6 million units is a small product by Apple's standards, and a small run can sell out quickly even if demand is only moderately strong.

That does not rule out strong demand. Foldables have been a niche part of the smartphone market, and an Apple entry may draw buyers who were waiting for it. But a sell-out that was expected in advance says little about whether the product is a hit. The better test is whether demand holds once supply catches up in 2027.

Who else is affected

The supplier angle matters beyond Apple. If Samsung Display is the only panel source, as reported, a yield problem there becomes an Apple problem. A strong Duo would also boost a company that already competes with Apple in phones.

Apple is also trying to move faster. John Ternus became chief executive on 1 September, and The Next Web reports he has been cutting management layers to ship products sooner. A first-generation product that cannot be bought for three months is an awkward test of that. Apple's shares rose 1.8% in pre-market trading on the day pre-orders opened, so investors read the sell-out as good news.

What to watch

The first signal is whether Apple's delivery estimates move beyond late January, or shorten. The second is Apple's next quarterly report, where it may say something about foldable supply. The third is any word from Samsung Display on panel yields. If estimates improve without new capacity being announced, the problem was a launch bottleneck. If they keep slipping, the Duo's supply chain is the limit on the whole foldable category, and demand may matter less than whether Apple can build enough phones.
