Trump says the US is receiving millions of barrels of Venezuelan oil

In one sentence
Trump says millions of Venezuelan barrels are reaching the US, but no volumes or dates back it, and crude is moving more on Iran than on Venezuelan supply.

President Trump said the United States is receiving "millions of barrels" of Venezuelan oil. The claim, as reported by Benzinga, came with no volumes, dates or vessel data. Whether it is a supply story depends on numbers it did not supply.

The claim is not implausible. It does not show anything new about the oil market either.

What the record shows

In January, Trump said Venezuela's interim government would hand the US between 30 million and 50 million barrels of "high quality, sanctioned oil", to be sold at market prices with the proceeds under his control. At the time, Venezuela's acting president, Delcy Rodríguez, pushed back on US claims to run the country. A German outlet noted that the actual sale price could fall below the world market price.

The flows since then are patchier than a single remark suggests. Venezuelan exports rose 14% in April to 1.23 million barrels a day, the highest in more than seven years, according to the trade reports found. In the second quarter, Citgo and Valero together accounted for 80% of the 534,000 barrels a day of Venezuelan crude reaching US shores. In August, a US Energy Department under secretary said about half of Venezuela's output, near 1.25 million barrels a day, was going to the US.

These sources are dated inconsistently, and one trade report describes imports dipping to just under 340,000 barrels a day. No official October or November import figure was found. The EIA's monthly import tables, published at month's end, are the test. "Millions of barrels" is a small number against that scale. At roughly 500,000 barrels a day, the US takes about 15 million barrels a month from Venezuela. Even the high end of the January pledge would amount to a few weeks of that flow, against US crude imports many times larger.

The price warning

Senator Adam Schiff warned of "another oil-price reversal". No benchmark, level or date was given, so the warning cannot be checked against a price series. Crude has been moving mainly on Iran, not Venezuela. Oil fell about 7% on hopes of a US-Iran deal, as covered elsewhere in this edition. On that evidence, Venezuelan barrels are a minor input to the price path Schiff describes.

What to watch

The claim can be settled by two data sets. The EIA's monthly figures for October imports from Venezuela will show whether volumes have risen or fallen since the summer. Tanker-tracking data for November loadings would show whether the barrels are arriving at a pace beyond the second-quarter average. Until either shows a clear step up, the remark is an assertion, not a supply shock.

The president's separate remark about protecting Elon Musk if SpaceX is threatened by Democrats is a political item. It is not a market one, and it is not covered here.
