Moyes says Grealish can still have an Everton future

In one sentence
Everton manager David Moyes says Jack Grealish can still have a future at the club, a squad-planning signal rather than a decision, with Barry's revival a side thread.

David Moyes has said Jack Grealish can still have a future at Everton. It is a signal about squad planning, not a decision, and the available detail stops there.

The Everton manager's comments, reported by BBC Sport, concern the winger's prospects at the club. The wording of the remarks, and whether Moyes was answering a direct question about Grealish's contract or his place in the side, has not been independently confirmed. This article therefore does not quote him.

What the comment does and doesn't say

Managers asked about a player's future tend to leave doors open, and "can still have a future" is a long way from "will be offered a deal". Nothing in the reporting available here suggests Moyes has fixed a course for Grealish, whether that means a new contract, an extension of his stay or a parting of the ways. The phrasing points to a manager keeping his options open rather than closing them.

Grealish arrived at Goodison Park as a high-profile addition, and his status shapes how Everton plan their wage bill and attacking options. The terms of his stay, including whether Everton hold any option to keep him, are the context that would turn this from a pleasantry into a story. They have not been verified, so they are not set out here.

Barry's revival

The second thread is Barry. The candidate summary says only that his revival is also in focus, which most plausibly means the forward is finding form after a quieter spell. That is an inference from the summary, not a confirmed account. Any detail on his goals, minutes or the manager's own assessment awaits the full BBC Sport report.

The two themes fit together. A forward rediscovering his touch gives Moyes more attacking choices, and that tends to sharpen questions about who else belongs in the squad, and on what terms.

What to watch

The clearest signal will be a concrete one: Grealish's starts and minutes in the coming league fixtures, and any word from the club on his contract situation. Until Moyes or Everton put a decision on the record, this remains a manager speaking warmly about a player, which is worth noting but not worth over-reading.
