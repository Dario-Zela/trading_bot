Royal Mail unveils its Christmas 2026 stamps

In one sentence
Royal Mail's Christmas 2026 stamps went on sale on 3 November, an annual release with no confirmed design details and little market consequence.

Royal Mail has put its Christmas 2026 stamps on sale, the latest entry in a calendar fixture that arrives every autumn. The issue was reported by BBC UK and appears in Royal Mail's own 2026 stamp calendar under today's date, 3 November.

The release is the only fact in the brief that has been confirmed. Published details of this year's designs, the artists and the denominations were not available when this piece was prepared, so none are described here. Anyone wanting the images will find them on royalmail.com, at Post Office branches and in the Royal Mail app, which is where last year's set was sold.

A fixture of the calendar

The Christmas issue is one of the most predictable events in British philately. Royal Mail publishes its special-issue programme a year ahead, and the Christmas stamps come last, in early November, in time for the card-writing season. The 2026 calendar sits alongside other issues this year, from Military Jet Aircraft to Football.

Last year's set offered a useful comparison. The 2025 issue ran to five stamps with Nativity-themed designs by Paula Doherty. Royal Mail also sold them in collectible formats, including a souvenir stamp sheet. The pattern is a small set of stamps for the cards and a range of keepsakes for collectors.

The practical date

More useful than the artwork may be the postal deadline. Royal Mail's Christmas page says last posting dates for 2026 are now available. A third-party site puts the last recommended second-class date at Friday 18 December. That has not been checked against Royal Mail's own guide, and anyone relying on it should consult the company's guidance first.

Why it is only a brief

There is little market consequence in a stamp launch. A fuller story would need figures that the release does not supply. They would cover Christmas parcel and letter volumes, the effect of falling letter traffic on the business, and the position of the company's owner, International Distribution Services, which is controlled by the Czech billionaire Daniel Kretinsky's EP Group. None of those is established by this announcement, and the brief makes no claim about them.

What to watch next is the posting deadlines. They fall in mid-December, and the company's own guide is the source to check.
