Carla Lockhart quits DUP over Drumcree row

In one sentence
Upper Bann MP Carla Lockhart's break with the DUP follows a row over Drumcree and Stormont, trimming the party's five Commons seats to four if she sits outside it.

Carla Lockhart, the MP for Upper Bann, has left the Democratic Unionist Party, according to the edition's reporting, after a fortnight of strain over the Drumcree parade dispute and the party's course at Stormont. The weeks before her exit are well documented. The final step is the part this piece could not independently confirm.

How the row built

Drumcree, on the edge of Portadown in her constituency, is the site of Northern Ireland's longest-running Orange Order parade dispute. Orangemen have been barred from walking the Garvaghy Road section of their traditional route since the late 1990s. This autumn the dispute flared again, with a rally in Portadown in support of the Drumcree Orangemen and renewed argument over what unionist politicians should do about it.

On 23 October Lockhart said she was stepping down from an internal DUP role. The disagreement was over whether the party should pull Emma Little-Pengelly, the deputy First Minister, out of the power-sharing executive. Lockhart argued that the DUP "can do no other than remove Michelle O'Neill" as First Minister. DUP party officers met on 29 October amid the fall-out.

By 30 October the leader, Gavin Robinson, was publicly saying he did not want her to go. "Well, I hope that's not the case," he told BBC Radio Ulster when asked about speculation she would leave. In a five-page letter to colleagues, Lockhart had called the DUP her "home", something she "greatly values". She said she would set out her path "early next week". The Irish News said a split looked increasingly likely after the Traditional Unionist Voice leader, Jim Allister, praised her.

What it does and does not change

The Westminster arithmetic is modest. The DUP won five Commons seats in 2024. If Lockhart keeps her seat but sits outside the party, its bloc falls to four. That is a loss of standing more than of votes: the DUP's MPs have not been decisive in a House where Labour holds a large majority.

The pressure falls mainly on Stormont and on unionism. Lockhart's argument, that the DUP should collapse or quit the executive over O'Neill's position, puts her to the party's right. Her path from here will show whether it is a one-person exit or the start of a drift towards the TUV. She may also face questions in Upper Bann over whether voters elected her as a DUP candidate. Nothing in the reporting so far suggests she intends to give up the seat.

What to watch

Two things will show how far this travels: whether other DUP representatives, particularly councillors in the Portadown area, follow her; and whether Robinson changes the party's line on the executive. Lockhart's own account of where she will sit in the Commons is the next marker.

Editor's note: reporting available to this desk confirmed the dispute, her resignation from a party post and the speculation up to 30 October. The final departure is as reported in the edition's source, BBC Politics, and should be checked against it.
