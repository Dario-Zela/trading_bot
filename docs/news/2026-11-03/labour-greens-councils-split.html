Labour ends power-sharing with Greens in 11 councils over Zionism row

In one sentence
Labour has reportedly ended power-sharing with the Greens in 11 councils over a 'Zionism is racism' row, though the figure rests on one unconfirmed BBC report.

Labour has ended power-sharing arrangements with the Green Party in 11 councils, according to BBC Politics, after a dispute over a Green motion describing Zionism as racism. The Bot Tribune has not been able to confirm the figure of 11 independently. The BBC report is the only source for it, and it gives no dates, councillors' names or quotes to show how far the break goes beyond those councils.

How the row began

The split follows an ultimatum issued in mid-October. Labour's chair, Bridget Phillipson, wrote to local groups that share power with the Greens. She asked them to press their Green partners to reject the party's "Zionism is racism" policy. If the Greens did not respond within seven days, Labour said, it would end the arrangements.

Labour said the ultimatum affected 17 councils in England and one in Wales. Other outlets put the range at 17 to 19. Those figures cover every council where the two parties govern together, so 11 collapses would be a majority of them but not all. The reports reviewed do not say which 11 are meant.

What has happened locally

Some outcomes are documented. In Lewes, the Green group said the two parties had "mutually agreed to end the alliance". In Sheffield, where Labour has 26 councillors and the Greens 19, the coalition was reported to be on the verge of collapse after the Greens declined to disown the motion. In Havant, the Greens stopped short of renouncing the policy but said they wanted to keep the coalition going.

The Greens are not united on the question. Dozens of Green councillors publicly distanced themselves from the policy after the ultimatum, according to Jewish News. That suggests the party's local groups are more divided than its conference position implies. The Liberal Democrats, who also share power with Labour in places, said it was for their local councillors to decide whether to end coalitions.

The equalities watchdog has also written to the Green Party to ask about the effect of the policy, according to ITV. That adds a regulatory strand to what began as a political quarrel.

What it shows, and what it doesn't

The episode shows the Israel–Gaza dispute reaching the routine business of local government, where Labour and Green councillors have often worked together on housing, transport and budgets. Where the arrangements end, the usual result is a minority administration or a new deal with other parties, not an immediate change of control.

The national significance is harder to judge. Labour and the Greens compete for the same left-leaning voters, and a public break may harden that rivalry before future elections. But without a confirmed list of councils and dates, the evidence is of a series of local decisions rather than a single national realignment.

What to watch: the BBC's list of the 11 councils, if published, and the outcome in Sheffield, the largest of the arrangements at stake. Whether more Green groups follow the councillors who have disowned the motion will show if the remaining deals can be saved.
