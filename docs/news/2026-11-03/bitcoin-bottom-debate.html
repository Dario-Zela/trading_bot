Bitcoin bulls say the bottom is in; bears eye $73,000

In one sentence
Crypto sentiment is split on whether bitcoin has bottomed, with Bloomberg's McGlone saying bitcoin and gold have peaked, but the debate lacks flow, positioning or dated price evidence.

Bitcoin's holders are arguing over whether the slide that has taken the token well below its highs has run its course. The bulls say the low is behind it. The bears point to $73,000 as a level to watch. The disagreement is mostly about sentiment, because neither camp has offered much hard evidence: no fund-flow figures, no positioning data and no price level tied to a date.

The bearish case

The most pointed bear is Mike McGlone, the Bloomberg Intelligence commodity strategist. He has argued that bitcoin and gold have both "blown their tops". That wording comes from the triage notes for this piece. It could not be matched to an original Bloomberg note or interview, so the attribution and date are unconfirmed.

The view fits McGlone's earlier calls. In January 2025 he told Kitco that 2024 had been bitcoin's peak, and he has since cast the token as a "highly speculative asset" that tends to lead risk assets lower. Coverage of his recent commentary, mostly on crypto news sites rather than from Bloomberg itself, singles out two levels. One is the mid-$70,000 region, which bitcoin would need to hold. The other is $64,000, and a decisive break below it would strengthen his case. One aggregator page showed bitcoin near $64,000 when the search was run. That figure comes from a price widget and may not reflect the time of publication, so it should be treated cautiously.

No source found ties the $73,000 figure to a specific analyst, date or chart basis. As a bear target it is plausible, since it sits in the zone McGlone describes, but it is unsourced here.

The bullish case

The bulls' argument is thinner still. The claim that the bottom is in rests on the length of the drawdown rather than on a catalyst. No flow data, such as spot exchange-traded fund inflows, or derivatives positioning was found to support it. Without those, "the bottom is in" is a statement of conviction rather than something a reader can check.

What the call is worth

Two things limit the usefulness of McGlone's call. First, he has been bearish on bitcoin for a long time. A forecast that has been repeated for years carries less new information each time it is made. Second, the claim that gold has also peaked is the more consequential one. If both assets have topped, it would suggest the weakness reflects a wider shift in appetite for non-yielding stores of value, not a crypto-specific problem. That claim needs gold-side evidence, and none was offered.

The original timing hook has also expired. The warning was framed around the period before the US midterm elections, which are held today, 3 November. Whatever weight that framing carried goes with the vote.

What to watch

The test is price. A hold above the mid-$70,000s would favour the bulls, and a close below $64,000 would back McGlone. Beyond the level itself, ETF flow data and the bitcoin-to-gold ratio, which one report put near 20, would show whether the two assets are moving together as the bear case implies.
