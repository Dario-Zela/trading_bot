Djokovic vomits through five-set US Open exit as Norrie falls, Samuel wins first Slam match

Novak Djokovic, whose first-round US Open exit to Mariano Navone was his earliest Grand Slam departure in two decades. — Peter Menzel / Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Djokovic vomited repeatedly in a five-set first-round US Open loss to Mariano Navone, his earliest Slam exit in 20 years, as Norrie fell and Toby Samuel won his first Grand Slam match.

Novak Djokovic has lost matches at the US Open before — semi-finals, finals, the odd upset in the second week. He had never lost in the first round. Not in twenty appearances, not as a teenager, not deep into his late thirties with a body that increasingly announces its own limits before his opponents do. On Sunday night in Flushing Meadows, that streak ended in the ugliest way available: a four-hour, thirty-six-minute collapse against Argentina's Mariano Navone, punctuated by repeated vomiting on court and, by the final set, visible tears.

The scoreline — 7-6(5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1 — tells only part of it. Djokovic won the opening set in a tiebreak and looked, briefly, like the player who has spent two decades making these draws look routine. Then his body started to fail him. He was doubled over between points, sick beside the baseline more than once, and visibly cramping as Navone, ranked outside the world's top 60, worked out that he no longer needed to out-hit the 39-year-old — just outlast him.

"It's something that I've been carrying, unfortunately, pretty much every match this year — vomiting, throwing up, a serious issue there," Djokovic said afterwards. "Then my whole body starts to collapse, basically cramping and pain." It is not a new complaint from him this season, but rarely has it played out this visibly, or cost him this much: his earliest exit at any major in twenty years, since a fourth-round loss at the 2006 Australian Open.

A rough day for British hopes, and one bright spot

Djokovic's exit was the marquee story of the day, but two British campaigns turned on similarly compact margins elsewhere on site. Cameron Norrie, the 29th seed, lost in four sets to Frenchman Luca Van Assche, 7-6(6), 2-6, 2-6, 3-6, racking up 49 unforced errors to Van Assche's 21. Norrie was candid about the reason: "I was pretty nervous and it showed in the beginning of the first set," he said, adding he was never able to sustain the level or momentum that had carried him to steal that opener.

The rare good news for the home nation came from Toby Samuel. The 23-year-old qualifier beat Czech player Tomas Machac 6-2, 6-3, 6-2 in under ninety minutes to claim his first Grand Slam main-draw win — a tidy, unfussy result that stood in sharp contrast to the drama unfolding elsewhere in the draw.

The thread that ties it together

Three results, three very different stories, but one theme running underneath: the gap between seeding and outcome on the first Sunday of a major is often decided by something less visible than a serve or a return — conditioning, nerves, composure under a five-set clock. Navone did nothing spectacular; he simply kept the ball in play long enough for Djokovic's body to run out of road. Van Assche did the same to Norrie's nerve. Samuel, with nothing to lose and no reputation to defend, played some of the freest tennis of the day.

There is no market angle here, no signal for anyone trading on anything other than results. It is, simply, a tournament reminding everyone early that seeding is a prediction, not a guarantee — and that even a 24-time major champion is still, eventually, subject to the clock and his own stomach.

Djokovic said he will assess his condition before deciding on the rest of his season; his team is expected to give an update in the coming days. For Norrie, the next chance to reset comes on the ATP's autumn hard-court swing. For Samuel, the second round — and a considerably higher-profile opponent — arrives within 48 hours.
