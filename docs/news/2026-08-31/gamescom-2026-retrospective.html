Gamescom 2026: what stood out from Europe's biggest games showcase

The Koelnmesse exhibition centre in Cologne, home to Gamescom since 2009. — Holek / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)
In one sentence
Gamescom 2026 drew record crowds to Cologne but its biggest reveals were remakes and remasters, not new ideas.

Cologne's Koelnmesse sold out its exhibition floor for the first time in Gamescom's history this year — 233,000 square metres, 1,743 companies from 66 countries, and a crowd of 368,000 that fell just short of the pre-pandemic record. By every measure of footfall and spectacle, Europe's biggest games showcase had a good week. By the measure of what was actually announced, it had a quieter one.

Opening Night Live, the two-and-a-half-hour trailer marathon hosted by Geoff Keighley that now serves as the show's de facto keynote, ran through more than 50 reveals on August 25 to a claimed 78 million views. But scan the list of what actually got the biggest reactions and a pattern emerges: The Witcher 3 is getting remastered again, this time with a new expansion called Songs of the Past; Ubisoft is rebuilding Heroes of Might and Magic III in 3D and 4K; Xbox's marquee console exclusive, Gears of War: E-Day, is a prequel set before the original 2006 game. Even Square Enix's headline moment — a playable build of Final Fantasy VII Revelation — is the closing chapter of a remake trilogy of a 1997 game, not a new property.

The remake economy

None of this is accidental. Remasters and remakes carry lower development risk than new intellectual property, and in a games industry still working through the layoffs and cancelled projects that followed the post-pandemic spending slowdown, risk-aversion from the largest publishers has become the default rather than the exception. Gamescom 2026 mostly confirmed the trend rather than reversing it: familiar franchises, familiar engines, incremental visual upgrades sold as events. Where genuinely new ideas showed up, it was mostly from smaller studios — Swedish developer Neon Giant's cyberpunk action title No Law drew notice on the show floor precisely because it stood out amid the sequels and re-releases.

The hardware side told a similar story of iteration over invention. Samsung used its Gamescom slot to unveil a 2027 Odyssey monitor lineup and a frame-generation technology it calls Neural Frame Fusion, aimed at squeezing more performance out of existing chips rather than introducing anything categorically new. Useful engineering, but not the kind of announcement that reshapes a product roadmap.

Why it barely registers here

None of this moved any share price, and none of it was expected to. Gamescom is a consumer and press event, not an earnings call, and the publishers with the most at Cologne this year — Microsoft, Ubisoft, Square Enix — treated it as marketing rather than guidance. The nearest thing to a read-through for investors is the confirmation, again, that the industry's biggest names are leaning on catalogue rather than creativity to fill release calendars, a pattern that shows up eventually in engagement numbers and live-service revenue rather than in a single trade-show trailer.

The next test comes at each publisher's own results: Ubisoft's next quarterly update will show whether Heroes of Might and Magic III nostalgia translates into pre-orders, and Xbox's holiday slate — anchored by Gears of War: E-Day on October 6 — will be the first real signal of whether a prequel can do the commercial work a new franchise used to.
