Man City, Hull City perfect after two Premier League rounds

The Etihad Stadium, where Manchester City opened their title defence with a stoppage-time win over Bournemouth. — Cléria De Souza / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)
In one sentence
Man City and promoted Hull City have won both their opening Premier League games, but two-match records tell a 38-game season almost nothing.

Two matches into the 2026-27 Premier League season, exactly two clubs have won both their games: Manchester City, champions in six of the past eight campaigns, and Hull City, back in the top flight for the first time since 2016-17. One of those facts is unremarkable. The other made Michael Carrick's Manchester United sit through a very long train journey home.

Hull's win over United on the opening weekend was not a fluke of scheduling. Semi Ajayi headed in from a corner after 17 minutes, Nobel Mendy doubled it before half-time, and United — beaten twice from set pieces in the same afternoon — never laid a glove on the game after that. A week later, Hull followed it up away at fellow-promoted Coventry City, where substitute Liam Millar settled a stubborn 1-0 win with a goal from inside his own half of the pitch, despite Coventry out-shooting them on expected goals, 1.28 to 0.73.

City's route to two wins from two has been less startling but, this being Enzo Maresca's first fortnight since replacing Pep Guardiola, not without drama. Marcus Tavernier had Bournemouth level with 84 minutes gone at the Etihad before Josko Gvardiol's stoppage-time strike, initially flagged offside and restored by VAR, won the opener 2-1. Away at Crystal Palace four days later, City were more comfortable: Erling Haaland scored either side of a Rayan Cherki brace, with an own goal off Gianluigi Donnarumma's back the only blemish, for a 4-1 win.

Two games, no signal

None of this tells anyone much. A 38-match season generates its table from roughly 1,140 individual goal events; two rounds supply about 60 of them, distributed across 20 clubs. City's squad depth and Haaland's finishing make their perfect start plausible on priors alone. Hull's does not — newly promoted sides have opened a Premier League season with two wins only a handful of times, and the base rate for staying unbeaten through August is not the base rate for staying unbeaten through May. Millar's 55-yard sprint from his own half and Ajayi's set-piece header were separated by wide margins on expected-goals models in both matches; two-legged variance of that size resolves itself with more data, not with more narrative.

Elsewhere, Sunderland provided the weekend's other headline, beating Fulham 1-0 at the Stadium of Light after substitute Wilson Isidor struck the post and then converted from Habib Diarra's cross with 15 minutes left. Fulham, playing their second match under new manager Álvaro Arbeloa, failed to register a single shot on target and now have two league defeats from two. Whether that is a pattern or a small sample dressed up as one is, again, a question for October's table rather than August's.

The next data point worth watching is the international break in early September, after which sides return to a run of fixtures dense enough — six matches by mid-October — to start separating form from noise.
