White House says Trump, Putin and Xi will meet in China next week on nuclear arms control

In one sentence
The White House says Trump, Putin and Xi will meet in China next week on nuclear arms control, a first for the three powers, though US officials do not expect a deal.

The White House says Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping will meet in China next week to discuss nuclear arms control. If the meeting goes ahead, it will be the first time the leaders of the three largest nuclear-armed powers have sat down together on the subject. Arms control has been negotiated almost entirely between Washington and Moscow, in pairs, since the Cold War.

Most of the detail so far comes from reports of the White House announcement. Those reports place the talks in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, and one gives Thursday as the day. The Jerusalem Post, which reported the confirmation, did not specify a date or time. Beijing and the Kremlin's own accounts of the arrangements should be treated as the test of whether the summit takes place as described.

A framework that lapsed

The summit follows the end of the last treaty limiting US and Russian strategic arsenals. New START, signed in 2010, expired in February. It was the final surviving pact of its kind, and it left no replacement in force. Trump has called repeatedly for a new nuclear agreement, and in February 2025 he said he wanted talks with both Moscow and Beijing on cutting stockpiles.

The case for including China is that its arsenal is growing, and a bilateral US-Russia deal would leave the third major power outside any limits. Beijing has long refused to join, arguing that its stockpile is far smaller than the two largest and that cuts should start with them. A seat at the table is not an agreement to be bound, and the gap between the two is where the summit will be judged.

What is on the table

Reports say Trump plans to raise a possible reduction in global nuclear stockpiles. The Jerusalem Post also reported that the war involving Iran is expected to be discussed.

Senior US officials have been cautious. They have said the administration does not expect the first meeting to produce an arms control agreement. They describe it as something that might begin a process. That is a modest framing for an event billed as unprecedented, and it suggests the immediate value is procedural: agreeing that a three-way channel exists, who staffs it, and what it would cover. Whether it covers warhead numbers, delivery systems, testing or something narrower will show how serious the exercise is.

The context is not friendly. Putin has instructed his government to assess the feasibility of possible nuclear weapons tests. That is a step in the opposite direction from limits. The meeting would also be Trump's first in-person encounter with Putin since their Alaska summit in August 2025. Putin has meanwhile been hardening his terms for peace in Ukraine, which is likely to colour any discussion with Washington.

What to watch

The first question is whether the meeting happens as announced, with all three leaders present. The second is the readout. A joint statement, or an agreement to set up working groups of officials, would indicate substance. Separate national accounts that differ on what was discussed would suggest a photo opportunity. Allied capitals and defence planners will also look for any sign that Europe's and Asia's concerns are reflected in talks on which they have no seat.
