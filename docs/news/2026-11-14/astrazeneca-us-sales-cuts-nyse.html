AstraZeneca reportedly cuts 600 US sales roles ahead of its move to a New York primary listing

In one sentence
Reports of 600 AstraZeneca US sales cuts are unconfirmed, and the company said its New York listing would leave London as its primary venue.

AstraZeneca is reported to be cutting about 600 sales roles in the United States. The reports come from retail-investor and aggregator sites, not from the company. The framing that links the cuts to a move of its primary listing from London to New York does not hold up against the public record.

What is reported, and what is not

The job-cut figure comes from Stocktwits and Simply Wall St. Neither is a primary source. A search of company releases and trade press found no AstraZeneca statement, no filing and no report from an established outlet confirming the number. Nor is there any stated savings target or reinvestment figure. The claim that the cuts "fund the pipeline" is therefore the aggregators' gloss, not a company explanation.

The company has done this before. It cut about 1,150 US sales and leadership positions in 2011 and 700 US commercial roles in 2016, both times citing patent expiries on older drugs. Trimming a US field force is a recurring part of how a large drugmaker manages its cost base, and 600 roles would be modest against a company of AstraZeneca's size.

The listing is a different story

The listing claim is where the brief is weakest. In late September 2025 AstraZeneca announced it would replace its American depositary receipts with a direct listing of its ordinary shares on the New York Stock Exchange, with trading expected to begin on 2 February 2026. It said the change would not affect its primary listing in London or its UK headquarters, and the London Stock Exchange said the company's FTSE 100 place was unchanged.

The move was widely read as a knock-back for London. It was not, on the company's own terms, an exit. The distinction matters. A secondary listing that replaces ADRs makes the shares easier for US investors to buy and widens the pool of capital. It does not shift the company's primary venue, its index membership or its tax residence. This reporting could not confirm any later change to that plan, so the premise that London is losing a primary listing remains unsupported.

What the two stories do and do not share

Both items point in the same direction. AstraZeneca earns a large share of its revenue in the US, and it has leaned toward American investors and American commercial spending. That is a fair reading of the strategy. It is not evidence that the job cuts and the listing were planned together, and nothing in the available record links them.

What to watch

Two things would settle the matter. The first is a confirmation or denial from AstraZeneca of the 600 figure, most likely through a US media statement or a restructuring note in its next results. The second is the company's own filings: any proposal to change its primary listing would need shareholder approval and a regulatory filing, and none has been reported. Until then, this is an unconfirmed job-cut report attached to a listing story that has been overstated.
