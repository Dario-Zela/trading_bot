Goalkeeper in fatal collision given 12-game ban

In one sentence
Spanish football's disciplinary ban of 12 matches, the Article 102 maximum, follows the on-pitch death of Tenerife's Jesús de Miguel; the ruling's reasoning on fault is not yet confirmed.

A goalkeeper has been banned for 12 matches after a collision in which an opposing player died. The sentence is the longest Spanish football's rulebook allows for a violent act on the pitch that causes serious consequences.

The case is that of Jesús Owono, the FC Andorra goalkeeper, and Jesús de Miguel, the Tenerife forward. The two met late in the Segunda División match at Tenerife's Heliodoro Rodríguez López stadium on 2 November. The disciplinary report, as quoted in Spanish coverage, says Owono was sent off in the 90th minute for "impacting sharply with his leg raised on an opponent with the use of excessive force when the ball was in dispute". De Miguel lay unconscious on the pitch and died in hospital the following morning.

The match and the rule

The referee, Manuel Ángel Pérez Hernández, stopped play in the 96th minute because the player's condition had not improved and sent both teams to the dressing rooms. The match was recorded as abandoned by agreement of all parties. The competition's sole judge later accepted the 2-2 scoreline from just before the tragedy, so the game will not be resumed. He called ending it the most respectful option, given the family and the players involved.

The sanction rests on Article 102 of the Spanish federation's disciplinary code. It covers violent acts against an opponent that cause serious or harmful consequences, and it carries a suspension of four to 12 matches. A 12-game ban is therefore the ceiling, not an innovation. The article ties the penalty to the consequence of the act as well as to the act itself, and that is what makes this case unusual. A red-card challenge that would ordinarily bring a handful of games sits at the top of the range because of how it ended.

What the ban does and does not decide

Whether the ruling sets a precedent depends on its reasoning, and that text could not be checked for this report. Coverage available in the days before the ban was reported described the committee as having deferred its decision. Both clubs still had time to submit arguments and evidence. A Portuguese outlet's reference to a longer suspension was not supported by the text retrieved. The ban's length is taken here from the reported ruling. Its stated reasoning on fault could not be confirmed.

The point is more than technical. Football discipline normally asks whether a player acted recklessly or with excessive force. Article 102 lets the outcome raise the penalty. If the committee has said that a goalkeeper committing to a ball in dispute can still be sanctioned at the maximum, defenders and keepers across the Spanish leagues will read it closely. If it has said only that the maximum applies to the worst consequence, the ruling will matter little outside this case.

The sporting sanction is also separate from any legal one. Spain's national police have opened an investigation into the circumstances of De Miguel's death, and it is not yet known whether criminal provisions will apply. Neither club was expected to challenge the result or any disciplinary decision, given the mourning.

What next

The next test is the published reasoning, and any appeal. Owono has the right to challenge the sanction before the federation's appeals body. The police inquiry will determine whether the question of fault moves from the disciplinary committee to a court.
