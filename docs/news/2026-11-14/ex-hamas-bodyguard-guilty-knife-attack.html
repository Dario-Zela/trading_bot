Former Hamas bodyguard found guilty of UK knife attack

In one sentence
A Birmingham jury convicted a former Hamas bodyguard of wounding a bailiff, without hearing of his militant past or the separate German weapons-plot case behind his extradition fight.

A jury at Birmingham Crown Court has convicted a 40-year-old man of wounding a bailiff with a knife. The jury was not told that he is a former bodyguard to Hamas leaders. Mohammed Alfatih, who also uses the name Mohammed Naim, was found guilty by a majority verdict on Friday after more than six hours of deliberation, according to reports of the case. He is due to be sentenced on 11 January.

The attack

The offence itself was a domestic-scale dispute. In October 2025 a bailiff called at Alfatih's home over unpaid parking fines owed by his wife. The encounter ended with the bailiff stabbed outside the property. One report says Alfatih was also convicted of two counts of unlawful knife possession. The other accounts mention only the wounding charge, so that detail is not settled.

On the reporting available, nothing in the prosecution case treated the attack as terrorism. It was tried as an ordinary violent offence.

The Hamas question

The militant link comes from outside the courtroom. Alfatih was born in Erlangen, Germany, and grew up in Gaza. He is reported to have worked as a bodyguard for Hamas leadership in the 2000s, and one account names Ismail Haniyeh as the figure he protected. That service has not been tested in a court. He later gained asylum in Britain and became a British citizen in 2020.

The jury, reports say, heard nothing of this, nor of a separate German investigation. That is standard practice. Evidence of a defendant's unrelated background is normally kept from jurors unless it bears on the charge, because it risks prejudicing them. The Hamas history appears to have had no role in the verdict.

A second case in Germany

The more serious matter lies abroad. German prosecutors want Alfatih over allegations that he helped move firearms from Berlin to Vienna as part of attacks planned around the second anniversary of the 7 October 2023 Hamas assault on Israel. Hamas has previously denied links to the suspects arrested in Germany over the alleged plot. Alfatih also faces extradition proceedings in London. The German allegations are unproven.

That is why the story draws attention in the UK. The conviction concerns a bailiff and a parking dispute. The extradition case concerns an alleged European weapons-trafficking plot. The two have so far run on separate tracks.

What to watch

The sentencing hearing on 11 January is the next fixed date, and the judge's remarks may show whether the court took any wider view of the offence. The extradition proceedings will test the German evidence. It would also be worth knowing how a man with a reported Hamas past came to be granted asylum and citizenship, but nothing in the available reporting answers that.
