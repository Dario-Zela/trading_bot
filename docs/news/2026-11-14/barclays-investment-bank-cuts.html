Barclays reportedly cutting about 200 investment banking jobs in New York and London

In one sentence
Barclays is reportedly trimming about 200 investment banking jobs in New York and London, an unconfirmed, modest cut that signals cost discipline rather than a strategic shift.

Barclays is reportedly preparing to cut about 200 jobs in its investment bank, with the roles split between New York and London, according to a report carried by Investing.com. The bank has not confirmed the figure in the material available, and no second outlet could be found corroborating this particular report. The detail should be read as unconfirmed.

If accurate, the number is small. The cuts would fall in the two cities where the bank's trading and advisory staff are concentrated, and they would amount to a trim to the division rather than a restructuring of it.

What is reported

The core claims are few. Barclays, a FTSE 100 lender, is said to be removing roughly 200 investment banking positions. New York and London are named as the locations. The report does not, in the material provided, say which desks or seniority bands are affected, what the timing is, or what the saving would be. Those omissions limit how much can be drawn from it.

A familiar pattern

The reported cut would not be the first of its kind at Barclays. In June 2025, Bloomberg reported that the bank was preparing to cut more than 200 investment bank jobs as part of chief executive C.S. Venkatakrishnan's drive to lift the division's profitability. That round was reported to touch investment banking, research and global markets, and to lean towards expensive managing directors. Barclays declined to comment on names at the time.

The similarity of the figures suggests that periodic headcount trims are part of how the bank manages the division's costs. It is not evidence that the two reports describe the same event, and the current one should not be treated as a repeat of the earlier one without confirmation.

Why it matters, and why not much

For staff and for rivals watching cost discipline across the big investment banks, the report is a data point. For shareholders, the question is whether the investment bank can keep returns up while revenues from trading and deal-making move with the cycle. A trim of about 200 roles is a modest lever for that purpose. Set against a division employing thousands, it changes little about the bank's overall strategy.

It would take further cuts, a stated savings target or a change in the bank's returns guidance to turn this into something larger. None of those is reported here.

What to watch

The first test is whether Barclays or a second outlet confirms the number and the locations. The second is whether the bank, at its next results or investor update, says anything about investment bank costs or headcount. Without either, the report stays a single, unconfirmed line about a small trim.
