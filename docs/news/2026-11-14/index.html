WORLD · UK-EU BORDER · By C. Tanaka

UK and EU agree airport-style checks at Eurotunnel to avoid border queues

Britain and France are negotiating a deal to run passport checks for Channel Tunnel passengers in a single juxtaposed booth at the terminal, so that the EU's new biometric Entry/Exit System does not create queues at the crossing. The EES began operating on 12 October 2025, with a phased rollout to full operation by April 2026, and it requires biometric registration at the border. Juxtaposed controls, where one country's officers check travellers on the other's soil, have been used at Folkestone and Coquelles since the early 2000s under the Le Touquet and Sangatte arrangements. Le Shuttle, which carries cars and coaches between the two sites, is a single high-volume checkpoint where delays build quickly. Hauliers, Eurotunnel passengers and the Kent economy have the most at stake. The test is whether the two governments can cut biometric-check delays without reopening the wider post-Brexit border question. What to watch is whether the arrangement is concluded and whether it holds up at peak traffic.

Heat pumps: how much heat does your home really need?

Heat pump installs often go wrong at the sizing stage. Under the MCS (Microgeneration Certification Scheme) installer standard, a unit should be sized from a room-by-room heat-loss calculation, not from the boiler it replaces. That calculation estimates how much heat each room loses on a cold day, and the total sets the output. An oversized pump cycles on and off, losing efficiency; an undersized one leaves rooms cold. Homeowners weighing an install under UK decarbonisation policy should ask to see the calculation before signing, and check current grant terms, which are revised periodically.
