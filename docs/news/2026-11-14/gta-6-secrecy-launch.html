Even GTA's original creators haven't played GTA 6, five days before launch

In one sentence
Even Dave Jones, who led the team behind the original GTA, reportedly hasn't played GTA 6, showing how tight Rockstar's secrecy is as Take-Two's $8bn forecast rides on 19 November.

Five days before the most anticipated entertainment launch of the year, the people who started the franchise are in the same position as everyone else: waiting. Dave Jones, who led the Dundee team that made the original Grand Theft Auto in 1997, and Brian Baglow, who wrote for it, have not played Grand Theft Auto VI, according to a report that draws on a BBC interview. Neither has been offered anything resembling preferential access. "We're all waiting to see what they've done," Jones is quoted as saying.

The detail is small, but it carries weight. GTA VI arrives on 19 November on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, after Rockstar Games and its parent, Take-Two Interactive, pushed it back from 26 May. If the franchise's own founders are outside the tent, the secrecy is not a marketing posture. It is a policy.

A note on sourcing: the BBC original could not be retrieved for this article. The Jones and Baglow account is taken from a same-day reprint that cites the interview, and exact wording should be checked against the BBC text.

From Dundee to the vault

The first game came out of DMA Design, a Dundee studio better known at the time for Lemmings. DMA was later renamed Rockstar North and became the engine room of a series that has grown into one of the largest properties in entertainment. GTA V has sold more than 200 million copies, which makes it a pillar of Take-Two's earnings.

The founders' exclusion fits a pattern. Dan Houser, the Rockstar co-founder who left the company, has said he has not watched the game's reveal material and will "try and avoid it", partly to keep his own new work uncluttered. Former Rockstar staff have also described strict non-disclosure agreements. Rockstar, for its part, has released very little: no hands-on previews, and a marketing push that has leaned on trailers rather than access. The gaps between its announcements have become a kind of content in themselves.

The scarcity has a cost. When a studio says almost nothing, leaks fill the space. Last year's unauthorised material prompted the company to pursue the people sharing it, and fans described the episode as a nightmare scenario for the game.

What the money assumes

For investors the question is whether mystique can carry a record launch. Take-Two's 9 November results kept its fiscal 2027 forecast at $8 billion to $8.2 billion in net bookings, a range unchanged since August. Second-quarter bookings came in slightly below the company's own range. Management said the launch date holds and that pre-loads are rolling out worldwide. Analysts have estimated that GTA VI contributes roughly $3 billion to $4 billion of that year's bookings, so a slip beyond the fiscal year would force a cut of that size.

With no preview coverage, buyers are working from trailers and the franchise's reputation, not from independent hands-on impressions. That has worked for Rockstar before, since GTA V sold on reputation too. But the stakes are higher now. The forecast rests on a single title arriving on schedule and landing well on day one.

What to watch

The first test is the launch itself: server stability, the state of the game at release, and how reviews read when they land. The second is the commercial run-rate, which Take-Two will address when it reports next, covering the quarter that includes the launch. Jones and Baglow, meanwhile, will presumably queue like everyone else.
