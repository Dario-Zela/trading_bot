Chelsea women's striker problem eased by a returning scout

In one sentence
Melvine Malard's six goals in six games explain Chelsea's form, but the evidence points to one signing by a manager who knew her, not a new scouting process.

Chelsea's attack is scoring freely again this season. The BBC credits one signing, Melvine Malard, and a relationship that goes back twelve years, not a change in how the club recruits. The headline calls Sonia Bompastor a "scout". The article does not. It makes this a story about one player's fit, not a repeatable scouting process.

The reunion

In 2014, Malard was 14 and had travelled from Réunion Island to a tournament at Clairefontaine, France's national football academy. Bompastor, then Lyon's head of youth development, spotted her there and persuaded her to move to mainland France. She phoned Malard's mother to say she could be trusted "as a manager but also as a woman", according to the BBC's Adam Millington and Jo Currie.

The move was not easy. Lyon is some 6,000 miles from Réunion, and its winters are far colder. Malard wanted to leave early. She stayed, she told the BBC, because chances like Lyon's come "maybe just one time in your life". Bompastor also kept an eye on her schooling. Malard reached Lyon's first team at 17, in 2017, and started the 2022 Champions League final, a 3-1 win over Barcelona under Bompastor.

Bompastor is now Chelsea's manager, and Malard, 26, joined from Manchester United in July. The BBC says she was filling the gap left by Sam Kerr's departure. It gives no details of Kerr's exit.

What the numbers show

The data backs the claim that Malard is working. She has six WSL goals in six appearances, 341 minutes in all. That equals her tally for the whole of last season at United, which took 17 matches and 1,206 minutes.

The underlying figures moved too, per 90 minutes and against her United season:

  • Shots rose from 3.4 to 5.4.
  • Touches in the opposition penalty area rose from 6.2 to 11.8.
  • Expected goals more than doubled, from 0.5 to 1.1.
  • Shot conversion rose from 10.5% to 22.2%.

The expected-goals rise suggests she is getting better chances, not just finishing better. A conversion rate of 22.2% over 341 minutes is a small sample and could settle lower.

A process or a player?

The causal claim is a stretch. The BBC piece describes no scouting process behind the Chelsea move, and it mentions no fee or recruitment structure. The only scouting in it is Bompastor noticing a teenager in 2014. A manager signing a forward she knows well is a sound decision, but it is not a method other clubs can copy.

The wider picture also fits a squad effect. Chelsea have had 13 different WSL scorers this season, and Malard's six is the most. After nine games they have the league's best attack and its meanest defence. That follows a poor year by their standards: only the League Cup last season, third in the WSL, and an end to six straight titles. A home draw with Everton let Manchester City go top.

What's next

Chelsea host City at Stamford Bridge on Saturday, live on BBC One. It will test whether Malard's form holds against the side now above them. Bompastor has said she wants Malard to be France's number nine at next summer's World Cup.
