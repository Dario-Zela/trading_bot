Pochettino leaves USA for Manchester United, tearful over a 'difficult' decision

In one sentence
Pochettino has left the U.S. national team for Manchester United, reportedly via a release clause of about $10 million, months after signing a deal running to 2030.

Mauricio Pochettino has left his post as head coach of the U.S. men's national team to take charge of Manchester United, ending a tenure that began in September 2024 and was extended only in August. U.S. Soccer and the Premier League club announced the move on Friday. Pochettino, by his own account, did not find it easy.

"It's difficult now to talk for me because I think I am a very emotional guy."

He was reported to be in tears as he explained himself, and he told U.S. Soccer chief executive JT Batson, "You never lied to us."

The deal

United moved fast. Michael Carrick was dismissed as manager, and within roughly a day the club had its replacement. Agreement in principle had been reported days earlier, conditional on Carrick going.

The mechanics matter, because the contract was not a short-term one. Pochettino signed an extension in August that was meant to keep him through the 2030 World Cup, and he had spoken of 2030 as "the project". United are reported to have triggered a release clause, widely put at about $10 million. Neither figure has been officially confirmed. Contract length and start date for his United deal have not been disclosed in the sources reviewed.

The timing of his exit is reported inconsistently. One account has him seeing out the two-legged CONCACAF Nations League quarterfinal against Haiti, on Saturday in Miami and Tuesday in Cincinnati. Another has him leaving on 17 November, with Steve Cherundolo taking interim charge for the December camp. U.S. Soccer has named no permanent successor. Pochettino is expected on the touchline for United's league match at Liverpool on 22 November.

What he leaves behind

Pochettino took the Americans to the round of 16 at the 2026 World Cup, played on home soil. That is a respectable result rather than a breakthrough, and the August extension read as a vote of confidence in a build towards 2030. Its value to the federation has now shrunk to a release fee and an unfinished project.

The cost to U.S. Soccer is practical. It must appoint an interim coach and then run a search while the squad is mid-competition, and it faces the awkward fact that a contract through 2030 did not hold a coach for three months.

What United are getting

The club he is joining is in a poor state. United are reported to sit 18th in the Premier League, a relegation position, which makes this a rescue job as much as an appointment. Pochettino knows the league well from his years at Tottenham and Chelsea, and the club has plainly judged that experience worth more than a patient search.

The angle that gives the story its edge is whether a Premier League club still outbids a national-team project. One case is thin evidence, but it points one way. A national job offers stability, a fixed horizon and a World Cup. United offered a chance at a famous club in trouble, and a coach who called his own decision the toughest of his career took it anyway. Money was a factor, though the fee was paid by United and not to Pochettino, whose salary at the federation was reported at about $6 million a year.

What next

The immediate tests are close at hand: the Haiti ties, the identity of the interim coach for December, and Liverpool on 22 November. Beyond that, U.S. Soccer has to name a permanent coach with a World Cup cycle that has barely begun.
