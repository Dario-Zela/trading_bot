Hamilton says Ferrari need 'serious talks' after 'big problems'

In one sentence
Hamilton says Ferrari must hold 'serious talks' after a first-lap clash with Charles Leclerc cost the team victory in Mexico City, putting driver management in the spotlight.

Lewis Hamilton has asked for "serious talks" at Ferrari after a first-lap collision with team-mate Charles Leclerc at the Mexico City Grand Prix. He says the clash cost the team a win it should have had. The complaint is aimed as much at how Ferrari's two drivers are managed as at the car.

The incident

The two Ferraris made contact on the opening lap. Max Verstappen went on to win. Hamilton's argument, as reported, is that two cars working together through the first corners would have been running one-two, not fighting for the remainder of the afternoon.

"Ultimately, that lost us the win today," Hamilton said afterwards, adding that the team must "have some serious talks behind closed doors." The BBC's headline version has him saying "we've got big problems". The phrase is easily read as a verdict on the whole operation. On the evidence available, though, it refers to this clash and to how Ferrari handles its drivers in the opening moments of a race. Nobody has published a wider list of faults, and the quotes do not point to one.

Some outlets reported that Leclerc was penalised over the contact. Others framed it as a racing incident. The reports do not settle the point, and Ferrari has issued no official statement.

The response

Leclerc's reply was conciliatory but not contrite. "I will speak with Lewis," he said, adding that he could equally feel aggrieved at being pushed wide, but that this was racing. Team principal Fred Vasseur was reported to have sided with Hamilton in his post-race remarks. It is a delicate position for him, with a seven-time champion on one side of the garage and a home favourite on the other.

The public nature of the complaint is what gives it weight. Drivers say "we'll talk internally" every weekend. Saying so in front of the cameras, then naming the missed win as the cost, puts the matter on the record in a way a quiet debrief would not.

Why it matters

Hamilton's Ferrari tenure has already produced awkward moments, including a bruising Monza weekend in 2025 and Vasseur's comment then that the champion sometimes exaggerates problems. A fresh complaint, this time about team-mate relations, fits that pattern. It does not yet amount to a break.

The weekend was also overshadowed elsewhere. Kimi Antonelli finished third and was reported to have been crowned, at 20, the youngest world champion in the sport's history. Ferrari's squabble was a sideshow to that story, and an expensive one for a team that wanted the win.

The next test is the closed-door meeting Hamilton asked for and the next race start. Whether Ferrari publishes any rules of engagement for its drivers, or simply lets the talk fade, will show how seriously the team treats the request.
