Alphabet's AI-search worry spreads as hyperscaler capex nears $1tn and Asia sells off

In one sentence
Alphabet fell about 12% on 29 October after weak search growth and negative free cash flow, and AI-capex doubts spread to Nasdaq and Asian markets on Monday.

Alphabet's shares fell by about an eighth on Thursday 29 October, after the company reported a quarter in which search advertising growth slowed and free cash flow turned negative. The loss in market value was about $500bn, according to press reports. By Monday the unease had spread to the wider AI trade: US technology stocks were reported lower, and South Korea's KOSPI was reported down 4.9% in Asian trading. The question now is whether the companies buying the chips can fund them from the businesses that already exist.

One caution on sourcing. The figures below come from press coverage of Alphabet's results, not from a reading of the filing. Several details differ between outlets, and the Monday market moves rest on single headlines.

What Alphabet reported

Press accounts agree on the shape of the day. The stock closed down about 12.3% at $282.32, which is roughly $500bn of market value. Reports describe it as the worst one-day fall since the 2004 flotation. Intraday figures ran higher, with one report citing $511bn lost mid-morning.

Two numbers drove the reaction:

  • Search. Search advertising rose 12% to $63.35bn, which was below expectations. Reports say Alphabet cited lower monetisation of queries answered through its AI Mode. That is the cannibalisation worry stated plainly: a user who gets a synthesised answer clicks fewer ads, and the company's own product is the cause.
  • Cash. Free cash flow was reported at negative $9.07bn, against a consensus of positive $2.7bn. It was the second consecutive negative quarter. Capital expenditure was about $51.3bn, up from $44.9bn in the previous quarter.

The cash figure needs care. One source says a $5.2bn cash payment to settle a 2018 European regulatory fine contributed to it. That is a one-off, not AI spending. The same source reports that management raised 2026 capex guidance to $195bn–$205bn and said 2027 spending would rise significantly. That comes from a single outlet and should be checked against the company's statement. If it holds, the negative quarter reflects a spending path that continues, not an aberration.

The market's reading, that AI search is eating the ad revenue, is an inference. Search revenue still grew 12%. The evidence for cannibalisation is a miss against expectations and a management comment on monetisation. It is not a revenue decline. Whether the miss marks a trend will not be clear from one quarter.

Spending near $1tn

Alphabet is one of several companies in the same position. Reports on Monday put combined hyperscaler capital expenditure at close to $1tn. The exact figure, the period it covers and the companies included could not be confirmed here, so the number is best read as an order of magnitude.

The arithmetic concerns the funding. Until recently, spending of this kind could be paid for out of operating cash flow at the largest companies. A quarter of negative free cash flow at Alphabet shows how little room is left if the core business slows while the bill rises. Borrowing is the alternative. Coverage on the BBC's technology pages has pointed to lesser-known AI infrastructure and financing firms, where leverage is harder to see than at the large labs. The detail was not available for this article, and no names or figures are given.

Asia and the week ahead

Asian markets opened the week lower. KOSPI fell 4.9%, according to a single report from investingLive, which attributed the move to AI-spending concerns and a Binance-related shock. The nature and size of the Binance event were not specified. The Tribune's separate report on the exchange's record withdrawals and stablecoin freezes covers that side. South Korea's market is heavily weighted towards memory-chip makers, so doubts about AI capex reach it quickly. The report said the selloff extended across the region, but no other index moves could be confirmed.

No central bank decision interrupts the week. The Federal Reserve's last meeting was 27–28 October and the next is 8–9 December. US midterm elections take place on Tuesday 3 November, which makes any market reading of the vote hard to separate from the AI-spending question. The final-weekend campaign push is covered separately.

The next hard data points are the filings themselves. Alphabet's full report would settle how much of the cash shortfall was the fine and how much was spending. Other hyperscalers' capex guidance would show whether Alphabet's path is typical. Until then, the fall measures investor doubt about returns on AI spending, not proof that the returns are absent.
