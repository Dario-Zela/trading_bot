MPs warn UK is making China policy 'as it goes along'

In one sentence
A Commons committee says Labour has no coherent China strategy and is improvising, leaving firms with China exposure to plan without a clear government baseline.

A cross-party group of MPs has accused the government of making Britain's China policy up "as it goes along", warning that the lack of a settled strategy leaves businesses unable to plan and the country exposed to decisions made elsewhere.

The verdict comes from the Commons Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee, chaired by the Labour MP Liam Byrne, in a report published on 2 November. It says Labour has not delivered the coherent, long-term China strategy it promised in 2024. The result, the committee argues, is "the worst of both worlds": the government has neither opened new commercial opportunities for British firms nor managed the growing and poorly quantified risks in the relationship.

"Ministers promised clarity and consistency," Byrne said. "Instead, we have opportunities missed, risks unmanaged and decisions left until the 11th hour."

Two cases of improvisation

The committee points to two recent decisions as evidence. One was the block on Ming Yang's planned £1.5bn wind-turbine plant in Scotland, in March. The other was the emergency takeover of British Steel from its Chinese owners. Both, the report says, were marked by reactivity, inconsistency and a lack of transparency. Scotland's First Minister, John Swinney, has urged ministers to reconsider the Ming Yang decision.

The committee does not cast China as simply a threat. It notes that trade brings real benefits: profitable British operations in China, access to Chinese innovation, and cheaper imports for consumers, electric vehicles among them. Its complaint is that the government has not said where the line falls.

What the committee wants

The report calls for a new China strategy, drawn up alongside preparations for trade remedies against unfair competition, including possible tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles. It asks ministers to set "clear new rules of the game", and proposes a division of the economy into two zones:

  • a "red zone" of industries closed to Chinese investment;
  • a "green zone" where trade should be actively encouraged.

It also urges the government to consider "dynamic alignment" with the EU, including on EV tariffs. The aim would be to secure British firms' access to the EU's "Made in Europe" initiative and to guard against Chinese dominance. The EU has already imposed tariffs on Chinese EVs and is weighing a "kill switch" against trade coercion, while China is on course for a record trade surplus. Without alignment, the committee warns, UK policy risks cutting British companies off from major manufacturing contracts in their largest export market.

The report also warns that without a strategy of its own, Britain risks having its China relationship "shaped for us in Washington or Beijing".

The corporate reading

For listed companies with Chinese revenue or supply-chain exposure, the practical point is the absence of a published baseline. Which sectors might be fenced off, whether EV tariffs are coming, and how closely London will track Brussels are all open questions. Each affects investment and sourcing decisions that run on multi-year timescales. The report does not name individual FTSE companies, and its argument is about uncertainty rather than any specific measure.

The government's position remains unsettled. Downing Street has not ruled out tariffs on Chinese EVs, and Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds has said he will decide in what he judges to be the UK's best interests. No formal response to the report had been published at the time of writing.

The nearest test is the EV tariff decision, since it would force ministers to show whether they lean towards Brussels or towards a separate path. Governments are normally expected to respond formally to select committee reports within two months, which would give the committee its first answer on whether the red-zone, green-zone framework is under consideration.
