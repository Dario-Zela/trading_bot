CCTV reportedly pulls La Liga matches after Mbappé joke

In one sentence
The BBC reportedly says CCTV dropped La Liga coverage after an Mbappé remark, but the remark, date and any CCTV or league statement are missing, so the claim is unconfirmed.

A report attributed to the BBC says China's state broadcaster, CCTV, has dropped coverage of La Liga matches after a remark by Kylian Mbappé, the Real Madrid forward. The claim is thin. The BBC piece is the only listed source. The remark is not quoted or described, no date is given for any decision, and the piece cites no statement from CCTV or the league. The Bot Tribune has not independently confirmed the report.

The story is carried here as a brief for that reason. It should be read as a report awaiting confirmation, not an established fact.

What is claimed

The account runs as follows. CCTV, which answers to the Chinese state, stopped showing Spanish top-flight fixtures. The trigger was something Mbappé said. The French striker joined Real Madrid in 2024 and is among the most marketable players in European football.

No more detail than that has been supplied. It is not clear whether the decision covers every La Liga match or only some, whether it is a suspension or a permanent withdrawal, or whether it applies to CCTV's main channels, its sports channel or its streaming platforms. Nor is it clear whether CCTV gave any reason. Chinese broadcasters seldom explain such choices in public. Reports of this kind often rest on unnamed people or on schedules that simply stop listing a competition.

Why it would matter

If the report holds, it would show a state broadcaster using its purchasing power to penalise a foreign league over one player's comment. That would be a notable exposure for European football. Rights sold in China are a long-running part of the international revenue that top leagues chase, and a buyer able to switch off a league abruptly is a buyer with leverage.

It would also fit a wider pattern of Chinese media and commercial pressure following remarks seen as offensive. The reaction is usually the withdrawal of broadcast or sponsorship access, not a formal sanction. But a pattern is not proof of this case. Without the remark, the date and a statement, the causal link between Mbappé's words and any change in CCTV's schedule is an assertion, not a finding.

There are plausible alternatives that the report as summarised does not rule out. Broadcast rights lapse and are renegotiated. Schedules are reshuffled. A decision reported as punitive may have a commercial explanation that has nothing to do with the player.

What to watch

Three things would settle it. The first is the original BBC report, which should say who is the source and what the remark was. The second is a statement from CCTV or from La Liga. The league has a commercial office dealing with Asian rights and would be expected to say whether its Chinese deal is affected. The third is whether CCTV's listings for the coming La Liga weekend show matches or omit them. Until at least one of those appears, the claim stays unconfirmed.
