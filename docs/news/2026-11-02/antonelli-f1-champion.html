Antonelli reported to be F1 world champion at 20

In one sentence
Kimi Antonelli clinched the 2026 F1 title in Mexico City aged 20 years and 68 days, beating Sebastian Vettel's youngest-champion record by about three years.

Kimi Antonelli is Formula 1 world champion. The Mercedes driver clinched the 2026 drivers' title at the Mexico City Grand Prix, finishing third on Sunday. He is 20 years and 68 days old, which makes him the youngest champion the sport has had.

The BBC report that prompted this piece was a single-source claim when it arrived. It has since been confirmed by Sky Sports, ESPN, Formula 1's own website and PlanetF1. One detail in the original framing was wrong: the record Antonelli broke was not Max Verstappen's. Verstappen won his first title in 2021 at 24, but the age record belonged to Sebastian Vettel, who was 23 years and 134 days old when he won in 2010. Antonelli beat that by about three years.

The race that did it

Antonelli did not need to win in Mexico. Going into the weekend he had 365 points after 18 of 23 rounds, with Mercedes team-mate George Russell on 242 and Lewis Hamilton on 241. A finish in the top nine would have been enough whatever his rivals did, and he took third, 4.006 seconds behind the winner. That left him with four rounds to spare.

The race itself was untidy. Max Verstappen won his sixth Mexico City Grand Prix for Red Bull. Hamilton was second for Ferrari, 0.799 seconds behind. Charles Leclerc was fourth, Lando Norris fifth and Russell sixth. Leclerc cut Turn 4 to pass Hamilton on the opening lap and was given a five-second penalty. Hamilton blamed that for costing him the win. Oscar Piastri was penalised for a collision with Arvid Lindblad, and both Alpines were penalised for yellow-flag infringements. Reports differ slightly on the penalty-adjusted order, but none disputes Antonelli's third place.

Sky Sports quoted him afterwards: "I'm speechless. I'm trying to process what has just happened."

A season built early

The title came from a lead he built early and never gave back. Antonelli won in Japan in March and became the youngest championship leader in the sport's history. He then won in Miami and again in Spain, where his lead reached 81 points. He has won nine races this year, and in the races he has finished he has been off the podium only twice.

He is also the first Italian champion since Alberto Ascari won in 1953, a 73-year wait.

The record itself deserves some context. Vettel's mark stood for 16 years, but the sport has been moving younger for some time. Verstappen made his debut at 17 and won races at 18. Antonelli went straight from Formula 2 to a race seat at Mercedes, and was a champion in his second season. Whether that says something about how early F1 talent can peak, or just that he happened to be handed the quickest car, is the argument his rivals will make.

What comes next

Four rounds remain, and the fight now is for second place. Russell and Hamilton are separated by a single point in the table before Mexico, and Mercedes has the harder task of managing a team-mate battle with a champion who no longer has anything to defend. Ferrari, meanwhile, has Hamilton's complaints about its race management to deal with. Hamilton has said the team needs "serious talks" after its "big problems". The next round is the first chance to see whether Antonelli races as freely as a champion as he did as a leader.
