Airbnb's new house rules, including no washing-up, divide hosts and guests

In one sentence
From 20 November Airbnb bars hosts from demanding checkout chores such as washing dishes and limits them to seven standard house rules, drawing objections from some hosts.

Airbnb has told its hosts they can no longer ask guests to wash the dishes before they leave. From 20 November, a short list of standard house rules will replace the free-text instructions many listings carry, and some hosts are not happy about it.

The company emailed hosts on Tuesday 21 October under the subject line "Important Changes to House Rules", according to the travel-industry site Skift. Hosts are now "generally not allowed" to require checkout chores such as washing and putting away dishes, stripping beds, or taking rubbish off the property. The test Airbnb applies is whether a task needs significant effort or time, or would normally be done by a cleaner. Guests can still be asked to leave used dishes in the sink.

What changes

The policy is a more formal break from the old arrangement than the dishes row suggests. Hosts will pick from seven standard house rules, which must be shown in full at booking. They cover topics such as overnight guests, quiet hours, pets and smoking, and whether guests must lock up when they go.

Skift reports that it is the first time Airbnb has published a policy saying which host requests are reasonable and which are not. Other provisions in the same package:

  • Hosts cannot charge for amenities already included in a listing, or impose their own fines. Airbnb points to its AirCover scheme for property damage.
  • Hosts cannot require a separate rental agreement. Exceptions include monthly stays, Airbnb Luxe, optional extras such as golf-cart use, and cases where local rules or a homeowners' association demand one.
  • Hosts cannot collect or keep copies of guests' IDs unless local law or an association requires it.

There is room for local conditions. Airbnb says it may be reasonable to ask guests to use an animal-proof bin in bear country, or to limit showers during drought restrictions.

Who is unhappy

Airbnb's reasoning is that guests who already pay a cleaning fee should not also be doing a cleaner's work, and that house rules had become confusing and hard to police. Guests have complained for years about long checkout to-do lists attached to cleaning fees.

Some hosts see it differently. Skift counted about a dozen hosts objecting in Airbnb's community forum by Wednesday afternoon, mostly over the dishes ban. Two French hosts said they might leave the platform. That is anecdote, not a measure of host sentiment. Airbnb has published no figures on how many listings carry chore requirements, or on the disputes and cancellations behind the change.

A Quebec City property manager told Skift that adapting is part of running rentals professionally and takes operational work. That points to the likely split: professionally managed properties, which already budget for turnover cleaning, will find it easier than individual owners who rely on guests to cut their workload.

Who sets the rules

The dishes are the visible part of a bigger shift. Airbnb is a marketplace, but it increasingly acts as the rule-maker for what is, in each case, someone's own property. Hosts set the price and the house; the platform now decides what they may ask for. Enforcement gives the policy weight. Repeat violations can bring a notice on the listing, lower search visibility, or action under the terms of service. Skift says that can extend to cancelling bookings, removing Superhost or Guest Favorite status, or suspending an account.

Hosts who want guests to do more work are left with an awkward option: cut the cleaning fee, absorb the cost, or leave. Whether any leave in numbers will not be clear until after 20 November, when the standard rules take effect and the first complaints under the new enforcement regime start to surface.
