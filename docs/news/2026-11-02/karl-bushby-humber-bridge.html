Karl Bushby 'overwhelmed' at Humber Bridge welcome

The planned route of the Goliath Expedition, from Punta Arenas in Chile to Hull. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Karl Bushby, who set out to walk round the world in 1998, was greeted at the Humber Bridge on his final stretch into Hull, and BBC UK says he was 'overwhelmed'.

Karl Bushby has walked into Hull. The former paratrooper left the city in 1998 with a plan to go round the world on foot, and BBC UK reports that the welcome at the Humber Bridge on his final stretch left him "overwhelmed". The BBC's own account has the exact wording and the size of the crowd. Neither could be checked independently here, so neither is described in detail below.

A walk with no fixed end

The project is the Goliath Expedition, a name taken from the hope of going from Punta Arenas, at the tip of Chile, to his home city without taking a flight. Wikipedia gives the start as 1 November 1998 and the planned distance as about 36,000 miles (58,000 km). He had covered more than 17,000 miles by 2006, the year he crossed the Bering Strait.

The route runs north through South and Central America, across the Darién Gap, through Mexico and the United States to Alaska. Then comes Russia, Mongolia, China, Central Asia and, in 2024, a swim across the Caspian Sea to Azerbaijan. From there he went through Turkey and the Balkans, then Hungary, Austria, Slovakia and Belgium.

What it took

Most of the delay came from paperwork rather than terrain. Russia detained him in 2006, refused a visa in 2012 and imposed a five-year ban in 2013. He walked across the United States in protest. He was also detained in Xinjiang in 2018. Covid paused the walk, and post-Brexit Schengen limits on stays complicated the European leg.

The last obstacle was water. Eurotunnel turned down his request for access to the Channel Tunnel on safety and operational grounds. The French government then gave him special permission to swim the Channel instead. Per his official site and Wikipedia, he landed near Dover on 5 October 2026 with roughly 400 km left to walk to Hull.

That gives a rough sense of the closing stretch. It was about a month of walking after 28 years, and it ended on a bridge he would have known long before he left.

What comes next

A Hull date of 13 November is listed on Bushby's website, so a further public celebration may be planned. The dates in the sources do not quite agree, though. Earlier coverage aimed for 1 November, the 28th anniversary, and Wikipedia expected the finish on 2 November. Which day counts as the formal end will depend on how Bushby and the organisers mark it.
