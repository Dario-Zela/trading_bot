Arsenal's Anfield win overshadowed by penalty row

In one sentence
Arsenal won 1-0 at Anfield for the first time since 2012, but Liverpool coach Andoni Iraola's remarks about going down to win fouls after a rejected Isak penalty claim dominated the aftermath.

Arsenal ended a 14-year wait for a league win at Anfield on Sunday. The talking point afterwards was a penalty Liverpool did not get, and what their head coach said about it.

Kai Havertz scored the only goal in the 16th minute, converting at the back post after a low cross from Declan Rice. The 1-0 win was Arsenal's first at the ground since September 2012. It also took them to the top of the Premier League, nine points clear of fifth-placed Liverpool, according to The Globe and Mail.

One correction to the story as first circulated: Andoni Iraola is not a Bournemouth manager in this story. He is Liverpool's head coach, and the comment came after his own side's defeat.

The incident

Liverpool had two penalty appeals turned down, and referee Andy Madley rejected both, ETV Bharat reported.

The bigger one came in the first half. Arsenal defender Jurriën Timber pulled at Alexander Isak's shirt as the striker tried to meet a cross from Bradley Barcola. Isak stayed on his feet and no spot-kick was given. The second claim involved Cody Gakpo, who appeared to be held by Christos Tzolis. A video review did not award a penalty there either.

The first appeal is the one that stuck. Iraola's complaint was less about the referee than about what the decision rewards. He said Isak stood his ground despite the shirt pull, and that staying upright had cost his side.

What Iraola said

As quoted by ETV Bharat, Iraola said: "we have to tell them to go down because they're not going to call it otherwise." He added: "if you don't go down, you don't call it."

The wording matters. The quotes are a pointed complaint about how fouls are officiated, and the full context is not in the reports available. They are not obviously a literal instruction to cheat. Some headlines, including one on IOL, framed it as an order to dive. The body of that piece, as summarised by other reports, centred on Iraola's point that honesty goes unrewarded. Media framing split along the same lines, with Sky Sports treating the incident as a missed penalty and Reuters calling it a penalty shout that was turned down.

This is not a wider confirmed row about simulation. It is one coach, in the aftermath of one defeat, arguing that a shirt-pull is ignored unless the player falls. Whether he meant it as instruction, protest or irony will probably be put to him again.

Arsenal's side of it

Arsenal's account was happier. Mikel Arteta called the result "the magical thing about football" and said outcomes are unpredictable, without addressing the penalty claims in the quotes carried by the Globe and Mail. Arsenal have won eight of their first nine league games.

Iraola has history here. When he managed Bournemouth he accused Havertz of diving against Arsenal and said he did not think it was a penalty. That is a separate occasion, but it sits oddly beside Sunday's remarks.

What next

Liverpool, fifth and nine points behind, need results rather than refereeing grievances. Whether the Premier League or referees' body engages with Iraola's comments, and whether Madley's handling of the Timber incident is raised in the next officiating review, are the points to watch.
