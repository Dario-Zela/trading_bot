Saracens sack academy player Auvaa after further incident

In one sentence
Saracens have sacked academy forward Totoa Auvaa, the player behind June's Stokes–Atkinson nightclub row, after an undisclosed incident in St Albans on 10 October.

Saracens have sacked Totoa Auvaa, the academy forward whose missed punch at a London nightclub in June set off the Ben Stokes and Gus Atkinson row, after a fresh incident at the weekend.

The club said on Monday that it had terminated the 21-year-old back-row's contract with immediate effect. The decision followed an internal investigation into an incident in St Albans on the evening of Saturday 10 October. Saracens did not say what happened, and no detail of the St Albans episode has been made public.

The June incident

Auvaa's name became public after an early-morning incident on 8 June outside a London nightclub where England cricketers were present. He aimed a punch at Atkinson, missed, and struck a member of England's security staff. Stokes and Atkinson were initially dropped from the second Test as the affair unfolded. Both were recalled for the third after an investigation by the independent Cricket Regulator found insufficient evidence against them.

Saracens ran their own review and, in late June, said they would take no further action against Auvaa. The club called the episode "regrettable" and said it remained supportive of him. Jamie George, the England hooker, called the behaviour unacceptable but described Auvaa as a good kid who needed support. "We will make sure that he's got role models around him," he said.

What is not known

Several outlets describe the dismissal as following a second conduct incident, but Saracens have not linked the two cases. The club has said nothing about the nature of the St Albans incident, whether anyone else was involved, or whether police have been contacted.

RugbyPass reports that a second academy player has been stood down over a separate matter. That is not corroborated by the other reports reviewed, and it is not treated here as established.

The sequence is clear enough. Having decided in June that the club's own review was sufficient, Saracens have now taken the harshest step open to them over a later matter. Whether the club regards the two as connected is the question its statement leaves unanswered.

What next

The next development depends on Saracens or Auvaa saying more about the St Albans incident, or on any police involvement becoming known. Neither has happened so far.
