Fernandes: symptom or cause of Manchester United's poor form?

In one sentence
Fernandes's numbers are poor, but United's six points from six games and a fall in team output suggest he is the most visible symptom of a collective slump, not its cause.

Manchester United have six points from six league games, which one report calls their worst start in 40 years. Their captain, Bruno Fernandes, has the most visible share of the blame. The question is whether he is the cause of the slump or just the place where it shows.

The numbers against him are easy to find. According to WhoScored figures cited by Yahoo Sports, Fernandes has lost possession 106 times in the Premier League this season, more than any other outfield player. He has four goals and one assist in eight appearances. Last season he set a Premier League record of 21 assists and took the FWA and PFA player of the year awards, so the comparison is a harsh one. Sunday's 1-1 draw at home to Tottenham, in which Spurs equalised through Solanke after being reduced to ten men, brought the criticism to a head.

The case for the prosecution

Fernandes is the captain and the creator, and he takes the most risks. A player who attempts the most difficult passes will lose the ball most often. That is part of the job, but it looks worse when the team isn't winning.

He is also a long way from last season's output. One assist from eight games is a big drop from a record-setting 21. His niggle on international duty has added to the concern. Michael Carrick has called it "not a major deal" and said he is "pretty much over it".

The case for the defence

Two things work against making him the main culprit.

First, the slump is collective. Reports describe the team as struggling to match its form from the second half of last season, and Fernandes's creative numbers depend on teammates finishing the chances. His assists need someone at the end of them. One of his assists this season went to Bryan Mbeumo.

Second, there is his form for Portugal. In early October he was reported to be the standout in a 4-2 win over Denmark, with two assists and five key passes. If he had simply declined, that would be hard to explain. A player who is still creating chances for one team but not for another points to a problem with the team around him.

Carrick has framed it this way. "This is not on Bruno, this line," the head coach said. "Nobody is guaranteed a place in the team." He added: "He's done some incredible things for this football club and he will do the same in the future, there's no doubt about it."

The two sentences do different work. The first defends Fernandes. The second tells the whole squad that nobody is safe, Fernandes included.

Symptom, probably

The evidence favours symptom over cause, with a caveat. The captain's armband and his central role make him the obvious face of the slump, and his possession losses are a real concern. But the six-points-from-six record belongs to the whole team, and a single player's form rarely explains that on its own. He is part of the problem and mostly a measure of it.

The next test is Atlético Madrid in the Champions League, where Fernandes is expected to start. Carrick has not guaranteed him the place, and the league table will show whether the team's output improves with him or without him.
