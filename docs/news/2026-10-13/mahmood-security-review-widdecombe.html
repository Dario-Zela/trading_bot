Mahmood orders security review after reported killing of Ann Widdecombe

In one sentence
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood has extended police protection to former MPs, peers and party spokespeople and ordered a review of security for public figures, after Ann Widdecombe's killing.

The Home Secretary has widened police protection beyond sitting MPs, three months after the killing of Ann Widdecombe, the former Conservative MP and Reform UK spokeswoman. Shabana Mahmood told the Commons on Monday that police support would extend, with immediate effect, to political figures who are not MPs. She also announced an independent review of how the state protects people in public life.

The killing is confirmed. It is no longer a reported event. Widdecombe, 78, died on 8 July at her isolated home in Haytor, on the edge of Dartmoor in Devon. Police said soon afterwards that the attack was targeted. The Press Association reports that she was struck over the head with a hammer.

What Mahmood announced

The expanded cover reaches former MPs, peers, party spokespeople and the leaders of parties without Commons seats. Each will have access to a named police officer and help with wider security measures. Until now, this kind of dedicated support has largely followed the parliamentary role.

The independent review will be led by Sir Stephen Lovegrove, a former national security adviser. It will examine how protective security is provided to people in public life, and whether funding and measures are being used effectively. It builds on the earlier review by Sir Robert Buckland that followed the murder of Sir David Amess in 2021. The reports of the announcement give no date for it to finish.

Mahmood told MPs it was "clear there is insufficient protection for those who serve". She added: "An attack on any individual in public life is an attack on every one of us."

The case so far

The early police response drew criticism. Devon and Cornwall Police first said the killing was not believed to be terror-related. Counter-terrorism officers took over the investigation in mid-July after new evidence emerged. Laurence Taylor, head of National Counter Terrorism Policing, then called it a "brutal attack on a 78-year-old lady in her own home".

Joshua Kerry, 28, is accused of her murder. He has also been charged over an alleged terror plot against Reform UK leader Nigel Farage. According to the PA account, prosecutors say he spent nearly two years researching prominent people and institutions. He is alleged to have tried to enter Farage's south London home on 19 April 2025. A list found in the inquiry reportedly names Kemi Badenoch, Richard Tice, Lee Anderson and Robert Jenrick. Mahmood said at the time of the arrest that the suspect was not known to Prevent, the government's counter-extremism programme. The charges are allegations, and Kerry has not been convicted.

Reaction

The Speaker, Sir Lindsay Hoyle, said he had not been told which MPs were on the list. He said it was "much harder" to learn of it from newspapers. The Jo Cox Foundation welcomed the announcement but said the "root causes", including online harms and education, must also be addressed. Reform UK's Zia Yusuf called the review welcome. He said it was disappointing that it took a murder and months of campaigning for the government to act.

What to watch

The main open questions are the review's reporting date and its funding. Lovegrove's terms of reference should show whether it covers people outside politics, such as royals, which Mahmood had flagged in July. The other marker is the prosecution of Kerry, which will set out the evidence on planning and motive.
