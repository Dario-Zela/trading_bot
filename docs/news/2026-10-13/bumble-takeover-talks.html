Bumble shares jump on report of $1bn takeover talks

In one sentence
Bumble shares rose almost 50% after a report of roughly $1bn takeover talks with Ithaca, a deal neither company has confirmed.

Shares in Bumble rose almost 50% on Monday after a report that the dating-app company is in talks to be bought for about $1bn (£757m). Neither Bumble nor the prospective buyer has confirmed it. The move is a price on a possible sale, not on the company's trading.

The BBC's account draws on a Wall Street Journal report. The Journal said Ithaca, a technology holding company, could announce a deal for the Texas-based firm as early as this week. The BBC said it had contacted Bumble and Ithaca and did not report a response from either. Terms, including a price per share, have not been published. Until a deal is announced, the $1bn figure is a press estimate and talks can fail.

What the market priced

The jump lifted Bumble's market value to just over $580m. That is well below the roughly $1bn reported for the talks, which suggests investors are not assuming a deal will happen. A takeover at that level would imply a premium of well over 50% to Monday's close, and a gap of that size is a measure of doubt as much as hope. It could close if a deal is announced, or reopen if talks collapse.

The figure is also small against the company's history. Bumble listed on Nasdaq in 2021 and at its peak was worth more than $14bn. A $1bn sale would mean a drop of more than 90% from that high.

A business under pressure

The backdrop explains why a buyer might see value and why the price is modest. Growth at Bumble has slowed. In 2025 the company said it would cut almost a third of its workforce. It has recently dropped its rule that only women could start conversations, a change that prompted debate among users. Founder Whitney Wolfe Herd said in August that online dating was at an "inflection point".

The wider sector has struggled too. Match Group, owner of Tinder, Hinge and OkCupid, has also seen its shares fall sharply from their 2021 peak. Ofcom reported in 2025 that UK adults were spending less time on dating apps than three years earlier.

What to watch

The test is whether a deal is announced this week, as the Journal suggested, and at what price per share. Confirmed terms would give a data point for what a buyer will pay for a listed consumer-app business with slowing growth and a shrinking workforce. That could read across to Match and other smaller rivals. If no announcement comes, Monday's gain is the part of the move most exposed to reversal.
