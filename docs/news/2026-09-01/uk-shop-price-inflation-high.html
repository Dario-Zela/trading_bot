UK shop price inflation hits two-year high as food and energy costs bite

A Tesco supermarket in the UK, where food inflation hit a four-month high in August. — Peter Trimming / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)
In one sentence
UK shop prices rose 1.5% year-on-year in August, the fastest since 2024, narrowing the Bank of England's room to keep cutting rates.

UK shop prices rose 1.5% in the year to August, the fastest annual pace since February 2024, as retailers passed on rising food and energy costs to shoppers already squeezed by a stagnant economy. The reading from the British Retail Consortium and NielsenIQ marks a sharp acceleration from July's 0.9%, and puts the three-month average at 1.2% — evidence that what looked like a blip is turning into a trend.

Food was the biggest driver. Prices in that category rose 2.8% year-on-year, up from 2.2% in July and the steepest food inflation reading in four months. BRC chief executive Helen Dickinson said "higher energy, input and commodity costs" are filtering through into prices, "particularly for ambient foods which are typically imported and processed" — the tinned and packaged goods that make up a large share of the weekly shop.

Non-food prices climbed too, up 0.9% from 0.2% in July, also the fastest since February 2024. Here the story is less about energy than semiconductors: Dickinson pointed to rising electrical prices "amid the ongoing AI boom, which is forcing up the price of memory chips and storage." Data centre buildout has been soaking up global supply of memory chips for months; laptops, phones and televisions on UK shelves are now catching the spillover.

Why the Bank of England is watching

The BRC index is a private-sector gauge, not the ONS's official consumer prices measure, but it tends to lead the official data by a few weeks and policymakers watch it closely for that reason. Official CPI has been running at 3.8% for July, with economists expecting August's reading — due later this month — to nudge up to around 3.9%, roughly double the Bank's 2% target.

That matters because the Bank has already cut Bank Rate three times this year, in February, May and August, taking it to 4%. Markets currently price barely a 1-in-50 chance of a fourth cut when the Monetary Policy Committee meets on 18 September. Governor Andrew Bailey has said there is now "considerably more doubt" about the path of further easing, and a shop-price series accelerating at its fastest clip in over two years does nothing to settle that doubt.

Gilts have already registered the shift: yields have stayed elevated near multi-week highs as traders pare back rate-cut bets, with the 30-year gilt trading near a 27-year high amid separate concerns about the government's fiscal position. Sticky retail inflation reinforces the same trade — higher-for-longer UK rates — even as growth remains soft.

What's next

The ONS publishes official August CPI data later this month, the number that will actually move the MPC's 18 September decision. Dickinson struck a cautious note on the outlook, warning that "the months ahead look challenging for households, with rising bills putting further pressure on budgets," while retailers face "persistently high operating costs, limiting their ability to absorb further increases." If food and electronics costs keep climbing into the autumn, the BRC series suggests the pass-through to shoppers — and to the Bank's rate calculus — is far from over.
