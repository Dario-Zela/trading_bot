Djokovic suffers earliest-ever Grand Slam defeat at US Open, fuelling retirement questions

In one sentence
Djokovic, 39, lost in the US Open first round to Mariano Navone — his earliest Slam exit since 2006 — and admitted uncertainty over how much longer he can keep competing.

Novak Djokovic has lost at every stage of every Grand Slam over a 20-year career — quarter-finals, semi-finals, a fistful of finals. What he had never done, not once in 71 previous major appearances, was lose in the first round. On Sunday night at Arthur Ashe Stadium, four hours and 36 minutes into a match against a 25-year-old Argentine he had never faced, that streak ended.

Mariano Navone, ranked 48th in the world, beat Djokovic 7-6(5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1. It was the longest opening-round match of Djokovic's career, and by the end he was, in his own description, barely a competitor. "I didn't enjoy myself," he said afterward. "It was an awful feeling on the court for me."

Twenty years, one afternoon

Djokovic's last Grand Slam first-round exit came at the 2006 Australian Open — before he had won any of his 24 majors, back when he was an 18-year-old qualifier nobody outside tennis circles had heard of. Everything since has been building toward the opposite kind of history: most men's Slam titles ever, 428 weeks at world No. 1, a career that redrew the sport's ceiling.

Sunday inverted that. He led two sets to one before his serve, and then his body, gave way. He described a problem that has trailed him through most of 2026: "Vomiting, throwing up, a serious issue there. Then my whole body started to collapse, basically cramping and pain." It echoed his French Open exit in the spring, when he told reporters he was "barely standing on my legs" after a five-set loss to Joao Fonseca amid a Paris heatwave, and cited "nerves and everything involved that make it worse" in humid conditions.

Navone, for his part, played the match of his career. He had never beaten a top-10 opponent at a major. He grew up idolising Djokovic. "He's a nice guy, a fighter," Djokovic said of him, declining to frame the result as anything other than a defeat he simply lost, not one that was given away. "I didn't want to take too much out of his win."

The numbers behind the collapse

The defeat carries a ranking penalty that makes the moment harder to wave off as a bad night. Djokovic entered the tournament ranked fifth with 3,770 points; a first-round loss is worth only 10. He will fall out of the world's top 10 for the first time since July 2018 — an eight-year run, the longest active streak of its kind before Sunday. He committed 70 unforced errors against Navone, a total more typical of a player well outside his class than of a 24-time major champion.

It is also the third straight year Djokovic has gone without a Grand Slam title, despite reaching two finals and six semi-finals across that stretch — evidence, by his own account, that fitness rather than form is now the limiting factor. "I feel when I'm healthy, I'm still able to play as a top-five player, still able to compete at the highest level," he said. The qualifier is doing most of the work in that sentence: healthy has become the exception this year, not the baseline.

What comes next

Djokovic has not announced retirement, and has given no indication he intends to. But he has also stopped ruling anything out, and the arithmetic of a 39-year-old body that cramps and vomits its way through best-of-five matches is not one more major titles reverse easily. The Australian Open in January — a tournament he has won ten times, more than any man in history — is the obvious next marker, either as a stage for one more run or, if the health issues persist, a natural point to stop.

For now, the top 10 will operate without him for the first time in eight years, and the rest of the sport gets a rare data point: what the draw looks like when the most durable player of his generation is no longer a fixture in the second week.
