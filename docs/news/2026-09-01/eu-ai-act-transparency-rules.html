EU's AI Act transparency rules take effect, forcing chatbot and deepfake labelling

In one sentence
Since 2 August, EU-regulated AI systems must disclose AI involvement and mark AI-generated content, as Brussels enforcement ramps up alongside a new ban on AI-generated CSAM and non-consensual intimate imagery.

Since 2 August, EU-regulated AI systems must disclose AI involvement and mark AI-generated content, as Brussels enforcement ramps up alongside a new ban on AI-generated CSAM and non-consensual intimate imagery.

This marks the first major test of the EU's AI governance regime. Success or failure here will set the template for similar regulatory efforts globally and determine whether transparency rules become genuine guardrails or performative exercises. For trading-focused readers, watch whether penalties on AI companies trading on US/EU markets move those stocks.

Key facts:
- EU AI Act transparency provisions came into force August 2, 2026, requiring systems to disclose AI involvement and machine-mark AI-generated content
- Enforcement of large language model provisions is ramping up, though specific enforcement mechanisms, penalty amounts, and compliance verification standards have not been publicly detailed
- New ban added for AI-generated child sexual abuse material and non-consensual intimate imagery, but scope of enforcement resources allocated remains unclear
