Manchester City open talks with Chelsea over £120m+ Enzo Fernandez transfer

Enzo Fernandez, whose £106.8m move to Chelsea in 2023 set a British transfer record. — Lazaric12 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Man City have agreed personal terms with Enzo Fernandez and are negotiating a ~€140m fee with Chelsea, driven by his old boss Enzo Maresca.

Manchester City have opened formal talks with Chelsea over a transfer for Enzo Fernandez, with the two clubs discussing a fee in the region of €140m (£120m+) as the summer window closes on Tuesday night. The Argentina midfielder has verbally agreed personal terms with City, according to Fabrizio Romano, though the deal still hinges on the clubs agreeing a number — Chelsea's asking price and City's opening moves have not yet converged.

What makes the pursuit notable is not just the size of the fee but the identity of the man pushing for it. Enzo Maresca, who signed Fernandez's biggest admirer in himself while managing Chelsea, left Stamford Bridge in June to succeed Pep Guardiola at City after Guardiola stepped down at the end of last season. Xabi Alonso, appointed in May and in the Chelsea dugout since July, now finds one of his manager's most influential midfielders being courted by the very predecessor who developed him.

A reunion three years in the making

Fernandez joined Chelsea from Benfica in January 2023 for a British transfer record of £106.8m (€121m), a fee that triggered his release clause after a World Cup-winning tournament with Argentina in Qatar. He has since added the 2024 Copa America to his medal collection and made himself a Chelsea mainstay — 118 Premier League appearances, 19 goals, 15 assists across three and a half seasons. That a club spent that kind of money less than four years ago and is now willing to let the player go says less about Fernandez's form than about the churn in Chelsea's dugout: Graham Potter, Mauricio Pochettino, Maresca and now Alonso have all taken charge since BlueCo's takeover, each with their own view of what the midfield should look like.

Alonso has been diplomatic in public. "Enzo is a good professional. He's training well," the Spaniard said last week, after Fernandez was left out of Chelsea's Carabao Cup squad — a decision the club has described as tactical rather than a sign the player is being frozen out. Chelsea have been lining up cover regardless, with Bournemouth's Alex Scott and Roma's Manu Kone among names under consideration should Fernandez leave.

For City, the move fits a broader midfield rebuild forced by departures on Guardiola's watch and after it: Rodri to Barcelona, Bernardo Silva out of contract, Nico Gonzalez to Newcastle. Elliot Anderson and Ayyoub Bouaddi have already arrived this summer; a player Maresca knows intimately, and trusts to run a game the way he wants it run, would be the marquee addition to sit alongside them.

Whether it happens by Tuesday's 11pm deadline is the immediate question. The bigger one — whether Chelsea's revolving cast of coaches can hold onto its record signings for longer than a manager's tenure — will outlast the window.
