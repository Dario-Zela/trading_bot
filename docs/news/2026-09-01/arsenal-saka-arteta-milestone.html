Saka sees off Villa as Arsenal go top; Arteta reaches 250 league wins

Mikel Arteta, who marked his 250th Premier League match in charge of Arsenal with a 1-0 win at Aston Villa. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
Bukayo Saka's second-half goal sealed a 1-0 win at Villa Park that put Arsenal top, while Aston Villa remain pointless and without a shot on target through two games.

Arsenal did not so much dismantle Aston Villa on Sunday as quietly finish them off. Bukayo Saka's 59th-minute strike settled a scrappy, low-event contest at Villa Park 1-0, and by full time the scoreline was almost beside the point. Arsenal are top of the Premier League after two matches, unbeaten and unscored-against for a fourth straight game across competitions. Aston Villa are bottom of it, without a goal, without a win, and — more strikingly — without a single shot on target in either of their opening two fixtures.

A goal built by committee

The move that decided the game had Arsenal's summer signings written through it. Eberechi Eze slid the ball to Riccardo Calafiori on the overlap; the Italian full-back cut it back into the six-yard box; Saka arrived to finish from close range. It was Saka's second goal in as many matches to start the season, and Arsenal's opener continued a pattern that has quietly become their signature under Mikel Arteta: doing just enough. It was Arsenal's 14th Premier League win by a 1-0 scoreline across the last three seasons — six more than any other club in that span — and their eighth clean sheet since March, three clear of the next-best side.

Arteta, sent to Match of the Day afterwards, called it "a great performance," adding: "With 11, it wasn't enough, the finishers came in and did a magnificent job to finish the game and win it. This is a really difficult place to come and win. Last season we lost in the last kick of the game." The result also happened to be his 250th Premier League match in charge of Arsenal — a landmark that puts him in select company. Only Pep Guardiola, Alex Ferguson and Jürgen Klopp have overseen at least 250 games for a single club with a better win rate than Arteta's 60.4%, and he is just the second man, after Mark Hughes, to both play and manage 250-plus Premier League fixtures. It is a tidy statistic and, on its own, not much more than that — Arsenal's actual business this season is decided in the Champions League and against the two Manchester clubs, not in milestone counts. But it is a marker of how far the project has travelled since Arteta's shaky early seasons, and a reminder that Sunday's grinding, low-quality win — the kind Arsenal used to fail to find a way through — is now the house style.

Villa's blank sheet

The more urgent story belongs to the other end of the pitch. Aston Villa have now played two Premier League matches this season, at a combined running cost of nine goals conceded, and have not managed to force a single shot on target. That is not a slow start; it is close to a statistical anomaly at this level. The opener was worse than Sunday's — a 4-0 collapse away at Brighton in which Victor Lindelöf's own goal put Villa behind inside minutes and João Gomes was sent off before half-time, leaving ten men to be picked apart. Against Arsenal, with Villa Park's crowd behind them, they at least kept the arithmetic respectable. They still could not test Arsenal's goalkeeper once.

The injury list explains some of it. Ollie Watkins, Villa's most reliable source of goals in recent seasons, was absent from the matchday squad entirely; Tammy Abraham and Brian Madjo are also out, and Emiliano Martínez — the goalkeeper who anchored Villa's rise under Unai Emery — has been left out of the picture altogether while he pushes for an exit after a move to Juventus collapsed. Debutants Nicolas Jackson and Zion Suzuki started instead. Emery, for his part, framed the defeat as a case of honest competition rather than crisis: "We lost but we were competing. We competed fantastically collectively, tactically, and the team responded fantastically." He pointed to Jackson and Suzuki settling in well and leaned on the idea that "Villa Park is always our fortress" as a platform to build from.

That reading is not unreasonable on its own terms — a heavily reshuffled squad, missing its two most proven attacking outlets, competing well without the ball against the champions is not nothing. But it does not explain two matches without troubling an opposition goalkeeper. Shots-on-target droughts of this length are usually the product of either a squad stripped of its finishers or a system that is not creating clean sight of goal, and right now Villa have visible evidence of both: Watkins unavailable, Martínez estranged, and a back line still being organised around a raft of new arrivals — Leon Goretzka, Gomes, Aaron Wan-Bissaka — who have barely trained together.

What to watch

Arsenal travel into the September international break with a maximum six points and the look of a squad whose depth, not just its front three, is now doing the winning — a theme Arteta leaned on post-match, saying strength in depth was "what we need" this season. Villa's next fixture after the break will be the real test of Emery's diagnosis: if the shot count stays at zero with a fuller squad and Watkins available again, this stops being an injury story and starts being a tactical one.
