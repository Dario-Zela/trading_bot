JPMorgan lifts ASML price target to $2,400, keeps overweight

ASML's headquarters in Veldhoven, the Netherlands. — A ansems / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
In one sentence
JPMorgan raised its ASML price target to $2,400 from $2,200 and kept its overweight rating after the chipmaker's Q2 beat and raised 2026 guidance.

JPMorgan raised its price target on ASML Holding to $2,400 from $2,200 on Friday, maintaining its overweight rating on the Dutch chip-equipment maker two days after the company posted second-quarter results that beat estimates and lifted its full-year outlook for the second time in 2026.

The target implies roughly 34% upside from ASML's Friday price of around $1,790, where shares slipped about 1.4% even as the bank turned more bullish. It is JPMorgan's second target increase on the stock since June, when it raised the figure to $2,200 from $1,813.

In one sentence: JPMorgan raised its ASML price target to $2,400 from $2,200 and kept its overweight rating after the chip-equipment maker's Q2 beat and raised 2026 guidance.

The numbers behind the call

ASML reported €9.3 billion in net sales and €2.9 billion in net income for the second quarter, released Monday. The company raised its full-year 2026 sales guidance to a range of €43 billion to €45 billion, up from the €36 billion to €40 billion range it had set in April — the second upward revision this year. Gross margin guidance for the year now sits between 54% and 56%, and the company pointed investors to a stronger third quarter still, forecasting €11 billion to €12 billion in net sales with margins of 55% to 57%.

Chief executive Christophe Fouquet described order intake through the first half of the year as "extremely strong," attributing the pace to customers accelerating capacity plans tied to AI-related chip production. ASML said it is now largely booked for 2027 and plans to expand capacity by 30% in each of the next two years across both its extreme ultraviolet (EUV) and deep ultraviolet immersion tool lines, with EUV order books already extending into 2028.

Why the stock dipped anyway

Despite the beat, ASML shares fell on the day of the report — a reminder that in a stock that has more than doubled over the past year, a strong quarter is not automatically a rally trigger when expectations have already priced in most of the good news. ASML remains roughly 11% below its 52-week high near $2,000, even after the run.

That gap between operational performance and share reaction is the backdrop for JPMorgan's move. The bank's target bump is less a fresh insight than a mechanical response to the guidance raise — the kind of routine analyst housekeeping that follows almost every earnings beat from a company this closely tracked. It carries no new rating change, no revised thesis, and no departure from the overweight stance JPMorgan has held through ASML's price target climbing from $1,813 to $2,200 to $2,400 across three revisions this year alone.

The chokepoint argument

What gives the note weight beyond routine coverage maintenance is ASML's position in the chip supply chain. The company is the sole global supplier of the EUV lithography systems needed to print the most advanced logic and memory chips — machines that run to roughly €150 million apiece, weigh around 150 tonnes, and ship in some 250 crates. No competitor makes an equivalent tool, which gives ASML unusual pricing power and visibility into demand trends across the entire AI infrastructure buildout, from Nvidia's GPU suppliers to memory makers like SK Hynix and Samsung.

That chokepoint status is why ASML's order commentary tends to move sentiment across the broader chip-equipment and foundry complex, even when the news itself — a target bump, a maintained rating — is unremarkable on its own terms.

What's next

ASML's next scheduled update is its third-quarter report, expected in October, which will show whether the €11 billion to €12 billion sales guidance holds and whether 2027 order books — which the company says are already largely full — continue to build. Until then, JPMorgan's $2,400 target functions mostly as a marker of how far Wall Street now expects the AI-driven capex cycle to run, rather than a signal of anything new.
