Apple hits record high as July rally nears best month in four years

In one sentence
Apple hit a record high Thursday, up 15.2% in July, as HSBC's $366 target outran a Street consensus below current prices.

Apple shares touched a fresh record on Thursday, rising 1.8% to extend a July run that has now reached 15.2% for the month — the stock's best monthly showing in roughly four years. Within the Magnificent Seven, only one member has outrun it this month; Apple sits second, and it has done so while the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) has actually shed close to 5% in July as semiconductor names sold off.

That divergence is the actual news here. Apple has spent much of the past two years as the group's laggard on AI narrative grounds — no chip franchise, no cloud arm generating the kind of capex headlines that moved Nvidia or Microsoft. This month the rotation has run the other way, with investors funneling into a company whose AI story is still, largely, a promise.

HSBC gave that rotation a fresh nudge Thursday, upgrading Apple to "Buy" from "Hold" and setting a $366 price target — about 10% above Thursday's close. The bank's move stands out against the broader analyst field: of 47 analysts covering the stock, 28 rate it Buy or higher, 16 sit at Hold, and three are at Sell or below, with an average price target of $315.79, more than 5% under current levels. HSBC, in other words, is more bullish than the Street's center of gravity, not confirming it.

What's actually driving it

The fundamentals underpinning the rally are thin. Apple has pushed through price increases on MacBooks, iPads, HomePods and Apple TV hardware, alongside a modest AppleCare+ subscription bump, and there is speculation the next iPhone cycle carries higher price tags too — pricing power rather than volume growth. Reports that Apple is exploring an AI chip acquisition have added a speculative kicker. And the company's leadership handover is now on the calendar: Tim Cook moves to executive chairman on September 1, handing the CEO role to hardware chief John Ternus after 25 years at the company, most recently overseeing the Apple Silicon transition.

None of that is a catalyst in the earnings sense — there's no print, no guidance raise, no product launch driving the move. What's visible is money moving within the Mag Seven cohort as AI-capex anxiety weighs on the more chip-exposed names, and Apple, insulated from that specific worry, catching the flow.

The next real test arrives with Apple's fiscal third-quarter earnings, expected in late July, when the market will find out whether the pricing moves are showing up in margin — or whether the rally has been running ahead of the numbers.
