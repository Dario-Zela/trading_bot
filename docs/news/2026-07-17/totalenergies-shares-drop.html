TotalEnergies shares slip 2.5% despite strong oil trading

Tour Total, TotalEnergies' headquarters in La Défense, Paris. — Wikipedia
In one sentence
TotalEnergies shares fell 2.5% on softer LNG-trading guidance even as the company raised its overall earnings outlook on strong oil trading.

TotalEnergies shares fell as much as 2.5% in early Thursday trading, sliding to around $78.71 and underperforming rivals BP and Shell, after the French major told investors its oil-trading desk would stay strong but its liquefied natural gas business would not.

That is, on its face, an odd reason for a sub-3% move. TotalEnergies wasn't cutting its overall earnings outlook — it was raising it. Wall Street had been modeling second-quarter earnings per share of $2.75, up from $1.57 a year earlier, and the company's own trading update pointed to stronger downstream and exploration results on top of resilient oil trading. But investors zeroed in on the one line that read weaker: integrated LNG.

What actually moved

Oil trading, TotalEnergies said, is set to hold at the same strong level it reached in the first quarter, when the war between Israel and Iran disrupted crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz and pushed prices to their highest since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The company estimated the Middle East conflict is still trimming its own production by about 210,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day for the quarter — down from a 360,000 bpd hit flagged three months ago, a sign the disruption is easing rather than worsening. Downstream — refining and petrochemicals — is expected to strengthen sharply from the first quarter, and exploration and production cash flow should rise by roughly $1 billion as average liquids prices climbed $17.90 a barrel sequentially.

Set against that, integrated LNG — a division that accounts for roughly a fifth of TotalEnergies' results — is headed for a significant decline. The company said gas-trading activity underperformed after a strong first quarter, hurt by a European market that stayed flat to lower and by volatility that kept counterparties on the sidelines rather than restocking storage ahead of winter. Notably, the average price TotalEnergies realized on its own equity LNG sales actually rose, to $10.20 per million British thermal units from $9.10 a year earlier — the weakness is specifically in trading margins, not in what the company is paid for its gas.

Reading the reaction

A move this size, on guidance this mixed, is closer to noise than signal. TotalEnergies didn't announce a writedown, a guidance cut to full-year numbers, or any structural change to its gas business — it flagged that one trading desk had a softer quarter than the last, inside a release that otherwise pointed to higher group earnings. Analysts still rate the stock a consensus Hold with an average price target near $83, comfortably above Thursday's level, and the shares trade at under 12 times earnings. Scotiabank has a price forecast as high as $97.

The distinction matters for anyone using this as a signal rather than a headline. A quarter of underperforming gas trading, in a market where volatility itself discouraged the position-taking that produces trading profits, is a wobble, not a repricing — unless it turns out to be the first data point in a longer run of thin European gas margins. That's a different, slower-moving story than a single day's stock move can tell.

TotalEnergies reports full second-quarter results on July 23, when it will disclose actual segment earnings rather than directional guidance. That release — and whether integrated LNG's softness looks like a one-off or the start of a trend — is the more useful thing to watch than Thursday's 2.5%.
