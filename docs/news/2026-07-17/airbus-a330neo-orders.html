Airbus eyes fresh A330neo orders after $10bn SAS deal

An Airbus A330neo, the widebody jet at the center of Airbus's latest order push. — Alex Cheban / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Airbus is in advanced talks to sell flynas and Condor more A330neos, deals that could land at next week's Farnborough Airshow.

Airbus is trying to turn one big order into a pattern. Two weeks after Scandinavian carrier SAS signed for up to 40 A330 family widebodies in a deal worth more than $10 billion, the planemaker is in advanced talks with Saudi budget carrier flynas and Germany's Condor over further A330neo purchases — agreements that people familiar with the discussions say could be signed as early as next week's Farnborough International Airshow.

In one sentence: Airbus is closing in on fresh A330neo orders from flynas and Condor that could be signed at Farnborough, building on SAS's $10 billion deal.

The SAS deal as backdrop

SAS's order, signed in Copenhagen on June 30 between chief executive Anko van der Werff and Airbus commercial sales chief Benoît de Saint-Exupéry, is the largest aircraft purchase in the carrier's 80-year history. It covers new A330-900neo jets alongside additional A330-300s, with list-price value above $10 billion and firm orders reported at 18 aircraft within the wider 40-jet program. The A330-900neo, powered by Rolls-Royce Trent 7000 engines, seats 287 to 303 passengers in three-class configuration and burns roughly 25% less fuel per seat than the aircraft it replaces — the pitch Airbus has used to sell the type since it entered service in 2018.

Flynas and Condor talks

Bloomberg and Reuters reported this week that flynas, the Saudi low-cost carrier expanding under the kingdom's Vision 2030 tourism push, is close to firming up eight more A330neos, which would take its total order book to roughly 30 of the type with first deliveries expected in 2027. Condor, the German leisure carrier, is separately discussing six aircraft that would extend a fleet already built around the A330neo to around two dozen jets — though industry sources have cautioned that the Condor talks are less advanced than flynas's and could still fall through before Farnborough opens.

Airbus's commercial aircraft chief, Christian Scherer, framed the flynas discussions in terms of commonality rather than raw growth: "The A330neo will allow flynas to further grow into widebody markets by building on the A320, benefiting from Airbus' unique commonality." That logic — narrow-body operators graduating to widebodies within the same parts and training ecosystem — is the same case Airbus has made to SAS and to Middle Eastern carriers building out long-haul networks.

What a cluster of orders would signal

No single order settles an argument about aircraft demand, but three widebody deals inside a month is a different signal than one. It would suggest airlines are still willing to commit billions to long-haul capacity even as macro conditions — tariff uncertainty, mixed signals on inflation, and jittery capital markets — have made other corporate spending more cautious. It also keeps pressure on Boeing, whose competing 787 program is contending with its own production and certification questions, to defend its widebody backlog at the same airshow.

For Rolls-Royce, the sole engine supplier on the A330neo via its Trent 7000, each additional order is effectively locked-in aftermarket revenue for decades of servicing. For Airbus, the more immediate value is momentum: a Farnborough news cycle stacked with widebody signings reinforces the sense that the A330neo, a jet some analysts once dismissed as a stopgap ahead of a re-engined successor, still has commercial legs.

None of the flynas or Condor terms are finalized, and neither airline has confirmed aircraft counts publicly. The Farnborough Airshow opens next week, and that is the first point at which any of this converts from reported talks into signed paper.
