Deals and restructuring: Lithium Africa's conditional DRC deal, Nestlé's French job cuts

In one sentence
Lithium Africa's conditional Manono West deal could bring Ganfeng and Glencore into a DRC lithium project, but terms are unpublished; Nestlé begins voluntary departures in France.

A little-known lithium developer has agreed a deal that, if it completes, would put two of the biggest names in the battery-metals chain next to one of Africa's most contested lithium districts. Lithium Africa has agreed a conditional transaction for Manono West in the Democratic Republic of Congo, according to IntelliNews and MT Newswires. Ganfeng, the Chinese lithium producer, and Glencore, the Swiss-listed miner and trader, are named in connection with the project.

The reports available give no price, no stake sizes and no list of conditions. The roles of Ganfeng and Glencore, whether as investors, offtakers or something else, are not confirmed either. The word that matters is conditional: the deal can still fail.

Who is lining up

The interest of the two named companies is easier to read than the deal itself. Ganfeng is a large integrated lithium producer. Glencore is one of the world's biggest commodity traders and a major operator in the DRC, where it is best known for copper and cobalt. A project that attracts both would pair a lithium specialist with a trader and miner that already has a Congolese footprint.

Manono sits in the DRC's Tanganyika province and has long been regarded as one of the country's significant lithium districts. Several parties have competed for rights there. That is why the identity of the backers carries more information than the headline.

There are gaps. Whether Ganfeng and Glencore are committed or only interested is not established. Nor is the size of any stake, or which conditions must be met, such as regulatory approvals, financing or licence confirmations. A deal that needs approvals in a jurisdiction with a history of contested mining rights has more ways to stall than most.

Why it matters

If completed, the deal would give two large players in the lithium chain exposure to a DRC asset. That bears on battery-metal supply and on the wider competition for Congolese resources, in which Chinese and Western-linked buyers have both been active. Until the terms and conditions are published, it is a watch item rather than a finished development.

Nestlé trims in France, buys more in Mexico

The other restructuring item is smaller in scale but points in a similar direction. Nestlé has begun 'voluntary departures' at a waters site in France, according to Just Drinks. Voluntary schemes are the usual first step in European workforce cuts, since they avoid compulsory redundancies, which are slower and more politically costly in France. The headcount and the site's name were not in the material available.

At the same time, Nestlé is increasing its coffee purchases from Mexican growers, who are protesting. The brief does not say what they are protesting about, and it would be wrong to guess at a link to Nestlé's buying. What the two items show together is a company trimming a legacy European bottled-water operation while leaning harder on a Latin American raw-material source. The France story also lands in a week when the country's economy is under pressure; see France slips into recession as exports sag.

What to watch

For Manono West, the next data point is a formal announcement or filing setting out the terms, the conditions and the long-stop date, and confirming what Ganfeng and Glencore are actually committing. Until then the named backers are the only hard information. For Nestlé, the questions are the number of posts affected in France and the grievance behind the Mexican growers' protests. Neither is yet on the record.
