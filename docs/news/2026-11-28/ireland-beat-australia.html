Ireland beat Australia with five tries in autumn finale

In one sentence
Ireland beat Australia 38-14 with five tries in London, closing a four-win November and a 10-from-12 year despite missing several senior players.

Ireland ended their year with a win. They beat Australia 38-14 at Allianz Stadium in London on Friday night, scoring five tries to two, in the fifth-place play-off of the inaugural Nations Championship. It was the last of four November wins and ended a calendar year of ten victories in twelve Tests.

The scoreline was more comfortable than the match. Ireland led 17-7 at the break after an error-strewn first half, in which Robbie Henshaw (26 minutes) and Nick Timoney (37 minutes, from a maul) crossed. Australia's Jeremy Williams had opened the scoring around the 22nd minute. Robert Baloucoune extended the lead early in the second half, and Australia's second try, from Kalani Thomas, kept them within reach for a while. Then James McNabney, off the bench, scored twice in the last seven minutes to put the result beyond doubt. Sam Prendergast kicked five conversions and a penalty, and was player of the match.

A makeshift side, a settled result

The win is better judged against who was missing. According to Tournaments.com, Ireland got through the month without Caelan Doris, Jack Conan, Garry Ringrose and Jack Crowley. They still beat Argentina, Fiji, South Africa and now Australia. It was Ireland's seventh successive win over the Wallabies.

Baloucoune scored five tries across the month, and Prendergast started all four November Tests. Those two are the clearest evidence that Ireland's depth is holding up. That is a narrower claim than "the attack has been transformed". Ireland were not fluent for much of Friday's first half, and Andy Farrell's own assessment was measured. He said he saw "greater determination within the squad to improve", as paraphrased in the Tournaments.com report. He added that more progress is needed. He was encouraged by how clinical Ireland were with their chances, given the problems they had with possession, territory and penalties.

That reading fits the match. Five tries from a side that struggled for control suggests a team that finishes well. It does not by itself show a team that dominates. Australia were not far behind for long stretches.

What the year leaves behind

The one blemish on 2026 came in July, when New Zealand beat Ireland 40-21 in Auckland. Fifth place in the Nations Championship is also a modest finish for a side with Ireland's recent record. So this is a good year without being a dominant one.

The question for the next World Cup cycle is whether the November results survive the return of the absentees. Doris, Ringrose and Crowley would normally be first choices. Farrell now has evidence that the squad around them can win, which gives him more selection options. Whether that depth turns into a better attack, rather than a deeper bench, will show when the Six Nations begins in the new year.

The score and try count come from several match reports, which agree on the final result. Their accounts of the order of scoring differ slightly.
