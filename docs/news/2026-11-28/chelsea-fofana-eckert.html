Chelsea lose again as fans turn on Fofana; Eckert irked by Southampton jibes at Boro

In one sentence
Chelsea's slump is reportedly turning fan anger on Wesley Fofana, while Southampton's Tonda Eckert is annoyed by Boro-related jibes, with details in both cases unconfirmed.

Chelsea's difficult run has found a focus. According to BBC Sport, supporters are now directing their frustration at defender Wesley Fofana after another poor result. Fans are looking for someone to blame for a slump that has no single cause.

The reporting available to this desk is thin. It gives no opponent, scoreline or league position for the latest defeat. It also gives no account of what was said or sung, or how many fans were involved. The claim that supporters have turned on Fofana therefore rests on BBC Sport's summary alone, and the extent of it is not established.

Why a defender takes the heat

The pattern is a familiar one. When a team loses repeatedly, the criticism tends to settle on whoever made the most visible mistake. A centre-back's error is easy to see and easy to replay. A midfield that gives up control, or a forward line that stops scoring, is harder to pin on anyone.

Fofana has been here before. His time at Chelsea has been interrupted by injuries and has drawn plenty of online argument. Fan reaction to him has been split even after wins. After a 1-0 victory over Bournemouth, some supporters on social media still called for him to be sold, despite match statistics that were solid.

The risk for Chelsea is that individual blame hides the wider problem. Whatever is behind the poor results, one defender cannot account for it. It becomes a bigger story only if the criticism spreads to the dressing room or becomes a question for the manager. Nothing in the reporting so far shows that it has.

Eckert and the Boro rivalry

Elsewhere, BBC Sport reports that Southampton head coach Tonda Eckert is irked by jibes aimed at the club's meeting with Middlesbrough in the Championship. The detail of who said what, and when, could not be confirmed.

The history between the two clubs explains the tension. Last season's play-off semi-final was marked by the "spygate" row. Eckert later took responsibility for the affair, which led to Southampton's expulsion from the play-offs. Middlesbrough were reinstated, but lost the final to Hull, who were promoted to the Premier League. Eckert walked out of one post-match press conference when asked whether he was a cheat. Boro's head coach, Kim Hellberg, was visibly angry about the episode. Southampton's owner called it a "mistake" and said it should not cost Eckert his job.

Against that backdrop, any jibe about the incident, whether from Boro supporters or others, is likely to be taken badly. This looks like a continuation of an old grievance rather than a new dispute.

What to watch

The two stories have little in common beyond the fixture list. In both, the pressure falls on a person who is in the spotlight for the wrong reasons. For Chelsea, the next fixture will show whether Fofana is still the target, and whether the manager defends him in public. For Eckert, the question is whether the Boro jibes lead to anything beyond a complaint, such as a fine or a charge from the football authorities.
