Police seek 100+ gig-goers in Co-op Live probe; Captain Tom charity applies to close

In one sentence
Greater Manchester Police want to trace 100+ people from a Don Toliver gig at Co-op Live, where an unknown substance prompted an evacuation and suspected organised robberies.

Greater Manchester Police are trying to trace more than 100 people who were at a Don Toliver concert at Co-op Live. They believe an unknown substance was released in the crowd so that a small group could rob concertgoers.

More than 12,000 people were evacuated from the Manchester arena. One woman was taken to hospital and 88 others were treated at the scene, according to reports of the police briefing. Some people described a "horrible smell", and some said they were coughing up blood. No arrests have been made, and the substance has not been identified.

What police are asking for

Detectives want to speak to anyone who saw the substance released, was robbed, or filmed the crowd. Phone footage and CCTV are being reviewed. Police had already logged nine complaints of jewellery theft from the night.

Tom Harding, the senior Greater Manchester Police officer leading the response, said detectives were working to identify the robbery suspects. Reports differ on his exact rank. Police believe organised criminals picked out individuals in the crowd, which would make this a targeted theft operation rather than a random disturbance. That is the force's working theory, not a finding.

One report says a spray or gas was released at two earlier Don Toliver shows in Europe, in Zurich and Paris. That comes from secondary coverage, and the BBC original should be treated as the reference.

The case raises a plain question for arena operators and promoters: how a substance can be released in a packed hall, and how a crowd of that size is cleared safely when it is. Co-op Live's operators have faced scrutiny before. The venue opened in 2024 after a troubled run of postponements.

A quieter ending

A separate item in the Beyond the tape pile concerns the Captain Tom Foundation. The charity took its name from Captain Sir Tom Moore, who walked laps of his garden in spring 2020, turned 100 that April and raised about £32m for NHS Charities Together. He died on 2 February 2021.

The foundation is reported to be applying to be struck off the charity register. That closure application could not be independently confirmed here. The record does show a long wind-down. The Charity Commission disqualified Moore's daughter, Hannah Ingram-Moore, and her husband, Colin, as trustees. The charity was later renamed the 1189808 Foundation after the family demanded his name be removed. The regulator said it continued to engage with the remaining trustee as the charity's future was determined.

What to watch

The next development in the Manchester inquiry is likely to be police releasing CCTV stills or suspect descriptions, or naming the substance once testing is done. The Charity Commission register for charity number 1189808 will show whether the closure application goes ahead.
