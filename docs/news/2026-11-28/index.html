DWP CYBER INCIDENT · By C. Tanaka

DWP takes almost all online pensions and benefits systems offline after 'serious cyber incident'

The DWP pulled universal credit, pension credit and other portals offline after a 'serious cyber incident'; payments continue, but claims that no data was compromised rest on an unfinished inquiry.

The Department for Work and Pensions has taken almost all of its online pensions and benefits systems offline after what it calls a 'serious cyber incident'. Unusual activity was detected on Thursday 26 November, and the department then pulled down the universal credit and pension credit portals along with other online services. The National Cyber Security Centre is investigating.

Regular benefit and pension payments are continuing. Downing Street says there is no evidence that personal records have been compromised, but that judgement is being made while the investigation is still running. For the millions of claimants who use these portals to manage their claims, the outage means disruption for as long as the systems stay down.

Two things are worth watching. The first is whether the NCSC's findings support the no-compromise line on personal data. The second is how quickly the portals return. Together they will show how well the state's digital services can withstand an attack on the systems people depend on.
