FTSE and Wall Street extend gains as AI fears ease; HSBC makes a bullish 2027 call

In one sentence
FTSE and Wall Street equities rose again as AI-selloff worries eased, while HSBC reportedly expects 2027 gains even with tech weak; no levels or primary note were verified.

Equities extended their gains on 28 November as worries about an AI-led selloff receded. The advance covered both London's FTSE 100 and Wall Street, according to the candidate reports behind this piece. Those reports gave no index levels, percentage moves or named trigger. They also did not say what, if anything, changed the mood. The move is therefore recorded here as direction only.

That limit matters. A rally described as a reversal of AI fears is a claim about causation, and nothing in the available material supports it. Equity markets rise for many reasons. These include rate expectations, earnings, positioning and quarter-end flows. "Fears receded" is the standard label for a day when prices went up and no one has yet found a better explanation.

The HSBC call

The more specific item is a forecast. HSBC's strategists are reported to expect equity gains in 2027. The report came from a secondary aggregator, GuruFocus, which says the call comes while technology stocks are struggling. If that is accurate, it is a bet that the broader market can rise without its tech leaders.

The primary research note has not been checked. The target level, the index it refers to and the earnings assumptions behind it are unknown. So is whether HSBC means a global, US or UK benchmark. A search found no HSBC 2027 target for the FTSE 100 or any other index. HSBC's strategists have been constructive on the AI trade throughout 2026. The bank lifted its S&P 500 year-end target earlier in the year on earnings optimism. A 2027 call that leans away from tech leadership would be a change of emphasis. Without the note, that reading is only an inference from the aggregator's summary.

What the two threads share

The two items sit awkwardly together. One says markets are calmer about AI. The other says tech is weak yet the wider market can still advance. Both can be true. A rally that does not depend on the biggest AI-linked stocks would explain how indices rise while tech lags. The material gives no breakdown of sectors or stocks to show whether that happened on the day.

The wider context is that AI exposure is under scrutiny across markets. This edition also covers Asian credit exposure to Nvidia and HSBC's discounted sale of a Stargate-linked loan. Neither is evidence about today's equity move. They do show that the funding side of the AI build-out is being questioned as well as the share prices.

What to watch

Two things would turn this from a market wrap into a story. The first is the HSBC note itself, which would give a level, a date and the reasoning for a market that rises without tech. The second is closing data showing whether the gains came from the AI-linked names or from the rest of the market. Until either is available, the supportable statement is narrow: equities rose again on 28 November, and a bank has said they should keep rising next year.
