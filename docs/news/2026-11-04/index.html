UK-EU · FOOD DEAL · By C. Tanaka

UK and EU agree draft food deal to cut Brexit paperwork, but industry concerns and political opposition remain

A draft UK-EU food-safety deal could cut Brexit paperwork from mid-2027, but industry concerns are unresolved and the 20 November summit has already slipped once.

The UK and European Union have agreed a draft sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement, the food-safety framework that governs animal and plant products crossing the border. It could cut the paperwork food exporters face, but it is only a draft. The Grocer reports that industry concerns about the text remain unresolved, and political opposition has not gone away.

SPS rules determine whether meat, dairy and produce need certificates and physical checks at each crossing. Those checks add cost and delay to every shipment, which is why the rules matter to shelf availability as well as to trade policy. A workable deal would also determine how closely the UK can align its food standards with EU rules, a question that goes beyond the paperwork itself.

The next milestone is a summit on 20 November, the date by which the draft is expected to be settled. Until then, exporters are working under the existing checks, and whether the text satisfies the businesses that would have to use it remains the open question.
