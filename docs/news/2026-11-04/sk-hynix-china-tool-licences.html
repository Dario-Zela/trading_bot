US delays licences for chip tools SK Hynix needs in China

In one sentence
Reuters reports Commerce has not approved US tool licences for SK Hynix's Dalian plant, stalling its China expansion and clouding a $45bn New York investment.

The US Commerce Department has not approved export licences for American chipmaking tools that SK Hynix wants to install at its Dalian plant in China, according to Reuters reports on 3–4 November. The reports say the delay is also holding up a planned $45bn investment in New York state. Washington's licence squeeze, aimed at Chinese firms, now bears on an allied memory maker's overseas factory.

What is being held up

The tools are for Dalian, the NAND flash plant SK Hynix acquired from Intel in a $9bn deal agreed in 2020, shortly before the US imposed its sweeping equipment export controls. According to Reuters-based accounts, Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler said at a meeting last week that Washington did not want to allow more American tools into China to "upgrade or expand capacity". That is a source's account of his position, not a direct quote.

The department has not formally denied a licence. The status is delay, not refusal, and negotiations continue. SK Hynix declined to comment on export licences.

The company's case rests on a distinction. It built the Dalian cleanroom in 2023, and says that installing equipment to fill the empty space would not expand capacity. Commerce has not accepted that reading.

Why it reaches New York

SK Hynix argues that more output from Dalian would help fund its US expansion. Its Solidigm unit plans about $45bn for two NAND plants in Marcy, New York, which could grow to roughly $100bn over time. Sources told Reuters that the Dalian decision is the key variable in that plan.

The pieces are tangled together:

  • Treasury would need to waive outbound investment restrictions for some of the tool purchases. It said it would wait to see what Commerce does.
  • Solidigm executives have met Commerce officials nearly 10 times since late August. Solidigm says it is "exploring options for building a NAND memory fab at potential locations around the world", with no decision made.
  • Solidigm has halted its IPO review, citing market conditions and share price trends, according to an SK Hynix statement of 23 October.
  • Oneida County Executive Anthony Picente said he hoped for an answer "in the next couple of weeks". A federal investment tax credit expires at the end of the year.

Separately, Solidigm and Commerce are negotiating the equipment export licences needed to keep Dalian running in 2027.

Background: the end of the exemption

In August 2025 the US revoked the exemptions that let Samsung, SK Hynix and Intel ship advanced tools to their Chinese fabs without case-by-case approval. In December, Reuters reported that Samsung and SK Hynix had been granted an annual licence covering 2026 shipments, after their validated end-user status lapsed on 31 December. The present dispute is about what falls outside that arrangement: new tools for expansion or upgrades.

This article could not verify the share of SK Hynix's output that comes from its Chinese plants. The reports reviewed do not give it.

What to watch

A tool delay of this kind constrains growth more than existing production, since running equipment is covered. A refusal would cap Dalian's capacity at today's level, which matters for the NAND market and for rivals including Samsung. It would also signal that US export controls reach the overseas plants of allied chipmakers.

The first test is the answer on the Dalian cleanroom, which Picente expects within weeks. The second is the 2027 licence negotiation. The third is the end-of-year expiry of the tax credit, which sets the deadline for the New York decision.
