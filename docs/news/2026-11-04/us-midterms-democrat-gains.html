Democrats claim Senate wins in Georgia and Texas as midterm results come in; Trump says he will accept the outcome

In one sentence
Democrats are credited with Senate wins in Georgia and Texas, but none of it is confirmed by AP calls or official counts, and the 73% figure is a market price.

Democrats are being credited with Senate victories in Georgia and Texas, and in some accounts South Carolina, after Tuesday's midterm elections. As of publication, none of those claims could be matched to an Associated Press race call or an official count. The one figure circulating with them is a price, not a result: Democratic Senate odds on prediction markets stood at about 73%.

What is claimed, and what is not shown

The account that has spread most widely says Jon Ossoff held Georgia for the Democrats. It also says Texas state representative James Talarico won the Texas Senate seat, which would be the first Democratic Senate win in the state since 1988. A third claim, that a Democrat took South Carolina, comes from a candidate's own statement. It has not been checked against any count.

A search for AP calls on these races returned only coverage of Ossoff's 2021 runoff win over David Perdue, which he took by roughly 0.56 percentage points. Nothing from 2026 turned up, so no vote totals, margins or call times can be given here.

The 73% figure needs the most care. A prediction-market price is the aggregate view of traders about who will control the chamber. It was set before the results were known, and it moves with every batch of returns. A figure of 73% means traders favoured Democrats. It does not mean Democrats have won anything, and it says nothing about any single state. A Democratic Senate majority would also depend on more seats than these.

The word "sweeping" has the same problem. A sweep implies several decisive calls. What is on offer so far is a few unconfirmed ones, one of which rests on a candidate's say-so.

Why Texas would matter

If confirmed, Texas would be the largest part of the story. Republicans have won every statewide race there for decades, and the state's size makes it central to both parties' calculations about where the Senate map can be redrawn. A Democratic win would force both parties to reassess how far the Sun Belt has moved. It would not by itself show a national shift, since one candidate, one year and one electorate can all be unusual.

Other reports in the same cluster are also unconfirmed. One says Governor Greg Abbott, in office since 2015, conceded to Democrat Gina Hinojosa. The sources cited are NBC News and Fox 7 Austin, and no margin or quotes accompany the claim. Another says Florida elected its first Black governor, a Republican, according to a BBC World report. Neither could be checked against official results. A Republican Florida win would complicate any story of a uniform Democratic wave, and an Abbott defeat would point the other way. Both claims cannot yet be used to support a pattern.

Trump and the question of acceptance

The day after the vote, Trump reportedly told the New York Times he is "absolutely willing" to accept the results and would "work something out" with Democrats. Benzinga carries the same account, so it appears to rest on a single source. The Times text could not be found, and the wording has not been verified.

The statement also sits awkwardly with his earlier remarks. In other interviews this year he said he would accept the midterm results if the elections were "honest", and that if not, "something else has to happen". A pledge to accept results while they are still being counted costs little. The test comes if races he cares about go against his party, and the language he has used leaves room for him to dispute them.

What to watch

The first checks are the AP calls and the certified counts in Georgia, Texas and South Carolina. Texas and Georgia will also need to be set against how many seats each party must win for control. Close margins could mean recounts, and certification deadlines would then decide when the results become final. Until those arrive, the supportable statement is that Democrats had a good night in some races, that traders favoured them, and that the full scale of it remains unconfirmed.
