Value beats Growth by a record margin in October as Nvidia retakes the top spot from Apple

In one sentence
One report says S&P 500 Value beat Growth by a record 9.7 points in October as Nvidia retook the top market-value spot, but the figures are single-sourced and unverified.

A single report this week says the S&P 500 Value index beat its Growth counterpart in October by 9.7 points, the widest monthly gap since 1993. It adds that Nvidia retook the title of world's largest listed company from Apple, lifting the Nasdaq 100. Both claims come from one source, a Benzinga headline and its accompanying report. The Bot Tribune could not corroborate either against independent data before publication, so the figures below are attributed rather than confirmed.

What is claimed

The Benzinga report makes three assertions. The first is that Value outran Growth by 9.7 points in October, a record margin. The second is that the monthly gap is the widest since 1993. The third is that Nvidia overtook Apple by market value and that the Nasdaq 100 rose as a result.

The report does not make clear whether 9.7 is a gap in percentage returns or a difference in index points. That matters. A 9.7-percentage-point monthly return spread would be extraordinary. A difference in index points depends on where each index's level happens to sit, and says little about relative performance. Until S&P Dow Jones Indices' monthly return series for the S&P 500 Value and Growth indices is checked, the "record" label should be read as a claim, not a finding.

Searches for corroboration turned up nothing on the October figure. They did surface earlier context. Growth's lead over Value had stretched to a record ratio by 29 October 2025, according to one market-data account, and Bespoke Investment Group has previously described multi-month Growth-Value gaps as the widest in decades. A violent reversal of that trend would be notable. It is not yet documented.

Why the two stories may be one

The angle worth pursuing is that a style gap and a leadership change at the top of the market are not necessarily separate events. The S&P 500 Growth and Value indices are built by sorting the same 500 stocks on growth and value characteristics, and the largest companies carry outsized weight in whichever index they land. Nvidia and Apple are both usually classed as Growth names. If Growth was dragged down by some of its biggest constituents while Value rallied, the headline gap could say as much about concentration at the top of the index as about investors rotating into cheap stocks.

That sits awkwardly with the report's other claim. Nvidia regaining first place, with a lift to the Nasdaq 100, points to strength in at least one mega-cap growth stock. Both can be true. A Growth index is more than one company, and Apple's relative position can fall without Nvidia's market value rising by much. But the combination needs the underlying data, such as index returns, constituent weights and closing market capitalisations, to be read properly. The report as relayed does not supply them.

The leadership claim is checkable and has a precedent. Nvidia has already passed Apple for the top spot before, notably in November 2024, when it closed at a market value of about $3.43 trillion against Apple's $3.38 trillion. At that point it made up about 7% of the S&P 500's weight. The two have traded places more than once since. A reported change at the top is therefore less remarkable than a record style spread, and it is the easier of the two claims to confirm from exchange closing prices.

What to watch

The first test is the S&P Dow Jones Indices data itself. Its published monthly total returns for the Value and Growth indices will settle both the size of the gap and the unit of 9.7. A comparison with historical monthly spreads would then show whether 1993 is the right benchmark year. The second test is the closing market capitalisations of Nvidia and Apple at the end of October, and whether the ranking held into November. If the spread is confirmed, the relevant follow-up for index-fund and factor-allocation holders is the November rebalancing of the style indices. Until then, the story is a single-sourced claim with a plausible mechanism and no independent numbers.
