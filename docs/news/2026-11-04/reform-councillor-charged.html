Reform UK councillor charged with 12 counts of rape and other offences

In one sentence
Reform UK's Hampshire group leader George Madgwick faces 35 charges including 12 of rape, which he is yet to answer in court; what the party knew about him is not yet established.

A Reform UK councillor who leads the party's group on Hampshire County Council has been charged with 35 offences, 12 of them rape, in a case that has put the party's candidate selection under scrutiny. Whether that scrutiny is deserved depends on what Reform knew and when. The public record does not yet say.

George Madgwick, 37, represents North East Havant on the county council. The Crown Prosecution Service announced the charges on Tuesday. Alongside the 12 counts of rape, he is charged with two counts of sexual assault, two of assault by penetration and two of making indecent images of a child. He also faces eight counts of actual bodily harm, two of controlling and coercive behaviour, and single counts of threatening to kill, false imprisonment and stalking, according to Novara Media. The allegations involve four complainants and date from 2008 to 2021.

He is due to appear at Portsmouth Magistrates' Court on 25 November. No plea has been reported. ITV News reports that he has said he will step down as Reform's leader on the council and sit as an independent until cleared. Reform UK said it had suspended him with "immediate effect".

The timeline

Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary began investigating on 24 July 2025, after a report of a sexual offence. Madgwick was arrested on 6 January 2026. Prosecutors say they found sufficient evidence and that a prosecution is in the public interest.

The CPS also reminded the public that the defendant has a right to a fair trial and urged against reporting or commentary that could prejudice the proceedings. Reform leaders have already been warned over comments on a separate rape case, so the caution is a live one. This article therefore sets out the charges as allegations only.

What is not established

The vetting question is the obvious one, and the available material does not answer it. The offences are alleged to have happened between 2008 and 2021. The police investigation began only in July 2025. Reporting so far does not say when Madgwick was selected as a candidate or elected. It does not say what checks Reform ran, or whether anyone in the party had been told of complaints before the police inquiry.

Those gaps matter. If the allegations were unknown to the party and to the police until 2025, a failure of vetting would be hard to show. Candidate checks by any party rely largely on self-disclosure, public records and local intelligence, and they cannot surface what nobody has reported. If the party had earlier warnings and did nothing, that would be a different story. Nothing published so far points to either.

Hampshire County Council has said that being charged does not in itself disqualify a councillor from holding office. In practice that leaves the seat with Madgwick unless he resigns or is convicted and sentenced, a point on which local government law is much less strict than the party's own suspension powers.

What to watch

The first marker is the hearing in Portsmouth on 25 November, when the case will probably be sent to the Crown Court. The second is whether Reform says when it first heard of any complaint, and whether it reviews how its candidates are checked. A party that has expanded rapidly in local government in the past two years is likely to be asked for that account. Until it gives one, the charges remain allegations and the vetting question remains open.
