Welsh mini-budget passes after Labour-Plaid deal

In one sentence
The Senedd passed a £354m supplementary budget 54-39 after Labour backed Plaid Cymru's minority government in return for £60m for additional learning needs.

The Welsh government has got its second supplementary budget for 2026-27 through the Senedd, but only after the largest opposition party was bought in. On Tuesday evening MSs approved the £354m spending package by 54 votes to 39. The vote came nearly four months after the first version was defeated, and it passed only because Welsh Labour agreed to back a Plaid Cymru-led minority administration in return for extra money for children with additional learning needs.

The arrangement reverses the usual picture of Welsh politics. Labour ran Wales for the whole of the devolution era. It now sits on the opposition benches and holds the votes that decide whether the government's spending plans survive. Plaid Cymru ministers lack a majority, so every budget has to be negotiated with someone.

A defeat in July, a deal in October

The earlier supplementary budget was rejected by the Senedd on 14 July. Most of its measures were carried over into the version voted on this week. The sticking point was funding for additional learning needs (ALN), the support system for pupils with special educational requirements, which schools and councils say is under-resourced.

Labour sought £100m. Before the July vote, ministers had offered £40m a year for three years. The deal struck in October settled on £60m for this financial year, which Labour's finance spokesperson, Huw Thomas, presented as 50% more than ministers had originally put forward. Of the total, £44.8m goes to schools, £11.2m to local authorities, £2.5m to further education and £1.5m to a sustainability plan. Funding beyond this year was left to later budget talks.

The wider £354m package also pays for NHS waiting-time reductions, childcare for two-year-olds (£55m), school repairs and free school meals for secondary pupils from families on Universal Credit. Reports differ on the exact NHS figure, so the main lines are given here rather than every number.

What each side said

Finance Minister Elin Jones spoke of "constructive discussions" with Labour and a "broad recognition that these pressures are real and require action." Mr Thomas was less warm. He said the negotiations had been harder than they needed to be, and urged ministers to move quickly: "Schools cannot afford lengthy delays and bureaucratic obstacles."

The other opposition parties stayed outside the bargain. Conservative Peter Fox called the budget "business as usual wrapped in different colours," while Reform's finance spokesperson, Cai Parry Jones, questioned Labour's support and called for cuts to overseas spending.

Academics at Wales Fiscal Analysis described the ALN money as a "stopgap measure" and said wider pressures on public services remain unresolved. The NAHT teaching union is also still in dispute with ministers over school leaders' pay and ALN funding, and the deal does not appear to settle it.

Bargaining as the norm

This is not new. Welsh budgets have rarely passed on a single party's numbers: earlier Labour governments relied on deals with Plaid Cymru and, in March 2025, the Liberal Democrats' Jane Dodds. In January, the 2026-27 budget cleared after Plaid struck a deal worth nearly £300m with Labour, then in government. What has changed is who is doing the asking, and the July defeat showed that failure is a real possibility rather than a formality.

The result is a budget process in which the Senedd's opposition extracts specific, costed commitments, and ministers must find the money within a fixed Welsh block grant. That favours narrow, headline-friendly concessions of the £60m kind over larger structural choices, which are harder to settle in a bilateral negotiation.

What comes next

The supplementary budget is the foundation for the Welsh Government's draft budget for 2027-28, due on 17 November. The ALN dispute was settled for this year only, so Labour is likely to press the same issue again, and the same arithmetic of 54 votes against 39 will have to be assembled once more.

Note: this report rests mainly on Nation.Cymru and Senedd Research; the BBC report the brief cited was not independently retrieved.
