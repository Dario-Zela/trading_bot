ANC under pressure to reverse South Africa's decline

In one sentence
South Africa votes in municipal elections today, with the ANC, which lost its majority in 2024, under pressure to show it can fix failing services, unemployment and corruption.

South Africans vote in municipal elections today, and the party that has governed the country since apartheid ended arrives at the polls with its authority weaker than at any point in three decades. The African National Congress is being asked, by voters and increasingly by its own leadership, to show that it can fix the things that have gone wrong: failing local services, high unemployment, crime and corruption.

President Cyril Ramaphosa announced the date, Wednesday 4 November, on 30 April, and the local government minister, Velenkosini Hlabisa, gazetted it in August. The election date was chosen to avoid the festive season and the January school return. The Electoral Commission says the number of registered political parties has risen from 508 to 620 as the vote approached. A more crowded field is one sign of how far the old certainties have loosened.

From majority to coalition

The pressure has a clear starting point. At the general election on 29 May 2024 the ANC took about 40% of the national vote. That was its first result below 50% and its first loss of a parliamentary majority since 1994, and it was some 17 points below its 2019 showing. The party then formed a Government of National Unity with the Democratic Alliance and smaller parties, an arrangement that would have been hard to imagine a decade ago, given how bitterly the two had fought.

Municipal contests matter because that is where voters meet the state: water and electricity, refuse collection, roads, policing. They are also where the ANC has been losing ground for years. In the 2021 local elections it fell below 50% nationally for the first time, to roughly 46%, and several large metros have since been run by shifting coalitions. Rivals on the left, notably the Economic Freedom Fighters, have been taking votes from it, while the DA has competed for those who want competent administration.

An admission of trouble

The party has not pretended otherwise. In December 2025 it said it faced a crisis of corruption, weak governance and persistent racial inequality, and pledged to reform. Reports this year describe a six-point plan aimed at tackling unemployment, corruption, inequality and crime, with the explicit aim of reversing the slide in support.

That candour carries a risk. A party that concedes its problems invites voters to judge it on whether they have eased. I could not locate a specific new poll or a coalition rupture to explain the timing of the latest commentary; the trigger is the election itself, and the underlying complaints are long-standing rather than new.

What to watch

The first test is the ANC's national share against the roughly 46% it won in 2021 and the 40% of 2024. A figure clearly below the earlier mark would suggest the 2024 result was a floor not yet reached. A steadier showing would give Ramaphosa's reform push some credibility.

The second is what follows in the councils. Where no party wins outright, coalition bargaining in the big metros will test whether the national partnership between the ANC and the DA carries down to the local level, and whether the unity government's central claim, that parties with different views can govern together, survives contact with the municipalities. Results from the Electoral Commission are expected over the coming days.
