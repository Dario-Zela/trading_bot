Roche shares jump as Morgan Stanley flags drug pipeline breakout

Roche's headquarters towers in Basel, Switzerland — Michielverbeek / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Morgan Stanley upgraded Roche to overweight, citing a bigger-than-expected US launch for its giredestrant breast cancer pill, lifting shares toward a three-year high.

Roche shares climbed toward a three-year high this week after Morgan Stanley analysts Kapila and Boutherin upgraded the Swiss drugmaker to "overweight" from "equal-weight" and named it a top pick, replacing AstraZeneca in that slot. The bank lifted its price target to CHF 410 from CHF 295 — a jump that implies roughly 12% upside from Roche's CHF 364.70 close on August 6 — and raised its ADR target to $63 from $46.

Unlike much of the analyst chatter that moves pharma stocks on vague optimism, this note comes with a specific trigger: giredestrant, Roche's oral treatment for early-stage, ER-positive breast cancer, which the bank now expects to launch faster and bigger in the US than previously modelled.

What Changed the Analysts' Minds

Morgan Stanley forecasts roughly CHF 660 million ($800 million) in first-year US sales for giredestrant, with upside toward $1-1.2 billion, and puts peak sales across the full adjuvant program near CHF 7 billion. Combined with other indications still in the pipeline, the bank's total peak-sales estimate for the drug reaches CHF 9 billion ($11 billion) — well above the Street's CHF 6.6 billion consensus. Two FDA decisions are due before year-end: November 30 for the lidERA study and December 18 for evERA.

The giredestrant story is not free of scar tissue. The drug failed its primary endpoint in a first-line metastatic breast cancer trial in March, a setback that forced Roche's own management to walk back some of the more expansive sales talk. Chief executive of pharmaceuticals Teresa Graham has since pegged peak sales at "well north of" CHF 3 billion — a more conservative figure than Morgan Stanley's, and a reminder that the bank's numbers sit at the bullish end of a wide analyst range; Jefferies, for one, has modelled peak sales closer to CHF 1.2 billion.

Beyond the Headline Drug

Morgan Stanley's case for Roche rests on more than one asset. The note also flags afimkibart, an ulcerative colitis treatment the bank sizes at more than CHF 3 billion in peak sales; Columvi, a blood cancer therapy where new SKYGLO trial data points to a roughly CHF 1.6 billion opportunity; and trontinemab, an Alzheimer's candidate the bank thinks could exceed $5 billion at peak. None of those figures are confirmed regulatory outcomes — they are Morgan Stanley's projections, built on trial data that in giredestrant's case has already surprised in both directions this year.

On the back of the pipeline case, the bank also lifted its earnings estimates: 2026 core EPS of CHF 20.73, about 3% above consensus, rising to CHF 22.96 in 2027, roughly 5% ahead of the Street. Group sales are pegged at CHF 62.7 billion for 2026. At around 15.5 times 2027 estimated earnings, Roche now trades at roughly a 15% premium to its European large-cap pharma peers — a premium that only holds if the pipeline delivers on schedule.

What to Watch

The two FDA decisions in November and December will be the first real tests of Morgan Stanley's thesis, giving a concrete date for the market to mark the call against actual regulatory outcomes rather than analyst modelling. Until then, the share move reflects one bank's reappraisal of trial data and launch timing — a legitimate reason for a re-rating, but not yet the kind of company-disclosed catalyst that settles the debate between the CHF 9 billion bull case and the more guarded numbers coming from Roche's own executives and rival analysts.
