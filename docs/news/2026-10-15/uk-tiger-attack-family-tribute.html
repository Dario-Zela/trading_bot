Family of man killed in UK tiger attack pay tribute

In one sentence
A 23-year-old man died after entering a tiger's enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park; his family have paid tribute, and the questions now concern how he got in, not big-cat licensing.

The angle that prompted this piece does not hold. A man's death in a tiger enclosure in England has been followed by a public tribute from his family, but the animal was not privately kept. Reporting so far puts it in a zoological park, and the open questions are about how he got in, not about who licensed the tiger.

He was William Chapman, 23, according to ITV News. He died on Saturday 10 October at Yorkshire Wildlife Park near Doncaster. South Yorkshire Police were called at about 1.35pm after reports that a man had entered the tiger enclosure. He died at the scene. Police said there was no wider risk to the public. How he came to be inside the enclosure was not immediately clear.

The family's tribute

His family described a warm, engaging young man, ITV reports, and said they were devastated. They said he had lived with significant mental illness over a number of years. They asked people to stop sharing offensive material about him, and said they hope the authorities establish why it happened. His former stage school said it was saddened and would remember him fondly.

The request about material is aimed at footage that circulated on social media, apparently showing a man in the enclosure shortly before the attack. South Yorkshire Police had already urged people not to share it.

The tiger and the park

The animal is Altai, an Amur tiger living in the park's Land of the Tigers reserve, which opened in 2011. He was not hurt. The park says he is "safe and well and will not be euthanised". The park was evacuated on the day, stayed closed on Sunday, and said staff were receiving specialist support. It says it is working closely with the authorities to establish what happened.

That is a different picture from the one a big-cat story usually raises in Britain. The Dangerous Wild Animals Act 1976 governs private keepers, and the long-running argument about it concerns individuals holding lions, tigers and other species at home or on small sites. Zoos are licensed separately, under the Zoo Licensing Act 1981, and are inspected. Nothing in the reporting seen so far suggests Altai's keeping arrangements are in dispute, and no regulator has said it is examining them.

What comes next

The immediate questions are about the perimeter: how a person reached the enclosure, and whether that route was open, breached or climbed. Police will establish that, and the park's local authority licensing inspectors and the Health and Safety Executive could be expected to take an interest in any safety findings. An inquest will follow in due course. The park has not said when it will reopen.
