Hospitality sector pledges up to 100,000 jobs for unemployed young people

In one sentence
The hospitality industry's offer of up to 100,000 youth jobs is a ceiling rather than a commitment, and it arrives alongside a Budget lobbying push on employer costs.

Britain's hospitality industry has offered up to 100,000 jobs to unemployed young people. The offer comes while ministers fret over youth joblessness and while the sector itself says higher employer costs are squeezing it. The figure comes from the Guardian's political coverage and has not been independently verified. The detail that decides its worth is whether the jobs are new or already planned vacancies, and the pledge as reported does not say.

A volunteered offer

The wording matters. "Up to 100,000" is a ceiling, not a commitment, and an industry pledge is not a contract. The version of this exercise run in 2013 is a useful yardstick. The British Hospitality Association's "Big Hospitality Conversation" campaign then promised more than 30,000 opportunities for young people by 2015, a mix of permanent roles, apprenticeships and paid placements. The new figure is more than three times that, and it is made for a labour market the industry describes as much harder.

The sector has a good claim to being the right place to look. UKHospitality says hospitality accounts for 31% of first jobs for people aged 16 to 24. Pubs, hotels and restaurants hire young workers in volume, with few formal qualification barriers, and they staff shifts that suit people who are studying or just starting out.

The cost argument

The same trade body has spent recent weeks lobbying on cost. It says the sector has lost around 100,000 jobs over two years. It warns that a further 100,000 could go, and it blames minimum wage rises, business rates changes and employer National Insurance. It wants the Budget to cut employer NI costs and to raise the hospitality employer threshold to £10,000 by the end of the Parliament.

The pledge and the lobbying therefore point in opposite directions. An industry that reports shedding about 100,000 jobs is now offering up to 100,000 to a group the government wants in work. The reasonable reading is that the offer is also a bargaining position ahead of the Budget. That does not make it insincere, but the offer and the Budget ask belong together, and it is fair to ask whether the jobs depend on the cost relief.

What would make it count

Three things would turn a headline into a measurable change:

  • Additionality: whether the roles are net new rather than ordinary turnover relabelled.
  • Funding and terms: hours, pay and duration, and whether any support from the Treasury is expected.
  • Tracking: published figures on starts and retention, which the 2013 campaign had little of.

Youth unemployment is a measure ministers will be judged on. The sector's cost lobbying and any Budget response will show soon how far the pledge is conditional. The Budget is the next point at which that becomes visible. Thin August growth already leaves the chancellor little room for manoeuvre, as covered in our Budget squeeze piece.
