Jackson steps up again as Aston Villa's revival continues

In one sentence
Nicolas Jackson has scored in consecutive league games, including Villa's 2-2 draw with Brentford, but one win from six leaves Villa mid-table and short of a revival.

Aston Villa's season is better described as a slow start that is easing than as a revival. The evidence for the second reading is a striker's goals and a draw. It is not yet the league table.

Nicolas Jackson, signed from Chelsea on 28 August, scored the equaliser in Villa's 2-2 draw with Brentford at Villa Park on 10 October. He has now scored in consecutive league games. Yehor Yarmoliuk had put Brentford ahead, and Jackson levelled within two minutes, in the 12th minute. John McGinn put Villa in front in the 76th minute, but Kristoffer Ajer equalised less than three minutes later.

A table that does not yet say 'revival'

The standings are the awkward part. After six league matches Villa had five points and a single win, and reports put them 15th. Wikipedia's season page lists them 16th. Whichever is right, it is the position of a club still finding its feet, and the home record under Unai Emery has been a particular complaint. A draw after leading late is not the stuff of momentum.

Why Jackson matters

The fee was a club record, reported at £65m including add-ons, with Reuters putting the initial payment at £47.5m. He signed a five-year deal and is reunited with Emery, who managed him at Villarreal. He replaced Ollie Watkins, who left for Al-Hilal. Replacing the club's long-serving centre-forward was always going to be judged on goals, and consecutive league scores are the first sign the investment is paying back. Two games are a small sample.

What to watch

The test is whether Jackson's scoring turns draws such as the Brentford one into wins, particularly at Villa Park. One victory from six is the number he has to move. Until it changes, Villa's progress is more a promising trend than a recovery.
