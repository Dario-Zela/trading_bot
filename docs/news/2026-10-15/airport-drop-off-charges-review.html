Ministers to review airport drop-off charges after sharp rises

In one sentence
Ministers will review UK airport drop-off fees under a clause in the Civil Aviation Bill, after average charges rose £1.70 to £7 and Gatwick reached £10, but the scope is unset.

UK ministers have said they will review what airports charge to drop passengers at the terminal. The move follows sharp rises in the fees, and it puts a routine cost of air travel under government scrutiny. The review's terms and timetable have not been settled.

The review is not a new announcement from nowhere. It stems from a clause in the Civil Aviation (Consumer Protection and Regulatory Reform) Bill. Peers added the clause in July, and the government has since said it will not resist it.

How the review came about

The Lords backed the amendment narrowly, by 142 votes to 138, on 13 July, according to the Bill tracker Beyond the Vote. As drafted, the Transport Secretary would have to review the effect of drop-off charges on airport users within six months of the Act passing, and lay the findings before Parliament (UK Parliament).

At the Commons second reading on 9 September, Transport Minister Keir Mather said the government would accept a review. He added that ministers "need to think carefully about the terms of such a review", according to Taxi Point's account of the debate. Terms of reference and a deadline have not been published. In Scotland, the government has recommended that Holyrood consent to the clause and has said any recommendations would be non-binding (Scottish Parliament).

What the fees look like

The rises are well documented. The RAC found that 16 of the UK's 20 busiest airports had raised drop-off charges since last summer, with the average fee up £1.70 to £7 (Motoring Research).

At the top end, Gatwick's charge for a 10-minute stay rose by £3 to £10 from 6 January. It had been £6 in 2024 and £7 in May 2025. The airport blamed in part a more than doubling of its business rates. Reports differ on Heathrow, which most sources put at £7 from January, though one July table lists £6. Manchester charges £5.50 for five minutes, and drivers who overstay face a £100 parking charge notice. In the Commons, the shadow transport secretary, Richard Holden, said Luton's fee had gone from £2 to £7. Another MP said Manchester's charges ran up to £25 for half an hour.

What MPs want it to cover

The scope is the open question. MPs raised transparency, the level of charges, the ability of people to pay and the provision of free drop-off points. Scott Arthur MP called for Blue Badge holders to be exempt. Taxi and private-hire operators want the review to examine access fees and waiting facilities, and the effect on fares.

Ruth Cadbury, who chairs the Transport Committee, said a review of charges alone would be "just scratching the surface", and called public transport to airports "woeful" against international competitors. The government's own line is that the charges help manage congestion, meet public transport targets and fund infrastructure. Ministers also say the fees are already covered by consumer and competition law.

What to watch

Two things will decide whether the review has teeth. The first is the Bill's progress through the Commons and Royal Assent; the six-month clock starts only when it becomes law. The second is the terms of reference, which will show whether ministers examine pricing or only disclosure. The Scottish Government's description of the outcome as non-binding suggests the latter is the likelier ceiling.
