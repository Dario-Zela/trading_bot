Southgate does not see a return to Premier League management

In one sentence
Gareth Southgate has ruled out a return to Premier League management, BBC Sport reports, though no club approach or new detail has emerged.

Gareth Southgate does not expect to manage in the Premier League again. The former England manager has closed the door on a return to club football in England's top flight, BBC Sport reports.

The report as summarised to this desk names no club, no approach and no timetable. This is a statement of intent, not a job story. No vacancy is attached to it and no rival bidder is involved.

A long way from Middlesbrough

Southgate's only previous spell in club management was at Middlesbrough, where he took charge in 2006. The club were relegated from the Premier League in 2009 and he left that October. He then spent several years away from the touchline before the Football Association moved him from the under-21s to the senior job in 2016.

That is a thin club record set beside what followed. England reached the 2018 World Cup semi-finals, and the Euro 2020 final, which was played in 2021, ended in a penalty shoot-out defeat to Italy at Wembley. He resigned in July 2024, after England lost the Euro 2024 final 2-1 to Spain.

Why the door is worth noting

Since leaving, Southgate has been a name that surfaces whenever a big job opens, and every vacancy brings fresh speculation about whether the man who restored England's tournament credibility might want the daily grind of a league season. Ruling out the Premier League removes one obvious destination from that guessing.

It does not settle the rest. The reported comments, as relayed, cover the English top flight only. They say nothing about a return to international management, a role abroad, or a post in the game's administration. Nor do they say whether this is a firm decision or a reading of his present mood.

The English game also has little visible need of him at the moment. Club managers are judged week by week, and the sort of patient, squad-building work Southgate did with England sits awkwardly with that rhythm. His own record suggests he knows it.

What to watch

The test is the next high-profile vacancy, in England or elsewhere. If his name is linked to a job and he says nothing, the door may be less shut than it sounds. If he repeats the line, the speculation should fade.
