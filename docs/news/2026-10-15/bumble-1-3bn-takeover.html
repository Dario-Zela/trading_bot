Bumble agrees $1.3bn takeover

In one sentence
Ithaca Holdings will take Bumble private for $6.05 a share in cash, valuing it at about $1.3bn, a 138% premium to its pre-news price and a tenth of its 2021 peak.

Bumble has agreed to be taken private by Ithaca Holdings in an all-cash deal that values the dating-app group at about $1.3bn (£980m). The price is roughly a tenth of the company's 2021 stock-market peak. Ithaca's two principals, Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman, will become co-chief executives, and they describe the purchase as "a bet on the future of humanity".

The phrase comes from the buyers themselves, and it is promotional. The terms say more about what is being bought.

What the terms say

Bumble's Class A shareholders will receive $6.05 a share in cash. That is a premium of about 138% to the unaffected close on 9 October, and about 119% above the 30-day volume-weighted average price, according to the company's announcement. Working back from those figures, the shares were trading at around $2.50 before the news. The stock jumped after the announcement, but the sources differ on the resulting market value: one report puts it above $580m, another near $900m. Neither figure is confirmed here.

The $1.3bn headline is also not fully defined. The company says the deal "values Bumble at $1.3 billion" but does not say whether that is equity value or enterprise value, so the treatment of Bumble's debt is unclear. The announcement does not name the financing sources, and it gives no closing date. It does say Bumble will file its third-quarter 10-Q by 9 November.

The approval route is unusually quick. An independent special committee recommended the deal, and the board approved it unanimously with founder Whitney Wolfe Herd recused. Affiliates of Blackstone, the early backer, and of Wolfe Herd then delivered shareholder approval by written consent, so no vote of the wider shareholder base is needed. Shareholders will receive an information statement instead. Wolfe Herd has said she will remain "actively involved".

What Ithaca is buying

The business is shrinking. Paying users fell 16.4% year on year in the second quarter, to 3.2 million, according to one commentary on the filings. Younger users are spending less time on dating apps, and the company has posted losses. In August Bumble dropped its signature rule that only women could start a conversation, which suggests management was looking for growth outside its original product.

That context explains the premium. A 138% jump on a share price near $2.50 is a rescue price for holders as much as an expensive one for the buyer. The 2021 peak above $14bn is not a useful benchmark for what Ithaca is paying.

Ithaca describes itself as a long-term holding company for technology businesses. It plans to develop Bumble's dating and friendship products with "advanced technology", including AI. The sources give no detail on Ithaca's funding or track record, and nothing in the announcement supports the grander language. Gupta and Hegeman attach the "future of humanity" phrase to a plan to strengthen human connection with technology. That is a statement of intent. It is not evidence of what the deal is worth.

What to watch

The first test is the information statement and the 10-Q due by 9 November. They should show the debt position, the financing and any closing conditions, and so settle whether $1.3bn is an equity or enterprise figure. After that, the question is whether other listed dating groups are priced as takeover targets. A single sub-$1.5bn take-private of a company in decline does not establish that they are.
