Netflix to cut 1,000 jobs in its deepest reduction since 2022

In one sentence
Netflix is cutting about 1,000 jobs, roughly 6% of staff and its largest reduction since 2022, while saying it is on track for its full-year forecast ahead of results on 20 October.

Netflix is cutting about 1,000 jobs, roughly 6% of its global workforce, in what reports describe as its deepest reduction since 2022. The company's co-chief executives have told staff the business is strong and on track to meet its full-year forecast. That raises an awkward question for investors: if the business is healthy, why does it need the largest headcount cut in four years?

What was reported

The cuts were reported on 14 October. Bloomberg, citing the Wall Street Journal, put the figure at about 1,000 positions. The BBC described them as the steepest since 2022. Most of the reductions fall on product, technology, legal and human resources teams, according to the reports. Managers are due to tell affected employees shortly. The reports give no country-by-country breakdown or severance terms.

The sums are simple. A 6% cut equal to about 1,000 jobs implies a workforce of around 16,000 to 17,000. That is consistent with earlier press estimates of the company's size.

The 2022 comparison

Netflix's last notable cuts came in 2022. They followed its first subscriber loss in a decade, a net decline of about 200,000 in the first quarter of that year. The company shed roughly 150 staff in May and about 300 in a second round in June, on the order of 450 jobs in all.

The present round is more than twice that size. The circumstances differ. In 2022 the cuts followed shrinking subscriber numbers and a sharp share-price fall. This time the company says it is on track, and the justification offered is slower growth rather than contraction. Co-CEO Ted Sarandos has said: "Overall, we're not growing as fast as I want us to, and we're working on making that move faster."

Reset finished or still deepening?

The composition of the cuts suggests a trim of overhead, not a retreat from content. Product, technology, legal and HR are support and platform functions. That fits a company protecting its programming spend while holding back the cost of everything around it. It is an inference from the reported mix and not something Netflix has stated.

The test is in the numbers. Three things will show whether the post-2022 cost reset is complete:

  • Operating margin guidance. If the full-year forecast holds and margins do not widen, the cut is maintenance. If the company lifts its margin target, the savings are being banked.
  • Headcount trend. A rising headcount after a reduction would suggest a one-off adjustment. A flat or falling one would point to a continuing squeeze.
  • Content spend. A cut that spares programming budgets reads very differently from one that precedes a lower spending plan.

Other streaming and media groups have followed a similar course of repeated rounds of cuts in place of a single reset. A cut at the sector's largest subscription platform will be read as a sign that the discipline has not eased.

What to watch

Netflix reports third-quarter results on 20 October. That is the first chance to see whether management quantifies the savings, books any restructuring charge, or revises the full-year guidance it says it is on track to meet. Its commentary on hiring, and on whether further rounds are planned, will say more about the direction of the cost base than the headline figure.
