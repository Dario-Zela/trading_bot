Trump says Taiwan will commit to Alaska LNG offtake and an equity stake

In one sentence
Trump reportedly says Taiwan will commit to Alaska LNG offtake and a $4.4bn stake, but the claim rests on one outlet and Taipei has not confirmed it.

President Donald Trump has said Taiwan will commit to buying liquefied natural gas from, and take an equity stake in, the Alaska LNG project. That is according to a single report from Benzinga, which put the offtake at $70-80bn and the stake at $4.4bn. The figures come from a characterisation of a Trump remark. Taiwan's government has not confirmed them, and no contract, filing or counterparty statement has surfaced to support them.

What is claimed

The reported package has two parts. One is a long-term purchase commitment, valued at $70-80bn over the life of the contracts. The other is a $4.4bn equity holding in the project. Neither number has been traced to a document. Benzinga attributes both to Trump, and the headline-level figures could not be matched to a second outlet.

The two figures also differ in kind. An offtake commitment is a promise to buy gas at agreed terms. An equity stake means owning part of the plant and pipeline, with the capital at risk that comes with it. A reader should not treat them as one commitment.

What is already on the record

Taiwan has been in this conversation for some time. In March 2025, Alaska LNG's developer signed a non-binding letter of intent with CPC Corporation, Taiwan's state-owned energy company. It covered offtake and a possible investment in the project, which has been put at about $44bn. The investment amount was left open.

Taiwanese officials have also been cautious about equity. The economy minister was reported as saying Taiwan was exploring a stake of up to 50%. A later statement said its investment would "absolutely not account for half" of the project. The developer's own project lead has said the likely structure is a loose joint venture, with Asian buyers signing for large LNG volumes. That would not necessarily give Taiwan, Japan or South Korea direct equity, though the developer has said it is open to it. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has said those three governments are interested in financing the project and taking a substantial share of its output.

Against that background, a $4.4bn stake is plausible in scale: about a tenth of a $44bn project. A search found no earlier reporting of that number, though, and a letter of intent is not a binding contract.

Why it would matter

If confirmed, the deal would be among the largest commitments tying an Asian buyer to a US export project. Alaska LNG has needed firm buyers and financing partners to move toward a final investment decision. Taiwan imports nearly all of its gas, and a long-dated contract for Alaskan cargoes would give it a supply route that avoids some shipping chokepoints. It would also fit Taipei's trade strategy: buying US energy is a familiar way for Asian economies to answer Washington's tariff and trade-balance demands.

The $70-80bn figure should be read with care. Headline contract values depend on assumed volumes, duration and gas prices. They are estimates, not payments due, and they can shrink or grow with the price formula.

What to watch

The test is paper. A signed sale-and-purchase agreement or a binding investment agreement from CPC, or a statement from Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs or the project's developer, would settle whether the commitment exists and on what terms. Until one of those appears, this is a claim attributed to Trump and reported by one outlet.
