Reform UK faces fresh vetting questions after undercover footage of councillors and candidates

In one sentence
ITV undercover footage is reported to show Reform UK councillors and candidates making racist and misogynistic remarks, testing the party's vetting as it defends its local record.

Reform UK is again being asked how closely it checks the people it puts forward for office. An ITV Exposure investigation, reported on 14 October, says undercover reporters recorded Reform councillors and candidates making racist, anti-Muslim and misogynistic remarks. The footage arrives while the party is defending its record in the town halls and county halls it now runs.

The account here rests on Channel 4 News's report of the ITV investigation and on secondary coverage that quotes the programme. The footage has not been independently verified for this article, and some detail should be checked against ITV's original film.

What the footage is said to show

Channel 4 News, reporting on the ITV investigation, says councillors and candidates were recorded making racist, anti-Muslim and misogynistic comments. It adds that two of those filmed discussed keeping their views private until after they were elected. That detail bears most directly on vetting, because it suggests candidates may have known which views would not survive scrutiny.

Secondary reports say the reporters worked across England and Wales during the May local elections and several by-election campaigns. They say the remarks included describing Muslims as "parasites" and black children as "animals". Those quotations come from outlets summarising the programme rather than from the film itself, so they should be read with that caveat.

Two councillors have been named in that coverage. Alan Feeney, of Solihull, is reported to have resigned from Reform by mutual agreement and to be continuing as an independent councillor. Dean Miller, of Bromley, is reported to have made claims about King Charles being a Muslim. Neither man's account of events is set out in the material seen here.

The party's answer

Reform has said investigations have begun into the findings. According to secondary reports, it has also conceded that "a handful of individuals who do not share our values will initially make it through vetting". That is a defensible position for a fast-growing party, but it is also an admission that screening is imperfect.

The admission sits awkwardly with history. Nigel Farage promised in September 2024 to professionalise the party and improve candidate checks. Since then, Reform has gained a large number of local seats and several councils, and each win has multiplied the number of people who need to be checked. Earlier cases show the same pressure point. In Staffordshire, the council leader Ian Cooper had his membership revoked after an investigation into a failure to declare social media accounts during vetting. Lynn Dean, a councillor in the county, was suspended on similar grounds and quit her role at Newcastle-under-Lyme Borough Council.

Why it matters now

The political risk is less any single remark than the pattern. A party can describe one councillor as an aberration. It is harder to do so when journalists, working undercover across several areas, are said to have found comparable views in more than one place. Opponents also now have a concrete line of attack: that rapid expansion has outrun the party's checks.

Labour has moved quickly. Bridget Phillipson called the behaviour "truly sickening", and there have been calls for the councillors involved to resign. Their resignation matters in practical terms, since councillors who leave the party but keep their seats stay in office as independents and keep their allowances.

What the party does next will be telling. The tests are whether the inquiries it has announced produce suspensions or expulsions, whether it publishes what changes to vetting follow, and whether further candidates appear in later footage. Reports differ on when the full programme airs, with one suggesting 18 October, so the broadcast itself may bring further names and further reaction ahead of the next round of local campaigning.
