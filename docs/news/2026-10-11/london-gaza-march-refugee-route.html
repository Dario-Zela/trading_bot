Thousands march in London for Palestinians; UK Gaza refugee route criticised as too narrow

In one sentence
Thousands marked three years of the Gaza war with a London march, as campaigners said a new UK refugee route for Gazans is too narrow to help most applicants.

A large demonstration in London on Saturday marked three years since the war in Gaza began. It came as campaigners accused the government of offering Gazans false hope with a new UK refugee route they say is too narrow to help most of those who apply.

The march was reported by the Guardian as drawing "thousands". This article could not independently confirm turnout, the organisers' estimate, the number of police deployed or any arrests, and none is given here. Nor was there any confirmed policing dispute or government response to the march itself.

A familiar march, a long war

The war began on 7 October 2023, with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel and the Israeli military campaign in Gaza that followed. London has seen national marches on the conflict ever since. Organisers and media put the November 2023 march in the hundreds of thousands, and the Metropolitan Police said it would deploy 1,850 officers that Saturday. A January 2024 march was policed by around 1,700 officers, with nine arrests. At the one-year anniversary in October 2024, thousands marched from Russell Square to Downing Street, calling for a ceasefire in Gaza and Lebanon. Coverage of the two-year march in 2025 described hundreds of thousands taking part, with scuffles and some arrests of pro-Israel counter-protesters who police said had breached conditions on where they could assemble.

Against that record, a turnout described as "thousands" is not a like-for-like comparison, because the earlier figures came from different sources and counting methods. The wording is the only evidence available, and it cannot support a claim that the movement is fading. Whether the third anniversary shows endurance or fatigue will depend on police and organiser figures that had not been published when this was written.

The route campaigners say does not reach people

The second thread is policy. Campaigners say a new UK safe route for Gazans is too narrow to help most applicants, and they accuse ministers of giving Gazans false hope. The report comes from the Guardian's politics coverage, and the scheme's eligibility criteria and application numbers could not be verified.

The complaint has a history. Solicitors and refugee groups have argued since 2024 that Gazans had almost no legal way to reach the UK. They said a route that required people to leave Gaza and register with the UN refugee agency was out of reach for those still inside the territory. In early 2025 a family of six won an appeal to use the Ukraine Family Scheme, the first known case of non-Ukrainians doing so. Campaigners said the government's reaction was to close what it saw as a loophole, not to build a route.

The new scheme therefore arrives into a debate in which "safe route" has often meant something narrower than the phrase suggests. The test campaigners are setting is simple: how many people can actually qualify, and whether those who qualify can physically leave Gaza.

What to watch

Two figures would settle much of this. One is the Metropolitan Police's account of the march: attendance, arrests and any conditions imposed. The other is the Home Office's data on applications and grants under the new route. If grants stay in the dozens against thousands of applicants, the false-hope charge will be hard to rebut. If the numbers are larger than campaigners expect, the criticism softens. Neither number was available at the time of writing.
