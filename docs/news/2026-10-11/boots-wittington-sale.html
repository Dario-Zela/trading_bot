Boots set to pass from Sycamore to the Weston family's Wittington for about £7bn

In one sentence
Wittington, the Weston family's holding company, agreed on 7 October to buy Boots from Sycamore for US$8.9bn including debt (about £6.7bn), with completion expected in Q1 2027.

The reports that Boots was heading to the Weston family have been overtaken by events. Wittington Investments, the Weston family's holding company, announced on 7 October that it had agreed to buy the pharmacy chain from the US private equity firm Sycamore Partners and the Pessina family. The price is US$8.9bn including debt, which PA puts at about £6.7bn. The deal has not closed. Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals.

What is changing hands

The earlier reports, which cited the Wall Street Journal and the Telegraph, said a signing could come within weeks. They put the value at about £7bn. The announced figure is a little lower in sterling terms and includes assumed debt. The gap reflects currency conversion and the date of the report, so the figures need not conflict. Coverage of the announcement has quoted the price in dollars, Canadian dollars and sterling, so the headline number depends on the outlet.

The perimeter matters as much as the price. Wittington takes Boots' UK and Ireland retail and pharmacy business, Boots Opticians, the No7 Beauty Company, and the Thailand and franchise operations. Sycamore and the Pessina family keep the group's other interests: Farmacias Benavides in Mexico and Alliance Healthcare Deutschland in Germany. Fairfax Financial is partnering on the acquisition, but Wittington will have operational control at closing. Galen Weston is expected to become Boots' chairman.

How Boots got here

Sycamore's ownership was short. It took over Walgreens Boots Alliance, Boots' parent, in August 2025, then split the business into divisions. A sale of the UK arm now ends the speculation that Boots might instead be floated on the London stock market.

For a private equity owner, selling a large, cash-generative consumer brand to a long-term family holder is a clean way out. The usual alternative is a flotation, which depends on market conditions and a public-market valuation.

The Weston fit

The Weston family already has a significant UK retail footprint. Its holdings include a controlling interest in Associated British Foods, the owner of Primark. It also owns Fortnum & Mason and, in Canada, the Shoppers Drug Mart pharmacy chain. Boots therefore pairs a drugstore operator the family already understands with a UK high-street brand that sits beside Primark. Wittington has said it will upgrade stores, improve the online experience and expand healthcare services.

The result would be a major concentration of UK consumer and retail assets under one family's control. The two businesses sell different things, clothing and pharmacy and beauty, so any competition review is likely to focus on overlap in beauty and health retail. That is an inference from the deal structure. No regulator has commented in the coverage reviewed.

What to watch

The next checkpoint is the regulatory process ahead of the expected first-quarter 2027 completion. Details of any UK review, the final treatment of the debt, and the future of the businesses Sycamore is keeping will show how much of the original £7bn estimate holds up.
