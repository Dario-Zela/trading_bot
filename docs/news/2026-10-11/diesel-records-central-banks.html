Diesel stays near records as the Iran-war energy shock reaches central banks

In one sentence
US diesel is about $6.27 a gallon, a little under its late-September record, and the Iran-war energy shock now weighs on whether central banks keep tightening or look through it.

US diesel has stopped setting records but has not come far off them. The national average was about $6.27 a gallon on Friday, according to AP reporting carried by News4Jax. The all-time high came in late September, and the Iran war that lifted prices is now showing up in central-bank deliberations as well as at the pump.

The record itself is hard to pin to one number. Trackers differ: AAA had the national average at $5.85 on 4 September, which broke the June 2022 peak set after Russia invaded Ukraine. It passed $6 a gallon in mid-September, and ABC News cited a high of $6.49 around 20–21 September. Measured against that, Friday's $6.27 is roughly 20 cents below the peak. Regional data points the same way. News4Jax's AAA figures showed Jacksonville diesel at $5.79 on 5 October, down from $6.04 a week earlier.

Why diesel, and why now

The war has disrupted crude flows and refining capacity, according to MercoPress. Reports tie the price surge to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which carries about a fifth of the world's oil. Diesel is the sharper edge of the shock, because the fuel runs trucks, trains, farm equipment and ships. Its price reaches food, freight and manufacturing costs, and shows up in headline inflation with a lag.

What central banks are saying

The policy question is whether the shock is a one-off level shift that can be looked through, or a persistent pressure that feeds into wages and expectations. In April, TD Economics expected central banks to look through the supply shock. Later events have undercut that view.

Euro-area inflation was 3.2% in the final August estimate, against 2.0% a year earlier. One analysis reports that the ECB raised its three key rates by 25 basis points on 10 September, taking the deposit rate to 2.50%. I could not confirm that against a primary source. A BNY note quotes Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel as naming energy as the deciding factor for any further increases.

In the US, headline inflation was reported at 3.4% in August, with core at 2.4%. That gap is the argument for looking through: energy is driving the headline while underlying prices are calmer. Previews before the September meeting put a 25 basis point hike as the main expectation, from a 3.50%–3.75% range held since July. I could not find a report of the actual decision, so that outcome is unverified here. The Bank of England has been reported as facing market pricing for further tightening, though the source for that is undated.

The gap between the 2.4% core reading and the 3.4% headline is the test. If diesel's pass-through into goods and services costs lifts core measures in the coming months, policymakers will have a hard time calling the shock transitory.

What to watch

The next US consumer price report will show whether the September diesel peak has reached core prices. The Bank of England's next decision, and the ECB's following meeting, will show whether Nagel's energy condition is being applied. Gilts remain exposed to the same inflation question ahead of the Budget. Meanwhile, the weekly AAA and EIA diesel figures will show whether the retreat from the record continues or stalls near $6.
