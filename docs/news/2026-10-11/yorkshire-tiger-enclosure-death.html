Man dies after entering tiger enclosure at Yorkshire wildlife park

In one sentence
A man died after entering the Amur tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park near Doncaster; police and, it is expected, the HSE will examine how he got in.

A man died on Saturday afternoon after entering the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park, near Doncaster. The park was evacuated, and it has said it will stay shut on Sunday while the inquiries continue.

South Yorkshire Police said they received reports at 1.35pm. The force said the tiger "subsequently attacked the man, resulting in serious injury." Despite efforts by medical staff, he was pronounced dead at the scene. Yorkshire Ambulance Service said it sent two land ambulances, the Yorkshire Air Ambulance and a doctor. Police said his family have been told and are being supported by specialist officers. He has not been named.

What is known

The tiger was uninjured and remained in its enclosure. Police said there was "no wider risk to the public." They also said they were aware of footage circulating online and asked people not to share it. Some reports say witnesses described the man as agitated before he went in. Police and the park have not confirmed that or explained how he got into the habitat.

The park said on social media that a "serious incident" had taken place at the tiger enclosure. It said emergency procedures were activated immediately and that it was working with the authorities to establish what happened.

The animals are Amur tigers, an endangered subspecies with about 500 left in the wild. They live in the Land of the Tigers, a two-acre reserve that opened in 2011, two years after the park itself.

The question of the barrier

The central question is how a member of the public reached the tigers. A visitor attraction is meant to be designed so that this cannot happen, with fencing, height, set-back and sightlines all doing the work. A death at an enclosure open to the public suggests either that a barrier was deliberately defeated or that it was not sufficient. At this stage nobody has said which.

The Health and Safety Executive is expected to take part in the inquiry alongside the police, as it has done after earlier fatalities at UK animal attractions. Those earlier cases involved keepers rather than visitors. Sarah McClay was killed by a tiger at South Lakes Wild Animal Park in Cumbria in 2013. Rosa King died at Hamerton Zoo Park in Cambridgeshire in 2017, after a safety slide was found open. Those inquiries examined systems and procedures rather than individual blame. A visitor case would put the public-facing perimeter under the same scrutiny.

What comes next

Police have not said whether they are treating the death as suspicious. The first things to watch are the man's formal identification, a coroner's opening of an inquest, and any statement from the HSE on its role. The park's decision on when to reopen, and whether it changes the enclosure's barriers first, will also show how seriously it regards the question. Any HSE finding on the perimeter could be read across to similar big-cat attractions.
