Court hears Widdecombe suspect had 'particular hostility to Reform'

In one sentence
The Old Bailey heard prosecutors allege Ann Widdecombe's accused killer held extreme left-wing views and was 'particularly hostile' to Reform; he has not pleaded and no motive is proven.

The man accused of murdering Ann Widdecombe is alleged to have held extreme left-wing or anti-establishment views and to have been "particularly hostile" to Reform UK, the Old Bailey heard on Friday. The prosecution says her killing was "inextricably linked" to a separate, earlier alleged plot against Nigel Farage. The case is therefore more serious than the word "attack" suggests: it is a murder charge alongside a terrorism-preparation charge.

What the court was told

Joshua Kerry, 28, from Rotherham, appeared before Mrs Justice Cheema-Grubb. Prosecutor Jonathan Polnay KC told the court that the Crown alleges Kerry held extreme left-wing and/or anti-establishment views and was particularly hostile towards Reform. He also argued that the murder carried a "terrorist connection".

Kerry is charged with murdering Widdecombe, the former Conservative minister who later became a Reform-aligned figure. He is also charged with preparing terrorist acts between 31 May 2024 and 8 July 2026, the date of the alleged killing. Prosecutors say he carried out online research on prominent people and institutions over nearly two years, and tried to gain entry to Farage's south London home on 19 April last year. Reports differ on that date: ITV's coverage of the earlier magistrates' hearing gave 20 April.

Police have said it was clear the attack on Widdecombe was targeted. Prosecutors allege she was struck 21 times on the head with a hammer. Press reports say a list found in the investigation names others, including Kemi Badenoch and the Reform MPs Richard Tice, Lee Anderson and Robert Jenrick. Press reports also say a search of Kerry's home found clippings about Reform, Farage and the party's immigration policies.

Allegation, not finding

None of this has been tested. Kerry has not entered a plea, and the Crown's description of his views and motives is an allegation made at a preliminary hearing. Police have said their enquiries into motive, including any political or terrorist connection, are continuing, and that they do not want to rule anything out. Farage has publicly called the suspect a hard-left activist. Police have not confirmed that characterisation.

The reported phrase also needs care. The "particular hostility to Reform" wording in the headline is a paraphrase of the prosecutor's submission. It is not a finding by the court.

Why it matters

If the Crown's case holds at trial, it would be a rare UK example of a killing prosecuted as part of a politically motivated terrorism plot, aimed at a party and its figures rather than at one individual. That bears on how police, courts and parties approach protection for politicians and campaigners, including those no longer in office.

The case now moves slowly. A plea and case management hearing is set for 29 January, and a trial before a High Court judge is provisionally listed for 8 June. The prosecution's evidence on motive will be tested then.
