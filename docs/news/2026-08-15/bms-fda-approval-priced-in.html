Bristol Myers Squibb wins FDA approval — is the stock already pricing it in?

Bristol Myers Squibb's Lawrence, New Jersey research and headquarters facility. — Wikimedia Commons
In one sentence
BMS won FDA approval for its first-in-class myeloma drug ZENBEXUS, but the stock rose just 1.5% — dwarfed by an earlier 6% pop on AstraZeneca merger speculation.

Bristol Myers Squibb picked up an FDA approval this week that would, in an ordinary year, count as a genuine event: a first-in-class cancer drug, a novel mechanism, a payer-friendly efficacy readout. The stock shrugged.

On August 13 the FDA granted accelerated approval to ZENBEXUS (iberdomide), BMS's first CELMoD — a class of drug that degrades disease-driving proteins rather than merely blocking them — for multiple myeloma patients who have relapsed after at least one prior treatment. The approval rests on the EXCALIBER-RRMM trial, in which the ZENBEXUS combination roughly doubled the rate of minimal-residual-disease-negative complete response versus standard care: 41% against 21%. BMS priced the regimen at $29,500 per 28-day cycle, higher than analysts at William Blair had modeled, and the firm now projects the drug could clear $1 billion in annual sales by 2031.

That matters more than it might sound, because BMS's existing myeloma franchise is being dismantled by generics in real time. Revlimid revenue fell 49% year-on-year in the second quarter to $425 million; Pomalyst dropped 71% to $204 million after generic entry. ZENBEXUS is the replacement plumbing.

And yet BMY shares rose just 1.5% on approval day, closing at $64.66. For a first-in-class oncology nod with above-expectation pricing, that is a thin reaction — the kind that tells you the market had already done its homework.

The bigger mover was merger chatter, not the pipeline

Compare that 1.5% pop to what happened eleven days earlier. On August 2, the Financial Times reported that AstraZeneca and BMS had held talks about a roughly $400 billion combination. BMY jumped about 6% in premarket trading the next session — four times the eventual approval-day move — while AstraZeneca fell as much as 7% in London on dilution and integration worries. Days later, a Reuters source pushed back, saying there were "no discussions" currently under way. The stock has round-tripped since: $65.89 on August 4, down to $63.70 by August 12, back up to $64.66 on approval day. Net effect of two weeks and two separate headline catalysts: essentially flat.

Sell-side price targets back up the muted reaction. The consensus among 11 Wall Street analysts sits at $64.82 — about 0.3% above where the stock closed on approval day. A handful of bulls, Guggenheim among them, have pushed targets into the low $70s on the back of a dense late-2026 readout calendar, but the average implies a market that has already extended BMS credit for its pipeline rather than one waiting to be convinced.

That is the actual signal in this week's approval: not that ZENBEXUS is a disappointment — the trial data and pricing both beat expectations — but that BMS shares are trading on deal speculation and generic-erosion arithmetic, not on individual drug approvals. The next test lands September 30, when the FDA is due to rule on expanding Camzyos into adolescent hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, followed by pivotal readouts on milvexian and Cobenfy later this year. Traders positioned for a repricing on regulatory news alone may find the real catalyst is whichever party revives — or definitively kills — the AstraZeneca conversation.
