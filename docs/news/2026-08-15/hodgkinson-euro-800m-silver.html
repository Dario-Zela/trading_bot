Hodgkinson beaten to European 800m gold by rival Werro

Keely Hodgkinson, pictured at a previous European Championships, took silver behind Switzerland's Audrey Werro in Birmingham. — Erik van Leeuwen / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Switzerland's Audrey Werro held off Keely Hodgkinson to win European 800m gold in a championship-record 1:54.81.

Keely Hodgkinson has made a habit of closing hard in the home straight. On Friday night at Alexander Stadium, it still wasn't enough.

The Olympic 800m champion was run down — or rather, never quite reeled in — by Switzerland's Audrey Werro, who took the European title in a championship-record 1:54.81, the third-fastest 800m in history. Hodgkinson, defending her 2022 European crown, closed the gap in the final metres but crossed second. Femke Broeders-Bol of the Netherlands took bronze, her first major medal over the distance.

Werro's win completed an unlikely 24 hours. She had fallen in Thursday's semi-final after a coming-together with another runner, and only made the final after officials reinstated her. She lined up in Friday's showpiece with a small bandage taped over her right hip, took the race out hard from the front, and simply refused to be caught.

A rivalry that's now even

This was not a shock result so much as a confirmation. The 22-year-old from Fribourg had already beaten Hodgkinson in June, becoming the first woman in 43 years to break 1 minute 54 seconds for the distance, and arrived in Birmingham with a personal best of 1:53.80 — set in Paris earlier this season. Friday's run was her first senior global or continental title, following age-group golds and a Diamond League Final win last year that made her the sport's form athlete outside Hodgkinson herself.

Hodgkinson, still just 24 and the reigning Olympic and world indoor champion, remains the bigger name and the bigger prize collector. But the balance of recent meetings — Paris in June, now Birmingham — has tipped toward Werro, and a rivalry that looked settled after Hodgkinson's Paris 2024 Olympic gold suddenly looks like the most competitive two-woman contest in middle-distance running.

"Today was my day," Werro said afterwards, with the bluntness of someone who knows the next one might not be.

For Hodgkinson, the silver extends a curious pattern: brilliant enough to be favourite in almost every field she enters, yet now beaten twice in three months by the same rival. The next meeting point is likely to be next year's global championship calendar, but nothing is scheduled to settle the argument before then — which, for a rivalry this young, is probably the point.
