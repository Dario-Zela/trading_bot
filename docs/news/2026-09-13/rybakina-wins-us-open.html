Rybakina beats Sabalenka to win US Open title

Elena Rybakina, who beat Aryna Sabalenka to win the 2026 US Open title. — Hameltion / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
In one sentence
Elena Rybakina beat world number one Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 to win the US Open and take her ranking.

Elena Rybakina beat Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 in Saturday's US Open final, denying the world number one a third straight Flushing Meadows title and, in the same stroke, taking her ranking.

It is the second major final between the two this year — Rybakina also beat Sabalenka in January's Australian Open final — and it means the Kazakh now holds two of the four Grand Slam titles on offer in 2026, alongside her 2022 Wimbledon crown. She sits one Roland Garros trophy short of the career slam.

A streak snapped

Sabalenka arrived in New York chasing a third consecutive US Open title and a round-number milestone: 100 straight weeks at world number one. She leaves with neither. Rybakina's win ends a 20-match unbeaten run for Sabalenka at Flushing Meadows and hands the Belarusian her third defeat in a major final in the last thirteen months, all three by different opponents.

The match itself took two hours and twenty-one minutes on Arthur Ashe Stadium and swung on a single break early in the deciding set, which Rybakina defended by playing the calmer tennis when it mattered — flatter, deeper, less prone to the loose errors that crept into Sabalenka's game as the set wore on. Rybakina afterwards struggled to characterise the win: "It's tough to find the words right now," she said. "Somehow everything aligned." She had also been managing a minor injury through the tournament, having retired mid-match at the Cincinnati event weeks earlier.

Sabalenka was gracious in defeat, telling reporters she intends to use the loss as fuel rather than dwell on it: "I only come back stronger." It is worth noting she is not short of majors of her own — this defeat cost her top ranking, not her legacy, and she remains among the most dominant players of her generation.

For Rybakina, the win is historically tidy: she is only the third woman since 2000 to win the Australian Open and US Open in the same season, after Angelique Kerber in 2016 and Sabalenka herself in 2024. She is also the first player to beat a defending US Open champion in the final since Serena Williams did so in 2002.

None of it moves a market or shifts a rate decision — this is a result that matters to the sport and to nobody's balance sheet. The next data point, for what it is worth, is the WTA Finals in November, where the two are likely to meet again.
