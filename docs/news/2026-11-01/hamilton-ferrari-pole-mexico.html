Hamilton takes first Ferrari pole at the Mexico City Grand Prix

Hamilton edges out George Russell to take pole position in Mexico City. — Sky Sports F1
In one sentence
Lewis Hamilton took his first Ferrari pole, and a record 105th overall, in Mexico City by 0.009s from Russell, with Antonelli able to clinch the title on Sunday.

Lewis Hamilton will start Sunday's Mexico City Grand Prix from pole position. It is the first pole of his Ferrari career, and it came by nine thousandths of a second.

Hamilton set 1:16.514 at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez on Saturday. George Russell's Mercedes was 0.009s behind, and Hamilton's Ferrari team-mate Charles Leclerc was third, 0.119s back. Russell had briefly held provisional pole before Hamilton found the margin on his final run. Kimi Antonelli, also of Mercedes, starts fourth.

Checking the claim

The angle depends on whether this really is his first Ferrari pole. Reports from the FIA, Sky Sports and others, all published after qualifying, say it is. Sky's coverage also notes that his only Grand Prix win for Ferrari came in Barcelona earlier this year, so he has now led a grid and won a race in red, but not the same weekend. This piece did not check the full qualifying record for his two seasons at Ferrari, and relies on those reports for the "first" claim.

The record figure also holds up. The pole is his 105th, extending his all-time record from the 104 he had at the end of 2024. It is his first since the 2023 Hungarian Grand Prix. It is also his second in Mexico, after 2016, and at 41 he is reported to be the oldest pole-sitter since 1982.

A first, at 41

Hamilton left Mercedes after 11 seasons for the 2025 season, in the sport's biggest team switch in years. Pole at one of the calendar's hardest tracks to read is a visible return on that move. Mexico City sits at high altitude, where thin air reduces downforce and cooling, and the long run to Turn 1 is a familiar hazard.

He did not have a clean afternoon. In Q2 he aborted his first fast lap and had to recover on his second run to progress, setting only the fourth-fastest time.

Hamilton played down the number but not the occasion.

"The number doesn't mean much in a sense, it feels like the first."

He also linked the moment to Michael Schumacher, with whom he shares the record of seven drivers' titles. "It was a dream my family have shared with me and I know so many people in the world grew up watching Michael," he said. "I'm having those firsts this year and I'm in a really good place."

What it means for Sunday

The title picture now matters more than the pole. Antonelli can be crowned world champion on Sunday by finishing in the top nine. If he finishes 10th or scores no points, a win for Hamilton or Russell would delay the decision.

That puts Hamilton in an awkward position. Pole gives him the cleanest route to the first corner, but the front row is shared with Russell, whose result could affect the championship for his own team-mate. Leclerc starts third, so Ferrari has two cars in the leading group.

The lights go out at 8pm UK time. A Hamilton win would be only his second for Ferrari, and it would also keep the championship from being settled in Mexico.
