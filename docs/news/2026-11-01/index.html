Ahead of the King's CHOGM speech in Antigua, Prime Minister Burnham says he is open to a formal slavery apology but rules out the reparations Caribbean states are pressing for.

The prime minister, Burnham, says he is open to a formal apology for Britain's role in the slave trade but has ruled out paying reparations, a position he set out ahead of the King's speech at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM) in Antigua.

The King's address at the summit puts the monarchy at the centre of a debate that has long sat uneasily between sentiment and policy. Britain's formal stance has shifted over the years, and the wording the government chooses for any apology will be closely read in the Caribbean.
