Almost 140 arrested at Palestine Action ban protest outside US embassy

In one sentence
Police arrested 139 people outside the US embassy in London at a protest against the Palestine Action ban, days before a Supreme Court hearing on the group's proscription.

Police in London arrested 139 people outside the US embassy on Saturday at a demonstration against the ban on Palestine Action. The Metropolitan Police said most were held on suspicion of expressing support for a proscribed organisation, and warned that more arrests could follow as officers remained at the scene.

The protesters held signs reading "I oppose genocide, I support Palestine Action". An earlier police statement put the number of arrests at 48. The final tally was far higher, and the figure was still being updated when the first reports were filed. Reports put the crowd at more than 100 people, which would mean most of those present were arrested.

A familiar pattern, a new location

Palestine Action was proscribed under UK terrorism law in June 2025, after activists broke into an RAF base in southern England to protest British military support for Israel. Supporting a proscribed group is an offence carrying a maximum sentence of 14 years.

Mass arrests have since become a recurring feature of the campaign against the ban. Police arrested 522 people at a Parliament Square protest in August 2025, thought to be the highest number at a single London protest. In April 2026, 212 people were arrested in Trafalgar Square. Previous demonstrations were mostly in central London. This one was held at the US embassy.

The organiser, Defend Our Juries, said: "It is truly dystopian that there have now been over 4,000 arrests under terror laws." That figure is the group's own count of people detained since July 2025. The coverage reviewed gives no official government total, so it should be treated as a campaigners' estimate.

Why the embassy?

The reports do not say why the embassy was chosen. One report notes that the US announced sanctions against Palestine Action in August, designating it a terrorist group. That report does not connect the sanctions to the choice of venue, and it would be wrong to assume a link. Nor do the reports say whether the protest was aimed at US policy on Gaza. The protesters' slogan refers to genocide, and the group's wider campaign targets arms links to Israel, but the organisers' reasoning remains unstated. A move from Westminster to the embassy could signal a shift in target, or it could simply be a new site for the same tactic.

A separate vigil took place outside the US consulate in Edinburgh. No arrests were reported there. Police Scotland said: "Evidence is being reviewed and further action will be taken as appropriate."

What comes next

The protest came days before a Supreme Court hearing on 4 November. The appeal challenges Palestine Action's proscribed status, and the government has maintained the ban. Earlier coverage reported that a High Court ruling went against the ban in the spring and that the Metropolitan Police paused arrests before announcing in late March that it would resume them. That detail comes from a search summary and was not confirmed in the articles read for this piece.

The Supreme Court outcome will matter beyond the group itself. If the ban stands, arrests of people showing support for the group seem likely to continue, and the embassy protest suggests demonstrators are willing to bring that confrontation to new sites. If it falls, the thousands of arrests under the regime will face fresh legal scrutiny. The Met has yet to give a final count for Saturday, and charging decisions will show how many of the 139 face prosecution.
