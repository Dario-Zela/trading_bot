Chelsea beat 10-man Manchester United, raising pressure on Carrick

In one sentence
Chelsea won 2-0 against a Manchester United side reduced to ten men when Lisandro Martínez was sent off, adding to the pressure on Michael Carrick.

Manchester United lost 2-0 at Chelsea on Saturday, and the defeat leaves Michael Carrick answering questions about his future. United played the last half-hour with ten men after Lisandro Martínez was sent off.

Martínez was booked for pulling back Morgan Rogers shortly before the hour. Within minutes he was shown a second yellow for a raised elbow on Wesley Fofana at a Chelsea corner. The referee, Peter Bankes, judged it a reckless challenge after a VAR review, and he awarded a penalty. The central defender will miss United's home game against Aston Villa on 8 November.

Reports differ on some of the detail. One account has Rogers chipping Senne Lammens early in the second half before Cole Palmer converted the penalty. Another credits only Palmer. Both agree on the score, the second yellow for Martínez and the penalty.

A sending-off, and a question about control

Carrick said he had not seen the incident. Asked whether Martínez, an experienced player, had let him down, he answered simply: "No."

A red card is not the whole story of a defeat. Reports of the game suggest United were already behind or struggling before Martínez went. Even so, the dismissal turned a difficult afternoon into one with little chance of a way back.

The pressure on Carrick

The result comes with United in a poor run. According to one report, they are 17th in the table on seven points, kept out of the relegation zone only on goal difference. They lost 3-0 at home to Bournemouth the previous weekend. They have also lost to Como and to Atlético Madrid in the Champions League, and that report says they have gone nine games without a win in all competitions. These figures come from a single outlet and should be checked against the league table.

Asked whether he still expected to be in charge for the next European fixture, against Roma, Carrick said: "Yes." On the club's position he added: "It's the truth, it's the hard truth at times."

Carrick took over on an interim basis last season and won at Stamford Bridge in April, so this defeat has an obvious contrast. That earlier result raised hopes. Seven months on, the question is whether the early credit has run out.

What comes next

United face Roma in Europe and then Aston Villa at Old Trafford on 8 November, without Martínez for the Villa game. The run of results, more than any one red card, will decide how long Carrick's patience lasts with the club's hierarchy.
