City of London chief tops the UK Black Powerlist

In one sentence
ITV reports City of London Corporation chief executive Ian Thomas tops this year's Powerlist of influential Black Britons, a recognition story with a modest signal on City diversity.

The person who runs the City of London Corporation, the body that looks after the Square Mile, has been named the most influential Black person in Britain. Ian Thomas, the Corporation's town clerk and chief executive, tops this year's Powerlist, according to ITV News, which reported the result on 31 October.

The Powerlist is an annual ranking published by Powerful Media. It has run since 2006 and is now in its third decade. It picks out the most powerful people of African and African Caribbean heritage in the UK, and the number one slot has gone to figures from business, sport and public life in recent years.

Who he is

Thomas is a CBE and the 51st person to hold the town clerk post. He took the job in February 2023, with responsibility for the Corporation's administration and for advising its Policy and Resources Committee. The Corporation also oversees the City of London Police.

His route there was through local government rather than finance. He ran Lewisham Council and then the Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames before moving to the Square Mile. His career began in children's services, and City AM described him on his appointment as the council boss who led Rotherham's response to its child abuse scandal.

He was already on the Powerlist last year, in the 2026 edition, which was topped by the Bank of England's finance chief, Afua Kyei, with Ian Wright in second place. That edition also followed Dean Forbes of Forterro at the top the year before.

A signal, but a modest one

The result is a recognition story, and it should be read as one. Thomas is not a bank chief executive or a fund manager, and the Corporation is a civic and promotional body rather than a market regulator. It does not set rates or write rules for lenders. Its job is to represent the City's interests, run the Square Mile's services and champion its place in global finance.

That makes the headline a more limited signal than it first sounds. Back-to-back Powerlist winners in the City's orbit, a Bank of England executive last year and the Corporation's chief this year, suggest that Black leaders are reaching senior positions in the institutions around UK finance. It does not show the same thing about the banks, insurers and asset managers that sit at its centre. The list says who is influential; it does not count who runs the firms.

What to check

One caveat applies. The ranking rests on a single report, from ITV. A BBC piece, the source named in the original brief, could not be found, and the Powerlist's own site was not available to confirm the full top ten. The edition was also described as the 2027 list in the ITV headline structure, though the year the publishers use should be taken from Powerful Media directly.

The next point of interest is the rest of the list. If more heads of banks, exchanges and big asset managers appear in the top ten, that would say more about who runs UK finance than a single honour for a civic chief.
