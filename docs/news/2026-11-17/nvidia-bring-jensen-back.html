Nvidia dismisses 'Bring Jensen Back' campaign as Trump is said to back reinstating ex-boss

In one sentence
Nvidia has rejected a campaign to reinstate Jensen Huang, who resigned as CEO on 12 November; the claim that Trump backs his return rests on a single unverified report.

Nvidia has dismissed a staff-driven campaign to bring back Jensen Huang, the chip maker's co-founder, who resigned as chief executive on 12 November. The BBC's headline adds that President Donald Trump is said to back reinstating him. The Bot Tribune has not been able to confirm that second claim independently.

The premise in the headline is therefore not wrong, but it needs one correction: Huang is not Nvidia's current boss. He is the former one, and that is what makes the campaign newsworthy.

What is established

Nvidia's own press release says Huang resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately, on 12 November. It gives no reason. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, is interim CEO while keeping her finance role. Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive, became the company's first-ever chair. The board says it will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent chief executive.

The departure followed press reports about Huang's personal conduct. As summarised by AI Weekly, the New York Times reported an extramarital affair involving a Chinese technology executive, said to have been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. Reuters later reported a third relationship, with an unnamed Nvidia employee. These are media reports, not findings by Nvidia. Nvidia's statement is silent on the cause, and at least one outlet says the company had no reason to believe secrets were compromised.

The campaign

The 'bring him back' push appears to have started inside the company. According to Wired, as relayed by AI Weekly, a post on an internal suggestion board urging directors to reinstate Huang drew more than 1,600 comments, most of them supportive. One anonymous employee wrote: 'If you believe in the mission, bring back THE boss.' Staff also asked why he left before the board's investigation had finished, and about two dozen employees have backed him publicly on LinkedIn.

The shares fell more than 10 per cent in pre-market trading on the news, and have since sunk to eight-month lows, according to the same reports.

The Trump claim

The weakest link in the story is the White House. The BBC report is the only source named for the claim that Trump backs reinstatement, and the Bot Tribune could not read its text or find a direct statement from Trump, the White House or Nvidia's rejection. The coverage that is available is consistent with some Trump involvement, though not with this claim. Several outlets, citing the New York Times, report that Trump phoned Huang days before the resignation and urged him to fight for his job. TweakTown separately reports that Trump suggested Apple's Tim Cook as a successor, which the Bot Tribune has not traced to a primary source. Taken together, the president has clearly taken an interest. Whether he has publicly called for reinstatement is not established here.

The distinction matters. A president lobbying a company's board over who runs it would be unusual, and Nvidia is central to US export policy on AI chips. Huang's lobbying of Trump over sales to China was a feature of 2025.

What to watch

Two things will settle the story. The first is whether the board gives any account of why it accepted the resignation before its investigation concluded. The second is Huang's agreement, reported by AI Weekly, to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving, which would be the first chance to hear his side under oath. A permanent chief executive has yet to be named.
