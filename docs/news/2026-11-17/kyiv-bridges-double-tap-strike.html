Two Kyiv bridges hit as Ukraine says a 'double-tap' strike on rescuers killed 72

In one sentence
Russia hit two Kyiv bridges overnight, but Ukraine's claim of a 'double-tap' strike on rescuers killing 72 remains uncorroborated in available reporting.

Russian missiles struck two major bridges over the Dnipro in Kyiv overnight, according to the city's mayor. Separately, Ukrainian officials are said to be alleging that a repeat, or "double-tap", strike on a rescue operation killed 72 people. The bridge strikes are documented. The rescuer allegation and the death toll are, so far, Ukraine's claim alone. This reporting could not find them corroborated by a second source, and they should be read that way.

What is established

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said on Telegram: "According to preliminary information, there have been hits on the North and South bridges in the capital." The attack came overnight into Tuesday 17 November. The extent of the damage to the structures was not clear in early reports.

Russia's defence ministry described a large strike on what it called military-industrial, transport and energy facilities, port infrastructure and cargo ships. It named Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia, Cherkasy and Dnipropetrovsk. In Kyiv, reports describe partial damage to the metro, water and power cuts, and traffic restrictions. Witnesses reported a series of loud explosions. Three people were reported injured in the Cherkasy region. Reuters said it could not independently confirm the reports.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said intelligence had shown the strikes were planned for the late hours of the previous day and for the day that followed. Russian forces had also attacked river bridges earlier this month and in October, so this was a continuation of a pattern.

The claim that is not yet confirmed

The more serious allegation is that a second strike hit rescuers who had arrived at the scene of the first, and that the attacks killed 72 people. None of the published accounts reviewed for this article mention rescuers, a double-tap strike or a toll of 72. That does not make the claim false. Early casualty figures in Kyiv often come through the State Emergency Service and the city administration hours after an attack, and they can change. It does mean the number cannot be treated as established. Nor is it clear whether the 72 dead are a total across all of Ukraine, a Kyiv figure, or a figure tied to one incident.

The sequence also matters. A reader needs to know when the bridges were hit, when the rescue teams arrived and how long the gap was before any second strike. Without those details, "double-tap" is a label rather than a finding.

Why the allegation carries weight

The charge is a familiar one. Ukraine's emergency service has repeatedly accused Russia of striking the same site twice to hit those who respond. A Kyiv Independent report put the number of rescuers killed in such attacks at 91, with 348 injured, as of 2024. More recently, two firefighters were reported killed in strikes on Kyiv Oblast on 12 November, and rescuers were killed in a double-tap strike in Kharkiv earlier this year. Moscow and Kyiv both deny deliberately targeting civilians.

If the Kyiv claim were verified, it would change the character of the story. Hitting bridges and power links is an attack on infrastructure, which is an established, if contested, part of the war. Deliberately hitting rescuers is an attack on protected people under the laws of armed conflict, and it would harden demands for investigation and for further Western air-defence support.

The infrastructure logic

The Institute for the Study of War has warned that intensified strikes on Kyiv's bridges may be meant to split the capital's electricity network along the Dnipro. Bridges carry power cables and water mains as well as traffic. Damage to them can therefore cut services in ways a direct hit on a power station does not.

The strikes came a day after what Russian data showed to be the largest overnight Ukrainian drone attack on Russia since the full-scale invasion began in 2022. Russia said it downed 1,441 drones overnight, nearly 800 of them heading for the Moscow region. The Crimea Bridge was hit again, and two people were reported killed in Russian-controlled Crimea. The exchange suggests both sides are going after transport links.

Diplomacy is running in parallel. Zelensky said Ukraine held new online talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner and European representatives. Kushner said progress had been made but that important challenges remain.

What to watch

The first test is whether Ukraine's State Emergency Service or the Kyiv city administration publishes a detailed breakdown of the dead, including how many were rescuers, and whether independent journalists or monitors confirm it. The UN human rights monitoring mission usually issues its own assessment of such strikes within days. The second is the state of the bridges and the power grid in the capital, which will show whether Russia can keep the city divided as winter sets in. The third is political. The next round of talks and European energy-security discussions will show whether a claimed attack on first responders moves anyone's position.
