Scotland's Pocognoli under pressure; Portugal without Ronaldo face Wales

In one sentence
Scotland's new coach Sebastien Pocognoli faces early questions over results, while Portugal's reported lack of Ronaldo frames their meeting with Wales.

Sebastien Pocognoli has been Scotland's head coach for three months, and the questions have already started. The BBC's sport desk asks whether it is time to go. The evidence for a full-blown crisis is thinner than the headline suggests, and the record has to be handled carefully.

A short tenure, a long shadow

Pocognoli, a Belgian, was named in August on a two-year contract. He replaced Steve Clarke, who left after Scotland went out of the World Cup without getting through their group. It is his first senior international job and only his third head coach post in senior men's football.

His first match was a goalless draw in Slovenia, which he described as a game in which Scotland had to "suffer together". Later reports quote him saying Scotland "deserved nothing more" after a Slovenia defeat. The two headlines appear to describe different matches, and a full list of his results has not been checked here. The BBC piece that prompted the debate was not available either, so this report cannot say how many games have been lost or how loud the criticism is.

Pocognoli himself addressed pressure early on. Drawing on his years around Belgium's "golden generation", he said he would treat it as something to "embrace" rather than a burden. Clarke's tenure raised the bar, so any successor was always going to be judged against it. A new coach with a two-year deal and a home European Championship in 2028 as the target is usually given room. Whether the Scottish FA is minded to do so has not been stated by anyone on the record.

Portugal, Wales and a quieter night

The same evening brings Portugal against Wales, billed as a meeting without Cristiano Ronaldo. Date, venue and Ronaldo's absence have not been independently confirmed. A fixture listing shows Portugal at Wales on 17 November, with no lineups or result attached.

The previous meeting is on record. Portugal won 1-0 in Lisbon on 24 September in the Nations League, with João Félix scoring. Ronaldo started that game and had an effort ruled out. Craig Bellamy's Wales were praised for a big-hearted display in defeat. The "post-Bale era" label is by now old news. Gareth Bale retired in 2023, and the real test is whether Wales can stay competitive against a side of Portugal's depth.

What ties them together

Both stories ask the same thing of a national side. Can it cope with the departure of a defining figure? Scotland are working out life after Clarke, Wales after Bale, and Portugal, if the reports hold, perhaps for the first time without Ronaldo. None of this is settled on the evidence supplied.

The next data point is Scotland's next result and what the Scottish FA says afterwards. Silence from the governing body would suggest the debate is more a media one than a boardroom one.
