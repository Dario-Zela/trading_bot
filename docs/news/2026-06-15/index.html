GEOPOLITICAL RISK · By M. Halloway

Wall Street builds catastrophe models to price military conflict

Catastrophe modeling firms best known for pricing hurricanes are adapting their methodology to quantify military conflict risk for Wall Street — with early results that impress and limitations that should concern.

Wall Street is systematizing geopolitical risk, adapting catastrophe models designed for natural disasters to forecast military conflict and price war as a tradable liability. Firms across investment banking, portfolio management and insurance are beginning to incorporate conflict scenarios into risk models and hedging strategies—marking a formal institutional shift from treating tail geopolitical events as unquantifiable to pricing them as systematic risk. The methodology mirrors decades of work in natural catastrophe modeling, translating historical conflict data into probability distributions and potential portfolio impacts. The move promises more rigorous geopolitical risk assessment. But critics warn it risks false precision: wars are fundamentally less predictable than earthquakes, and models claiming to quantify them could embed dangerous confidence into investment decisions precisely when volatility matters most.
