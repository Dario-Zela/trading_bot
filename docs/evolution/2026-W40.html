RESEARCH · FITS

Factor crowding and alpha decay: models fit in-sample but forecast poorly

Chorok Lee's arXiv 2512.11913 (v3, revised 2026-09-26) tests whether residuals from return-decay curves can forecast factor tail losses. It found no robust advantage across 8 US factors, and all 48 sequential confidence intervals included zero. A Gaussian counterexample also shows a spurious same-month crash-risk ratio of 1.61. A related snippet claims momentum has gone from roughly 10%/yr in the 1990s to about 2%, with hyperbolic decay fitting best (R²=0.65 vs 0.51 for linear), but I did not verify that claim against the paper. The practical lesson is that crowding-based timing of simple 'mechanical' signals is unreliable, so don't build a decay-timing overlay.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader — treat the weak decay evidence as a reason to monitor its rolling live Sharpe and keep sizing conservative. Don't add a crowding-timing layer.

RESEARCH · FITS

Cost-aware ML anomaly strategies and reversal in large caps

Azevedo, Hoegner and Velikov (SSRN 4702406) find that ML-based anomaly strategies lose about 57% of gross performance to transaction costs, post-publication decay and post-decimalization liquidity. The best (LSTM-type) models still net up to 1.42%/month out of sample despite turnover above 50%. Separate reversal work reports that restricting to large caps and using turnover-aware portfolio construction removes much of the cost drag, with roughly 30-50bp/week net. These are search-result summaries, so I have not checked the figures against the papers.

Implication. fits existing: mean-reverter — restrict the universe to large caps, add a turnover or hysteresis rule on entries and exits, and benchmark its net-of-cost edge against these figures.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

LLM / agentic news nowcasting of next-day stock returns

arXiv 2601.11958 (Jan 2026) uses AI agents with real-time web search to rank Russell 1000 stocks daily. Going long the top 20 gives a five-factor-plus-momentum alpha of 18.4bp/day and a Sharpe of 2.43, with costs under 10% of gross alpha. The alpha is concentrated in the top tier and diluted quickly beyond it. The sample is short (data collection began April 2025), so treat the Sharpe with scepticism.

Implication. spawn-candidate: 'agent-ranked-top-N' — an LLM scores the morning brief's candidates and the strategy holds only the top ~5-10 for 1-3 days. This would be a stricter, ranked variant of news-reactive.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Textual signals propagated through supply-chain networks

arXiv 2606.29290 (June 2026) embeds 10-K MD&A text with FinBERT for 255 S&P 500 firms over 2011-2025 and propagates the signals through supply-chain knowledge graphs. The long-short portfolio has a Sharpe of 0.86 and a five-factor alpha of 7.27%/yr (t=2.30). The effect survives controls for momentum, volatility and size, plus placebo and out-of-sample tests. The long-short construction needs shorting, but the long leg could be used alone.

Implication. spawn-candidate: 'network-text-tilt' — a monthly-refreshed long-only tilt toward firms whose supply-chain neighbours have favourable text signals, held 1-5 days around filings. Expect a weaker edge than the long-short Sharpe, since only the long leg is usable.

RESEARCH · FITS

Multi-dimensional LLM sentiment for oil returns

arXiv 2603.11408 (March 2026) extracts five sentiment dimensions from energy news with GPT-4o, Llama 3.2-3b and FinBERT: relevance, polarity, intensity, uncertainty and forwardness. It finds that intensity and uncertainty are among the most important predictors of weekly WTI returns, and the best results come from combining GPT-4o with FinBERT. The abstract gives no Sharpe or hit-rate figures, so the size of the edge is unverified. The paper targets futures, but the signal can feed a USO-based strategy.

Implication. fits existing: commodity-momentum — add intensity and uncertainty scores from the news brief as an entry filter or sizing input for USO, and validate on paper trades before relying on it.
