What we read this week 5 findings

Four themes stand out this cycle: crowding-aware alpha decay models for momentum, rank-based ML targets that roughly double predictive Sharpe, cross-asset (crypto-inclusive) regime detection, and conformal-prediction confidence gating for trade sizing — plus a caution from Man AHL that ETF-level sector rotation is less diversifying than cross-sectional trend within sectors.

RESEARCH · FITS

Hyperbolic alpha decay & factor crowding

A game-theoretic model derives a hyperbolic decay curve alpha(t)=K/(1+λt) for factor returns and finds momentum and reversal signals fit it tightly (R²=0.65 vs 0.51 linear, 0.61 exponential), while value/quality factors don't — implying momentum-type edges erode in a predictable, measurable way as capital crowds in. Caveat: the paper was withdrawn in Dec 2025 for revision after a crowding-timed factor strategy underperformed a plain factor-momentum benchmark (Sharpe 0.22 vs 0.39), so the decay curve looks more useful as a monitoring signal than a standalone alpha source.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader / commodity-momentum — use the decay curve as a retirement/re-tune trigger (flag when a momentum variant's realized edge falls off the fitted curve), not as a new entry signal.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Prediction-target design in ML return forecasting

Cakici & Zaremba show that for ML-based return prediction, transforming the target to cross-sectional rank rather than raw return level is the single highest-leverage design choice — roughly doubling both returns and Sharpe ratios in large-cap universes, across a study of 80,000+ stocks in 35 global markets, 1994–2024.

Implication. spawn-candidate: a rank-based cross-sectional scorer that ranks the daily universe by predicted relative return rather than firing on an absolute threshold (RSI<30, X-day gain, etc.) — could be tested as a variant on mean-reverter or momentum-trader entry logic.

RESEARCH · FITS

Regime-aware ML with cross-asset (crypto-inclusive) features

A regime-switching LightGBM framework (HMM-detected regimes, 63 technical/macro/cross-asset features) finds Bitcoin acts as the strongest leading indicator for equities, macro indicators outperform technical indicators specifically for high-beta names, and the model's optimal logic shifts from mean-reversion in bear regimes to risk-appetite/momentum tracking in bull regimes.

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned — add a crypto-market (e.g. BTC trend/vol) feature alongside the existing yield-curve/DXY regime inputs.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Conformal prediction for trade-level confidence

Recent work applies conformal prediction to stock-selection models to produce calibrated, distribution-free confidence sets per trade instead of a bare point forecast; empirical coverage matches the nominal confidence level and most prediction sets collapse to a single actionable class, meaning it's cheap to layer on top of an existing classifier without retraining it.

Implication. spawn-candidate: a confidence-gated overlay that sizes or filters entries by conformal prediction-set width rather than a fixed rule threshold — most naturally tested on news-reactive or mean-reverter, which currently fire on hard cutoffs.

RESEARCH · FITS

Cross-sectional equity trend within sectors vs. ETF-level rotation

Man AHL's trend-following research argues that directional (long-only sector-ETF) trend-following stays highly correlated to equity-index-futures trend, especially in tail events, and that cross-sectional trend applied to individual names/styles within a sector is a genuinely more diversifying return stream than rotating at the ETF level.

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator — consider ranking constituent stocks within the currently-favoured sector by trend strength rather than rotating only between sector ETFs.
