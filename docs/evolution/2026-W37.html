What we read this week 5 findings

Backtest hygiene and factor-crowding evidence dominate the last two months, alongside fresh doubts about LLM-generated signals, a repriced earnings-day edge, and finer-grained regime detection.

RESEARCH · FITS

Point-in-time universe bias inflates backtest Sharpes

Dead Signals Lab reconstructed true point-in-time S&P 500 membership and re-ran a standard backtest: net Sharpe collapsed from 0.63 to 0.06 once survivorship/look-ahead in index constituents was corrected — a bigger effect than any signal choice in the study.

Implication. fits existing: applies to all seven strategies' validation pipeline, not one strategy — audit whether our backtests use point-in-time (not current) constituent lists before trusting reported Sharpes

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Earnings-day reaction beats classic post-earnings drift

Quanter Lab tested nine profitability/predictability cohorts across 20 sealed one-year windows (2006-2025): the classic quarterly-surprise PEAD book nets -0.5%/yr in large caps, but entering on the earnings-announcement day itself nets +2.2%/yr, peaking at a 10-day hold before fading.

Implication. spawn-candidate: earnings-day event entry variant for news-reactive — trigger on same-day earnings releases with a ~10-day hold instead of drift-chasing after the fact

RESEARCH · FITS

Momentum crowds differently (and more safely) than reversal factors

"Not All Factors Crowd Equally" (arXiv 2512.11913) models crowding via ETF-flow proxies and finds momentum crowding accelerated sharply post-2015, but out-of-sample (2001-2024) crowded momentum shows lower crash risk than crowded reversal strategies.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader and mean-reverter — add a crowding/ETF-flow filter to momentum-trader as a minor tune, but treat mean-reverter's reversal signal as the higher crash-risk one warranting a tighter stop

RESEARCH · FITS

LLM-generated consensus signals may be echo chambers, not new information

Quantpedia's write-up of "Do LLM 'Crowds' Produce Investment Signals? An Empirical Test" queries 100 distinct LLM investor personas and finds the aggregated consensus largely reproduces existing factor exposures rather than adding independent cross-sectional predictability.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive — before trusting brief-derived picks as edge, check they aren't just restating consensus sentiment already priced in

RESEARCH · FITS

Finer-grained multivariate regime detection outperforms simple regime splits

A 2025/26 Gaussian HMM study (ResearchGate) using PCA-preprocessed multivariate inputs over 2010-2025 identifies nine distinct market regimes rather than the usual two or three, with expansion regimes showing ~20-30% annualized returns at ~12% vol and contraction regimes flagged well ahead of drawdowns.

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned — tune the regime layer from a coarse yield-curve/DXY split toward a multivariate HMM with more granular states
