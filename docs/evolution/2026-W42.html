What we read this week 4 findings

Four themes this week: idiosyncratic-only reversal, LLM news scoring as a momentum filter, simple models beating transformers out of sample, and regime-gated reversal. Several of the headline numbers are unverified.

RESEARCH · FITS

Asymmetric idiosyncratic reversals

NBER w35840 (Baldi-Lanfranchi, Collin-Dufresne, Daniel, Oct 2026) splits returns into a systematic part and an orthogonal residual. Only the residual reverses, and positive shocks reverse more slowly than negative ones. The authors say a factor built on this absorbs the idiosyncratic-volatility factor and several other short-horizon anomalies. I read only the abstract-level summary, so I have no Sharpe or beta figures.

Implication. fits existing: mean-reverter. Compute RSI and SMA20 distance on market- or sector-residualised returns instead of raw returns. Weight dip-buys on negative idiosyncratic shocks more heavily, since the paper says negative shocks reverse faster.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

LLM-scored news as a filter on momentum

Anic, Barbon, Seiz and Zarattini (arXiv 2510.26228, Oct 2025) use ChatGPT to score firm-specific news inside an S&P 500 cross-sectional momentum strategy. Secondary write-ups (Alpha Architect, Interactive Brokers) report out-of-sample Sharpe rising from 0.79 to 1.06 for Jan 2024–Mar 2025, with the post-training-cutoff window and 2 bps costs. I did not confirm those figures against the paper. A newer paper, arXiv 2609.23703, tests news-based trading with 5 bps costs and a 10% volume cap and finds a LLaMA-3-based model best. I did not see its Sharpe values.

Implication. spawn-candidate: momentum-trader variant that ranks stocks by multi-day momentum and keeps only names whose LLM-scored news sentiment is positive. It would reuse the news-reactive pipeline. Validate the claimed Sharpe lift on paper trading before trusting it.

RESEARCH · FITS

Model complexity versus out-of-sample decay

arXiv 2604.00064 (Mar 2026) argues that extra transformer expressivity adds spurious fluctuation under weak signal. In its EUR/USD tests, transformers had larger errors than a linear benchmark in most windows. arXiv 2608.23761 finds that a claimed market-timing coefficient loses significance under cross-validated tuning, and flags data-mining bias from testing several window sizes. Earlier work (arXiv 2105.01380) finds that more flexible predictors decay more out of sample.

Implication. fits existing: all strategies. Treat tuning of lookback windows and thresholds as a data-mining risk. Prefer fewer free parameters and cross-validated or walk-forward selection. Don't spawn a deep-learning predictor.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Drift-regime gating of reversal (extraordinary claim)

arXiv 2511.12490 (Singha, Nov 2025) claims a value and short-term-reversal factor trades profitably only in stock-specific 'drift regimes'. These are periods with more than 60% positive days in the trailing 63 days, and the paper reports an out-of-sample Sharpe above 13 on S&P 500 data for 2004–2024. A Sharpe that high is implausible for a real equity factor. I found no peer review or replication, so treat it as probable leakage or overfitting.

Implication. spawn-candidate (low confidence): test a regime gate on mean-reverter that only takes dip-buys when the stock's trailing-63-day share of up days is above 60%. Run it as a cheap A/B test with no capital weighting until it is replicated.
