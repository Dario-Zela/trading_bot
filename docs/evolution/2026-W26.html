What we read this week 5 findings

Mid-2026 quant research converges on regime precision and signal decomposition: adaptive regime routing beats rolling retraining (SP500 Sharpe 1.14), sentiment disagreement outperforms mean tone, and the overnight window explains the entire negative VRP.

RESEARCH · FITS

Regime-Adaptive Continual Learning Beats Rolling-Window Retraining

ReCAP (arXiv 2606.00143, KDD 2026) segments history into variable-length regimes using six live signals — VIX, market turbulence, Bollinger upper/lower bands, 5-day price return, RSI-30 — then routes each new period through a learned policy-library via a gated module. On SP500 it achieves Sharpe 1.14 with 19.2% max drawdown; on NAS100 it posts 164.9% cumulative return vs. 124.2% for the next-best rolling-window baseline over the same compute budget. The counter-intuitive finding: the edge comes not from more data but from not retraining on the wrong regime — reusing a stale policy calibrated on the right regime outperforms fresh retraining on a mixed one.

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned — the six ReCAP signals (VIX, turbulence, Bollinger, RSI-30, 5-day return) are drop-in additions to the current yield-curve/DXY gate; the gated policy-library architecture could replace the current binary regime switch with a soft multi-regime blend

RESEARCH · FITS

Mean-Reversion Strength Decays as a Power Law with a Hard Crossover at 2–3 Months

SSRN 6380118 (Dean, 2026) fits a stochastic damped-harmonic-oscillator to 32 years of SPY daily prices plus 462 combined years of equity history, finding mean-reversion coefficient k(τ) = Aτ^(−α): strictly positive at every horizon but negligible at the 2–3 month scale — exactly where the Jegadeesh–Titman momentum anomaly activates. A companion paper (SSRN 6371558, Gao et al. 2026) adds a structural detail: individual stocks revert strongly over 1 month, while industry and factor portfolios display short-term momentum, with a negative cross-term — idiosyncratic momentum return predicts lower future systematic return — implying the two effects are not just co-present but inversely linked.

Implication. fits existing: mean-reverter — confirms RSI+SMA20 signals operate in the power-law 'active zone'; enforce a hard 3-week position cap to avoid inadvertently crossing into the momentum regime where the edge inverts

RESEARCH · FITS

Sentiment Dispersion (Cross-Source Disagreement) Is the Top Daily Signal, Not Mean Tone

arXiv 2505.16136 (Oliveira et al., May 2026) trains XGBoost on FinBERT-scored GDELT news to predict next-day returns in FX and Treasury futures (2017–Apr 2025, expanding-window CV). SHAP analysis ranks 'sentiment dispersion' — variance of tone across independent news sources — as the primary predictor: high dispersion (sources disagree on the same event) clusters with negative SHAP values, forecasting a downward move; low dispersion (consensus) predicts continuation. Out-of-sample Sharpe: 5.87 on EUR/USD, 4.65 on Treasuries, vs. 0.66–0.76 for logistic baselines. A concurrent hedge-fund-perspective LLM review (arXiv 2605.05211) flags that sentiment signals without regime conditioning produce fragile out-of-sample results.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive — add a cross-source sentiment dispersion filter using FinBERT scored over 3+ distinct news sources per ticker; only enter when source consensus is high (low dispersion), treat high-dispersion events as noise, consistent with the macro-aligned regime gate already in the slate

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Overnight VRP Is the Entire Negative Risk Premium; Intraday VRP Is Positive

Harbour Fronts (April 2026) decomposes the variance risk premium into trading and non-trading windows across US equities and indices. The well-documented negative VRP — where implied vol persistently exceeds realised — is entirely concentrated in the overnight session; intraday VRP is positive and statistically insignificant, reversing the common narrative. A day-of-week pattern holds: long-vol strategies opened at the session open are profitable Mon–Thu but reverse on Fridays. Intraday realised vol predicts equity returns 1–3 months out; overnight vol forecasts 6–12 months, enabling horizon-matched signal routing.

Implication. spawn-candidate: an open-gap fade strategy that takes contrarian long-equity entries on stocks with large overnight gaps on Mon–Thu (when gap-reversal probability is highest), skipping Fridays — implementable as long-only equity with market-open or limit entries, no options required

RESEARCH · FITS

Sector Rotation: Median-Ranked Sector Outperforms Top-Ranked Sector

A 2026 MDPI Journal of Risk and Financial Management study of TSX 60 sector ETFs (out-of-sample 2000–2025) finds that quarterly rebalancing into the median-ranked sector (by trailing relative strength) generates Sharpe 0.922 vs. 0.624 for equal-weighted buy-and-hold, outperforming both top-sector chasing and momentum-ranked selection. The explanation: top-ranked sectors have fully priced their relative-strength signal by rotation date; median sectors sit in the early-appreciation phase of the rotation cycle before institutional crowding compresses the premium.

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator — pilot a variant that ranks 11 SPDR sector ETFs by 12-1 momentum and selects rank 5–6 (median) rather than rank 1–2 (leaders) on the quarterly rebalance to capture the early-rotation edge
