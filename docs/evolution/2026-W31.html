What we read this week 5 findings

Momentum and regime research is converging on 'crash-risk management' and 'regime-gated allocation' — volatility-scaling overlays and conditional (relief-gated) rotation signals are outperforming static rules, while a new durability metric (Minimum Regime Performance) offers a way to flag strategies that are quietly decaying.

RESEARCH · FITS

Volatility-managed / crash-risk-aware momentum

A 2026 JPM-forthcoming study (Baltussen, Dom, Van Vliet & Vidojevic, cited via AQR's 'Factor Momentum Everywhere' line of work) finds median momentum Sharpe of 0.61 (range 0.38-0.94) but with drawdowns as deep as -88% and fat-tailed, left-skewed returns; the same literature shows that managing time-varying risk (vol-scaling exposure down before known crash conditions) 'virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio' of plain momentum.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader — add a volatility-scaling/exposure-throttle overlay (reduce size when realized/implied vol spikes or after a sharp reversal) rather than a static 1-5 day hold rule.

RESEARCH · FITS

Minimum Regime Performance as a strategy-durability diagnostic

arXiv:2604.08356 ('Measuring Strategy-Decay Risk') proposes Minimum Regime Performance (MRP) — the lowest realized risk-adjusted return across distinct historical regimes — as a robustness lower bound distinct from Sharpe or drawdown, showing strategies with high long-run Sharpe don't necessarily have high MRP (i.e. look great on average, fragile in specific regimes).

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned — use an MRP-style per-regime performance floor (not just aggregate Sharpe) as a retirement/tuning criterion for regime-based strategies in the evolution pipeline itself.

RESEARCH · FITS

Conditional / relief-gated relative rotation

arXiv:2607.06117 ('Relief-Gated Relative Rotation for QQQ-DIA Allocation') rotates between growth and value sleeves only when rate, volatility, or credit 'relief' signals align, rather than on pure relative strength. Out-of-sample it posted 15.19% CAGR / 0.87 Sharpe vs 14.65%/0.70 for a static QQQ benchmark in the 2022 window, though at high turnover (354-506%/yr).

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator — gate relative-strength sector signals with a simple macro 'relief' filter (rates/credit-spread direction) instead of rotating on relative strength alone; watch turnover cost given retail commission/slippage constraints.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

LLM-sentiment-gated reinforcement trading with an alpha-relative reward

arXiv:2607.16028 trains DRL agents with an 'alpha reward' (outperform buy-and-hold rather than maximize absolute return) using LLM-derived news sentiment as a trade-gating signal; on TSLA test data DDPG returned 54.96% (Sharpe 1.44, MDD 19.2%) vs a 16.45% buy-and-hold baseline, but the paper flags a 'pronounced validation-to-test generalization gap' when the regime flips bull-to-bear.

Implication. spawn-candidate: a news-reactive variant that uses sentiment as an exit/de-risk gate (cut exposure when sentiment turns negative) rather than only as an entry trigger — the paper's own caveat about bull/bear generalization gaps argues for treating this as a filter, not the primary signal.

RESEARCH · METHODOLOGICAL

Uncertainty-aware (distributional/epistemic) RL for regime shifts

arXiv:2607.02864 ('Trading Confidence') combines distributional, epistemic (MC Dropout), and aleatoric uncertainty estimation plus SHAP-weighted reconstruction to make RL trading agents more robust to sudden regime shifts, tested across five major US indices.

Implication. out of scope: requires an RL training/inference stack (MC Dropout ensembles, SHAP attribution pipeline) well beyond our current rule-based mid-frequency strategies and £10k-per-strategy capital footprint — worth revisiting only if the pipeline adds an ML-strategy track.
