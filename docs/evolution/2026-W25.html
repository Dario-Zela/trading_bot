What we read this week 5 findings

W25 research converges on three ideas: stock-level and sector-level momentum are opposite signs (not the same signal); Sharpe-only backtests overfit by ~2× versus composite objectives; macro-aware mixed-frequency attention outperforms price-only neural forecasters, especially in turbulent regimes.

RESEARCH · FITS

Systematic vs Idiosyncratic Momentum Bifurcation

Gao, Li, Yuan & Zhou (SSRN 6371558, March 2026) document a clean sign-flip: individual stocks exhibit strong one-month reversal while industry, characteristic, and factor portfolios display short-term momentum — a 'residual-to-systematic reversal' effect where high past idiosyncratic returns predict lower future systematic returns. The commodity analogue (SSRN 6425598, March 2026) confirms the same split: the residual return component predicts next-week continuation (+momentum) while the trading-flow component predicts reversal. This means aggregating picks to sector/ETF level preserves the momentum edge; stock-level selection amplifies the mean-reversion edge — they are not the same signal run at different speeds.

Implication. fits existing: mean-reverter — RSI-based stock-level reversion is in the right regime; however, filter entries against the prevailing industry-level trend direction to avoid buying idiosyncratic dips inside structurally trending sectors. Also relevant to sector-rotator: confirms short-term sector momentum is a real, distinct signal from stock reversal.

RESEARCH · FITS

Non-Parametric Market-State Similarity Beats Regression for Sector Timing

Zakamulin (SSRN 6792618, May 2026) shows that linear regression from lagged market returns to forward industry returns is weak — but quantile regressions reveal predictability concentrated in tail/downside states. A non-parametric strategy based on historical market-state similarity (find past market environments resembling today's using recent return history; look at which industries outperformed) delivers higher Sharpe ratio, lower volatility, and substantially smaller drawdowns than a passive equal-weighted industry benchmark in out-of-sample simulation on Kenneth French industry portfolios. Counter-intuitive: the signal is almost entirely in the left tail — the strategy adds value mainly by avoiding losers during bad markets, not by picking winners during good ones.

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator — replace or augment the current relative-strength ETF ranking with a market-state-similarity ranking conditioned on the prevailing market return environment; gains are concentrated in drawdown protection, which is critical for a long-only retail book.

RESEARCH · FITS

GT-Score Composite Objective Cuts In-Sample Overfitting by 98%

Sheppert (arXiv 2602.00080, Feb 2026; published in Journal of Risk and Financial Management 19(1), 60) introduces the GT-Score — a composite optimisation objective combining performance, statistical significance, consistency, and downside risk. Walk-forward validation on RSI, MACD, and Bollinger Band strategies across 50 S&P 500 stocks (2010–2024): GT-Score achieved a generalization ratio (out-of-sample ÷ in-sample returns) of 0.365 vs 0.185 for Sharpe-only optimisation, a 98% improvement (p < 0.01). The counter-intuitive finding is that strategies optimised purely on in-sample Sharpe generalise almost no better than a simple return baseline — the disciplining effect of the composite objective is what matters, not the Sharpe signal itself.

Implication. fits existing: all strategies — replace single-metric parameter sweeps (Sharpe/Sortino) with a GT-Score-style composite objective during any parameter-tuning pass; highest priority for mean-reverter (RSI thresholds, SMA20 lookback) and momentum-trader (multi-day lookback windows) where overfitting risk is highest.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Macro-Aware Mixed-Frequency Attention for Return Forecasting (HANET)

Oliveira, Wood, Zohren, Cucuringu & Fujita (arXiv 2606.00624, June 2026, Oxford Man Institute) propose HANET, a hierarchical LSTM-attention model that nests daily asset returns inside monthly macroeconomic windows (CPI, yields, FX rates) via a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism. Tested across 55 liquid futures spanning multiple asset classes, HANET consistently outperforms neural forecasters that ignore macroeconomic information, with particular strength during turbulent periods and improvements in risk-adjusted returns and drawdown mitigation. The key architectural finding — 'regime selection as attention over macro contexts' — means the model learns implicitly which macro signals dominate in each regime without hard-coded regime labels.

Implication. spawn-candidate: macro-attention signal layer — add a monthly-frequency macro feature vector (DXY level, yield-curve slope 10Y–2Y, CPI YoY) as a conditioning input alongside daily price/volume feeds into macro-aligned; replaces the current hard-coded yield-curve / DXY threshold logic with a learned, soft-attention weighting.

RESEARCH · FITS

Sentiment Asymmetry: Negative News Impact Structurally Exceeds Positive

Ryu et al. (SSRN 6751823, June 2026) use consumer confidence indices across developed and emerging markets to show that negative sentiment has a larger, faster effect on equity returns and volatility than equivalent-magnitude positive sentiment — fear-driven drawdowns are deeper and more rapid than greed-driven rallies are wide or slow. Separately, Jarrow & Kwok (SSRN 6754305, June 2026, Cornell/Sydney) show a put-call disparity bubble metric that prospectively detected the 2000 dot-com, 2008, and COVID bubbles on S&P 500 options data 1996–2025. Both papers point to the same structural underweighting of negative signals in symmetric sentiment models — a morning-brief strategy that weights bullish and bearish news equally is leaving asymmetric edge on the table.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive — apply asymmetric signal weighting so that negative morning-brief signals carry materially more position-size influence than equivalent positive signals; consider adding a put-call ratio filter as a regime gate to avoid new longs during bubble-detection flag periods.
