What we read this week 4 findings

This week's search turned up three themes: LLM news-sentiment scoring is getting more rigorous about costs and look-ahead, factor crowding is being used for crash risk rather than alpha, and regime-switching allocation across asset-class ETFs. Fewer 2026 findings were verifiable than expected.

RESEARCH · FITS

LLM news sentiment under realistic frictions

Kirtac (ACL 2026 EvalEval) compares six sentiment models on a daily long-short strategy with 5 bps costs. LLaMA-3 returned about 180% from Jun 2024 to Jan 2026, RoBERTa about 120%, and the Loughran-McDonald dictionary about -9%. A related arXiv paper (2608.12283) argues syndicated stories get overweighted when each item is scored separately, and proposes collapsing related coverage before scoring. Anic et al. (2510.26228) report that ChatGPT-scored news improves a long-only S&P 500 momentum strategy out-of-sample after the model's training cutoff. I only saw search snippets, not the full papers, and the headline returns are long-short with a short sample. Treat them as indicative.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive. Deduplicate syndicated stories before scoring, and use an LLM scorer rather than keyword rules. Check how many of the morning brief's picks come from the same underlying story.

RESEARCH · FITS

Factor crowding predicts crash risk, not returns

Lee, "Not All Factors Crowd Equally" (arXiv 2512.11913, Dec 2025, just outside the window) fits hyperbolic alpha decay to eight Fama-French factors over 1963-2024. Out of sample (2001-2024), crowded momentum showed lower crash risk and crowded reversal factors showed higher crash risk. Crowding-based factor selection did not generate alpha, so the paper frames it as a risk-management tool. Its sample is long-short, so it may not carry over to our long-only book. A J.P. Morgan factor page reports momentum volatility at six-year highs and an elevated Momentum Fragility Index. I couldn't confirm its date.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader. Add a momentum-volatility scaler or exposure cap instead of treating crowding as an entry signal. Keep mean-reverter's position sizes modest, since crowded reversal is the riskier case.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Regime-switching allocation across asset-class ETFs

Verma, Putri and Lesupi (arXiv 2605.27848, 27 May 2026) combine a three-state Gaussian HMM, chosen by BIC, with reinforcement learning to allocate across SPY, TLT and GLD. A Medium post using the same three ETFs with VIX as the regime indicator reports 19.41% annualised and a Sharpe of 1.22 over 2004-2026. That post is a blog, not research, and its backtest is unverified. I found no paper applying HMM regimes to sector-ETF relative strength.

Implication. spawn-candidate: a long-only regime-switching ETF rotator (equity / bonds / gold) using an HMM or VIX state. It would complement macro-aligned and could also gate sector-rotator. Treat the Medium numbers as unverified until we backtest ourselves.

RESEARCH · FITS

Overnight vs intraday return split

A Turku master's thesis (May 2026, not peer-reviewed) finds a statistically significant negative overnight-to-intraday reversal in US stocks over 2011-2024. This conflicts with Branch and Ma (2018), who find the reversal weakened and absent among S&P 500 stocks. Lou, Polk and Skouras (JFE 2019) found momentum profits arise overnight. This is an older finding, but it bears on our entry timing. Results depend on sample period and stock size.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader and control-rule-based. Test whether entering at the open after a strong overnight gap underperforms entering later in the day. This is a low-cost timing tweak, not a new strategy, and the evidence is weak.
