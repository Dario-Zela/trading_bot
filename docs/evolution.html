What we read this week 4 findings

Research this week covers LLM news-signal gains that shrink out of sample, regime models that now cover factors, the decay of the long-only value/momentum premium, and reversal edges that costs erode.

RESEARCH · FITS

LLM-scored news as a momentum conditioner, with a skeptical counter-result

Barbon et al. (arXiv 2510.26228) use LLM news scores to condition momentum weights. They report higher Sharpe and Sortino than a plain long-only momentum benchmark, including in a sample after the model's pre-training cutoff, with gains concentrated in a few high-conviction signals. Didisheim/Kelly et al. (May 2026) report a pure-news long-short Sharpe of about 3.1, against 1.4 for the best JKP factor. Yerra (arXiv 2605.06730) finds that naively appending sparse LLM features hurts ridge forecasts, and only high-conviction tilts recover price-only Sharpe. The long-short Sharpe figures come from search summaries, not the papers, and are unverified. They are also gross and long-short, so they are not directly usable for our long-only book.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive (use the LLM news score as a conviction filter or gate on momentum-trader entries, and trade only the top-conviction names rather than every headline)

RESEARCH · FITS

Statistical jump-model regime allocation, now applied to factors

The Journal of Portfolio Management article 'Allocation-Focused Regimes and Applications to Dynamic Factor Investing' combines statistical jump models, XGBoost regime forecasts, and performance-driven hyperparameter tuning on US factor portfolios (1960-2024). It reports better Sharpe than benchmarks and positive information ratios. The related arXiv 2410.14841 reports an information ratio of about 0.4-0.5 versus equal weight across long-only smart-beta indices. The jump-model results here are from 2024 arXiv papers plus one JPM article of unconfirmed date, so this is not fresh 2026 evidence.

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned (replace hand-set yield-curve/DXY thresholds with a jump-model bull/bear state, and use it to switch exposure among our strategies)

RESEARCH · FITS

Factor crowding and decay: the long-only value/momentum premium has faded

Lee's 'Not All Factors Crowd Equally' (arXiv 2512.11913, posted December 2025, so older than the 4-8 week window) fits a hyperbolic decay curve to factor alpha (R² 0.65 for momentum). Over 2001-2024 it finds crowded reversal factors have 1.7-1.8x higher crash probability, while crowded momentum has lower crash risk (0.38x, p=0.006). Alpha Architect reports the long-only US 50/50 value/momentum CAPM alpha fell from 3.03% (t=5.55) before publication to -0.66% (t=-0.44) after. These are hypothetical paper portfolios with no costs. A Journal of Beta Investment Strategies study links more frequent VIX spikes to momentum disruptions, with less tail risk for long-only implementations.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader (add a VIX-spike or volatility-regime throttle on position size, and treat the decay evidence as a reason to demand costs-net edge before raising its allocation)

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Overnight-to-intraday reversal and the cost wall

A 2026 Risks paper (MDPI) finds overnight/daytime reversal strategies beat buy-and-hold and static rules, but the outperformance drops significantly after transaction costs. A university thesis (utupub.fi) reports a persistent overnight-to-intraday reversal, while older work by Branch and Ma finds the effect strongest in small caps. NBER w35840 and SFI RP 2666 confirm short-term reversal is robust and tie it to liquidity provision. Groot, Huij and Zhou argue the edge survives costs only in larger stocks.

Implication. spawn-candidate: large-cap-only 1-2 day reversal variant of mean-reverter that buys liquid names after a large overnight gap-down and exits at the next close. Gate it on a backtest with realistic T212 spreads, since the cost drag is the main risk.
