What we read this week 5 findings

Trend/momentum research this cycle is about *where* decay happens (tick-size, calendar day, regime) rather than whether momentum works at all — plus a sobering AI-agent replication study showing published anomalies mostly don't survive honest out-of-sample tests.

RESEARCH · FITS

Trend-following decay is microstructure-specific, not universal

Kurth, Eisler, Rej & Bouchaud (arXiv:2607.01550, posted this week) test ~100 liquid futures 1995-2025 and find short-term trend PnL collapsed post-2008 only on small-tick contracts, while large-tick contracts kept working across all signal horizons. They rule out capacity constraints and electronification as the cause and pin it on volatility-normalised tick size, a microstructural effect rather than a blanket 'momentum is dead' story.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader — add a tick-size / relative-spread filter so continuation signals concentrate on large-tick, higher-priced names rather than being applied uniformly.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Momentum returns are concentrated in a narrow calendar window each month

Nathan, Suominen & Tasa, "The Intramonth Momentum Cycle" (SSRN 6426026, posted 2026-03-16) show that in 1980-2025 US equity data, essentially all Winners-Minus-Losers momentum return comes from six trading days per month, ending four days before month-end ($1 -> $18.78 in those six days vs. $1 -> $2.37 the rest of the month). The effect is driven by predictable month-end liquidity-driven selling of loser stocks ("asset dispensability"), not by winners.

Implication. spawn-candidate: a momentum-timing overlay that only takes momentum-trader continuation entries in the identified intramonth window and sits out (or shorts losers) in the remainder, rather than holding a flat multi-day momentum book all month.

RESEARCH · FITS

Regime-switching factor allocation using momentum signals beats static equal-weight

Tai, Leung & Jimenez, "Dynamic Factor Allocation via Momentum-Based Regime Switching" (SSRN 6224058, posted 2026-02-12) build a regime-switching model across Value/Size/Momentum/Quality/Growth and report Sharpe 0.66 vs. 0.59 for an equal-weighted benchmark, annualized return 13.0% vs. 11.3%, over 1998-2025. The edge comes from a momentum-derived regime classifier rather than a new factor.

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned — the regime classifier design (momentum-of-factors rather than pure yield-curve/DXY) is a candidate addition to the existing regime signal set for sizing/rotating exposure.

RESEARCH · METHODOLOGICAL

Autonomous AI research agents replicating published anomalies find almost none survive honest out-of-sample tests

QuantPedia's "Guardrails Make the Researcher" (published 2026-06-30) describes an AI agent that faithfully replicated nine published US-equity anomalies on a survivorship-free universe with pre-locked in/out-of-sample windows; on the faithful build, none survived out-of-sample, and the one apparent survivor turned out to be a construction error the discipline caught. This is a meta-finding about anomaly fragility, not a tradeable signal.

Implication. out of scope: not a strategy itself, but a direct cautionary input for our own evolution pipeline — reinforces locking cost assumptions and OOS windows *before* reading backtest results when spawning new strategy variants, to avoid promoting anomalies that are backtest artifacts.

RESEARCH · FITS

News and earnings-call sentiment mostly agree only at the extremes

Tommi Johnsen's June 2026 Quantocracy note (2026-06-15 snapshot) compares news sentiment to earnings-call sentiment across 21 earnings events over a three-week window and finds the two measures agree mainly at sentiment extremes (very positive/very negative), diverging in the middle range. Explicitly flagged by the author as preliminary, single-window, small-sample.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive — a possible confirmation filter (only act on morning-brief signals that fall in the sentiment extremes, where corroborating earnings sentiment exists) but sample size is too small to size positions on yet; treat as a hypothesis to backtest, not adopt directly.
