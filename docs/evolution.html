RESEARCH · FITS

Abrupt breakdown of short-term trend-following

"Is Trend Still Your Friend?" (arXiv 2607.01550, July 2026) argues that short-term trend-following broke down abruptly, which looks more like a structural regime shift than gradual alpha decay. The authors say fast signals are hit hardest. Equity indices and currencies are strongly depressed, while yields and most commodities are largely spared. I only saw the abstract and search snippets, not the full text. A separate April 2026 paper (arXiv 2604.08356) proposes measuring strategy-decay risk through minimum regime performance. I saw only its listing.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader. Check whether its 1-3 day continuation signals have decayed since a break date. Consider lengthening the lookback or giving the strategy a live-performance kill switch. commodity-momentum may be less affected, since commodities were largely spared.

RESEARCH · FITS

Short-term reversal survives if measured industry-adjusted and around overnight gaps

Stosik & Zaremba (SSRN 6630998, April 2026) find that conventional short-term reversal earns negligible returns across 64 countries, but an industry-adjusted version earns 0.53% per month. Bahcivan, Dam & Gonenc (SSRN 4335622) find that overnight price jumps in 9,283 US stocks negatively predict short-term returns. The authors read this as temporary mispricing. A Korean natural experiment (Kim, SSRN 6752783) finds that the overnight reversal strategy's daily return falls by 6.4 bp for each 1pp rise in alternative-trading-venue volume share, because pre-market price discovery erodes the anomaly. I only saw abstracts and snippets for all three.

Implication. fits existing: mean-reverter. Replace raw distance from SMA20 with a residual return relative to the stock's sector ETF. Add an overnight-gap-down filter. Also consider a spawn-candidate: an industry-neutral gap-reversal variant, with care about pre-market price discovery eroding the edge in the UK/EU.

RESEARCH · FITS

LLM news-sentiment predictability, with lookahead-bias caveats

Kirtac (ACL Anthology 2026.evaleval-1.4) tests news from June 2024 to January 2026 with 5 bps costs. The LLaMA-3 long-short strategy earned about 180% cumulative return, while the Loughran-McDonald dictionary strategy lost money. A supply-chain propagation paper (arXiv 2606.29290) reports a gross Sharpe of 0.86 and a max drawdown of -16.9%. Its out-of-sample results are stronger than in-sample, which argues against data contamination. Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts (arXiv 2512.23847) warns that in-sample memorization inflates many headline Sharpe ratios. Results are mostly long-short and gross of realistic UK retail costs, so a long-only version would likely do worse. I saw only snippets.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive. Score the morning brief with an LLM and evaluate only on post-model-cutoff data. A spawn-candidate is a supply-chain-neighbour variant that buys peers of stocks with strong news, but it needs validation first.

RESEARCH · FITS

HMM / regime-routed sector rotation

arXiv 2605.27848 tests HMM regime-based allocation on a 30% out-of-sample window and reports a Sharpe of 0.83 for the RL approach, against 0.79 for top-1 rotation, so the gain from regime logic is small. A Quanterlab 19-year walk-forward study on US sector SPDRs (compiled August 2026) ranks sectors by 12-1 momentum and concludes that regime calls work as a steering wheel rather than a brake. That is a practitioner source whose selection process is not fully disclosed. A GitHub HMM project flags look-ahead leakage from batch Viterbi decoding as the main pitfall. Treat all three as weak evidence.

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator and macro-aligned. Use regime state to tilt sector weights rather than to move to cash. Use only filtered (causal) HMM state probabilities, not smoothed ones, to avoid look-ahead.
