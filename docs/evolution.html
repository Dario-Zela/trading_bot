What we read this week 4 findings

Alpha decay is uneven across factors, LLM-signal backtests are contaminated by look-ahead bias, overnight and intraday returns split by signal type, and industry trend-following holds up after costs. Most of the evidence is older than the 4-8 week window.

RESEARCH · FITS

Factor alpha decay: mechanical factors decay hyperbolically, and crowding is a poor selector

Chorok Lee (arXiv q-fin.PM, Dec 2025) fits momentum and reversal to a hyperbolic decay curve, with momentum at R2=0.65. Out of sample, selecting factors by crowding generated no alpha. Crowded momentum had lower crash risk (0.38x, p=0.006). This is counter-intuitive: crowding is not a reason to drop momentum. The paper is from Dec 2025, so it is outside the 4-8 week window. I read only a search summary, not the full text.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader. Track rolling decay of its edge and size it by measured decay, not by a crowding proxy.

RESEARCH · FITS

LLM look-ahead and memorization bias in news and sentiment backtests

FinCAD (arXiv 2605.24564, May 2026) defines 'parametric look-ahead bias', where model weights already encode the outcomes being backtested. It reports that in-sample returns on memorized dates fall by up to 67.1% once corrected. Out-of-sample 2025 returns stayed within $8K and Sharpe within 0.10 of baseline. MemGuard-Alpha (arXiv 2603.26797) cites FINSABER as finding that much LLM alpha disappears once look-ahead and survivorship bias are controlled. An earlier GPT-4 news paper (arXiv 2304.07619) found 1-2 day drift in the direction of the model's assessment, with returns shrinking as LLM adoption grows. I saw only search summaries of these papers.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive. Judge it only on forward or paper-traded results after the LLM's training cutoff, and discount any backtest of LLM picks on dates the model may have seen.

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Overnight vs intraday return decomposition

A May 2026 University of Turku master's thesis (Vainio) reports that large overnight gains in U.S. stocks partly reverse during the same day. It reports a long-short strategy built on the signal. Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019) found momentum returns are earned mainly overnight, while value, profitability and investment earn their premia intraday. Mamaysky et al. (arXiv 2507.04481, Jul 2025) find that much of the overnight effect is explained by overnight news. The thesis is not peer reviewed, and a Thai-market study found only limited predictive power, so evidence is mixed.

Implication. spawn-candidate: gap-fade variant that skips or delays entry on names with a large overnight gain and no news catalyst. This also applies to control-rule-based, which buys previous-day gainers, and to news-reactive. The long-only constraint rules out the long-short form, and any intraday timing needs data we may not have.

RESEARCH · FITS

Industry and sector trend-following holds up after costs

Zarattini and Antonacci test industry trend-following on 48 U.S. industry portfolios, 1926-2024. A secondary write-up reports an 18.5% average annual return and a Sharpe of 1.46, still profitable under high cost assumptions. Newfound Research notes that sector rotation by relative strength is mostly cross-sectional momentum. A Validea macro-plus-trend sector ETF model returned 9.1% a year since 2006, 1.9% below its benchmark, so the macro overlay did not clearly help. I did not verify the primary paper, and the sources are not new.

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator. Test an absolute trend filter on each sector ETF, so it holds cash when a sector is in a downtrend, and compare that with pure relative strength. Also check whether macro-aligned's overlay adds value.
