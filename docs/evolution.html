What we read this week 5 findings

LLM-driven event tagging, adversarial robustness, and regime-adaptive ML ensembles dominate this cycle's research, alongside fresh (if still preliminary) evidence that momentum's return has decayed via crowding without a corresponding rise in crash risk.

RESEARCH · FITS

Factor crowding decays momentum returns, but crowded momentum isn't crash-prone

A 2026 game-theoretic model of alpha capacity finds momentum's premium follows hyperbolic decay (R²=0.65 vs 0.51 linear/0.61 exponential) as ETF proliferation crowds the trade (crowding-vs-ETF correlation ρ=-0.63), with momentum's realized annual return falling from ~10% in the 1990s to ~2% today. Counterintuitively, crowded momentum shows *lower* crash risk (0.38x baseline, p=0.006) than crowded reversal factors (1.7-1.8x), i.e. crowding here predicts lower drawdown risk, not higher. Note: the paper was flagged as withdrawn pending revision for insufficient empirical validation in its later sections, so treat as a preliminary, not settled, result.

Implication. fits existing: momentum-trader, control-rule-based — don't retire on crowding fears alone, but recalibrate return expectations/position sizing downward; crash-risk argument for cutting exposure isn't supported by this data

RESEARCH · SPAWN CANDIDATE

Event-categorized LLM sentiment beats monolithic headline polarity, with some categories acting as contrarian signals

LLM classification of financial text into 70+ event types (Rumor/Speculation, Retail Investor Buzz, Geopolitical Tension, Brand Boycott, etc.) reveals that categories like 'Rumor/Speculation' and 'Retail Investor Buzz' carry significantly negative 7-day Sharpe (-0.376, -0.461) and significant IC (>0.10, p<0.01) when followed naively — i.e. they work better as fade/contrarian signals than as buy signals. A monolithic lexicon-polarity baseline strategy achieved an 8% annualized return, Sharpe 5.0.

Implication. spawn-candidate: news-reactive variant that tags morning-brief events by category (rumor/retail-buzz/geopolitical) and fades retail-buzz-driven pops rather than following raw headline sentiment

RESEARCH · FITS

Adversarial headline manipulation materially degrades LLM-driven trading returns

Imperceptible manipulations (Unicode homoglyph substitution in stock names, hidden-text sentiment injection in HTML) cut cumulative returns of an LLM-powered trading system by ~3.5pp on average over 14 months, up to 17.7pp worst case, while the system kept trading profitably (just less so) so operators didn't notice. Finance-tuned models (FinBERT, FinLLaMA, FinGPT) were most vulnerable; general models like O3 were more robust.

Implication. fits existing: news-reactive — add headline/source sanitization and cross-source corroboration checks to the morning-brief ingestion pipeline as a risk control, not a new strategy

RESEARCH · FITS

Regime-adaptive ML ensembles that swap signal logic by bull/bear state outperform static regime flags

An HMM-gated LightGBM ensemble using 63 cross-asset/macro/technical features (walk-forward tested on NASDAQ-100 and S&P 500) posts a portfolio Sharpe of 1.18 (95% CI [0.53, 1.84]), beating XGBoost, logistic regression, SMA 50/200 crossover, and plain time-series momentum baselines. The model autonomously learns to weight distance-from-200SMA mean-reversion in bear regimes vs. beta/risk-appetite momentum in bull regimes, and finds cross-asset features (Bitcoin as a leading indicator) more informative than technical indicators for high-beta names.

Implication. fits existing: macro-aligned — replace/augment the static yield-curve/DXY regime flag with an explicit bull/bear classifier that swaps which sub-signal (mean-reversion vs momentum) gets weight, rather than just gating exposure on/off

RESEARCH · FITS

Systematic sector rotation captures only 40-60% of its theoretical edge without multi-timeframe confirmation

Fidelity research (1962-2024) shows a perfectly-timed sector rotation strategy would have returned 18.4% annualized vs. 10.2% for the S&P 500, but realistic disciplined rotation captures 11.8-14.3%, and momentum-only systematic rotation captures roughly 40-60% of the theoretical maximum (~2-5% annualized alpha before costs, 1.5-4% after). Practitioner framing recommends confirming rotation signals across 1-month, 3-month, and 6-month relative-strength windows simultaneously rather than a single lookback.

Implication. fits existing: sector-rotator — layer a 1/3/6-month relative-strength confirmation filter before rotating, since current single-timeframe momentum likely leaves a large share of the theoretical edge uncaptured
